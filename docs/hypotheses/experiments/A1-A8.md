# Plans for A1-A8: Measuring the Worm, First Half

Experiment plans for hunches A1 to A8 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). Section A is about measuring, so most of these plans test a
*measuring instrument* rather than a brain: the hunch is refuted when the instrument fails to catch what it was
built to catch, on cases where the right answer is known in advance.

The leads were looked up in Crossref by title on 2026-10-13. Where a lead is *found*, the work exists as named and
its DOI is given; none of these was read in full, so every number a plan needs from them is still to be read at the
source when the experiment is prepared. Leads already cited elsewhere in the lab point there instead.

Costs are counted in simulated lives and in recordings. What one life costs in compute and memory is not known
until the arena is proven (STATE.md item 3), so no plan gives absolute figures yet; each will when it is prepared.

## A1. A validation suite with pass bands fixed before any tuning

- **Question**: does a battery of worm assays with bands read from the literature, fixed before tuning, pass real
  worms and fail worms known to be wrong?
- **Method**: for each assay of the hunch, read its reported statistic out of its source - the population mean and
  its spread (standard deviation or standard error, whichever the source gives, recorded as such), with the page or
  figure it came from. The band is the source's mean plus or minus two standard errors of that mean (a standard
  deviation is turned into one with the source's own number of animals; an assay whose source gives neither waits
  until one is found). A band is a statement about a *population*: an assay passes when the population statistic
  computed from a set of recordings lies inside the band, never when a single recording does. Compute each assay
  with the feature tool on a test set of real N2 recordings
  ([data/README.md § The Phase 0 Survey](../../../data/README.md#the-phase-0-survey)) - recordings that were not
  used to choose the assays, the feature definitions or the thresholds of the comparison space. Assays that need
  smell (the chemotaxis index, pirouettes against dC/dt, conditioned chemotaxis) are listed as *not comparable*
  until the Grid has a chemical sense, never dropped.
- **Baselines and controls**: positive control, the real test set must pass; negative controls, synthetic worms with
  one known defect each - reversal rate doubled, run durations halved, no omega turns, undulation frequency 1.5
  times the real worm's - each of which must fail exactly the assay that measures that defect and no other.
- **Refuted if**: the real test set fails an assay (the band is wrong, or does not carry over to these
  recordings); or a negative control passes the assay that measures its defect; or a negative control fails an
  assay other than its own (the suite blames the wrong thing).
- **Statistics**: the real set passes an assay when its statistic's 95% bootstrap interval overlaps the band; it
  fails only when the interval lies wholly outside. A negative control fails an assay when its interval lies wholly
  outside the band.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: the whole test set; 20 seeds for each synthetic defect.
- **Cost**: the feature tool's run over the recordings; no brain is run.
- **Waits on**: the feature tool (STATE.md item 2) and the comparison space ([docs/benchmark/FEATURES.md](../../benchmark/FEATURES.md)).
- **Leads**: found - Bargmann and Horvitz 1991
  ([doi:10.1016/0896-6273(91)90276-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/0896-6273(91)90276-6)); Iino and Yoshida 2009
  ([doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3633-08.2009](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3633-08.2009)); Gray, Hill and Bargmann
  2005 ([doi:10.1073/pnas.0409009101](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0409009101)). Cited elsewhere -
  Pierce-Shimomura et al. 1999, read on its PMC page for
  [the behaviour-measures note](../../research/behaviour-measures.md); Rankin et al. 1990, cited in
  [H6](../README.md#h6-small-bounded-learning). Uncertain - the Wakabayashi, Kitagawa and Shingai 2004 that Crossref
  finds is on the duration of forward runs ([doi:10.1016/j.neures.2004.06.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neures.2004.06.005)),
  not omega turns; whether it is the source meant is still open.

## A2. Score a population of seeds, never one worm

- **Question**: does scoring an ensemble of seeds, with its mean and spread both required in band, catch failures
  that a single seed misses - and can a seed that fails alone be traced to a mechanism?
- **Method**: score a placeholder Program over an ensemble of seeds and over one seed, with A1's bands. Plant a
  defect that shows on one seed in ten (a module that misfires when its seeded draw falls below 0.1). For every
  seed that fails alone, trace it: rerun it with each module switched off in turn until the failure goes, and name
  the module.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program without the planted defect, which must pass both ways.
- **Refuted if**: ensemble scoring catches the planted defect no more often than single-seed scoring; or a lone
  failing seed cannot be traced to the planted module; or the defect-free ensemble's spread falls outside the 95%
  interval of the spread between real worms (the ensemble is not a population of worm-like individuals).
- **Statistics**: the detection rate of each scoring rule over 100 repetitions, each with a 95% Wilson interval; the
  ensemble rule wins only if its interval lies above the single-seed rule's. The spread between real worms gets its
  bootstrap interval from the recordings.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: ensembles of 20 seeds; 100 repetitions of each scoring rule.
- **Cost**: twenty lives per ensemble evaluation, against one.
- **Waits on**: A1's bands, the arena (item 3), a placeholder Program and the feature tool.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A3. A blind test with a pre-registered bar

- **Question**: can a human judge, shown matched segments of real and Grid worms, tell them apart beyond a bar fixed
  in advance - and does the list of tells name real differences?
- **Method**: the hunch's protocol. Segments rendered identically for both sides (the same normalisation, view and
  length), drawn by seed with the selection rules written down before assembly; one expert and one naive judge;
  residual cues disclosed beforehand.
- **Baselines and controls**: real against real (two disjoint sets of real recordings) must come out at chance -
  if the judges beat chance there, the rendering leaks a cue and the test is void.
- **Refuted if**: the real-against-real control is beaten (the instrument is broken), or the judges' tells, checked
  by the per-feature statistics, name no measurable difference (the test reports noise).
- **Statistics**: the share of correct calls with a one-sided binomial bound; the verdict bands (indistinguishable,
  distinguishable, inconclusive) fixed from that bound before the test.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: enough segments to detect 65% correct calls against chance at 80% power, one-sided
  alpha 0.05, the number computed from the binomial distribution when the test is prepared; the control uses as
  many.
- **Cost**: people's time, not compute; run as a milestone gate only.
- **Waits on**: a Program worth testing, the arena, and the maintainer's agreement to find judges - a decision for
  the maintainer when the time comes.
- **Leads**: none named; the classifier two-sample test of [the plan](../../PLAN.md) is its machine counterpart.

## A4. Error fidelity: fail the way the animal fails

- **Question**: can a non-success of a worm, real or simulated, be sorted reliably into animal-plausible errors and
  machine-class errors - and do a Program's error-class rates match the real worm's?
- **Method**: define the error classes from the real recordings first (the hunch: build the error classifier before
  the mechanism) - for example a reversal that ends in a stereotyped loop, a recovery faster than any real worm's, a
  response at an impossible latency. Label a sample of real-worm and synthetic failures by hand, blind to their
  source, and test the classifier on them. Then, once a Program exists, compare its error-class rates with the real
  worm's - the hunch's own test, which waits on the Program.
- **Baselines and controls**: real-worm failures must be classed animal-plausible; synthetic machine-class failures
  (a fixed loop, a recovery in one tick) must be classed machine-class.
- **Refuted if**: the classifier's agreement with the blind hand labels has a kappa whose 95% lower bound is below
  0.6; or it classes real-worm failures as machine-class at a rate whose 95% upper bound exceeds 5%.
- **Statistics**: Cohen's kappa against the hand labels, with a bootstrap 95% interval; the false machine-class rate
  on real worms with a Wilson interval; for the Program, its error-class rates against the real worm's by a
  chi-squared test of the class counts.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: at least 200 labelled failures from each side, the number to be revised once the
  failure rate in real recordings is known; 20 seeds for the Program.
- **Cost**: small for the classifier; the work is in the labelling.
- **Waits on**: the feature tool, and a definition of success per assay (A1); the rate comparison waits on a Program
  and the arena.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A5. The exact trajectory difference as the first ablation measure

- **Question**: do coarse summary statistics miss most of the changes an ablation makes, as the hunch says - so that
  the exact difference of trajectories should come first?
- **Method**: with a deterministic harness, run a baseline and an ablated brain from the same seed. An ablation is
  *detected exactly* when the two trajectories differ at any tick, and *detected by summary* when any benchmark
  feature's two-sample test (the frozen benchmark's test, at its own threshold) separates the 20 baseline lives from
  the 20 ablated ones. Use a set of ablations from modules that never act in the test world to ones that act often.
  Two further parts of the hunch are rules of the harness: speed is never scored while a part of variable speed
  runs, and an ablation that changes nothing triggers a targeted test of that module before it is called inert.
- **Baselines and controls**: the A/A control - two runs of the same build and seed must be byte-identical, or the
  harness is broken and the run void. Whether two fresh lives from one seed are byte-identical is an open condition
  the arena must show first: master-control's README says a recorded life "replays bit for bit on any other
  [machine] running the same build", and also that "the world replays; the minds do not", so a fresh life is
  reproducible only if the Program and the world step in lock-step, each intent landing on the same tick whatever
  the timing over Link. Nothing cited yet establishes that.
- **Refuted if**: of the ablations detected exactly, the summary features detect at least half (coarse statistics
  do not miss most changes); the threshold of one half is the hunch's "most", fixed here.
- **Statistics**: the share of exactly detected ablations that the summaries also detect, with a Wilson 95%
  interval; refuted if the interval's lower bound is at or above 0.5.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per ablation; the A/A control on every seed; at least 20 ablations.
- **Cost**: 40 lives per ablation, and a byte comparison.
- **Waits on**: the arena's lock-step condition above, a Program with modules that can be switched off (the rule
  *Pre-register and ablate*), and the frozen benchmark.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A6. Tune by titration, not maximisation

- **Question**: does titrating a brain's settings against a sealed held-out probe set avoid the overfitting that
  plain maximisation of the score shows?
- **Method**: tune a placeholder Program by two procedures from the same start, each repeated with independent
  tuning seeds - plain maximisation of the benchmark score, and the hunch's titration: screen widely, freeze the
  knobs that do nothing, move one or two at a time, rank by a lower confidence bound, prefer a broad plateau to a
  sharp peak (a setting is accepted only if its neighbours within one step score within the measured spread of it),
  and accept a change only if it beats the default by more than that spread. Forbidden outcomes are hard failures
  for both procedures, never soft penalties. The held-out probe set measures emergent properties - behaviours no
  single knob sets directly - and stays sealed for both until the end.
- **Baselines and controls**: the untuned default; the held-out set is opened once, at the end, for both.
- **Refuted if**: on the held-out set, the one-sided test below does not find titration better than maximisation;
  or maximisation shows no overfitting to prevent - each run's gap between its tuning score and its held-out score,
  less the untuned default's gap on the same two probe sets (so that the two sets' different scales cancel), is not
  found above zero by a one-sided paired t test across runs at alpha 0.05.
- **Statistics**: the held-out score of each tuning run; a one-sided two-sample t test across tuning runs, not
  evaluation seeds, at alpha 0.05, with a bootstrap 95% interval of the difference reported beside it; the analysis
  fixed before the held-out set is opened.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 15 tuning runs per procedure. The test is one-sided, since the hunch predicts the
  direction (titration better held-out), at alpha 0.05; 15 runs give about 85% power to see a difference of one
  between-run standard deviation - by a Monte Carlo of the two-sample t test (100 000 draws, numpy generator seed
  20261013: 13 runs 0.800, 14 runs 0.825, 15 runs 0.850). The number is recomputed once the between-run spread is
  measured. 20 evaluation seeds per scored setting; each run given 400 scored evaluations.
- **Cost**: 2 procedures x 15 runs x 400 evaluations x 20 seeds - 240 000 lives, the largest cost of section A's
  first half, to be scaled down if a life proves expensive.
- **Waits on**: the frozen benchmark, a Program with knobs, and the arena.
- **Leads**: found - Strathern 1997, "'Improving ratings': audit in the British University system", commonly cited
  as the source of Goodhart's law in that phrasing
  ([doi:10.1002/(SICI)1234-981X(199707)5:3<305::AID-EURO184>3.0.CO;2-4](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1234-981X(199707)5:3%3C305::AID-EURO184%3E3.0.CO;2-4));
  Skalse et al. 2022, "Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking" (Advances in Neural Information Processing
  Systems, [doi:10.52202/068431-0687](https://doi.org/10.52202/068431-0687)).

## A7. Three axes, the third being how much is grown rather than wired

- **Question**: can the share of behaviour that comes from learned rather than designer-written structure be counted
  from a manifest - and do the three axes read together catch a special case added by hand?
- **Method**: keep a manifest of every innate unit, connection, reflex and constant of a Program. Count the innate
  share from it two ways and compare: by the manifest (the share of parameters marked innate) and by ablation (reset
  every learned parameter to its initial value; the innate share is, of the benchmark measures in band for the intact
  Program, the share still in band after the reset - behaviour that survives losing everything learned; a Program with
  no learning reads 1 by construction). A special case written by hand is not learned, so it survives the reset: if it
  carries a measure into its band, that measure now counts as innate, and the share rises; if it carries nothing, the
  share does not move - so the control can come out either way. The hunch asks for the manifest; ablation is added
  because a count of parameters says nothing about which of them carry behaviour, and the two disagreeing is itself a
  finding. Then read the three axes together - the diagnostic battery (A8), similarity to the real worm on held-out
  data, and the innate share - before and after a change.
- **Baselines and controls**: a Program with no learning (both counts must read fully innate); a change that improves
  the first two axes by a special case written by hand, which the third axis must record as a rise in the innate
  share.
- **Refuted if**: the ablation count does not record the special case as a rise in the innate share, judged by its
  95% interval excluding zero (the manifest count rises by construction, so the ablation count carries the test); or
  the no-learning Program reads as other than fully innate.
- **Statistics**: the ablation count over seeds with a bootstrap 95% interval; the manifest count is exact.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds.
- **Cost**: one extra life per seed with learning reset.
- **Waits on**: a Program that learns (H6 or H9), A8's battery, and the arena.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A8. A neurological examination below behaviour

- **Question**: does a battery of probes on a brain's internal state locate a lesion - each deliberate lesion
  producing its predicted failure pattern and only that?
- **Method**: the hunch's examination, in its order: vital signs, reflexes and gating, short-term holding, priming,
  attention, inhibition, habituation, long-run stability. Each probe reads internal state (an evaluation tool, which
  the rule *No cheating* allows; nothing flows back into the brain). Run on a frozen copy, restored between probes.
- **Baselines and controls**: the intact brain must pass every probe; a sham lesion (a switch thrown on a module that
  does nothing in the probe) must change nothing.
- **Refuted if**: a deliberate lesion fails a probe other than its predicted one, or none; or the sham lesion fails
  any probe; or a diagnosis is made without the two converging failures and the spared control the hunch requires.
- **Statistics**: the lesion matrix (lesion by probe) against the predicted matrix, scored by exact agreement per
  cell; each probe's band pre-registered.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per lesion; every probe on every seed.
- **Cost**: one examination per lesion and seed, each probe a short run on a frozen copy rather than a life.
- **Waits on**: a Program with switchable modules; habituation probes wait on H6.
- **Leads**: found - Rankin et al. 2009, "Habituation revisited"
  ([doi:10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012)); Casali et al. 2013
  ([doi:10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294)); Turrigiano et al. 1998
  ([doi:10.1038/36103](https://doi.org/10.1038/36103)); Goddard, McIntyre and Leech 1969
