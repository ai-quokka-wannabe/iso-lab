# Plans for A1-A8: Measuring the Worm, First Half

Experiment plans for hunches A1 to A8 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). Section A is about measuring, so most of these plans test a
*measuring instrument* rather than a brain: the hunch is refuted when the instrument fails to catch what it was
built to catch, on cases where the right answer is known in advance.

The leads were found in Crossref by title on 2026-10-13; none has been read in full yet, so every number a plan
needs from them is still to be read at the source when the experiment is prepared.

## A1. A validation suite with pass bands fixed before any tuning

- **Question**: does a battery of worm assays with bands read from the literature, fixed before tuning, pass real
  worms and fail worms known to be wrong?
- **Method**: for each assay of the hunch, read its band (a mean and spread, or an interval) out of its source and
  write it down with the page or figure it came from. Compute each assay with the feature tool on held-out real N2
  recordings ([data/README.md § The Phase 0 Survey](../../../data/README.md#the-phase-0-survey)). Assays that need
  smell - the chemotaxis index, pirouettes against dC/dt, conditioned chemotaxis - are listed as *not comparable*
  until the Grid has a chemical sense, never dropped.
- **Baselines and controls**: positive control, held-out real worms must pass; negative controls, synthetic worms
  with one known defect each (reversal rate doubled, runs halved, no omega turns, undulation frequency off by a
  factor) must fail exactly the assay that measures that defect.
- **Refuted if**: more than 5% of held-out real recordings fail an assay (its band is wrong or not portable), or a
  negative control passes every assay (the suite is blind to that defect).
- **Statistics**: for each assay, the share of real recordings inside the band with a 95% Wilson interval; each
  negative control is a yes-or-no detection.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: all held-out real recordings of the chosen subset; 20 seeds for each synthetic defect.
- **Cost**: the feature tool's run over the recordings; no brain is run.
- **Waits on**: the feature tool (STATE.md item 3) and the comparison space (item 1).
- **Leads**: found - Bargmann and Horvitz 1991
  ([doi:10.1016/0896-6273(91)90276-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/0896-6273(91)90276-6)); Pierce-Shimomura et al.
  1999, already read for [the behaviour-measures note](../../research/behaviour-measures.md); Iino and Yoshida 2009
  ([doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3633-08.2009](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3633-08.2009)); Gray et al. 2005
  ([doi:10.1073/pnas.0409009101](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0409009101)); Rankin et al. 1990, cited in
  [H6](../README.md#h6-small-bounded-learning). Uncertain - the Wakabayashi et al. 2004 found is on the duration of
  forward runs ([doi:10.1016/j.neures.2004.06.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neures.2004.06.005)), not omega turns;
  whether it is the source meant is still open.

## A2. Score a population of seeds, never one worm

- **Question**: does scoring an ensemble of seeds, with its mean and spread both required in band, catch failures
  that a single seed misses?
- **Method**: score a placeholder Program over an ensemble of seeds and over one seed. Plant a defect that appears
  on a known share of seeds only (say one in ten), and see which scoring catches it.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program without the planted defect (must pass both ways); the ensemble
  spread compared with the spread between real worms in the reference data.
- **Refuted if**: ensemble scoring catches the planted rare defect no more often than single-seed scoring, over
  repeated draws of the single seed; or the ensemble spread of a defect-free Program cannot be compared with the
  real worms' spread because the measure is undefined for one of them.
- **Statistics**: detection rate of each scoring rule over 100 repetitions, with 95% Wilson intervals; the
  difference is the result.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: ensembles of 20 seeds; 100 repetitions of each scoring rule.
- **Cost**: twenty times a single scored life, per evaluation.
- **Waits on**: the arena (item 4), a placeholder Program and the feature tool.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A3. A blind test with a pre-registered bar

- **Question**: can a human judge, shown matched segments of real and Grid worms, tell them apart beyond a bar fixed
  in advance - and does the list of tells name real differences?
- **Method**: the hunch's protocol. Segments rendered identically for both sides (the same normalisation, view and
  length), drawn by seed with the selection rules written down before assembly; one expert and one naive judge;
  residual cues disclosed beforehand.
- **Baselines and controls**: real against real (two disjoint sets of real recordings) must come out at chance -
  if the judges beat chance there, the rendering leaks a cue and the test is void.
- **Refuted if**: the real-against-real control is beaten (the instrument is broken), or the judges' tells, checked
  by the per-feature statistics, name no measurable difference (the test reports noise).
- **Statistics**: the share of correct calls with a one-sided binomial bound; the verdict bands (indistinguishable,
  distinguishable, inconclusive) fixed from that bound before the test.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: the number of segments set by the binomial bound for a chosen effect size, written into
  the pre-registration; the control uses as many.
- **Cost**: people's time, not compute; run as a milestone gate only.
- **Waits on**: a Program worth testing, the arena, and the maintainer's agreement to find judges - a decision for
  the maintainer when the time comes.
- **Leads**: none named; the classifier two-sample test of [the plan](../../PLAN.md) is its machine counterpart.

## A4. Error fidelity: fail the way the animal fails

- **Question**: can a non-success of a worm, real or simulated, be sorted reliably into animal-plausible errors and
  machine-class errors?
- **Method**: define the error classes from the real recordings first (the hunch: build the error classifier
  before the mechanism) - for example a reversal that ends in a stereotyped loop, a recovery faster than any real
  worm's, a response at an impossible latency. Label a sample of real-worm and synthetic failures by hand, blind to
  their source, and test the classifier on them.
- **Baselines and controls**: real-worm failures must be classed animal-plausible; synthetic machine-class failures
  (a fixed loop, a teleport-like recovery) must be classed machine-class.
- **Refuted if**: the classifier's agreement with the blind hand labels is no better than chance, or it classes a
  real worm's failure as machine-class more than 5% of the time.
- **Statistics**: Cohen's kappa against the hand labels, with a bootstrap 95% interval; the false machine-class rate
  on real worms with a Wilson interval.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: at least 200 labelled failures from each side, the number to be revised once the
  failure rate in real recordings is known.
- **Cost**: small; the work is in the labelling.
- **Waits on**: the feature tool, and a definition of "success" per assay (A1).
- **Leads**: none named.

## A5. The exact trajectory difference as the first ablation measure

- **Question**: does the exact difference of trajectories detect an ablation that summary statistics miss?
- **Method**: with the deterministic harness, run a baseline and an ablated brain from the same seed, and compare
  (a) the first tick at which their trajectories differ and how far they part, and (b) the benchmark's summary
  features. Use ablations of known size, from a module that never acts in the test world to one that acts often.
- **Baselines and controls**: the A/A control of the hunch - two runs of the same build and seed must be
  byte-identical, or the harness is broken and the run void.
- **Refuted if**: the A/A control differs; or, over the ablation set, the exact difference flags no ablation that
  the summary features miss (it adds nothing).
- **Statistics**: the share of ablations detected by each measure; exact equality for the A/A control.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per ablation; the A/A control on every seed.
- **Cost**: two lives per seed per ablation, and a byte comparison.
- **Waits on**: a Program with modules that can be switched off (the rule *Pre-register and ablate*), and the arena.
  The world replays bit-identically for one build ([master-control](https://github.com/ai-quokka-wannabe/master-control));
  whether a Program's decisions do is the Program's own determinism, which the lab's rules require.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A6. Tune by titration, not maximisation

- **Question**: does titrating a brain's settings against a sealed held-out probe set avoid overfitting that plain
  maximisation of the score shows?
- **Method**: tune the same placeholder Program twice from the same start - once by maximising the benchmark score,
  once by the hunch's titration (screen widely, freeze inert knobs, move one or two at a time, rank by a lower
  confidence bound, accept only a gain larger than the measured spread). Keep a held-out probe set sealed for both.
- **Baselines and controls**: the untuned default; the held-out set is opened once, at the end, for both runs.
- **Refuted if**: on the held-out set, titration does no better than maximisation; or maximisation shows no gap
  between its tuning score and its held-out score (no overfitting to prevent).
- **Statistics**: held-out score of each procedure over seeds, the paired difference with a bootstrap 95% interval,
  the analysis fixed before the held-out set is opened.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per evaluation; both procedures given the same evaluation budget.
- **Cost**: the tuning budget, the same for both - the largest cost of section A's first half.
- **Waits on**: the frozen benchmark, a Program with knobs, and the arena.
- **Leads**: found - Strathern 1997, "Improving ratings" ([doi:10.1002/(SICI)1234-981X(199707)5:3<305::AID-EURO184>3.0.CO;2-4](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1234-981X(199707)5:3%3C305::AID-EURO184%3E3.0.CO;2-4)),
  the usual source of Goodhart's law in Strathern's phrasing; Skalse et al. 2022, "Defining and Characterizing
  Reward Hacking" (Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems,
  [doi:10.52202/068431-0687](https://doi.org/10.52202/068431-0687)).

## A7. Three axes, the third being how much is grown rather than wired

- **Question**: can the share of behaviour that comes from learned rather than designer-written structure be
  counted from a manifest, and does it move the way the hunch says?
- **Method**: keep a manifest of every innate unit, connection, reflex and constant of a Program. Define the share
  by ablation - the drop in each behavioural measure when learned parameters are reset to their initial values -
  rather than by counting lines.
- **Baselines and controls**: a Program with no learning (share zero by construction) and one whose only change is
  a learned parameter; the measure must read zero and non-zero respectively.
- **Refuted if**: the measure cannot tell the two controls apart, or it rises when a special case is added by hand
  (the hunch's own test, inverted).
- **Statistics**: the share over seeds with a bootstrap 95% interval.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds.
- **Cost**: one extra life per seed with learning reset.
- **Waits on**: a Program that learns (H6 or H9), and the arena.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A8. A neurological examination below behaviour

- **Question**: does a battery of probes on a brain's internal state locate a lesion - each deliberate lesion
  producing its predicted failure pattern and only that?
- **Method**: the hunch's examination, in its order: vital signs, reflexes and gating, short-term holding, priming,
  attention, inhibition, habituation, long-run stability. Each probe reads internal state (an evaluation tool,
  which the rule *No cheating* allows; nothing flows back into the brain). Run on a frozen copy, restored between
  probes.
- **Baselines and controls**: the intact brain must pass every probe; a sham lesion (a switch thrown on a module that
  does nothing in the probe) must change nothing.
- **Refuted if**: a deliberate lesion fails a probe other than its predicted one, or none; or the sham lesion fails
  any probe; or a diagnosis is made without the two converging failures and the spared control the hunch requires.
- **Statistics**: the lesion matrix (lesion by probe) against the predicted matrix, scored by exact agreement per
  cell; each probe's band pre-registered.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per lesion; every probe on every seed.
- **Cost**: one examination per lesion and seed; small next to a life.
- **Waits on**: a Program with switchable modules; habituation probes wait on H6.
- **Leads**: found - Rankin et al. 2009, "Habituation revisited"
  ([doi:10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012)); Beggs and Plenz 2003, cited in
  [H7](../README.md#h7-poised-dynamics); Casali et al. 2013
  ([doi:10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294)); Turrigiano et al. 1998
  ([doi:10.1038/36103](https://doi.org/10.1038/36103)); Goddard 1969
  ([doi:10.1016/0014-4886(69)90128-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0014-4886(69)90128-9)); Hedge, Powell and Sumner 2018
  ([doi:10.3758/s13428-017-0935-1](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-017-0935-1)).
