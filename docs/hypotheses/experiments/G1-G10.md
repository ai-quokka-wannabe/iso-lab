# Plans for G1-G10: What the gr-fec Sandbox Leaves

Experiment plans for hunches G1 to G10 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format), under the conventions at the head of [E11-E15.md](E11-E15.md), which
are not repeated: refuted, passed or inconclusive by the rule at the head of [B.md](B.md), pass tests uncorrected and
refuting tests corrected, zero as the smallest effect of interest for a clause that predicts only a direction, and a
check that holds by construction written as an assertion, not a test. Each refuting clause says when it passes as well
as when it refutes; anything between is inconclusive. Intervals are bootstrap percentile intervals, 10 000 resamples,
over the unit a plan names; a plan that tests two contrasts which must both hold combines them by intersection-union
(each at alpha 0.05, no correction), and says so. Every test is one-sided at alpha 0.05, so an interval is read by its
one-sided 95% bound (a 90% two-sided interval). Where a plan has several refuting tests, it names them as one family
and corrects them by Holm's step-down procedure, giving the family's size; Holm here replaces the Bonferroni of
[B.md](B.md) and [E11-E15.md](E11-E15.md), which it dominates in power while keeping the same family-wise error.

These hunches come from the maintainer's sandbox, gr-fec ([docs/research/gr-fec.md](../../research/gr-fec.md)): a toy
world of simulated agar plates, a few dozen concepts, a hundred worms. Its results are gr-fec's own, from one run each
in most cases, and are cited below only as the hunch's origin, never as evidence. Three things set the shape of these
plans.

- **Some of it the lab has already tested** in gr-fec's toy world, on the nights of
  [gr-fec.md §§ 9-12](../../research/gr-fec.md#9-what-the-lab-found-on-taking-it-up-2026-10-26), in
  [H14](../README.md#h14-graded-slow-knobs-change-detecting-senses-and-individuality) § *Tested so far*, and in the
  fixed-point test of [#231](https://github.com/ai-quokka-wannabe/iso-lab/pull/231) (journal of 2026-11-05, § gr-fec's
  Worm by Equation: The Learning Layer's Fixed Point). Each plan says what is
  tested, cites the result, and plans only what is open.
- **The maintainer's ruling of 2026-11-05**, "Pause + equations"
  ([gr-fec.md § 12.4](../../research/gr-fec.md#124-recommendations-for-the-maintainer-to-decide)): the steering-gate
  and hunger-gain series is paused, and the equations route, STATE.md item 8 (e)-(h), is in progress. No plan here
  resumes the paused series; a plan whose toy-world part would is marked *paused by the ruling*. Where a hunch fits the
  equations route, the plan says which step and plans only what that step does not already cover.
- **Any toy-world behaviour test** that compares layouts follows recommendation 4 of § 12.4, which the ruling did not
  adopt and these plans take as their own standard: a development panel and a held-out panel of mirror-paired layouts
  drawn before the first run, the layout as the unit of analysis, a score that does not saturate (food eaten per hour
  over hours 24 to 48, in place of late energy), and the panel sized beforehand. The size, for a paired contrast, is
  n = (z₀.₉₅ + z₀.₈₀)² σ_d² / δ² = 6.18 σ_d² / δ² layouts (one-sided normal approximation; z₀.₉₅ = 1.645,
  z₀.₈₀ = 0.842), with σ_d read on the development panel and δ fixed by a rule in the plan before it is read. Being
  one-sided, it gives panels about 21% smaller than the two-sided (1.96 + 0.842)² = 7.85 behind § 12's figures. It is
  an approximation that flatters: a refuting test run at a Holm-corrected alpha has less power than it states, and at
  n near 20 the normal approximation understates the n a t-test needs, so each plan's n is rounded up and its achieved
  power reported. Layout seeds come
  from a range no journal has used, checked by `git grep` before the plan is pushed. A test on fixed lab plates (gr-fec's

## G1. Think by predicting value over a grown concept graph; turn when the prediction falls

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): one loop - learned edges from sensed concepts to a value node, updated by prediction
  error, and a turn whenever the predicted value falls - is enough for the learning part of H12's plain worm brain.
- **Already tested**, in gr-fec's toy world. gr-fec reports chemotaxis, thermotaxis memory, salt-avoidance learning,
  foraging search and a starvation effect from it (§ 3.1, one diagnosed fix at a time on fresh seeds, which gr-fec
  itself calls forking paths). The lab's own tests: the turning rule follows a closed-form theory, which matched
  simulation in 36 of 36 conditions and set four worms' drift within −9.5% to −1.6% of chosen targets; every decision
  and every learned weight audits exactly, by pathway and into exact ledgers (§ 12.2, point 1, quoting the journal of
  2026-10-26). The learning layer's fixed point was tested in #231 and **refuted**: e45's learners do not settle at
  the least-squares fit of reward on their own features (median D 0.34 to 0.47 against a bar of 0.20, four layouts,
  both learning rates); at the evolved rate they track their experience, beating every fixed fit over the window; and
  the feature map itself drifts inside the window (nodes born, prototypes moving), which the derivation had assumed
  fixed. The no-learning twin did as well as or better than the learner in the rich world (Agarland, 0.858 against
  0.757 for v4b, 0.860 against 0.854 for v5; § 3.2, one run each), which is G10's point, not a refutation of G1.
- **Where it fits the equations route**: G1's loop is the object of STATE.md item 8. Its turning half is (e) and (f);
  its learning half is (g) and its follow-ups (g2) to (g4), the frozen map among them; the composed test is (h). None
  is planned twice here.
- **Question** (what is open): on the Grid, does a Program built on this loop reach the reference statistics in the
  assays where learning is needed, and does it beat its own twin with learning switched off there?
- **Method**: a Rust Program, `g1-loop`: concept nodes over the Grid's senses (at the current pin the odour receptors
  `odour[2][8]` and the intake sense, item 3; heat and salt wait on the organs bump, STATE.md § Needs From Other
  Repositories), a value node predicted by W·F, W learned by the LMS rule of #231, a pirouette when the predicted value
  falls. Its pirouette rate and the length of its comparison window are set from the animal before any score is read:
  Pierce-Shimomura et al. 1999's measured rates (read at preparation) for the first, and the benchmark's pace measures
  for the second. Scored by the frozen benchmark on the assays it holds that need learning; at the current pin none
  does, which is why this plan waits.
- **Baselines and controls**: the twin with `learning = off` (η = 0, W = 0 for life). Switching learning off alone
  does not remove memory: the grown concept graph (which nodes exist, and where; G4) depends on the whole life, and
  so does any novelty or habituation trace of minutes (gr-fec's curiosity trace and touch habituation recover over
  300 s). So the twin is defined with `learning = off`, `growth = frozen` (the concept graph fixed at a stated birth
  set, the same for every twin) and every trace with a time constant over a minute pinned at its baseline.
  *Assertion*, for that twin only: its decisions depend on its past only through state of under a minute (its
  integrator, G8), so on an assay whose reference depends on experience hours earlier (cultivation before a
  thermotaxis test, a pairing before a learned avoidance) its test behaviour cannot depend on that experience; checked
  by raising it under two different histories and finding its test trajectories identical from a shared seed at the
  start of the test. A random walker at the
  same pirouette rate is the floor.
- **Refuted if**: the twin's distance to the reference minus the learner's has an interval wholly at or below zero on
  the learning assays taken together (the benchmark's aggregate distance over them, as defined when it is frozen);
  passed if wholly above zero. The direction is
  fixed: a smaller distance is better, and a positive difference favours the learner.
- **Statistics**: per seed, the benchmark's distance; the paired difference by bootstrap over seeds, one-sided, alpha
  0.05.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per arm, the default, paired by seed.
- **Switch**: `learning = off`, as above; the turning rule's own switch is (e)'s.
- **Cost**: one dot product and one update over the features per decision; at a few dozen features, a few hundred
  floating-point operations per simulated second. Lean.
- **Waits on**: the frozen benchmark with a learning assay, the arena (item 3), item 8 (h) - a loop that cannot yet be
  designed from its equations is not ported.
- **Leads**: Pierce-Shimomura et al. 1999 and Hedgecock and Russell 1975, found in Crossref, not read; Saeki et al.
  2001 and Mohri et al. 2005, found in Crossref, not read; Rescorla and Wagner 1972, not found.

## G2. Walls come from events, not silence

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): an input that fires for the *absence* of something, or that is always on, swallows the
  learning that an event-driven input would carry.
- **Already tested**: only in gr-fec, where three failures - absence nodes, gut fullness as a predictor, imagination
  stepped by clock time - were each fixed by a change to events (§ 2.3, v1; § 3.4). Each fix came with other changes
  in the same version, so none isolates the rule. The lab has not tested it.
- **The mathematics, stated correctly, and its assumptions.** This is a least-squares fixed-point identity, not an LMS
  identity. It assumes (1) a bias feature present; (2) the Euclidean minimum norm, in raw feature units; (3) LMS
  started in the row space of the features (at W = 0, for example), so that its fixed point, if reached, is that
  minimum-norm fit; and (4) convergence to it, which #231 found is *not* reached in gr-fec's worm (median D 0.34 to
  0.47), and which needs a fixed feature map, which gr-fec's is not. Under (1) to (4), take an absence input that is an
  exact affine function of features already present - a = 1 − c beside a cue c and a bias 1. It adds no direction to
  the span, so the least-squares *predictions* are unchanged; what changes is how the weight is split. **gr-fec's
  learner has no bias feature** (`Brain.features` returns the tuned pools, three intensities and their hunger copies,
  no constant), so there a = 1 − c is not in the span: it adds a direction, the predictions can change, and the
  identity applies only to the notebook's learner with a bias, not to gr-fec's closed loop. With reward R·c, the fits are
  w_c c + w_a (1 − c) + w_b = R c, so w_c − w_a = R and w_a + w_b = 0, and the minimum-norm solution is
  w_c = 2R/3, w_a = −R/3, w_b = R/3 (minimising (R + w_a)² + 2w_a² gives w_a = −R/3), against w_c = R with no absence
  input. In general, if the event-only fit is β_c c + β₀, the same algebra gives w_c = (2β_c + β₀)/3, so the cue's
  weight is two thirds of β_c only when β₀ = 0, and it *rises* above β_c whenever β₀ > β_c. So, under the assumptions:
  (i) a rule that reads only the prediction is unaffected at the fixed point, and any harm comes from the transient,
  from a non-affine absence code (gr-fec's absence nodes are tuned bins, not 1 − c), or from a learner without a bias;
  (ii) a rule that reads the cue's weights - G6's trust reads W_int + h·W_hyper - sees them changed by the split alone,
  cut to two thirds when β₀ = 0. "Overshadow" is therefore two claims, and the plan tests them apart. Gut fullness explaining away a
  meal is a third mechanism (a slow consequence of reward correlated with it), and imagination by clock time a fourth;
  neither is tested here.
- **Question**: does an absence input lower the cue's learned weight, and does it change behaviour that reads only the
  prediction?
- **Method**: a mechanism test in a notebook first, then tested Rust: the LMS learner of #231 on a recorded sense
  stream (open loop, so both arms see the same senses), three arms - event-only (cue and bias), exact absence
  (1 − c added), and tuned absence (a Gaussian bin centred at zero concentration, gr-fec's width). Measures: the
  cue's weight at the end of the stream, relative to the event-only arm's; and the RMS difference of the predictions.
  Then closed loop, the toy world on lab plates, gr-fec's A1 and A3: the same arms in gr-fec's worm, the absence input
  added by an evaluation-side wrapper of its features (gr-fec unedited).
- **Baselines and controls**: *assertions*, on the open-loop stream, each checked on the stream's own normal
  equations, not on LMS (which #231 showed does not settle at the fit): the exact-absence arm's predictions equal the
  event-only arm's to rounding, and its cue weight is (2β_c + β₀)/3; and a planted duplicate of the cue (two identical
  features) splits the event-only weight into two equal halves, under LMS from zero as well, since identical features
  receive identical updates. A failure of either is a fault in the harness; the second shows that the measure can see
  a split.
- **Refuted if** (one family of two refuting tests, (a) and (b), Holm-corrected): (a) for the tuned absence arm, the
  cue's weight relative to the event-only arm minus one has an interval wholly at or above zero - no cut; passed if
  wholly below zero. (b) in the closed loop, A1's CI for the
  tuned absence arm minus the event-only arm wholly at or above zero; passed if wholly below. (a) can pass with (b)
  failing; that outcome is the reading above - a split of weight without a change of behaviour - and is reported as
  such.
- **Statistics**: (a) over 20 recorded streams, (b) over gr-fec's 20 elites × 5 worms with the genome as the unit, as
  in #231; bootstrap, one-sided, alpha 0.05, (a) and (b) as a Holm family of two.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 streams; 20 genomes × 5 worms per arm.
- **Switch**: `absence = off`, the event-only arm.
- **Cost**: an evaluation's; one feature per absence node in a brain.
- **Waits on**: nothing beyond gr-fec and a notebook; it fits the equations route as part of (g)'s derivation (a
  feature map's span), and runs after (g4) has said whether the map's drift must be frozen for the open-loop arm.
- **Leads**: none.

## G3. Two kinds of concept node: intensity and tuned

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): "how much" nodes serve more-is-better drives and "which value" nodes serve where and
  which; swapping the kind for one sense changes behaviour in a predictable direction.
- **Already tested**: only in gr-fec (§ 2.3, v2): a tuned odour node born at the starting concentration made the worm
  stay put; tuned temperature nodes sharpened its memory. One version each, no interval.
- **The prediction, from the turning rule.** The turning rule turns when the predicted value falls. With one learned
  positive weight on an intensity node, whose response rises with concentration, the prediction falls whenever the
  concentration does, so the worm drifts up the gradient. With a tuned node centred on a value c₀, the prediction falls
  when the reading moves away from c₀ in either direction, so the worm is held near c₀. The direction of drift is the
  sign of the node's slope, and the turning theory of item 8 (e) should give its size; the plan asks the theory to
  predict, and the simulation to check. On the Gaussian plate a concentration c₀ is a ring about the peak, so "held
  near c₀" means held near that ring.
- **Question**: does the turning theory, given a node's response curve, predict the drift of gr-fec's worm on a lab
  plate, for an intensity and for a tuned odour node?
- **Method**: on gr-fec's odour plate (Gaussian bump, § 2.1), with learning off and the weight fixed by the evaluator
  so the theory's input is known: (i) an intensity node; (ii) a tuned node at c₀, the concentration 6 mm from the
  peak (a ring that stays inside the plate, whose rim is 10 mm beyond the peak); (iii) a tuned node at the peak. For
  each, from five starting distances to the peak, the theory predicts the mean drift towards the peak over the first
  ten minutes, and for (ii) the distance at which drift changes sign; the simulation measures both. The starting
  distances are chosen before any simulation, where the predicted drift is at least a quarter of its largest predicted
  value, so that a relative error is defined. This is a predict-then-check of the kind § 12.4,
  recommendation 3, asks for.
- **Baselines and controls**: *positive control*: arm (i), the code the theory was built for, must meet the bar below
  (median relative error with its one-sided upper bound at most 0.10; the 36-condition check of 2026-10-26 reached a
  median of 0.031) - if it does not, the
  harness or the extension is at fault, and nothing else is read. *Negative
  control*: a node of zero weight, whose predicted and measured drift are zero.
- **Refuted if** (one family of three refuting tests, Holm-corrected: the error, and the sign from inside and from
  outside the ring): the median relative error of predicted drift, over arms (ii) and (iii) and five starting radii, has
  an interval wholly above 0.10; passed if wholly below. Separately, the sign: in arm (ii), from a start inside the
  ring and from one outside it, the measured drift must be towards the ring; if either interval is wholly away from
  the ring, the hunch's mechanism is refuted; if both are wholly towards it, passed.
- **Statistics**: per condition, 100 worms; the median relative error by bootstrap over conditions, and each drift's
  sign by bootstrap over worms, one-sided, alpha 0.05, the three as a Holm family of three.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 100 worms per condition, as in gr-fec's assays; body seeds from an unused range.
- **Switch**: the node kind, per sense.
- **Cost**: none in a brain beyond the node; an evaluation's.
- **Waits on**: item 8 (e), the turning theory with a steering term, as its notebook; this plan extends that notebook
  to a tuned response and fits the equations route directly. The biological claim (odour by intensity, temperature
  tuned near a remembered value) waits on a source found and read at preparation; the hunch names none, and until one is
  read the claim is not used.
- **Leads**: none named by the hunch.

## G4. Concepts grown by a novelty gate

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): nodes born only for readings above three times the sensor's noise give a worm a small
  vocabulary - about one odour, one salt and half a dozen temperature prototypes - that saturates after a day.
- **Already seen, in part**: #231's verifier re-lived one lane (layout 610000, body seed 610100, η as evolved) and
  found that between hours 24 and 48, 74% of worms grew at least one node (median 1, at most 9, against a median of
  20 nodes at hour 24), and odour prototypes moved a median of 0.22 tuning widths, 24% of them more than half a width
  (journal of 2026-11-05, #231's results). One layout and one arm, descriptive. Births slow sharply after the first day
  but do not stop, and the prototypes keep moving: the count may saturate while the positions do not, and the plan
  measures both.
- **What the code does** (`reference/gr-fec/src/embodied.py`, `Brain.features`, read tonight): the three-times-noise
  gate applies to odour, salt and the neutral odour only. Those three and temperature are the only senses with tuned
  pools; in each, a node is born when the nearest prototype is further than the sense's width (0.15 for the three
  chemicals, 1.0 °C for temperature), up to 30 per sense, and otherwise the nearest prototype moves 0.005 of the way to
  the reading.
- **Question**: does the vocabulary saturate, and on what time scale; and how does its spacing compare with the
  worm's discrimination thresholds?
- **Method**: gr-fec's worm unedited, e45's 20 elites × 5, 96 h lives on a held-out panel of eight mirror-paired
  Hardland layouts (an unused seed range), the evaluator logging every birth and each prototype's position hourly.
  *Saturation after a day* is fixed here: in each layout, the ratio of totals - all its worms' births in hours 24 to
  48 over all their births in hours 0 to 24 - at most 0.10. The bar was set after #231's verifier lane, whose figures
  (a median of 1 birth against about 20 nodes) put that one lane near 1/20, so the bar is not blind to the data; it is
  kept, and the held-out panel, which no one has seen, decides. Every pool that reaches the cap of 30 is counted and
  reported, since a capped pool stops births for a reason that is not saturation.
  Hours 48 to 96 are reported, descriptively, with the prototypes' movement in tuning widths per day. Spacing: the
  distance between neighbouring prototypes of one sense, in that sense's units, at hour 96.
- **Baselines and controls**: *assertion*: with a sense's width set to infinity no node of that sense is ever born
  (`dmin > WID` is false when both are infinite, and an empty pool has dmin infinite), and with it at zero a node is
  born at every event step until the cap of 30; both checked on one life
  before the panel, and the count logger must report them exactly. The hunch's own count (one
  odour, one salt, six temperature) is reported against the measured counts, descriptively.
- **Refuted if**: the ratio of day-2 to day-1 births has an interval wholly above 0.10 - no saturation after a day;
  passed if wholly below. The movement of prototypes is not part of the hunch's claim and is not tested. The spacing
  comparison is descriptive until a threshold is read from a source; then the
  question is whether the spacing lies within a factor of two of the threshold, interval over layouts.
- **Statistics**: the ratio per layout, its mean over the panel, and a bootstrap that resamples the four mirror pairs
  (each pair kept whole), one-sided, alpha 0.05. Four pairs give a coarse interval, and the report says so.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: eight layouts (four mirror pairs) × one body seed, the mirror pair as the unit. Eight is a count, not a power
  calculation: the ratio is a description of the gate, not a contrast, and the plan says so.
- **Switch**: `growth = frozen` - no births and no drift after a stated hour - which is the evaluation-only ablation
  of item 8 (g4).
- **Cost**: an evaluation's; a brain's vocabulary is its memory, and the count is reported as such.
- **Waits on**: nothing beyond gr-fec; it shares its lives with item 8 (g4) and should run with it. The spacing clause
  waits on a source for the worm's thresholds of temperature, odour and salt discrimination, none named by the hunch.
- **Leads**: none.

## G5. Instinct and learning as separate pathways

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): an innate drive inside the learner's prediction is corrected by the prediction error and
  teaches the worm that what the instinct over-promised is disappointing; kept outside, it does no harm.
- **Already tested**: only in gr-fec: in Agarland, v4b (instinct inside) scored 0.757 and v5 (outside) 0.854, against
  0.858 and 0.860 for their no-learning twins (§ 3.2, one run each, fixed layouts).
- **The mathematics, stated correctly.** With the instinct I inside the prediction, the error is r − (W·F + I) =
  (r − I) − W·F, so the learner runs LMS on the target r − I. From W = 0 the LMS recursion
  W ← (1 − ηFFᵀ)W + η r F is linear in the targets for a fixed sequence of features, so on the same feature stream
  W_inside = W_outside − W[I], where W[I] is what the learner would learn with I alone as its reward. This holds exactly
  for any learning rate, stationary or not, without any fixed point - it is superposition, and it needs only that the
  features do not depend on W (true of gr-fec's `Brain.features`, which reads the senses). The harm is then
  W[I]: wherever the instinct predicts more than the reward, the features it co-occurs with learn a negative weight.
  In a closed loop the two arms' feature streams differ, and the identity no longer holds exactly. gr-fec writes the
  inside wiring as the learner's initial weights, W₀ = I_w on the intensity features (`reference/gr-fec/src/embodied.py`,
  read tonight), not as a term in the error; the two are the same by the substitution W = I_w + V, under which V runs
  LMS from zero on the target r − I_w·F, with I = I_w·F.
- **Question**: in the closed loop, does the inside wiring lower food intake where the instinct over-promises? (The
  inside drive differs from the outside one by exactly −W[I]·F on a shared stream, by the identity above; the question
  is whether the loop amplifies, keeps or washes out that difference.)
- **Genomes**: e45's `EvoBrain` is outside-only and carries trust, so an inside-wired e45 is undefined, and e45 is not
  used. The arms are gr-fec's hand-built `Brain` (`reference/gr-fec/src/embodied.py`) with `separate = False` (v4b,
  inside) and `separate = True` (v5, outside), every other argument at its default - `trust_k = 0`, so trust is off in
  both - and the same body seeds: only the wiring changes.
- **Method**: (i) open loop: a recorded sense stream through both wirings - an assertion, below. (ii) closed loop,
  gr-fec's worm with the two wirings, on a mirror-paired panel of rich
  layouts, the score food eaten per hour over hours 24 to 48. Before the panel runs, the expected harm is computed
  from W[I] on the development panel's recorded streams: the drive change it implies on food, through the turning
  theory of item 8 (e).
- **Baselines and controls**: *assertion*: on a recorded stream, V + W[I] equals W_outside to rounding, with

## G7. Inner senses gate; they do not predict

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): hunger and gut fullness work as gates on the senses' value, not as additive predictors.
- **Already tested**: in gr-fec, additive inner senses reversed thermotaxis memory (A2 −1.11 °C, v3) and gates
  recovered it (+3.10 °C, v3b; § 3.1); gr-fec's own test of hunger-dependent attraction to a neutral learned cue came
  out suggestive twice and never passed its threshold. "Inner senses as predictors" is in gr-fec's refuted list (§ 5).
  The lab's hunger-gate tests were gates on *steering*, not on value; they are the paused series (§ 10, § 12.2,
  H14) and are not re-run here.
- **The mathematics, stated correctly.** A linear readout W·F over features that include hunger h and a cue c
  separately cannot represent the product h·c: the product is not in the span of {1, h, c}. So "hunger AND cue gives
  value" needs a conjunction feature (a gated copy, gr-fec's hyperedge) for a *linear* learner. A non-linear turning
  rule downstream can produce some hunger dependence without one, so the claim is about the learner, not about every
  brain.
- **Question**: after pairing a neutral odour with food, is a hungry worm's attraction to it stronger than a fed one's
  by more in the gated wiring than in the pairwise one?
- **Method**: gr-fec's worm on lab plates, the neutral odour N (§ 2.2, y2): an hour of N paired with food, then a
  20-minute CI test toward N, hungry (starved an hour) or fed. Contrast: (CI_hungry − CI_fed)_gated −
  (CI_hungry − CI_fed)_pairwise, the interaction. *Genomes*: e45's 20 elites. The gated arm is each genome as evolved
  (`EvoBrain` with its hunger-gated copies); the pairwise arm is the same genome with the gated copies removed and
  hunger h added as one feature of its own. That arm is off-design for genomes evolved with gates, which the report
  says beside the result. The span claim needs a non-degenerate joint support of (h, c) in the training stream: the
  pairing hour is split so that N present and absent each occur both hungry and fed, and the evaluator checks that all
  four corners hold at least a tenth of the stream before any test is read.
- **Baselines and controls**: *negative control*: unpaired N (N and food in different hours), where neither wiring has
  a learned value of N for hunger to act on; the interaction's interval there must include zero, and if it is wholly
  away from zero the hunger state itself moves the CI test, and the paired result is not read.
- **Refuted if**: the interaction has an interval wholly at or below zero; passed if wholly above zero.
- **Statistics**: each genome run in both wirings, so paired by genome; bootstrap over genomes, one-sided, alpha 0.05.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 5 worms per genome, wiring, state and pairing. The power gr-fec lacked: with the smallest
  effect of interest fixed at half the between-genome SD of the paired interaction, the formula at the head of this
  file gives n = 6.18 / 0.25 ≈ 25 genomes. e45 has 20 elites, which detect 2.487 / √20 ≈ 0.56 of that SD at 80% power
  (the same formula, solved for δ). The plan runs with the 20 and states that figure beside the result, unless a second
  set of genomes of the same architecture, named before it is read, brings the count to 25.
- **Switch**: `inner = gate | additive`.
- **Cost**: one gated copy of each external feature per inner sense: the feature count doubles per gate. Reported.
- **Waits on**: nothing beyond gr-fec. Not the paused series, since it gates value, not
  steering; it fits the equations route through (g)'s feature map, as a choice of span.
- **Leads**: Mohri et al. 2005 (the starvation effect on temperature preference), found in Crossref, not read.

## G8. Honest senses are noisy, so integrate before deciding

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): with honest sensor noise, a worm must integrate its senses for about ten seconds before
  deciding, or foraging fails; the optimum depends on the rest of the brain.
- **Already tested**: in gr-fec, v4 (no integration) failed foraging (A5 ×1.42) and v4b (a leaky integrator, τ 10 s)
  passed (×5.82; § 3.1), one fix on fresh seeds; e45 then dropped the smoothing and turned more sharply (§ 3.3). The
  lab's audits split every decision into signal and sensor noise exactly (J10-26, § 9).
- **The mathematics, stated correctly.** For an exponential moving average x ← (1 − α)x + αy of a reading with
  independent noise of variance σ² per step (α = dt/τ), the stationary noise variance of x is σ² α / (2 − α), and
  the noise variance of a difference x(t) − x(t − Δ) over n = Δ/dt steps is 2σ² α / (2 − α) · (1 − (1 − α)ⁿ). (Both
  are standard, given from the recursion, and checked by simulation in the notebook before use.) Integration trades
  this variance against lag: the signal part of the difference is delayed by about τ. So there is an optimum τ for a
  given gradient, speed and threshold, and the turning theory of item 8 (e), with its noise term, should give it.
- **What gr-fec integrates.** Not a sense but the drive: `Dbar += (D − Dbar)/integ` in `EvoBrain.step`, so α = 1/integ
  per step exactly, followed by a fixed ten-step trend. The noise in D is the sum of every sense's noise through its
  weights, so σ² above is that sum's variance. τ = 0 maps to `integ = 1` (`integ = 0` would divide by zero), and the
  τ of each arm is written as its `integ`. Eleven of e45's 20 elites already sit at `integ = 1` (#234's verifier,
  from `e45_evolve.json`), so e45 evolved, in most genomes, the very setting clause (a) predicts fails; that bears on
  the direction of (a), which stays as written and is read with it.
- **Question**: does the turning theory, with the integrator's noise and lag written in, predict the foraging score's
  dependence on τ in gr-fec's worm, including whether τ = 0 fails?
- **Method**: (i) in the notebook of (e), the drift and the A5 turn ratio as functions of `integ` ∈ {1, 2.5, 5, 10, 20,
  40} steps (τ = 0 being `integ` = 1), predicted before any of them is simulated; (ii) gr-fec's worm at each τ, on lab plates, the same measures, with
  e45's own integration gene overridden by the τ of the arm (e45 had evolved little or no smoothing, § 3.3).
- **Baselines and controls**: *assertion*: the simulated noise variance of x matches σ² α / (2 − α) within the
  sampling error of the simulation, at each τ; a failure is a fault in the harness. τ = 0 is the baseline the hunch
  says fails.
- **Refuted if**: (a) the hunch's constraint: A5's turn ratio at τ = 0 has an interval wholly at or above gr-fec's bar
  of 2 - the worm forages without integration; passed if wholly below. (b) the theory: the median relative error of
  the predicted ratio over the six τ, bootstrap over genomes, has an interval wholly above 0.10; passed if wholly
  below.
- **Statistics**: genome as the unit, bootstrap, one-sided, alpha 0.05; (a) and (b) as a Holm family of two.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: e45's 20 elites × 5 worms per τ.
- **Switch**: `integ` itself; `integ = 1` is the switch off.
- **Cost**: one state per integrated sense.
- **Waits on**: item 8 (e); this plan is an extension of it and fits the equations route directly. On the Grid, the
  question is the same, but the noise is the Grid's and is never swept; and τ must match an integration time measured
  in the animal, a source the hunch does not name, found before the Grid part runs.
- **Leads**: none named.

## G9. Noise below the decision threshold is blur; above it, search

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): sensor noise smaller than the turning threshold only blurs the worm's orbits; noise
  comparable to the threshold changes decisions and becomes search; a smooth internal code needs no noise at all.
- **Already tested**: in gr-fec, an interior optimum of sensor noise in two worlds, a little noise worse than none, a
  deterministic worm with a smooth code at CI +1.00, and no edge-of-chaos optimum in decision temperature on eight seeds
  (§ 3.5); "a universal benefit of sensor noise" is in gr-fec's refuted list (§ 5). The lab has not re-tested it.
- **A rule this plan must keep.** On the Grid the senses' noise is the world's and is never swept: changing it to make a
  brain look better is tuning the senses. So the Grid version sweeps only noise added *inside* the brain, before the
  decision, and asks whether any is needed beyond what the Grid's senses already carry. Sweeping sensor noise is done
  only in gr-fec's toy world, as a mechanism test. The hunch's clause that randomness entered best through the senses
  cannot be tested on the Grid, where the senses' noise is not ours to vary, and is dropped there. The Grid part has
  no refuting clause yet: it is written here, not planned, and gets one when G1's Program exists.
- **Question**: in the toy world, does the turning theory predict where noise starts to change decisions - the noise
  level relative to the threshold - and the shape of CI against noise?
- **Method**: in the notebook of item 8 (e), the probability of a pirouette as a function of the drive's change, the
  threshold and the noise (gr-fec's rule, 0.3·σ((−ΔD/s₀ − 1)/τ_d), § 2.3, where gr-fec's τ = 0.3 is written τ_d
  here, a decision temperature, not G8's integration time), and from it CI and decoy escapes against sensor noise at
  eleven levels, 0 to 5 × gr-fec's default in steps of 0.5, predicted before simulation. The sweep scales the noise of
  the four chemical and thermal channels, y0 to y3 (§ 2.2), by one common factor, and leaves touch and the inner senses
  at their defaults. The sweep scales the body's noise in an evaluation-side wrapper (gr-fec unedited); the
  brain's event gate, 3 × `NOISE` on y0 to y2, reads the module constant `NOISE` in `embodied.py`, which the sweep does
  not change, so the gate does not follow it; at high levels noise crosses the gate and
  grows nodes, and births per worm are reported at each level beside CI. From the same notebook, the
  level σ* at which the predicted standard deviation of ΔD's noise equals s₀ is fixed before simulation. Then gr-fec's
  worm at the same levels, on the single-peak plate and the trap plate (§ 3.5), with steering off - this is not a
  steering test.
- **Baselines and controls**: noise 0, the deterministic worm. *Assertion*: at noise 0 two runs from the same seed
  give the same trajectory, bit for bit.
- **Refuted if**: the hunch's claim (a), "a little noise does worse than none": CI at the lowest non-zero level minus
  CI at zero has an interval wholly at or above zero; passed if wholly below. (b), "near the threshold, search": decoy
  escapes per hour at the level nearest σ* minus at zero, interval wholly at or below zero refutes, wholly above
  passes. (c), the theory: the median relative error of the predicted CI curve over the ten non-zero levels, interval
  wholly below 0.10 passes, wholly above refutes; the predicted and measured levels of highest CI are reported,
  descriptively.
- **Statistics**: genome as the unit, bootstrap, one-sided, alpha 0.05; (a) to (c) as a Holm family of three.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: e45's 20 elites × 5 worms per noise level, eleven levels.
- **Switch**: `noise = off` (the internal noise term set to zero), for the Grid part.
- **Cost**: one random draw per decision, from the brain's own seeded generator.
- **Waits on**: item 8 (e); it fits the equations route. The Grid part waits on G1's Program.
- **Leads**: Douglass et al. 1993 (stochastic resonance in crayfish mechanoreceptors) and Faisal, Selen and Wolpert
  2008, found in Crossref, not read.

## G10. Learning pays only when instinct is not enough

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): learning beats a no-learning twin only where food does not smell, smells lie, or layouts
  change between lives; elsewhere it pays nothing, and evolution under a predictable schedule trades it for instincts.
- **Already tested**: in gr-fec, the learner was at or below its twin in Agarland and above it in Hardland (§ 3.2);
  v7 under an unbalanced world schedule turned learning down fiftyfold and starved in unseen cold worlds (§ 3.3). Each
  is one run. The lab's sound CMA-ES (#126) chose slow learning again from two seeds (§ 10.1); it did not vary the
  world.
- **What has no test of its own.** The hunch's *Why* - that the lab's arenas must make learning necessary before a
  learning module can earn its place - is a design rule for the benchmark, written into G1's *Waits on*: no learning
  module is scored until an assay exists where its twin cannot match the reference.
- **Question**: does learning's gain over the twin depend on whether instinct suffices, in the way the hunch says?
- **Method**: gr-fec's worm, e45's elites, three kinds of world, each on its own mirror-paired panel: food that smells
  honestly (random Agarland layouts), food that does not smell (Hardland's odourless lawns with its smelly lawns and
  decoys removed), and smells that lie (random Hardland layouts, decoys kept). Arms: learner and `learning = off` twin,
  in each. The *gain* is learner minus twin, in food eaten per hour over hours 24 to 48. Whether layouts change
  between lives matters only to an evolved genome, so it belongs to the evolutionary half (the Baldwin trade), which
  needs an evolution per kind of world, costs more than a night, and is written here but not planned to run until
  the first half is in.
- **Baselines and controls**: the twin. *Assertion*: in the world with no smell the twin's smell and salt instincts
  have no odour to act on, which the evaluator checks by logging the odour channel (zero up to its noise).
- **Refuted if**: (0) the gain where food does not smell has an interval wholly at or below zero - learning does not
  pay even where the hunch says it must; passed if wholly above. (a) the gain where food does not smell minus the
  honest gain, and the gain where smells lie minus the honest gain: if either interval is wholly at or below zero the
  hunch is refuted; if both are wholly above, passed (intersection-union). (b) "only": the honest gain's upper bound
  below δ_eq passes and its lower bound above δ_eq refutes, δ_eq being an equivalence margin fixed as a quarter of the
  no-smell world's mean gain on the development panel. (a) and (b) are read only if (0) passes.
- **Statistics**: paired by layout within a world, unpaired between worlds; bootstrap, one-sided, alpha 0.05. The
  refuting tests form one Holm family of four: (0), the two sub-tests of (a), and (b).
- **Seeds and repetitions**: by the formula at the head of this file, per world, with δ = 0.5 of the development
  panel's mean no-smell gain and σ_d the largest of the three worlds' development σ_d; for the unpaired contrasts of
  (a), the formula's n is doubled (two independent panels). If the development no-smell gain is not positive, the
  held-out panels are not run and (0) is reported refuted on development, not tested. At least 20 layouts in each.
- **Switch**: `learning = off`.
- **Cost**: an evaluation's.
- **Waits on**: nothing for the toy part, which is not a steering test. The Grid part waits on G1's Program and on a
  smell source that is not food. It does not fit the equations route.
- **Leads**: Hinton and Nowlan 1987, not found in Crossref.
