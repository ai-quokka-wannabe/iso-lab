# Plans for E1-E5: Learning and Memory, First Part

Experiment plans for hunches E1 to E5 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). They follow the conventions at the head of [C1-C12.md](C1-C12.md),
[D1-D6.md](D1-D6.md) and [D7-D13.md](D7-D13.md) - among them the decision between *refuted*, *passed* and
*inconclusive* by the rule at the head of [B.md](B.md), zero as the smallest effect of interest for a clause that
predicts only a direction, and a check that holds by construction written as an assertion, not a test - and do not
repeat them.

Section E is about learning, and most of its hunches are about how a Program should learn. A mechanism built to do
something does it, so the Program side of each hunch is largely asserted. What is tested is whether the mechanism
brings the Program closer to the worm, where a source gives the worm's behaviour. Where no source does, the plan says
the test waits, rather than testing the Program against itself.

The leads were looked up in Crossref by title on 2026-10-31. Found means the work exists as named, with the DOI given.
Where an abstract was read in Europe PMC on the same date, the plan says so and what it takes from it; nothing else
was read, and no number is taken from any source yet.

## E1. Habituation is ten properties, not one decrement

- **Question**: does a single decrement fail most of the properties of habituation that the worm shows, as the hunch
  says, and does a habituation module built for the properties pass all of them?
- **Method**: the properties are those of [Rankin et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012), whose
  abstract says it revises the classic list of [Thompson and Spencer 1966](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0022681) and
  [Groves and Thompson 1970](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0029810) and adds one on long-term habituation. Only those the
  worm is documented to show are used: by the abstract of [Rankin, Beck and Chiba
  1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-4328(90)90074-O), the worm's reversal response to a mechanical stimulus shows
  short-term habituation, dishabituation, sensitisation, and retention of habituation for at least 24 hours. The full
  list of k properties the worm shows, each with its measure and the direction it predicts, is fixed from the sources
  at preparation, before any life is run. Two Programs receive the same tap trains, with a fixed number of trials and
  never a run to criterion, as the hunch asks. One has a single decrement with recovery. The other has a module
  built for the properties, such as the dual-process model of Groves and Thompson or the model of
  [Stanley 1976](https://doi.org/10.1038/261146a0).
- **Baselines and controls**: a fatigue Program, whose response falls with use of the motor side rather than the
  sensory side. It must fail stimulus specificity, as it does by construction; that is checked as an assertion. The
  harness control: a Program with no decrement at all must fail every property except those that need no decrement.
- **Refuted if**: each of the k properties is a test for each Program, a property passed when its effect's interval
  lies wholly on the worm's side of zero and failed when it lies wholly on the other. That makes 2k tests, alpha
  0.05/(2k) each, and the hunch's two claims are read from them. (1) The single decrement passes more than half of
  the k properties: the hunch says it fails most. (2) The module fails any of them. Either refutes the hunch.
- **Statistics**: per-life response amplitudes per trial; each property's effect by its own measure; intervals by
  bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program and protocol, the default.
- **Cost**: a few state variables per stimulus channel - negligible.
- **Waits on**: touch on the Grid (B1's audit of the interface); the worm's tap-habituation curves from the sources.
- **Leads**: Thompson and Spencer, "Habituation: a model phenomenon for the study of neuronal substrates of
  behavior", *Psychological Review*, 1966 ([doi:10.1037/h0022681](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0022681)) - found. Rankin et
  al., "Habituation revisited: an updated and revised description of the behavioral characteristics of habituation",
  *Neurobiology of Learning and Memory*, 2009 ([doi:10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012)) -
  found, abstract read. Groves and Thompson, "Habituation: a dual-process theory", *Psychological Review*, 1970
  ([doi:10.1037/h0029810](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0029810)) - found. Stanley, "Computer simulation of a model of
  habituation", *Nature*, 1976 ([doi:10.1038/261146a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/261146a0)) - found. Wang and Arbib,
  "Modeling the dishabituation hierarchy: the role of the primordial hippocampus", *Biological Cybernetics*, 1992
  ([doi:10.1007/BF00198760](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00198760)) - found. Added: Rankin, Beck and Chiba, "*Caenorhabditis
  elegans*: a new model system for the study of learning and memory", *Behavioural Brain Research*, 1990
  ([doi:10.1016/0166-4328(90)90074-O](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-4328(90)90074-O)) - found, abstract read.

## E2. Novelty as mismatch, proven by an omission

- **Question**: does the worm respond to the omission of an expected stimulus in a regular train - and if it does,
  does a Program with a comparator against a learned model of the train match that response better than one with a
  decaying filter?
- **Method**: that a comparator can respond to an omitted stimulus and a decaying filter cannot is true by
  construction, since a filter's input is zero at the omission; it is asserted, not tested. The question is the
  worm's. Whether the worm shows an omission response to a regular train of taps, or of any stimulus the Grid can
  deliver, is searched for at preparation. If it does, two Programs, the comparator and the filter, receive the
  sources' protocol, and their responses at the omitted time are compared with the worm's. The hunch's oddball
  control is included: a train of one frequent and many equally rare stimuli, so that a response to rarity alone can
  be told from a response to mismatch.
- **Baselines and controls**: the filter Program; the comparator with its model pinned (no learning). The harness
  control: a train with no omission must give no omission response in either.
- **Refuted if**: the comparator is no closer to the worm's response at the omitted time than the filter, the paired
  interval of the distances wholly at or above zero. One refuting test, alpha 0.05. If no source reports an omission
  response in the worm, the test is not run and the plan says so; if a source reports its absence, the hunch is not
  supported for the worm, and that is recorded.
- **Statistics**: per-life response at the omitted time, and to the rare stimuli; distances to the worm's; intervals by
  bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program, the default.
- **Cost**: a learned model of one train's timing - a few variables.
- **Waits on**: touch, or another stimulus the Grid can deliver in trains; the literature search.
- **Leads**: Näätänen et al., "The mismatch negativity (MMN) in basic research of central auditory processing: a
  review", *Clinical Neurophysiology*, 2007 ([doi:10.1016/j.clinph.2007.04.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2007.04.026)) -
  found; it is about people. Sokolov 1963, *Perception and the Conditioned Reflex* - not found as a work: Crossref
  holds only reviews of the book, so it stays an unverified lead.

## E3. Three kinds of learning in three places

- **Question**: does a Program with habituation in the reflex layer, conditioning by co-activation in its memory and
  operant learning by prediction error in its sequencer match the worm on blocking, overshadowing and latent learning
  better than a Program with one learner for all three?
- **Method**: that a prediction-error learner shows blocking follows from its rule (it is the Rescorla-Wagner result),
  so the Program's blocking is asserted. What is tested is fidelity: whether the worm shows blocking, overshadowing
  and latent learning, and how strongly, is read at preparation, and both Programs are run in the sources' protocols.
  The one-place foil uses a single associative learner for all three kinds of learning. The hunch's "no
  backpropagation" is a design rule: every update uses only quantities local to the connection it changes, checked by
  review of the code.
- **Baselines and controls**: the one-place foil. The harness control: a Program with learning switched off must show
  none of the three effects.
- **Refuted if**: the three-place Program is no closer to the worm than the one-place foil, summed over the paradigms
  the sources cover, the paired interval wholly at or above zero. One refuting test, alpha 0.05. If the sources cover
  none of the three paradigms in the worm, the test is not run; what each source shows is reported either way.
- **Statistics**: per-life effect sizes in each paradigm; distances to the worm's; paired differences by bootstrap
  over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program and paradigm, the default.
- **Cost**: three small learners against one - a few hundred numbers at most.
- **Waits on**: food and smell, for the cues; the worm's data on the three paradigms.
- **Leads**: Schultz, Dayan and Montague, "A neural substrate of prediction and reward", *Science*, 1997
  ([doi:10.1126/science.275.5306.1593](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593)) - found. Rescorla and Wagner
  1972, a chapter in *Classical Conditioning II* - not found as a work: Crossref returns other papers by Rescorla, so
  it stays an unverified lead. Tolman 1932, *Purposive Behavior in Animals and Men* - not found as a work: Crossref
  holds only reviews of the book, so it stays an unverified lead.

## E4. Hebbian learning needs a stabiliser and a "used together" rule

- **Question**: in an associative memory trained by Hebb's rule, does a pair of units co-activated many times capture
  the network's activity when unrelated cues are shown - unless the rule has a stabiliser and counts only pairs that
  were both used?
- **Method**: an attractor memory of the size the lab's Programs use, in three variants: Hebb's rule alone; Hebb's rule
  with [Oja's](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00275687) normalisation; and the same with the "used together" rule, which
  counts a pair as co-active only when both units were driven by input. A pair is co-activated many times, then 100
  unrelated cues are shown, and each cue's settled state is recorded. That Oja's rule bounds the weights holds by
  construction and is asserted; so does the "used together" rule's exclusion of units at rest. Capture is not set by
  any rule: it depends on the network's dynamics, so it is tested.
- **Baselines and controls**: the three variants against each other. The harness control: a network trained on no
  pair must show a capture share near the chance of landing in any one attractor.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the full variant captures: the mean over networks of the share of unrelated cues that settle in
  the pair's attractor has an interval, by bootstrap over networks, wholly above 0.05, a margin fixed here for
  "never"; (2) the stabiliser does not matter: Hebb's rule
  alone captures no more than the full variant, the paired interval of the shares wholly at or below zero. Two
  refuting tests, alpha 0.05/2 each. The hunch's keeping association apart from evidence, and its blocking, belong to
  E3 and are not tested here.
- **Statistics**: per-network capture shares; their mean and paired differences by bootstrap over networks.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 networks per variant, each with its own seed for weights and cues, the default.
- **Cost**: a few hundred units and their weights - well within *Lean brains*.
- **Waits on**: nothing on the Grid; it runs on the network alone, in tested Rust. It sharpens
  [H9](../README.md#h9-associative-memory-written-by-hebbs-rule). The worm is not tested here.
- **Leads**: Oja 1982 is cited and verified in [README.md § H9](../README.md#h9-associative-memory-written-by-hebbs-rule).
  Bienenstock, Cooper and Munro, "Theory for the development of neuron selectivity: orientation specificity and
  binocular interaction in visual cortex", *Journal of Neuroscience*, 1982
  ([doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.02-01-00032.1982](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.02-01-00032.1982)) - found. Turrigiano,
  "The self-tuning neuron: synaptic scaling of excitatory synapses", *Cell*, 2008
  ([doi:10.1016/j.cell.2008.10.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2008.10.008)) - found. Zenke and Gerstner, "Hebbian
  plasticity requires compensatory processes on multiple timescales", *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal
  Society B*, 2017 ([doi:10.1098/rstb.2016.0259](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0259)) - found.

## E5. Memories that bind what, where, when and how it felt

- **Question**: for the worm, the part of the hunch that applies: is a memory recalled better in the internal state or
  place where it was formed - and does a Program whose memories bind that state match the worm better than one whose
  memories do not?
- **Method**: binding place, sensory signature, body state, modulator state, a time tag and valence in one attractor
  is a design; that a bound memory is completed from a partial cue, and pulls the modulator state toward the stored
  one, holds by construction and is asserted. What is tested is the worm's side: whether its learned responses depend
  on the state or place in which they were learned is read at preparation. If they do, two Programs - one binding
  state and place into each memory, one storing the association alone - are trained in one state and tested in the
  same state and in another.
- **Baselines and controls**: the unbound Program. The harness control: a Program with learning switched off must
  show no learned response in either state.
- **Refuted if**: the binding Program is no closer to the worm's same-state-against-other-state difference than the
  unbound Program, the paired interval of the distances wholly at or above zero. One refuting test, alpha 0.05. If no
  source gives the worm's state or place dependence, the test is not run. The time tag and episodic-like recall are
  for later rungs.
- **Statistics**: per-life learned responses in each test state; distances to the worm's; paired differences by
  bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program, the default.
- **Cost**: a few more numbers per memory - negligible at the worm's scale.
- **Waits on**: food, smell and a learning module; the worm's data on state-dependent learning.
- **Leads**: Clayton and Dickinson, "Episodic-like memory during cache recovery by scrub jays", *Nature*, 1998
  ([doi:10.1038/26216](https://doi.org/10.1038/26216)) - found. Teyler and DiScenna, "The hippocampal memory indexing
  theory", *Behavioral Neuroscience*, 1986 ([doi:10.1037/0735-7044.100.2.147](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.100.2.147)) -
  found. Whittington et al., "The Tolman-Eichenbaum machine: unifying space and relational memory through
  generalization in the hippocampal formation", *Cell*, 2020
  ([doi:10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.024)) - found. None is about the worm.
