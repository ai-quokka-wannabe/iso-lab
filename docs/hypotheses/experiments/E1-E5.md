# Plans for E1-E5: Learning and Memory, First Part

Experiment plans for hunches E1 to E5 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). They follow the conventions at the head of [C1-C12.md](C1-C12.md),
[D1-D6.md](D1-D6.md) and [D7-D13.md](D7-D13.md) - among them the decision between *refuted*, *passed* and
*inconclusive* by the rule at the head of [B.md](B.md), pass tests run uncorrected and refuting tests corrected, zero
as the smallest effect of interest for a clause that predicts only a direction, and a check that holds by
construction written as an assertion, not a test - and do not repeat them.

Section E is about learning, and most of its hunches are about how a Program should learn. A mechanism built to do
something does it, so what a Program does by its equations is asserted, with the equations fixed here or at
preparation before any comparison. What is tested is whether the mechanism brings the Program closer to the worm,
where a source gives the worm's behaviour; where none does, the plan says the test waits. A Program's free parameters
are never fitted to the data it is scored on: as [C13-C24.md](C13-C24.md) does, each plan fits on one part of the
worm's data and scores on a held-out part, named in the plan, and reports the number of fitted parameters beside each
distance, so that a Program with more of them cannot win in-sample. The power of each test at
its corrected alpha is computed at preparation, by the format's Monte Carlo, and the number of lives raised if 20 is
too few.

The leads were looked up in Crossref by title on 2026-10-31. Found means the work exists as named, with the DOI given.
Where an abstract was read in Europe PMC on the same date, the plan says so and what it takes from it; nothing else
was read. What an abstract reports is quoted as the source's finding; no number from any source is yet used as a
parameter or a target.

## E1. Habituation is ten properties, not one decrement

- **Question**: does a habituation module built for the properties of habituation match the worm's habituation and
  recovery better than a single decrement, as the hunch implies?
- **Method**: the properties are those of [Rankin et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012), whose
  abstract says it revises the list of [Thompson and Spencer 1966](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0022681) and [Groves and
  Thompson 1970](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0029810) and adds one on long-term habituation. The worm shows several: by
  the abstract of [Rankin, Beck and Chiba 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-4328(90)90074-O), short-term habituation,
  dishabituation, sensitisation, and retention of habituation for at least 24 hours; by the abstract of [Rankin and
  Broster 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037//0735-7044.106.2.239), habituation that is faster and deeper at short
  interstimulus intervals, and recovery that is faster after short intervals than after long ones. Two Programs:
    - the *foil*, fixed here: one habituation variable h in [0, 1] per stimulus channel, response 1 - h; each stimulus
      raises its channel's h by a(1 - h); between stimuli h decays to zero with one time constant τ. Analytically it
      shows a negatively accelerated decrement, spontaneous recovery, stimulus specificity (one h per channel) and a
      frequency effect (less decay between short intervals, so deeper habituation). It cannot show dishabituation,
      since nothing but its own stimulus touches h, nor sensitisation. Its recovery runs at one fractional rate 1/τ
      whatever the interval, so after short intervals, where habituation is deeper, the time to recover to a given
      level is longer - the opposite of what Rankin and Broster report for the worm. These are assertions, listed with
      the foil, not tests;
    - the *module*: habituation on more than one timescale plus a sensitisation process, after Groves and Thompson's
      dual process, its equations fixed at preparation before any comparison with the worm, and its analytic
      properties listed the same way.

  Both receive the sources' tap protocols, with a fixed number of trials, never a run to criterion, as the hunch asks.
  Each Program's parameters (a and τ for the foil; the module's own) are fitted to the worm's curves at the shorter
  interstimulus interval the sources use, and scored at the longer one. Where a property cannot be settled
  analytically for a Program, it is measured and reported, not tested.
- **Baselines and controls**: the foil. A fatigue Program, whose response falls with use of the motor side rather than
  the sensory side, must not pass stimulus specificity (its interval must not lie wholly on the habituation side of
  zero). The harness control: a Program with no decrement must not pass any property that needs one.
- **Refuted if**: the module is no closer to the worm's habituation and recovery curves, at the sources' intervals,
  than the foil, the paired interval of the distances wholly at or above zero, at the held-out interval. One refuting
  test, alpha 0.05. The assertions above settle six of the properties for the fixed foil - four shown, two not - and
  that is not yet "most" of anything. The full list is read from Rankin et al. 2009 at preparation, the foil's outcome
  on each is settled analytically or by a run, and the hunch's "fails most" is judged on that list.
- **Statistics**: per-life response amplitudes per trial; distances to the worm's curves, each normalised by the
  worm's between-animal spread at that trial; paired differences by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program and protocol, the default.
- **Cost**: a few state variables per stimulus channel - negligible.
- **Waits on**: touch on the Grid (B1's audit of the interface); the worm's habituation and recovery curves, read
  from the sources at preparation.
- **Leads**: Thompson and Spencer, "Habituation: a model phenomenon for the study of neuronal substrates of
  behavior", *Psychological Review*, 1966 ([doi:10.1037/h0022681](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0022681)) - found. Rankin et
  al., "Habituation revisited: an updated and revised description of the behavioral characteristics of habituation",
  *Neurobiology of Learning and Memory*, 2009 ([doi:10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012)) -
  found, abstract read. Groves and Thompson, "Habituation: a dual-process theory", *Psychological Review*, 1970
  ([doi:10.1037/h0029810](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0029810)) - found. Stanley, "Computer simulation of a model of
  habituation", *Nature*, 1976 ([doi:10.1038/261146a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/261146a0)) - found. Wang and Arbib,
  "Modeling the dishabituation hierarchy: the role of the primordial hippocampus", *Biological Cybernetics*, 1992
  ([doi:10.1007/BF00198760](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00198760)) - found. Added: Rankin, Beck and Chiba, "*Caenorhabditis
  elegans*: a new model system for the study of learning and memory", *Behavioural Brain Research*, 1990
  ([doi:10.1016/0166-4328(90)90074-O](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-4328(90)90074-O)) - found, abstract read. Rankin and
  Broster, "Factors affecting habituation and recovery from habituation in the nematode *Caenorhabditis elegans*",
  *Behavioral Neuroscience*, 1992 ([doi:10.1037//0735-7044.106.2.239](https://doi.org/10.1037//0735-7044.106.2.239),
  registered with a doubled slash) - found, abstract read.

## E2. Novelty as mismatch, proven by an omission

- **Question**: does the worm respond to the omission of an expected stimulus in a regular train - and if it does,
  does a Program with a comparator against a learned model of the train match that response better than one with a
  decaying filter?
- **Method**: the two Programs are fixed here. The *filter* has a leaky trace of recent stimuli and an OFF output that
  fires when the trace falls through a threshold, set as a fixed fraction of the trace's steady level during the
  train, so it can respond after a missing stimulus. Its latency depends on the interval only through the trace's
  level, not through any learned model of the train, and is computed from its equations at preparation.
  The *comparator* learns the train's interval and responds when an expected stimulus does not
  come, so its latency follows the learned interval. With one interval the two can coincide; they part when the
  interval changes. Each Program's latency at the omission is therefore asserted from its equations, and the test is
  run at two or more intervals, chosen from the worm sources at preparation. Each Program's parameters are fitted at one
  interval and scored at the others. Whether the worm shows an omission
  response to a regular train of taps, or of any stimulus the Grid can deliver, is searched for at preparation. The
  hunch's oddball control is included: a train of one frequent stimulus and many equally rare ones. The response to a
  rare stimulus there, against its response as a lone oddball, is reported, not tested, to show whether the worm's
  response is to rarity or to mismatch.
- **Baselines and controls**: the filter Program; the comparator with its model pinned (no learning). The harness
  control: a train with no omission must give no omission response in either.
- **Refuted if**: across the intervals, the comparator is no closer to the worm's response latency after an omission
  than the filter, the paired interval of the summed distances wholly at or above zero. One refuting test, alpha
  0.05. If no source reports an omission response in the worm, the test is not run and the plan says so; if a source
  reports its absence, the hunch is not supported for the worm, and that is recorded.
- **Statistics**: per-life latency and size of the response after an omission, per interval; distances to the worm's,
  normalised by the worm's spread; paired differences by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program and interval, the default.
- **Cost**: a learned model of one train's timing - a few variables.
- **Waits on**: touch, or another stimulus the Grid can deliver in trains; the literature search.
- **Leads**: Näätänen et al., "The mismatch negativity (MMN) in basic research of central auditory processing: a
  review", *Clinical Neurophysiology*, 2007 ([doi:10.1016/j.clinph.2007.04.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2007.04.026)) -
  found; it is about people. Sokolov 1963, *Perception and the Conditioned Reflex* - not found as a work: Crossref
  holds only reviews of the book, so it stays an unverified lead. Sokolov's "Higher nervous functions: the orienting
  reflex", *Annual Review of Physiology*, 1963 ([doi:10.1146/annurev.ph.25.030163.002553](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.ph.25.030163.002553)),
  found by the second agent's review, is a verifiable work of the same year on the same subject, not read.

## E3. Three kinds of learning in three places

- **Question**: does a Program with habituation in the reflex layer, classical conditioning by co-activation in its
  memory and operant learning by prediction error in its sequencer match the worm's associative learning better than
  a Program with one learner for all of it?
- **Method**: in the three-place Program, a Pavlovian protocol goes through the co-activation learner, which has no
  error term, so whether it shows blocking or overshadowing is not given by its rule: it is an open question, measured
  and reported. The *one-place foil* is fixed here as a single prediction-error learner of the kind
  [Schultz, Dayan and Montague 1997](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593) relate to dopamine; that it blocks
  follows from its error term and is asserted. The worm's side: by the abstract of [Rankin
  2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.114.3.496), the worm's habituation shows context conditioning with extinction
  and latent inhibition. Whether it shows blocking, overshadowing and latent learning is searched for at preparation.
  Both Programs are run in the protocols the sources use, their parameters fitted on half of each source's reported
  conditions and scored on the other half. The hunch's "no backpropagation" is a design rule: every
  update uses only quantities local to the connection it changes, checked by review of the code.
- **Baselines and controls**: the one-place foil. The harness control: a Program with learning switched off must show
  none of the effects.
- **Refuted if**: the three-place Program is no closer to the worm than the one-place foil, summed over the paradigms
  the sources cover, the paired interval wholly at or above zero. One refuting test, alpha 0.05. Each paradigm's
  distance is divided by the worm's between-animal spread of that effect before summing, so that paradigms in
  different units weigh alike. If the sources cover no paradigm beyond Rankin 2000's, the test runs on that one.
- **Statistics**: per-life effect sizes in each paradigm; normalised distances to the worm's; paired differences by
  bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program and paradigm, the default.
- **Cost**: three small learners against one - a few hundred numbers at most.
- **Waits on**: touch and a chemical cue, for Rankin 2000's protocol; food and smell for the others; the worm's data.
- **Leads**: Schultz, Dayan and Montague, "A neural substrate of prediction and reward", *Science*, 1997
  ([doi:10.1126/science.275.5306.1593](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593)) - found. Rescorla and Wagner
  1972, a chapter in *Classical Conditioning II* - not found as a work: Crossref returns other papers by Rescorla, so
  it stays an unverified lead, and nothing here rests on it. Tolman 1932, *Purposive Behavior in Animals and Men* -
  not found as a work: Crossref holds only reviews of the book, so it stays an unverified lead. Added: Rankin,
  "Context conditioning in habituation in the nematode *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *Behavioral Neuroscience*, 2000
  ([doi:10.1037/0735-7044.114.3.496](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.114.3.496)) - found by the second agent's
  review, abstract read.

## E4. Hebbian learning needs a stabiliser and a "used together" rule

- **Question**: in an associative memory trained by Hebb's rule, does a pair of units co-activated many times capture
  the network's activity when unrelated cues are shown - and does a stabiliser prevent it, and does the "used
  together" rule prevent hubs at rest from capturing it?
- **Method**: an attractor memory of 200 units storing 10 dense random patterns (each unit ±1 with equal chance)
  besides the trained pair, fixed here: a load of 11/200 = 0.055. That is below the capacity of a Hebbian network of
  dense random patterns as [Amit, Gutfreund and Sompolinsky 1985](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.55.1530) are
  known for computing it, about 0.14 - a figure not read from the paper, so the capacity is measured first: every
  stored pattern must be a fixed point of the trained network before any test, or the load is lowered. Three
  variants:
    - Hebb's rule alone;
    - Hebb's rule with [Oja's](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00275687) normalisation, applied to each unit's incoming
      weights;
    - the same with the "used together" rule, which counts a pair as co-active only when both units were driven by
      input.

  Oja's rule bounds a unit's incoming weights only for a small enough learning rate, so the bound is checked on every
  step as a harness assertion: each row's norm stays between 0.5 and 2. A violation stops the run, and the learning
  rate is halved before any test is read. Two conditions:
    - *standard*: a pair is co-activated by input many times;
    - *resting high*: a hub of units is held at a high resting level without input while other patterns are learned.

  Then 100 unrelated cues are shown and each settled state is recorded.
- **Baselines and controls**: the variants against each other. The no-pair control: the same network trained without
  the pair, whose capture share into the pair's would-be attractor (or the hub's) is the chance level that every
  capture share is measured against.
- **Refuted if**:
    1. The full variant captures: in the standard condition, or in the resting-high condition - one test each - its
       capture share exceeds the no-pair control's by an interval wholly above 0.05, a margin fixed here for "never".
    2. The stabiliser does not matter: in the standard condition, Hebb's rule alone captures no more than Hebb with
       Oja, the paired interval of the shares wholly at or below zero.
    3. The "used together" rule does not matter: in the resting-high condition, Hebb with Oja captures into the hub
       no more than the full variant, the paired interval wholly at or below zero.

  Four refuting tests, alpha 0.05/4 each. The hunch's keeping association apart from evidence, and its blocking,
  belong to E3.
- **Statistics**: per-network capture shares; their differences from the control and between variants by bootstrap
  over networks.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 networks per variant and condition, each with its own seed for weights, patterns and
  cues, the default.
- **Cost**: 200 units and 40 000 weights - within *Lean brains*.
- **Waits on**: nothing on the Grid; it runs on the network alone, in tested Rust. It sharpens
  [H9](../README.md#h9-associative-memory-written-by-hebbs-rule). The worm is not tested here.
- **Leads**: Oja 1982 is cited and verified in [README.md § H9](../README.md#h9-associative-memory-written-by-hebbs-rule).
  Bienenstock, Cooper and Munro, "Theory for the development of neuron selectivity: orientation specificity and
  binocular interaction in visual cortex", *Journal of Neuroscience*, 1982
  ([doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.02-01-00032.1982](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.02-01-00032.1982)) - found. Turrigiano,
  "The self-tuning neuron: synaptic scaling of excitatory synapses", *Cell*, 2008
  ([doi:10.1016/j.cell.2008.10.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2008.10.008)) - found. Zenke and Gerstner, "Hebbian
  plasticity requires compensatory processes on multiple timescales", *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal
  Society B*, 2017 ([doi:10.1098/rstb.2016.0259](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0259)) - found. Added, found by the
  second agent's review: Amit, Gutfreund and Sompolinsky, "Storing infinite numbers of patterns in a spin-glass model
  of neural networks", *Physical Review Letters*, 1985
  ([doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.55.1530](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.55.1530)) - found, not read.

## E5. Memories that bind what, where, when and how it felt

- **Question**: for the worm, the part of the hunch that applies: is a learned response kept better when the context
  or state of testing matches that of learning - and does a Program whose memories bind context and state match the
  worm better than one whose memories do not?
- **Method**: the worm shows both. By the abstract of [Rankin 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.114.3.496),
  retention of habituation to tap was greater when training and testing shared a chemosensory cue. By the abstract of
  [Bettinger and McIntire 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1601-183x.2004.00080.x), olfactory adaptation acquired under
  ethanol is displayed only when ethanol is present again. Rankin 2000's protocol is the one the Grid can host,
  needing taps and a chemical cue; a drug state has no Grid counterpart yet. Two Programs - one binding the context
  into each memory, one storing the habituation alone - are trained with the cue and tested with and without it.
  Rankin 2000 reports both a 10 s and a 60 s interstimulus interval: parameters are fitted at 10 s and scored at
  60 s. The unbound Program's context difference is zero by construction, so the comparison is between the binding
  Program's held-out prediction and the worm, and the clause below is read that way.
  Binding, and completion of a memory from a partial cue, hold by construction only while the memory is within its
  capacity and the cue within its basin. The network's capacity and basin are measured first, and the assertion holds
  for the load and cue overlap used, which are fixed inside them before the test.
- **Baselines and controls**: the unbound Program. The harness control: a Program with learning switched off must show
  no retention in either test.
- **Refuted if**: at the held-out interval, the binding Program's predicted difference between same-context and
  other-context retention is no closer to the worm's than zero - the unbound Program's - is, the interval of the
  difference in distances wholly at or above zero. One refuting test,
  alpha 0.05. The time tag, valence and mood-congruent recall are for later rungs.
- **Statistics**: per-life retention in each test; distances to the worm's difference, normalised by its spread;
  paired differences by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program, the default.
- **Cost**: a few more numbers per memory - negligible at the worm's scale.
- **Waits on**: touch and a chemical cue on the Grid; the worm's retention figures, read from Rankin 2000 at
  preparation.
- **Leads**: Clayton and Dickinson, "Episodic-like memory during cache recovery by scrub jays", *Nature*, 1998
  ([doi:10.1038/26216](https://doi.org/10.1038/26216)) - found. Teyler and DiScenna, "The hippocampal memory indexing
  theory", *Behavioral Neuroscience*, 1986 ([doi:10.1037/0735-7044.100.2.147](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.100.2.147)) -
  found. Whittington et al., "The Tolman-Eichenbaum machine: unifying space and relational memory through
  generalization in the hippocampal formation", *Cell*, 2020
  ([doi:10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.024)) - found. None of the three is about the
  worm. Added, both found by the second agent's review, abstracts read: Rankin 2000 (above, under E3), and Bettinger and
  McIntire, "State-dependency in *C. elegans*", *Genes, Brain and Behavior*, 2004
  ([doi:10.1111/j.1601-183x.2004.00080.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1601-183x.2004.00080.x)).
