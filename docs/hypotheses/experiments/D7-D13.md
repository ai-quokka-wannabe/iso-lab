# Plans for D7-D13: Chemistry and Affect, Second Half

Experiment plans for hunches D7 to D13 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format), and the answer to the question about play that
[A9-A15.md § A10](A9-A15.md#a10-welfare-and-affect-read-from-behaviour-alone) left to this section. They follow the
conventions at the head of [C1-C12.md](C1-C12.md) and [D1-D6.md](D1-D6.md) - among them the decision between
*refuted*, *passed* and *inconclusive* by the rule at the head of [B.md](B.md), and zero as the smallest effect of
interest for a clause that predicts only a direction, with what that costs in power - and do not repeat them. As in
D1-D6, nearly every plan waits on food and a sense of intake (STATE.md, *Needs From Other Repositories*).

Several hunches in this half are design rules rather than claims about the worm (D7, D8), or are aimed at rungs above
the worm (D11, and the grief and social halves of D12). A plan for a design rule tests whether a Program built by it
avoids what the rule forbids; it does not test the worm, and says so. Where a check holds by construction - an
invariant the code enforces - it is listed as an assertion over every tick, not counted as a test.

The leads were looked up in Crossref by title on 2026-10-31; found means the work exists as named, with the DOI
given. Where an abstract was read, in Europe PMC on the same date, the plan says so and says what it takes from it;
nothing else was read, and no number is taken from any source yet.

## D7. Roles for a handful of modulators

- **Question**: does a Program that carries the hunch's modulator roles avoid each of the five outcomes the hunch
  forbids, over long lives?
- **Method**: this plan is a registry and a set of monitors, not a Program of its own. Each role is built by a plan
  that owns it and registered here with its forbidden outcome. The owners, where one exists: the noradrenaline-like
  reset at a change point by D4; patience by D9; the tonic level that sets vigour by
  [C13](C13-C24.md#c13-vigour-from-the-average-reward-rate-one-global-slowing-factor). The rest have no owning plan yet,
  and the nearest hunches are named so that a later plan can claim them: the novelty bonus (E2, E13), the weighting of
  corroborated against uncorroborated events (E7), the switch between encoding and retrieval (E11's gates), and sleep
  pressure (E14). The monitors read the Program's internal variables, which is allowed for an evaluation tool; nothing
  they read is fed back to the Program. Lives of the longest length the arena allows, in the worlds of the owning plans.
- **Baselines and controls**: each monitor is first run on a Program built to break it (a planted fault: an
  uncorroborated event given double weight, a novelty bonus that never habituates, a reset switched off), and must
  catch the fault, or the monitor is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) old focus dominates after a change point - the time to switch to the new option after a change
  point, with the reset on, is not shorter than with it off, the paired interval wholly at or above zero. One refuting
  test, alpha 0.05; it is a test because the switch time depends on what the Program learned and on the world, not on a
  rule. The other four forbidden outcomes - uncorroborated events writing more than corroborated ones, a write to
  memory while the retrieval flag is set, a novelty bonus that does not fall with repeats, and sleep pressure falling
  outside sleep - are properties of the update rules as their owners will write them, so each is asserted on every
  tick, and a single violation is a bug, fixed before any test is read. If an owner builds one of them by learning
  rather than by rule, its monitor becomes a test of the same form as (1), with its own line here and the alpha divided
  accordingly.
- **Statistics**: per-life switch times; intervals by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per arm, the default.
- **Cost**: the monitors only read; their cost is the logging of a few variables per tick.
- **Waits on**: the owning plans' Programs (D4, D9, C13), and plans for the roles that have none. The roles' worm
  counterparts - which of the worm's modulators, if any, does each job - are a literature question, read at
  preparation; this plan tests the design, not the worm. The social-salience term waits on a second creature on the
  Grid.
- **Leads**: the hunch gives none.

## D8. What not to build in chemistry

- **Question**: two checks on the hunch's design rules. When arousal rises, does a Program whose arousal amplifies the
  existing priority order (winner takes more) gather more food than one whose arousal scales every priority alike? And
  does every inverted-U curve the lab's Programs
  show come from two opposed monotone effects, never from one drawn by hand?
- **Method**: (a) a Program choosing between two cues of unequal value, with an arousal variable set to five levels
  on a frozen copy, in two versions: arousal multiplying the difference between the options' priorities (winner
  takes more), and arousal scaling every priority by the same factor. The second is the hunch's foil. Whether uniform
  scaling changes choice at all depends on the decision rule - under an argmax it changes nothing, under a softmax it
  sharpens choice - so the rule is fixed and stated before the run. That winner-takes-more raises the share choosing
  the stronger cue follows from the rule by algebra (under a softmax, the share is a logistic function of arousal times
  the difference), so it is asserted, not tested. What is tested is the hunch's claim that this beats uniform
  scaling: the food the two versions gather at each arousal level, in a world where the cues' values change over the
  life, so that sharper choice is not obviously better. (b) For every Program in the lab with a
  performance measure plotted against a modulator level, the curve is fitted with a quadratic term; where an interior
  maximum appears, each of the opposed effects that should produce it is switched off in turn.
- **Baselines and controls**: for (a), arousal pinned at its mean. For (b), the harness control is a Program with an
  inverted U planted directly in one map, which the check must flag.
- **Refuted if**: (1) at the highest arousal level, winner-takes-more gathers no more food than uniform scaling, the
  paired interval of the difference wholly at or below zero; (2) an inverted U survives with one of its opposed effects
  switched off - the quadratic coefficient's interval lies wholly below zero with the effect off - so that it was not
  made by the two effects. Clause (2) is applied to each of the two effects of each inverted U found, so with c curves
  there are 1 + 2c refuting tests, alpha 0.05/(1 + 2c) each, a rule fixed here. The hunch's other two rules - no single arousal
  scalar feeding opposite modes, no modulator that only annotates - are design reviews, the second already tested by
  [D5](D1-D6.md#d5-a-modulator-design-law)'s clause (1).
- **Statistics**: per-life food gathered and choice shares; quadratic fits with intervals by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per arousal level, five levels, the default.
- **Cost**: a multiply per option per decision - negligible.
- **Waits on**: two cues of unequal value (food, and smell or a second food); an arousal variable (D3 or D4's
  Programs). The worm is not tested here: whether the worm's arousal amplifies its priorities is a literature
  question, read at preparation.
- **Leads**: Mather and Sutherland, "Arousal-biased competition in perception and memory", *Perspectives on
  Psychological Science*, 2011 ([doi:10.1177/1745691611400234](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691611400234)) - found.
  Easterbrook, "The effect of emotion on cue utilization and the organization of behavior", *Psychological Review*,
  1959 ([doi:10.1037/h0047707](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0047707)) - found. Teigen, "Yerkes-Dodson: a law for all
  seasons", *Theory & Psychology*, 1994 ([doi:10.1177/0959354394044004](https://doi.org/10.1177/0959354394044004)) -
  found. All three are about people.

## D9. Patience is confidence that reward will come

- **Question**: does a Program whose persistence at a patch is set by a learned confidence that the pending reward
  will come persist longer after a history of fulfilment than after a history of failure - and does that bring its
  patch-leaving closer to the worm's than a fixed discount does?
- **Method**: a Program waiting at a patch where food arrives after a delay with probability p, with a learned
  estimate of p setting its giving-up time. Lives are run in blocks of high and low p, and the giving-up time is
  measured in a probe block of middle p after each. The foil has a fixed discount, set so that its mean giving-up time
  equals the learned Program's over all blocks. The worm's patch-leaving is the target: its leaving as food is
  depleted is studied, by the abstracts of [Milward et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1106134109) and
  [Bendesky et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09821); whether its leaving depends on its history of food
  is read at preparation.
- **Baselines and controls**: the fixed-discount foil; the learned estimate pinned at its mean. The harness control:
  a Program given p directly, by construction, must show the ordering.
- **Refuted if**: the learned Program is no closer to the worm's leaving times than the fixed-discount foil, the
  paired interval of the distances wholly at or above zero. One refuting test, alpha 0.05. That the giving-up time in
  the probe block is longer after high p than after low p is set by the design and its learning rate, so it is a
  harness property, checked and reported, not tested. The test runs only if the sources give the worm's leaving after
  different histories; otherwise the plan is reported as not testable and waits.
- **Statistics**: per-life giving-up times; distances to the worm's leaving-time distributions (Kolmogorov-Smirnov);
  paired differences by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per arm, the default.
- **Cost**: one state variable - negligible.
- **Waits on**: food that depletes and patches that refill with a set probability (STATE.md, *Needs From Other
  Repositories*); the worm's leaving data.
- **Leads**: Miyazaki et al., "Reward probability and timing uncertainty alter the effect of dorsal raphe serotonin
  neurons on patience", *Nature Communications*, 2018
  ([doi:10.1038/s41467-018-04496-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04496-y)) - found. Lottem et al., "Activation
  of serotonin neurons promotes active persistence in a probabilistic foraging task", *Nature Communications*, 2018
  ([doi:10.1038/s41467-018-03438-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-03438-y)) - found. Both are about mice. Milward
  et al., "Neuronal and molecular substrates for optimal foraging in *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *PNAS*, 2011
  ([doi:10.1073/pnas.1106134109](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1106134109)) - found, abstract read: the ASE neurons promote
  food-leaving as food becomes limited, and high ambient oxygen promotes leaving through the oxygen-sensing neurons. Bendesky et al., "Catecholamine
  receptor polymorphisms affect decision-making in *C. elegans*", *Nature*, 2011
  ([doi:10.1038/nature09821](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09821)) - found, abstract read: patch-leaving is regulated
  in part by variation in a tyramine receptor, *tyra-3*. Neither abstract names serotonin, so the hunch's *Why* - that
  the worm's decision to stay or leave is serotonin-modulated - still has no lead; Flavell et al. 2013, cited in
  [README.md § H8](../README.md#h8-brain-chemistry-as-modelled-substances), ties serotonin to dwelling on food, which is
  near it but not the same claim.

## D10. Watch for drift into an anxious regime

- **Question**: over long lives in a neutral world, does a Program with a threat modulator drift toward avoidant,
  inhibited behaviour?
- **Method**: a Program with a threat modulator (the lab's first will come with touch or noxious heat) lives in a
  world with a few threats in its first tenth and none after, at three life lengths, each four times the last. The
  share of time in inhibited states - paused, or turning away from open ground - is measured in each tenth of the life.
  The hunch asks for a vital sign, not a clamp: the drift, if it comes, is reported and watched, not removed.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program with the threat modulator switched off. The harness control: a Program
  whose inhibited share is set to rise by construction must show a positive slope.
- **Refuted if**: the Program does not drift by as much as the margin - the change in the inhibited share over the
  longest life, after the threats end, has an interval wholly inside ±5 percentage points, a margin fixed here. One
  refuting test, alpha 0.05. Passed if that interval lies wholly above +5 points and the switched-off control's lies
  wholly below +5 points, a finding in each arm, so that the drift belongs to the modulator; any other result is
  inconclusive. The
  hunch's first claim - that a diffuse value signal gating plasticity is among the oldest working patterns in
  neurorobotics - is a claim about the field's history, checked against the leads at preparation, not tested here.
- **Statistics**: per-life slopes of the inhibited share; intervals by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per length and arm, the default; the control runs at all three lengths.
- **Cost**: the longest lives dominate; two arms of three lengths of 20 lives each is 2 × 20 × (1 + 4 + 16) = 840
  base-length lives.
- **Waits on**: a threat modulator and a sense for it (touch or noxious heat); the arena's longest affordable life.
  A real worm's long-run behaviour changes with age and starvation, so the worm is not the comparison here.
- **Leads**: Sporns and Alexander, "Neuromodulation and plasticity in an autonomous robot", *Neural Networks*, 2002
  ([doi:10.1016/S0893-6080(02)00062-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0893-6080(02)00062-X)) - found. Krichmar, "The
  neuromodulatory system: a framework for survival and adaptive behavior in a challenging world", *Adaptive
  Behavior*, 2008 ([doi:10.1177/1059712308095775](https://doi.org/10.1177/1059712308095775)) - found.

## D11. Affect in several dimensions, emotions as regions

- **Question**: for the worm, the one part of the hunch that applies: how many slow dimensions does the worm's own
  behaviour need - the hunch says "only a few axes"?
- **Method**: the hunch is aimed at rodent-scale rungs, as its *Why* says; its emotions-as-regions belong to them and
  are planned there. For the worm, "slow" is fixed here as varying over a minute or more. The lab's per-frame features
  are computed on the reference recordings, their principal components taken, and each component's autocorrelation
  at a lag of 60 s measured. A component counts as a slow dimension when that autocorrelation exceeds the 95th
  percentile of the same statistic on the same recording with its frames shuffled in time, which destroys any slow
  structure while keeping each feature's distribution. A Program's modulator state is then given that many axes.
- **Baselines and controls**: the frame-shuffled recordings are the null, built into the count. The harness control:
  a recording made by adding two planted slow signals, with periods of minutes, to a frame-shuffled one must give two
  slow dimensions; if it does not, the method cannot find what it looks for, and the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: the worm needs more than a few axes - the number of dimensions retained, with its interval by
  bootstrap over worms, lies wholly above 3, a number fixed here for "a few". One refuting test, alpha 0.05.
- **Statistics**: principal components and lagged autocorrelations per recording, against the shuffled null;
  bootstrap over worms for the number retained.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: the reference draw's worms; the analysis has no seed but the bootstrap's, stated.
- **Cost**: an analysis over the reference recordings - minutes.
- **Waits on**: the feature tool and the reference statistics (STATE.md item 2).
- **Leads**: Panksepp, *Affective Neuroscience: The Foundations of Human and Animal Emotions*, Oxford University
  Press, 1998 ([doi:10.1093/oso/9780195096736.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780195096736.001.0001)) - found.
  Russell, "A circumplex model of affect", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1980
  ([doi:10.1037/h0077714](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0077714)) - found. LeDoux, "Rethinking the emotional brain",
  *Neuron*, 2012 ([doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2012.02.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2012.02.004)) - found; Crossref
  holds a second, one-page item under the same title in the next issue, not this one. Barrett 2017, *How Emotions Are
  Made* - not found as a work: Crossref holds a review of it, a response by its author and a critique, but no record
  of the book, so it stays an unverified lead.

## D12. Pain damped by an opioid-like system, as dynamics

- **Question**: does a Program whose nociceptive gain is damped by an opioid-like variable with its own dynamics -
  rising with sustained noxious input, recovering with its own time constant - match the worm's responses to repeated
  noxious stimuli better than the same Program with a fixed cap on the gain?
- **Method**: the worm probably has an opioid-like system: the abstract of [Cheong et al.
  2015](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.06683) suggests one, with NLP-24 peptides and the receptor NPR-17 regulating feeding, and by the
  abstract of [Nieto-Fernandez et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10158-010-0099-5), morphine and two endomorphins
  reduce the worm's thermal avoidance, an effect reversed by the antagonists naloxone and CTOP. Whether the worm's own
  system damps its avoidance after repeated noxious stimuli is read at preparation. The Program: a nociceptive
  pathway whose gain is divided by one plus an opioid-like variable; the variable rises with noxious input above a
  threshold and decays with a time constant matched to the sources. The foil caps the gain at a fixed value instead.
  Both are given trains of noxious heat at the intervals the sources use.
- **Baselines and controls**: the Program with the variable pinned at zero; and a Program with receptor
  desensitisation alone ([D6](D1-D6.md#d6-receptors-that-tire-ten-times-faster-than-they-recover)) and no
  opioid-like variable, since fading avoidance may be adaptation, not analgesia. The harness control: noxious input
  below threshold must leave the variable at zero.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the dynamic Program is no closer to the worm's avoidance across a train than the capped one, the
  paired interval of the distances wholly at or above zero; (2) it is no closer than the desensitisation-only
  Program, likewise. Two refuting tests, alpha 0.05/2 each. If the sources give no repeated-stimulus curve for the
  worm, both clauses are reported as not testable and the plan waits.
- **Statistics**: per-life avoidance per stimulus; distances to the worm's curve; paired differences by bootstrap over
  lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per arm, the default.
- **Cost**: one state variable - negligible.
- **Waits on**: noxious heat on the Grid (B1's audit of the interface); the worm's repeated-stimulus curve. The
  hunch's grief, liking and social reward are for later rungs and are not tested here.
- **Leads**: Cheong, Artyukhin, You and Avery, "An opioid-like system regulating feeding behavior in *C. elegans*",
  *eLife*, 2015 ([doi:10.7554/eLife.06683](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.06683)) - found, abstract read.
  Nieto-Fernandez et al., "The effect of opioids and their antagonists on the nocifensive response of
  *Caenorhabditis elegans* to noxious thermal stimuli", *Invertebrate Neuroscience*, 2009
  ([doi:10.1007/s10158-010-0099-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10158-010-0099-5)) - found, abstract read. Both were found
  by a search of Europe PMC; the hunch gives no lead.

## D13. Bad things are learned faster than good ones

- **Question**: in the worm, are aversive associations learned in less training, and kept longer, than appetitive
  ones - and does a Program with asymmetric learning rates match the worm better than one with symmetric rates?
- **Method**: first the worm, from the literature at preparation: acquisition and extinction curves for an aversive
  and an appetitive association. The worm learns both - aversive olfactory learning after pathogenic bacteria
  ([Zhang et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04216), cited in H8) and appetitive olfactory learning
  ([Nishijima and Maruyama 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2017.00080), found by title) - but curves from
  different paradigms differ in more than valence, so only a source that trains both with comparable cues and
  measures counts. Then two Programs, with the learning rate for losses a multiple of that for gains or equal to it,
  trained in the same paradigm.
- **Baselines and controls**: the symmetric Program. The harness control: a Program with no learning must show flat
  curves.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the worm learns the aversive association no faster than the appetitive - the ratio of their
  acquisition rates, from a comparable source, has an interval wholly at or below 1; (2) the asymmetric Program is no
  closer to the worm's curves than the symmetric one, the paired interval wholly at or above zero. Two refuting tests,
  alpha 0.05/2 each. Without a comparable source, clause (1) is inconclusive and says so. Retention - the hunch's "kept longer" - is
  measured by the extinction curves and reported, not tested: extinction differs between paradigms even more than
  acquisition, and a fair test waits on a source that measures both valences alike. The hunch's further claims -
  trust building slowly, memories becoming harder to reach rather than deleted, retrieval by association, context,
  goal and salience, and reinstatement of fear - are for E's plans (memory and retrieval), not tested here.
- **Statistics**: per-life acquisition and extinction rates, fitted; distances to the worm's curves; paired
  differences by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per arm, the default.
- **Cost**: one learning rate per sign - negligible.
- **Waits on**: food, smell and an aversive stimulus on the Grid; a learning module (E's plans); a comparable worm
  source.
- **Leads**: the hunch gives none. Zhang, Lu and Bargmann 2005 is cited and verified in
  [README.md § H8](../README.md#h8-brain-chemistry-as-modelled-substances). Nishijima and Maruyama, "Appetitive
  olfactory learning and long-term associative memory in *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *Frontiers in Behavioral
  Neuroscience*, 2017 ([doi:10.3389/fnbeh.2017.00080](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2017.00080)) - found by title in a
  Europe PMC search, not read.

## A10's Question: Do Nematodes Show Anything Like Play?

[A10's plan](A9-A15.md#a10-welfare-and-affect-read-from-behaviour-alone) left this to the task that plans section D.
The answer is that no report of play in a nematode was found, by a search too narrow to call the absence settled:

- Europe PMC's REST search, on 2026-10-31, with the query `TITLE:"play" AND (TITLE:"nematode" OR TITLE:"elegans" OR
  TITLE:"worm")`: 34 records. None is about play behaviour; all but one use "play" as the verb ("play a role"), and the
  one exception is about role play as a teaching method for schoolchildren.
- The query `(TITLE_ABS:"play" OR TITLE_ABS:"play behavior" OR TITLE_ABS:"play behaviour") AND (TITLE_ABS:"nematode"
  OR TITLE_ABS:"elegans")`: 4180 records, none of the first dozen about play behaviour. The search cannot tell the
  noun from the verb, so it was not read further.
- Play has been asked of an invertebrate: Galpayage Dona et al., "Do bumble bees play?", *Animal Behaviour*, 2022
  ([doi:10.1016/j.anbehav.2022.08.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anbehav.2022.08.013)) - found in Crossref; neither
  Crossref nor Europe PMC served its abstract, so nothing is taken from it here.

The criteria for play the field uses are Burghardt's ([*The Genesis of Animal
Play*](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/3229.001.0001), 2005, found and cited by A10, not read). Until a source says
otherwise, play is set aside as a measure of the worm's welfare, and the play hunch waits for a rung where the animal
is known to play. A later reader could search Burghardt's book for nematodes, and the bumble-bee paper's references.
