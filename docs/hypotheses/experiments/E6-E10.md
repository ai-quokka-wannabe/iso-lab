# Plans for E6-E10: Learning and Memory, Second Part

Experiment plans for hunches E6 to E10 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). They follow the conventions at the head of [C1-C12.md](C1-C12.md),
[D1-D6.md](D1-D6.md), [D7-D13.md](D7-D13.md) and [E1-E5.md](E1-E5.md) - among them the decision between *refuted*,
*passed* and *inconclusive* by the rule at the head of [B.md](B.md), pass tests run uncorrected and refuting tests
corrected (k refuting tests, each at alpha 0.05/k), zero as the smallest effect of interest for a clause that predicts
only a direction, a check that holds by construction written as an assertion and not a test, and a Program's free
parameters fitted on one part of the worm's data and scored on a held-out part that the plan names - and do not repeat
them. Every refuting clause below says when it passes as well as when it refutes; the two never overlap, and anything
between them is inconclusive.

Two kinds of plan are here. E6's first part, E9 and E10 test a mechanism on its own, with no Grid and no worm: the
outcome each tests is a property of the dynamics, not set by any rule the mechanism is built from, and each model is
fixed here in full - its units and what they do at zero input, its update order, its step limit and what happens to a
cue that never settles, the meaning of every measured term, and a capacity or a gate checked first on a network that
does not carry the effect under test. Seeds 1 to 20 are the tests' own; each model was first run on other seeds (1001
to 1003) by a design probe, [notebooks/e6_e9_e10_design_probes.ipynb](../../../notebooks/e6_e9_e10_design_probes.ipynb),
to check that it is not decided by a ceiling or a floor, and the choices that probe informed are named where they are
made. E6's second part, E7 and E8 compare Programs with the worm, where a source gives the worm's behaviour; where none
does, the plan says the test waits.

The leads were looked up in Crossref on 2026-10-31. Found means the work exists as named, with the DOI given. Where an
abstract was read, in Europe PMC on the same date, the plan says so and what it takes from it; nothing else was read,
and no number from any source is used yet. Worm sources not among the hunches' leads were searched for in Europe PMC
on the same date and are marked as added.

## E6. Separate, complete, consolidate, replay

- **Question**: in an attractor store, does a sparse separation step before recurrent completion reduce the
  interference between similar memories - and does a Program that consolidates memories only in a sleep-like state
  match the worm's long-term memory, with and without sleep after training, better than one that consolidates whether
  or not it sleeps?
- **Method**: two parts.
    - *The store, fixed here.* M = 1000 units, each 0 or 1. At every step exactly k = 50 are active: the 50 with the
      largest input (k-winners-take-all), ties going to the lower index, so that at zero input everywhere units 0 to
      49 are active. Each network holds 50 patterns of 50 active units, in one of two conditions built from the same
      seed: *random*, the patterns independent; and *similar*, ten families of five, where a family's prototype is 50
      units drawn at random and each member keeps 25 of them, drawn at random, and adds 25 drawn from the other 950.
      The overlap of 25 was set by the design probe: at 20 neither variant interfered, at 35 both failed on most cues.
      The probe ran both variants, so the choice was made having seen both; the tests use other seeds.
      Weights are written once, by the covariance rule, the sum over stored codes c of (c - f)(c - f)ᵀ with f = 0.05,
      self-weights zero, and do not change during retrieval.
    - *Separation, fixed here.* With separation, every pattern and every cue passes through a fixed projection R
      before the store - an M by M matrix whose entries are non-zero with probability 0.05 and then standard Gaussian,
      drawn from the seed - followed by k-winners-take-all; the stored code is that output. Without separation, the
      stored code is the pattern itself. Both variants store codes of 50 active units in 1000, so they differ only in
      the codes.
    - *Retrieval.* A cue is a stored pattern with 5 of its active units turned off and 5 inactive units turned on, at
      random; 100 cues per network, their targets drawn uniformly. The state steps synchronously, each step setting it
      to the k-winners of W times the state, for at most 50 steps; it has settled when the active set equals the
      previous step's. A cue that has not settled by then is counted as not retrieved, and the share of such cues is
      reported for every variant and condition. A cue is *retrieved* when the settled state's overlap with the
      target's stored code (the number of active units they share, divided by 50, so from 0 to 1 and never negative)
      is at least 0.9 and greater than its overlap with every other stored code. A network's *interference* is its
      share retrieved in the random condition minus its share retrieved in the similar condition.
    - *Capacity, first.* In the random condition, which carries no similarity and so not the effect under test, every
      stored code must be a fixed point and at least 0.95 of cues retrieved, in both variants, on every network. If
      not, the load is lowered to 40 and then to 30 patterns for both conditions and both variants, decided on this
      check alone and before the similar condition is run. In the random condition no unsettled cue is expected; if
      more than 0.1 of its cues fail to settle in either variant, the run is void.
    - *Gate.* Without separation, interference must be shown: its interval wholly above zero, at alpha 0.05. If not,
      there is nothing for separation to reduce, and clause 1 is reported inconclusive and not run.
    - *Assertion, not a test.* Separation is built to lower the overlap between the codes of similar patterns; the mean
      overlap within families, before and after it, is reported. What no rule sets is whether retrieval gains: the
      same step can also turn a cue's 5 moved units into a larger difference between the cue's code and the target's,
      and which effect wins is what clause 1 measures.
    - *Sleep and the worm.* By the abstract of [Chandra et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2023.05.006), spaced
      odour training and sleep after training induce long-term memory in the worm, and sleep is required for events
      immediately after training that drive its consolidation. By the abstract of [Raizen et al.
      2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06535), the worm's lethargus is a sleep-like state, with reversibility,
      reduced responsiveness and homeostasis. Two Programs, their equations fixed at preparation before any comparison
      with the worm: the *sleep Program*, with a fast store written in training and a slow store that gains from it,
      by replay, only while the Program is quiescent; and the *foil*, the same equations with the transfer running at
      the same rate awake and quiescent. Asserted from their equations: the foil's long-term retention does not depend
      on sleep; the sleep Program's falls when quiescence after training is prevented. The test is whether the size of
      that fall matches the worm's. Both are run in Chandra et al.'s protocols, read from the paper at preparation:
      the parameters are fitted to the worm's retention with sleep allowed, and scored on the condition with sleep
      prevented. The foil's predicted difference is zero by construction, so clause 2 compares the sleep Program's
      held-out prediction with the worm, as [E5](E1-E5.md#e5-memories-that-bind-what-where-when-and-how-it-felt) does.
    - *Not tested here.* The hunch's consolidation of high-arousal events first, and its pruning by age, rarity and
      low emotion: no worm source on either was found, and both wait on one.
- **Baselines and controls**: for the store, the variant without separation and the random condition. For the worm
  part, the foil. Harness controls: a store holding one pattern must retrieve it from every cue in both variants; a
  Program with learning switched off must show no retention with or without sleep.
- **Refuted if**:
    1. Separation does not reduce interference: interference without separation minus interference with it, paired
       over networks, has an interval wholly at or below zero. Passed if wholly above zero.
    2. With sleep prevented, the held-out condition, the sleep Program's predicted fall in retention is no closer to
       the worm's fall than zero - the foil's - is: the interval of the difference in distances (the sleep Program's
       minus the foil's) wholly at or above zero. Passed if wholly below zero.

  Two refuting tests, alpha 0.025 each.
- **Statistics**: per-network shares and interferences, their paired differences by bootstrap over networks; per-life
  retention, distances to the worm's normalised by the worm's between-animal spread, by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 networks, seeds 1 to 20; seed i draws network i's patterns in both conditions, its
  projection and its cues, the same with and without separation, so that every comparison is paired. 20 lives per
  Program and condition, the default.
- **Cost**: the store has a million weights - a test of the principle, far above what *Lean brains* allows a worm's
  Program; a version for a Program is sized when one is built. The sleep Program: two stores and a transfer rate.
- **Waits on**: the store, nothing - it runs alone, in tested Rust. The worm part: Chandra et al. read in full for its
  protocols and numbers; an odour other than food's on the Grid, which today smells only food orbs; a measure of
  quiescence in the benchmark.
- **Leads**: McClelland, McNaughton and O'Reilly, "Why there are complementary learning systems in the hippocampus
  and neocortex: insights from the successes and failures of connectionist models of learning and memory",
  *Psychological Review*, 1995 ([doi:10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419)) -
  found, abstract read: recent memories are first stored in the hippocampal system and reinstated in the neocortex,
  which learns slowly, so that new items are interleaved without disrupting its structure; it is the hunch's fast and
  slow stores, about mammals. Foster, "Replay comes of age", *Annual Review of Neuroscience*, 2017
  ([doi:10.1146/annurev-neuro-072116-031538](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-072116-031538)) - found, abstract
  read: a review of hippocampal replay, about rodents. Added: Chandra et al., "Sleep is required to consolidate odor
  memory and remodel olfactory synapses", *Cell*, 2023
  ([doi:10.1016/j.cell.2023.05.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2023.05.006)) - found, abstract read; the journal
  version of the work earlier found only as a conference abstract and a preprint. Raizen et al., "Lethargus is a
  *Caenorhabditis elegans* sleep-like state", *Nature*, 2008 ([doi:10.1038/nature06535](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06535)) -
  found, abstract read.

## E7. Promote after spaced recurrence; merge when predictions agree; never delete

- **Question**: does a Program that promotes a memory to long-term only after recurrences spaced in time match the
  worm's long-term retention after spaced and massed training better than one that promotes by the count of
  recurrences alone?
- **Method**: the worm forms long-term memory after spaced training and not after massed. By the abstract of [Rose,
  Kaun and Rankin 2002](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.46802), for habituation to tap: distributed training produced
  long-term retention and massed training did not; distributed training at long interstimulus intervals did and at
  short ones did not; long-term memory was graded with the number of training blocks; and the interval between blocks
  had to exceed 40 minutes. The abstract of the review by [Rose and Rankin 2001](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.37801) reports long-term
  retention after spaced training and not after massed, as does the title of [Beck and Rankin
  1997](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03209851). In an aversive olfactory
  paradigm, by the abstract of [Amano and Maruyama 2011](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.2224411), memory after spaced
  training was kept for 24 hours and memory after massed training was no longer seen within 3 hours. Two Programs,
  fixed here but for their fitted parameters and for E1's habituation module, whose equations are fixed when E1 is
  prepared:
    - the *promotion Program*: E1's habituation module for the short term, plus a long-term trace L in [0, 1] per
      stimulus channel. A training block is a recurrence; the first block counts, and a later one *counts* when it begins at least g
      after the end of the last block that counted, and each counted recurrence moves L by β(1 - L). L never decays - the hunch's "never
      delete" - and the response at a long-term test is the short-term module's response scaled by 1 - L;
    - the *foil*: the same, with every block counted (g = 0).

  A massed schedule is taken, by a convention fixed here, as its blocks with no interval between them, so that it has
  as many blocks as the spaced schedule it is compared with. Asserted from the equations: the foil's L is then the same
  after massed and spaced training; the promotion Program's L does not move for blocks closer together than g. The test is whether g, fitted on
  part of the worm's conditions, predicts the rest. Rose et al. 2002's conditions are listed in the order the paper
  reports them; the odd-numbered are fitted and the even-numbered scored. A parameter the fitted half does not
  identify - its fit flat over a range - is reported as such, and its range carried into the predictions as an
  interval. The number of fitted parameters is reported beside each distance: the promotion Program has one more, g.
- **Not tested here, each for a stated reason**:
    - *Different contexts.* The hunch promotes only after recurrences in different contexts. The worm's spaced
      training above is, as far as the abstracts say, in one context, and by the abstract of [Rankin
      2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.114.3.496) the worm's retention of habituation is greater when training
      and testing share a chemosensory cue - a memory bound in part to its context. Whether the worm's long-term memory
      after spaced training is "general" in the hunch's sense is not settled by the sources read; the context half
      waits on a worm source on generalisation across contexts.
    - *Salience by size, not sign.* No worm source comparing the decay of appetitive and aversive memories of matched
      strength was found; it waits on one, and on [D13](D7-D13.md#d13-bad-things-are-learned-faster-than-good-ones),
      which plans the neighbouring claim.
    - *Merging.* A rule that merges two representations when their predictions differ by less than a threshold loses,
      by construction, no more prediction than the threshold allows, and its gain is in size, which *Lean brains*
      already counts. Whether it recovers a process's structure from finite data is a question for when a Program
      holds a learned model of sequences to merge; no test is planned before then.
    - *Demote, never wipe.* A design rule: no Program memory is ever set to zero by forgetting, checked by review of
      the code and by an assertion over every tick.
- **Baselines and controls**: the foil. The harness control: a Program with learning switched off must show no
  long-term retention after either schedule.
- **Refuted if**: on the held-out conditions, the promotion Program is no closer to the worm's long-term retention than
  the foil, summed over conditions, each distance normalised by the worm's between-animal spread in that condition:
  the paired interval of the difference (the promotion Program's minus the foil's) wholly at or above zero. Passed if
  wholly below zero. One refuting test, alpha 0.05.
- **Statistics**: per-life response at the long-term test; normalised distances to the worm's; paired differences by
  bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program and condition, the default.
- **Cost**: one more variable per stimulus channel than E1's module, and a clock of the last counted block.
- **Waits on**: touch on the Grid (B1's audit of the interface), as for E1; Rose et al. 2002 read in full for its
  conditions and numbers. Amano and Maruyama's paradigm needs 1-propanol and acid, which the Grid lacks.
- **Leads**: Crutchfield and Shalizi, "Thermodynamic depth of causal states: objective complexity via minimal
  representations", *Physical Review E*, 1999 ([doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.59.275](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.59.275)) -
  found, not read. Stolcke and Omohundro 1993 - not found as a work: Crossref returns their "Inducing probabilistic
  grammars by Bayesian model merging", *Lecture Notes in Computer Science*, 1994
  ([doi:10.1007/3-540-58473-0_141](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-58473-0_141)), a different work, not read; the 1993
  lead stays unverified. Added: Rose, Kaun and Rankin, "A new group-training procedure for habituation demonstrates that
  presynaptic glutamate release contributes to long-term memory in *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *Learning & Memory*,
  2002 ([doi:10.1101/lm.46802](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.46802)) - found, abstract read. Rose and Rankin, "Analyses of
  habituation in *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *Learning & Memory*, 2001 ([doi:10.1101/lm.37801](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.37801)) -
  found, abstract read. Beck and Rankin, "Long-term habituation is produced by distributed training at long ISIs and
  not by massed training or short ISIs in *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *Animal Learning & Behavior*, 1997
  ([doi:10.3758/BF03209851](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03209851)) - found; no abstract in Europe PMC, title only. Amano
  and Maruyama, "Aversive olfactory learning and associative long-term memory in *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *Learning
  & Memory*, 2011 ([doi:10.1101/lm.2224411](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.2224411)) - found, abstract read. Rankin 2000 is
  under [E3](E1-E5.md#e3-three-kinds-of-learning-in-three-places).

## E8. Two strengths: forgetting loses influence, not the trace

- **Question**: does a memory with two strengths - a storage strength that never decays and a retrieval strength that
  falls with disuse - match the worm's long-term retention after spaced and massed training better than a memory with
  one strength; and does the worm relearn faster after forgetting, as only the first can?
- **Method**: two Programs, fixed here but for their fitted parameters, each a habituation memory per stimulus
  channel with the response 1 - R, as a fraction of the naive response:
    - the *two-strength Program*, with a storage strength S ≥ 0 and a retrieval strength R in [0, 1]. Between stimuli,
      R decays as exp(-Δt / (τ(1 + S))) and S does not change. At each stimulus, first S grows by b(1 - R), so that a
      stimulus met at a low retrieval strength - a hard retrieval - adds more, and then R moves by
      (1 - R)(1 - exp(-a(1 + S))). Fitted: a, b and τ;
    - the *foil*, one strength m in [0, 1]: at each stimulus m moves by a(1 - m), and between stimuli it decays as
      exp(-Δt / τ). Fitted: a and τ.

  Asserted from the equations: S never decays; after R has decayed, relearning raises R faster when S is larger, which
  is savings; and the foil, for the same number of stimuli, is at least as habituated after massed as after spaced
  training at any equal delay after the last stimulus, when every gap of the spaced schedule is at least the massed
  schedule's, since each stimulus leaves m higher the higher m was before
  it, and longer gaps leave it lower. Whether the two-strength Program shows the opposite, a spacing advantage, at
  its fitted parameters is computed from its equations at preparation and reported, not assumed. The worm's side is
  E7's: spaced training gives long-term retention and massed does not ([Rose, Kaun and Rankin
  2002](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.46802), abstract; [Amano and Maruyama 2011](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.2224411),
  abstract). The fit and score split is E7's, on Rose et al. 2002's conditions, odd-numbered fitted and even-numbered
  scored; the two-strength Program has one more fitted parameter than the foil, and that is reported beside each
  distance. E7's and E8's Programs are scored on the same conditions; how they compare with each other is reported,
  not tested.
- **Savings in the worm**: not found. Europe PMC was searched for the worm with savings, relearning, re-training and
  forgetting; the hits were reviews of forgetting, among them [Zhu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2025.108061),
  whose abstract describes forgetting in the worm as an active process and does not mention savings. [Rose and Rankin
  2001](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.37801)'s abstract reports habituation accumulating over repeated training blocks,
  which is within training, not relearning after forgetting. Clause 2 therefore waits on a worm source; if one is
  found at preparation, the two-strength Program's predicted savings is compared with the worm's against the foil's,
  which is small once m has decayed but not zero, as in clause 2 below. The number of refuting tests, and so alpha, is fixed
  from the literature alone before any run: one if no source is found, two if one is.
- **Baselines and controls**: the foil. The harness control: a Program with learning switched off must show neither
  long-term retention nor savings.
- **Refuted if**:
    1. On the held-out conditions, the two-strength Program is no closer to the worm's long-term retention than the
       foil, summed over conditions, each distance normalised by the worm's between-animal spread: the paired
       interval of the difference (two-strength minus foil) wholly at or above zero. Passed if wholly below zero.
    2. When a worm source is found: the two-strength Program's predicted savings is no closer to the worm's than the
       foil's is, the paired interval of the difference in distances (two-strength minus foil) wholly at or above zero. Passed if wholly below zero. If the
       source reports no savings in the worm, the hunch's *Why* is not supported for the worm, and that is recorded.

  One refuting test at alpha 0.05, or two at 0.025 each, as above.
- **Statistics**: per-life response at each test; normalised distances to the worm's; paired differences by bootstrap
  over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program and condition, the default.
- **Cost**: two numbers per memory against one.
- **Waits on**: as E7: touch on the Grid, and Rose et al. 2002 read in full; for clause 2, a worm source on savings.
- **Leads**: Bjork and Bjork 1992, a chapter in *From Learning Processes to Cognitive Processes* - not found as a work
  in Crossref, so it stays an unverified lead, and the equations above are the lab's own, not taken from it. Added, for
  the worm: those under E7, and Kauffman et al., "Insulin signaling and dietary restriction differentially influence
  the decline of learning and memory with age", *PLoS Biology*, 2010 ([doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.1000372](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1000372)) -
  found, abstract read: it reports that the worm's CREB homologue *crh-1* is needed for long-term associative memory
  and not for learning or short-term memory; the abstract says nothing of spaced or massed training, so nothing is
  taken from it here. Zhu et al., "Investigations of forgetting in *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *Neurobiology of
  Learning and Memory*, 2025 ([doi:10.1016/j.nlm.2025.108061](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2025.108061)) - found,
  abstract read.

## E9. Attention as a conserved economy

- **Question**: in an economy of importance where a fixed total circulates, units are paid for being used and charged
  rent only while in focus, and a controller sets the focus's size, does any unit come to hold most of the total
  under random input - and does the rent, against a flat levy, keep the largest holding lower, and does the controller
  hold the focus's size in its band?
- **Method**: the economy, fixed here, with no Grid and no worm.
    - *Units and tokens.* 32 units hold integer tokens, 100 each at the start, and a pool holds the rest, at first
      none; the total, 3200, is asserted after every tick, and a negative holding stops the run.
    - *Input.* Each tick, each unit has input with probability p, drawn independently: p is 0.1 for 2000 ticks, then
      0.5 for 2000, and so on, so that the controller meets steps in load. 20 000 ticks.
    - *Focus and controller.* The focus is every unit with input whose holding is at least a threshold θ, an integer
      starting at 0. After each tick θ moves by the focus's size minus 4, the target, and is clamped at 0. A unit
      without input is never in focus, and with no input anywhere the focus is empty and θ falls by 4.
    - *Use.* Each focused unit is *used* with probability 0.5, drawn independently: under random input, usefulness is
      independent of everything else.
    - *Rent and pay.* The economy charges each focused unit a twentieth of its holding, by integer division, into the
      pool. The *flat-levy foil* charges every unit holding a token one token a tick, in focus or not. In both, the
      pool is then shared equally among the used units by integer division, the remainder staying in the pool; with
      no used unit, it all stays.
    - *Measures, over the second half of the ticks.* The *largest share*: the largest holding any unit reaches at any
      tick, divided by 3200. The *share outside the band*: the share of ticks whose 100-tick moving average of the
      focus's size lies outside [3, 5].
    - *Assertions, not tests.* Conservation holds by construction, since every token moves by integer arithmetic
      between a unit and the pool; it is asserted every tick. In the design probe a first foil, a levy proportional to
      holdings, moved no token at all, every holding of 100 rounding it to zero; it was replaced by the flat levy.
- **Baselines and controls**: the flat-levy foil, with the same controller and the same draws. The harness control,
  seen failing once by the rule *Red-first tests*: a version that mints one token at a random tick must trip the
  conservation assertion.
- **Refuted if**:
    1. A unit monopolises: the per-run largest share has an interval wholly above 0.25, a margin fixed here - eight
       times the equal share of 1/32. Passed if wholly below 0.25.
    2. The rent does not matter: the largest share under the flat levy minus that under rent, paired by seed, has an
       interval wholly at or below zero. Passed if wholly above zero.
    3. The controller does not hold the band: the share outside the band has an interval wholly above 0.05, a margin
       fixed here. Passed if wholly below 0.05. The band, the window and this margin were written before the design
       probe ran.

  Three refuting tests, alpha 0.05/3 each.
- **Statistics**: per-run values; intervals of their means, and of the paired differences, by bootstrap over runs.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 runs, seeds 1 to 20; seed i draws the inputs and the uses of run i, the same for the
  economy and the foil, so that clause 2 is paired.
- **Cost**: 32 integers and a pool; a few operations per unit per tick.
- **Waits on**: nothing; it runs alone, in tested Rust. The worm is not tested here: no source on the worm was found
  for the hunch, which is a design rule. Whether such an economy earns a place in a worm's Program is judged on the
  benchmark, beside its cost.
- **Leads**: none were given.

## E10. The input reshapes the attractor landscape

- **Question**: in a Hopfield network, does weighting each stored pattern by its alignment with the current input
  make a blended or a shifting cue settle on one clean memory more often than the same network with every pattern
  weighted alike?
- **Method**: the network, fixed here, with no Grid and no worm.
    - *Units and patterns.* N = 200 units, each +1 or -1. P = 10 patterns, each unit +1 or -1 with equal chance, drawn
      from the seed.
    - *Weights.* The *baseline* has weights (1/N) times the sum over patterns of ξξᵀ, self-weights zero. The
      *modulated* network weights pattern μ by w_μ = max(0, m_μ(u))², where m_μ(u) = ξ_μ·u / N is the pattern's signed
      overlap with the current cue u, normalised so that the w_μ sum to P (all w_μ = 1 if every overlap is at or below
      zero), recomputed whenever the cue changes; its weights are (1/N) times the sum of w_μ ξ_μξ_μᵀ, self-weights
      zero. This form is the lab's own, after the hunch, not taken from [Betteti et al.
      2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adu6991), which was not read beyond its abstract. The normalisation was set
      by the design probe: unnormalised, the modulation shrank the recurrent drive and lost to the baseline whenever
      the cue's field was on.
    - *Input.* The cue is also a persistent field of strength γ = 0.5 on every unit, in both networks. γ was set by the
      design probe, where the baseline smeared a blended cue on most cues at 0.5 and on few at 0. That probe also ran
      the modulated network, so the choice was made having seen both; the tests use other seeds, and the probe's
      shifting condition, where the baseline was clean on most cues, may meet the gate below.
    - *Cues.* Two distinct patterns a and b are drawn uniformly. *Blended*: each unit of u is taken from a with
      probability 0.6, from b otherwise. *Shifting*: u is drawn that way afresh before each of the first ten sweeps,
      with the probability falling from 0.8 by 0.06 a sweep, and is held after. In both conditions the state starts at a
      cue drawn with probability 0.6. 100 cues per network.
    - *Dynamics.* Units update one at a time in index order, each taking the sign of its input, the weighted sum of
      the other units' states plus γ times its own unit of u; at zero input a unit keeps its state. A cue has settled
      when a whole sweep changes no unit, counted only after the shifting cue is held; it is stopped after 50 sweeps.
      A cue that has not settled is counted as not clean, and the unsettled share is reported for every network and
      condition; if it exceeds 0.1 in any, the run is void.
    - *Clean.* A settled state is *clean* when its signed overlap with some stored pattern is at least 0.9. A mirror
      state, overlap -0.9 or below, is not clean, and its share is reported. Also reported: which pattern a clean state
      matches - a, the majority of a blended cue; b, the last majority of a shifting one; or another.
    - *Capacity, first.* On the baseline network with no field and no modulation, which does not carry the effect
      under test, every stored pattern must be a fixed point on every network; if not, P is lowered to 8 for every
      network and condition, before any cue is shown.
    - *Gate.* If the baseline's clean share in a condition has an interval wholly above 0.95, at alpha 0.05, there is no smearing in
      that condition for the modulation to remove, and its clause is reported inconclusive.
    - *Not tested.* The hunch says the mechanism buys robustness to mixed cues, not capacity. Under the form fixed
      here, a cue close to one pattern gives that pattern nearly all the weight, so the network's capacity for such
      cues rises by construction: on this form the hunch's "not capacity" does not hold, and the plan says so rather
      than testing it. The largest P at which every pattern, given as its own cue, is a fixed point is measured for
      both networks and reported.
- **The worm**: by the abstract of [Ishihara et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(02)00748-1), the worm
  integrates two sensory signals presented together, and *hen-1* mutants are defective in that integration while
  sensing each normally. The abstract does not say whether the worm's choice under such a mixed cue is clean or
  graded; a test against the worm waits on reading the paper, and on a Program that uses this memory for a choice.
- **Baselines and controls**: the baseline network, with the same patterns, cues and field. Harness control: a cue
  equal to one stored pattern must settle on it in both networks.
- **Refuted if**, in each condition, blended and shifting: the modulated network's clean share minus the baseline's,
  paired by seed, has an interval wholly at or below zero. Passed if wholly above zero. Two refuting tests, one per
  condition, alpha 0.025 each.
- **Statistics**: per-network shares; paired differences by bootstrap over networks.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 networks, seeds 1 to 20; seed i draws network i's patterns and cues, the same for both
  networks, so that every comparison is paired.
- **Cost**: 200 units and 40 000 weights; the modulated network's input to a unit can be computed through the P
  overlaps, P times N operations, without forming its weights.
- **Waits on**: nothing for the network; it runs alone, in tested Rust. It sharpens
  [H5](../README.md#h5-settling-into-attractors). The worm's side waits as above.
