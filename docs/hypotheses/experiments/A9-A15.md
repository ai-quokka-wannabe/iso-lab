# Plans for A9-A15: Measuring the Worm, Second Half

Experiment plans for hunches A9 to A15 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). Like the first half ([A1-A8.md](A1-A8.md)), most of these plans test
an instrument - a dashboard, a checklist, a discipline - and are refuted when it fails on cases whose answer is
known in advance. Two of them (A12, A13) are rules for every experiment rather than experiments; their plans test
whether the rule catches the faults it names.

The leads were looked up in Crossref by title on 2026-10-13. Found means the work exists as named, with the DOI
given; none was read in full. A lead that could not be found, or could not be reached, says so; leads the lab already
cites elsewhere point there.

Costs are counted in simulated lives. What one life costs in compute and memory is not known until the arena is
proven (STATE.md item 4), so no plan gives absolute figures yet; each will when it is prepared.

## A9. Vital signs for a long-lived brain

- **Question**: do the hunch's task-free readings, on control charts against the brain's own history, warn of
  runaway excitation, capture or drift before behaviour visibly breaks?
- **Method**: compute the dozen readings each simulated minute of a long life. Induce each failure deliberately -
  a gain raised slowly until excitation runs away, one unit's outgoing weights grown until it captures unrelated
  cues, a learning rate left unbounded - and record when the chart first leaves its band and when the behavioural
  benchmark first fails.
- **Baselines and controls**: an unperturbed life of the same length must stay inside every band; each induced
  failure is its own positive control. Each reading's band is set as control limits at three standard deviations of
  its own history over the first tenth of the life, so a reading of normally distributed values would leave its band
  on about 0.3% of checks by chance - the design false-alarm rate.
- **Refuted if**: for an induced failure, no reading leaves its band before the behaviour fails (no early warning),
  or the unperturbed control's false-alarm rate has a 95% Wilson lower bound above 1% of checks.
- **Statistics**: per failure kind, the lead time (chart alarm to behavioural failure) over seeds, with a bootstrap
  95% interval that must lie above zero; the false-alarm rate with a Wilson interval against the band's design rate.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per failure kind and for the control.
- **Cost**: one long life per seed and failure kind; the readings are sums over units each step, and the
  perturbational complexity index adds one short probe run per reading - the costliest part, to be measured.
- **Waits on**: a Program with modulators and learning (H3, H6, H8, H9), and the arena for long lives.
- **Leads**: found - Casali et al. 2013
  ([doi:10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294)). Cited elsewhere - Beggs and
  Plenz 2003, in [H7](../README.md#h7-poised-dynamics).

## A10. Welfare and affect read from behaviour alone

- **Question**: do behavioural readings alone - approach latency to novelty, activity, sheltering, exploration, a
  judgement-bias task - track a creature's internal state the way welfare science reads an animal's?
- **Method**: manipulate the creature's state through its world only (an enriched or a barren arena, a run of
  rewards or of aversive contacts), then score the behavioural readings blind to the condition. A judgement-bias task
  needs a trained discrimination and an ambiguous cue; for the worm, only what it can sense on the Grid qualifies.
- **Baselines and controls**: an unmanipulated baseline, and a sham manipulation (the same handling, no change of
  state); the hunch's own test - judgement bias must shift after positive and negative manipulations in opposite
  directions.
- **Refuted if**: the bias shift after the positive manipulation, or after the negative one, has a 95% interval that
  includes zero or lies on the wrong side of it; or the sham differs from an unmanipulated baseline on any reading,
  by an interval excluding zero (the readings measure handling, not state). The hunch about play (absent in poor
  conditions) is tested only if the literature check finds play in nematodes to measure - otherwise it is set aside
  for later rungs of the ladder, and the plan says so then.
- **Statistics**: the bias shift per condition against sham, with a bootstrap 95% interval; the direction fixed in
  advance.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per condition.
- **Cost**: four sets of 20 lives per test (positive, negative, sham, baseline).
- **Waits on**: a Program with an affective state (H3, H8) and learning (for the discrimination), and the arena.
- **Leads**: found - Mendl, Burman and Paul 2010 ([doi:10.1098/rspb.2010.0303](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2010.0303));
  Boissy et al. 2007 ([doi:10.1016/j.physbeh.2007.02.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physbeh.2007.02.003));
  Burghardt 2005, *The Genesis of Animal Play* (MIT Press,
  [doi:10.7551/mitpress/3229.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/3229.001.0001)). Not found as a work -
  Pellis and Pellis 2009, *The Playful Brain*: Crossref has only a magazine review of it, so it stays an unverified
  lead.

## A11. A sentience-indicator checklist, read with care

- **Question**: can published indicator criteria for sentience be scored against an architecture reliably, and
  does the score tell an architecture built to meet them from one that meets them in function?
- **Method**: write each criterion as a test on the Program - for example, a motivational trade-off (does the
  creature accept a cost to escape an aversive contact, and does the accepted cost scale with the aversion?), or
  conditioned place avoidance. Score two Programs: one where each criterion is met by a hard-coded rule, and one
  where it emerges from the modules. Reading *with care*, as the hunch asks, means one more step per criterion met:
  perturb the situation slightly (a new arena, a cost of a new kind) and see whether the criterion is still met - a
  rule written for the test case should break, a capacity should carry over.
- **Baselines and controls**: a Program with no affective modules (should meet few criteria); the hard-coded
  Program is the control for the hunch's caution that indicators can be met by construction.
- **Refuted if**: the careful reading gives the hard-coded Program as many criteria carried over as the modular
  one (it reads construction, not function); or two independent scorers' agreement has a kappa whose 95% lower bound
  is below 0.6.
- **Statistics**: agreement between two scorers (Cohen's kappa with a bootstrap interval); per criterion, a
  yes-or-no outcome with its test fixed in advance.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds for each behavioural criterion.
- **Cost**: two lives per criterion and seed (the test, and its perturbed variant), for each Program.
- **Waits on**: Programs with nociception-like touch handling and affect (H1, H3, H8), and the arena.
- **Leads**: found - Birch, Schnell and Clayton 2020, "Dimensions of Animal Consciousness"
  ([doi:10.1016/j.tics.2020.07.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2020.07.007)); Birch 2024, *The Edge of
  Sentience* ([doi:10.1093/9780191966729.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.1093/9780191966729.001.0001)). Not checked -
  the Cambridge Declaration on Consciousness (2012) has no DOI, and the site that hosts it is not reachable from the
  lab's machine. The lab measures behaviour and makes no claim about sentience ([the vision](../../VISION.md));
  this checklist is an instrument, not a verdict.

## A12. Experiment hygiene

- **Question**: does the lab's pre-registration discipline catch the faults the hunch names, before they reach a
  verdict?
- **Method**: this is a rule for every experiment, so its test is an audit. Take each experiment the lab has run
  and check it against the hunch's list: thresholds, baselines, seeds and outcome rules fixed before the run; no
  verdict relabelled afterwards; every baseline in its working regime; a theoretical ceiling as an anchor; a zero
  coefficient of variation looked into; several metrics reported; memory tests run below capacity; a control
  matched on correlation structure.
- **Baselines and controls**: plant one known fault in a copy of a past experiment (a threshold moved after the
  data) - the audit must catch it.
- **Refuted if**: the audit misses the planted fault, or passes an experiment a verifier later finds at fault on a
  point the list covers.
- **Statistics**: a count of faults found per experiment, each named; no inference.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: not applicable; every experiment the lab has run.
- **Cost**: reading time.
- **Waits on**: nothing; the first audit can be of H10's first test, whose verifier already found an unspecified tie
  rule and an upward-biased estimator (2026-10-13 journal) - points the list should catch.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A13. A stored quantity is not what its name says until a known case has been read

- **Question**: does checking every internal quantity against a known case, before anything consumes it, catch
  quantities that do not hold what their names say?
- **Method**: for each named internal quantity of a Program (a baseline, a set point, a tonic level), write a known
  case - an input whose correct value is known - and check the stored value. Split a name that covers two
  timescales into two variables, each with its own case.
- **Baselines and controls**: plant a quantity that holds the wrong thing (a "baseline" that is really the last
  value) - its known case must fail.
- **Refuted if**: the planted fault passes its known case, or a fault of this kind is later found in a quantity
  whose case passed.
- **Statistics**: pass or fail per quantity; no inference.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: each known case run on 5 seeds, since a stored value may depend on history.
- **Cost**: a unit test per quantity, run in CI once the Program is in Rust.
- **Waits on**: a Program with internal state.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A14. Mine the field's failures first

- **Question**: does a model the lab builds forbid something - is there a behaviour it says cannot happen - and
  does it avoid the failures that ended earlier cognitive architectures?
- **Method**: before a module is built, write the behaviour it forbids, and test it: an observation of the
  forbidden behaviour refutes the module. Read the critiques named in the leads for the failure modes the hunch
  names - unbounded state, commitments that cannot be revoked, mechanisms that explain any result after the fact -
  and check each module against them.
- **Baselines and controls**: a deliberately unfalsifiable module (one whose parameters can fit any outcome) must be
  flagged by the check.
- **Refuted if**: a module passes the check without a forbidden behaviour named, or the unfalsifiable control
  passes.
- **Statistics**: per module, the forbidden behaviour and the number of runs in which it was looked for and not
  seen; a single observation refutes.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: the forbidden behaviour is looked for over 20 seeds of the module's own test.
- **Cost**: reading, and one test per module.
- **Waits on**: the first brain modules (Phase 2).
- **Leads**: found - Johnson-Laird, Herrmann and Chaffin 1984, "Only connections: a critique of semantic networks"
  ([doi:10.1037/0033-2909.96.2.292](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.96.2.292)); Laird, Lebiere and Rosenbloom
  2017, "A Standard Model of the Mind" ([doi:10.1609/aimag.v38i4.2744](https://doi.org/10.1609/aimag.v38i4.2744)).
  Not found as specific works - "post-mortems of LIDA, Soar and ACT-R's associative learner" names no paper; it stays
  a lead to search for.

## A15. Computational psychiatry on a frozen copy

- **Question**: do ablations of a Program reproduce the published behavioural phenotypes of mutant and ablated
  worms, module for module - and does a fix for a signature correct its mechanism rather than hide it?
- **Method**: for each module, find the worm mutant or neuron ablation whose published phenotype matches what the
  module is meant to do (for example, an ablated reflex arc, H1, against worms whose touch circuit is lesioned); ablate the
  module on a frozen copy and compare its behaviour with the phenotype on the benchmark's measures.
- **Baselines and controls**: the intact Program; a sham ablation. The hunch's guard rail - a knob that makes a
  signature vanish must leave unrelated probes alone (A8's examination), or it is Goodhart.
- **Refuted if**: an ablation's phenotype differs from the worm's on the measures the published phenotype covers,
  judged by the per-feature statistics; or a fix makes a signature vanish while unrelated probes change.
- **Statistics**: per feature, the ablated Program against the mutant data with the two-sample tests of
  [the plan](../../PLAN.md), the multiple comparisons corrected as the frozen benchmark prescribes.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per ablation.
- **Cost**: 20 lives per ablation, and 20 for the intact Program.
- **Waits on**: a Program with modules, the frozen benchmark, and mutant recordings. Yemini et al. 2013 report
  phenotypes for "305 strains" ([doi:10.1038/nmeth.2560](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.2560), read in PubMed
  Central); whether those recordings sit in the Open Worm Movement Database beside the N2 ones
  ([data/README.md § The Phase 0 Survey](../../../data/README.md#the-phase-0-survey)), and which match a module, is
  for this experiment's preparation.
- **Leads**: none named.
