# Plans for A9-A15: Measuring the Worm, Second Half

Experiment plans for hunches A9 to A15 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). Like the first half ([A1-A8.md](A1-A8.md)), most of these plans test
an instrument - a dashboard, a checklist, a discipline - and are refuted when it fails on cases whose answer is
known in advance. Two of them (A12, A13) are rules for every experiment rather than experiments; their plans test
whether each rule catches the faults it names, with a planted fault for every item it covers.

The leads were looked up in Crossref by title on 2026-10-13. Found means the work exists as named, with the DOI
given; none was read in full. A lead that could not be found, or could not be reached, says so; leads the lab already
cites elsewhere point there.

Costs are counted in simulated lives. What one life costs in compute and memory is not known until the arena is
proven (STATE.md item 3), so no plan gives absolute figures yet; each will when it is prepared. Seed counts follow
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format) unless a plan says why it differs.

## A9. Vital signs for a long-lived brain

- **Question**: do the hunch's task-free readings, on control charts against the brain's own history, warn of runaway
  excitation, capture or drift before behaviour visibly breaks?
- **Method**: compute the dozen readings each simulated minute of a long life. Induce each failure deliberately - a
  gain raised slowly until excitation runs away, one unit's outgoing weights grown until it captures unrelated cues, a
  learning rate left unbounded - and name in advance, for each failure, the reading it targets (the branching ratio
  for runaway excitation, the hub-win rate for capture, the weight growth rate for drift). An *alarm* is that reading
  outside its band for three consecutive checks. *Behavioural failure* is the first simulated minute at which a
  population statistic of A1's assays, over the last ten minutes, leaves its band by A1's rule. The perturbational
  complexity index is computed once per hour of simulated life, each time by one short probe run on a frozen copy.
- **Baselines and controls**: unperturbed lives of the same length. Each reading's band is set as control limits at
  three standard deviations of its own history over the first tenth of the life. For independent, normally distributed
  values that would leave the band on 0.27% of checks by chance (P(|Z| > 3)); readings a minute apart in a learning
  brain are autocorrelated, and the first tenth may not be stationary, so the real rate is measured on these control
  lives rather than assumed, and the three-check persistence rule is there to absorb it.
- **Refuted if**: the targeted reading's alarm does not come before behavioural failure, by a margin larger than
  chance - the median lead time's 95% bootstrap interval does not lie above zero, where a seed whose targeted reading
  never alarms before the failure counts as a lead time of zero; or the alarms cannot be told from noise - with every
  perturbation starting at the end of the baseline tenth, the time from that point to the targeted reading's first
  alarm in the perturbed lives is not shown to be shorter than the same time in the control lives, by a one-sided
  rank-sum test at alpha 0.05 (null: the two times have the same distribution).
- **Statistics**: per failure kind, the median lead time with a bootstrap 95% interval; the times to first alarm from
  the end of the baseline tenth, perturbed against control, by reading, pooled over seeds; the rank-sum test above.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per failure kind and for the control - the format's default.
- **Cost**: one long life per seed and failure kind; the readings are sums over units each step, and the complexity
  index adds one probe run per simulated hour.
- **Waits on**: a Program with modulators and learning (H3, H6, H8, H9), A1's bands, and the arena for long lives.
- **Leads**: found - Casali et al. 2013
  ([doi:10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294)). Cited elsewhere - Beggs and
  Plenz 2003, in [H7](../README.md#h7-poised-dynamics).

## A10. Welfare and affect read from behaviour alone

- **Question**: does judgement bias, read from behaviour alone, track a creature's internal state the way welfare
  science reads an animal's - and which of the hunch's other readings carry over to a worm?
- **Method**: manipulate the creature's state through its world only (a run of rewards, or of aversive contacts), then
  score a judgement-bias task blind to the condition - a trained discrimination between two cues the worm can sense on
  the Grid, then an ambiguous cue between them. Only judgement bias is tested here, because it is the hunch's own test
  and the only reading with a predicted direction under both manipulations. The others are sorted first: approach
  latency to novelty, activity over a day and exploration are measurable on the Grid and are reported beside it, not
  tested; sleep regularity has a worm counterpart (lethargus, [Raizen et al.
  2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06535), cited in H8) and waits on a Program with a sleep-like state (H8);
  sheltering and grooming are set aside - the Grid has no shelter, and no nematode grooming measure is known to the
  lab yet. Whether nematodes show anything like play was a literature question given to the task that plans section D
  (where affect lives); its answer, that no report was found, is in
  [D7-D13.md § A10's Question](D7-D13.md#a10s-question-do-nematodes-show-anything-like-play), and the play hunch waits.
- **Baselines and controls**: an unmanipulated baseline, and a sham manipulation (the same handling, no change of
  state).
- **Refuted if**: the bias shift after the positive manipulation, or after the negative one, against sham, has a 95%
  interval that includes zero or lies on the wrong side of it; or the sham differs from the unmanipulated baseline on
  the bias measure, by an interval excluding zero (the task measures handling, not state).
- **Statistics**: bootstrap 95% intervals over seeds for each of the three comparisons; three intervals are read, so
  each is a Bonferroni interval at 1 - 0.05/3 (about 98.3%), valid at a family-wise 95%.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per condition - the format's default.
- **Cost**: four sets of 20 lives per test (positive, negative, sham, baseline).
- **Waits on**: a Program with an affective state (H3, H8) and learning (for the discrimination), and the arena.
- **Leads**: found - Mendl, Burman and Paul 2010
  ([doi:10.1098/rspb.2010.0303](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2010.0303)); Boissy et al. 2007
  ([doi:10.1016/j.physbeh.2007.02.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physbeh.2007.02.003)); Burghardt 2005, *The Genesis
  of Animal Play* (MIT Press, [doi:10.7551/mitpress/3229.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/3229.001.0001)).
  Not found as a work - Pellis and Pellis 2009, *The Playful Brain*: Crossref has only a magazine review of it, so it
  stays an unverified lead.

## A11. A sentience-indicator checklist, read with care

- **Question**: can published indicator criteria for sentience be scored against an architecture reliably, and does
  reading them with care tell an architecture built to meet them from one that meets them in function?
- **Method**: sort the criteria into three kinds. Behavioural criteria - motivational trade-offs (does the creature
  accept a cost to escape an aversive contact, and does the accepted cost scale with the aversion?), conditioned place
  preference and avoidance, self-protection - are written as tests on the Program. Structural criteria - nociceptors,
  integrative regions and their connections - are scored from the Program's manifest (A7) against each criterion's
  published definition, with no behaviour run. Analgesia restoring normal behaviour is tested with a modulator switch
  as the analgesic analogue, once a Program has one (H8). Score two Programs: one where each behavioural criterion is
  met by a hard-coded rule, and one where it emerges from the modules. Reading *with care*, as the hunch asks, means
  one more step per behavioural criterion met: perturb the situation slightly (a new arena, a cost of a new kind) and
  see whether the criterion is still met - a rule written for the test case should break, a capacity should carry
  over. A criterion is *met* in a condition when it is met on at least 15 of its 20 seeds (15 is the smallest count
  whose lower 95% Wilson bound, 0.531, is above one half; for 14 it is 0.481).
- **Baselines and controls**: a Program with no affective modules; the hard-coded Program is the control for the
  hunch's caution that indicators can be met by construction.
- **Refuted if**: the careful reading gives the hard-coded Program as many behavioural criteria carried over as the
  modular one (it reads construction, not function); or the Program with no affective modules meets more than one
  behavioural criterion other than self-protection, which a reflex arc (H1) alone may meet (the tests are too easy to
  mean anything); or two scorers - two agents that share no context, as the lab's verifiers do - agree with a kappa
  whose 95% lower bound is below 0.6.
- **Statistics**: per criterion, met or not by the 15-of-20 rule; the count carried over per Program; Cohen's kappa
  between the scorers, with a bootstrap interval.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per behavioural test and its perturbed variant - the format's default, which
  also sets the 15-of-20 rule above.
- **Cost**: two lives per behavioural criterion and seed, for each of the three Programs; structural scoring is
  reading.
- **Waits on**: Programs with nociception-like touch handling and affect (H1, H3, H8), a manifest (A7), and the arena;
  the analgesia criterion waits on a modulator switch (H8).
- **Leads**: found - Birch, Schnell and Clayton 2020, "Dimensions of Animal Consciousness"
  ([doi:10.1016/j.tics.2020.07.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2020.07.007)); Birch 2024, *The Edge of Sentience*
  ([doi:10.1093/9780191966729.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.1093/9780191966729.001.0001)). Not checked - the Cambridge
  Declaration on Consciousness (2012) has no DOI, and the agent proxy refused the connection to the site that hosts
  it. The lab measures behaviour and makes no claim about sentience ([the vision](../../VISION.md)); this checklist is
  an instrument, not a verdict.

## A12. Experiment hygiene

- **Question**: does auditing an experiment against the hunch's list catch the faults the list names, before they
  reach a verdict?
- **Method**: this is a rule for every experiment, so its test is an audit. The list, from the hunch: thresholds,
  baselines, seeds and outcome rules fixed before the run; no verdict relabelled afterwards; every baseline in its
  working regime; a theoretical ceiling as an anchor; a zero coefficient of variation looked into; several metrics
  reported; memory tests run below capacity; a control matched on correlation structure but lacking the hypothesised
  structure. Make one copy of a past experiment per item, each with one planted fault of that item's kind, shuffle
  them among unaltered copies, and audit them blind to which is which.
- **Baselines and controls**: the unaltered copies, which the audit must pass.
- **Refuted if**: the audit misses any planted fault, or fails an unaltered copy on an item it does not violate.
- **Statistics**: a count of planted faults caught out of the number of items, and of false failures; no inference.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: one planted copy per list item, and as many unaltered copies.
- **Cost**: reading time.
- **Waits on**: nothing - it can be applied now, to H10's first test (2026-10-13 journal), with the copies prepared by
  one agent and audited by another that has read neither that test's review nor the preparation. Of the faults its
  verifier found, the unspecified tie rule falls under "outcome rules fixed before the run"; the upward-biased
  estimator is on none of the list's items, so an audit by this list would not be expected to catch it.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A13. A stored quantity is not what its name says until a known case has been read

- **Question**: does checking every internal quantity against a known case, before anything consumes it, catch
  quantities that do not hold what their names say - and does giving each timescale its own variable remove the
  confusions the hunch names?
- **Method**: for each named internal quantity of a Program (a baseline, a set point, a tonic level), write a known
  case - an input whose correct value is known - and check the stored value. Split a name that covers two timescales
  into two variables, each with its own case: a quantity read at two timescales (seconds and tens of seconds, say)
  must give each its correct value on a case built to tell them apart.
- **Baselines and controls**: one planted fault of each kind the hunch names - a quantity that holds the wrong thing
  (a "baseline" that is really the last value), and a single variable doing duty for two timescales - each of which
  its known case must fail.
- **Refuted if**: a planted fault passes its known case; or, after the split, a case built to tell two timescales
  apart is still answered by one variable's value (the split did not separate them).
- **Statistics**: pass or fail per quantity; no inference.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: each known case on 5 seeds, since a stored value may depend on history; fewer than the
  default because a known case has a right answer, not a distribution.
- **Cost**: a unit test per quantity, run in CI once the Program is in Rust.
- **Waits on**: a Program with internal state.
- **Leads**: none named.

## A14. Mine the field's failures first

- **Question**: does a model the lab builds forbid something - is there a behaviour it says cannot happen - and does
  it avoid the failures that ended earlier cognitive architectures?
- **Method**: before a module is built, write the behaviour it forbids. Then check the forbidding is real: refit the
  module's free parameters to data that show the forbidden behaviour (synthetic data, made for the purpose); if the
  refit reproduces it within the module's own error band, the module did not forbid it. For the failure modes the
  hunch names - unbounded state and commitments that cannot be revoked - give each module a stated bound on its state
  (the number of values it holds) and a test that every commitment it makes can be withdrawn by a later input. Read
  Johnson-Laird, Herrmann and Chaffin 1984, a critique of semantic networks, for its failure modes, and Laird, Lebiere
  and Rosenbloom 2017, a proposal of a common model, for the commitments such architectures share.
- **Baselines and controls**: a deliberately unfalsifiable module (one with enough free parameters to fit any
  outcome), which the refit must flag.
- **Refuted if**: the refit fails to reproduce the forbidden behaviour in the unfalsifiable control, so the check
  passes it (the check cannot catch the fault it exists for); or a module's state exceeds its stated bound over a
  life; or a commitment cannot be withdrawn by the test's withdrawing input.
- **Statistics**: per module, the refit's error against its error band; the largest state over each life against the
  bound; pass or fail per commitment.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds of the module's own test, the format's default; state is tracked on every one.
- **Cost**: one refit per module, and the module's own test lives.
- **Waits on**: the first brain modules (Phase 2).
- **Leads**: found - Johnson-Laird, Herrmann and Chaffin 1984, "Only connections: a critique of semantic networks"
  ([doi:10.1037/0033-2909.96.2.292](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.96.2.292)); Laird, Lebiere and Rosenbloom 2017,
  "A Standard Model of the Mind" ([doi:10.1609/aimag.v38i4.2744](https://doi.org/10.1609/aimag.v38i4.2744)). Not found
  as specific works - "post-mortems of LIDA, Soar and ACT-R's associative learner" names no paper; it stays a lead to
  search for.

## A15. Computational psychiatry on a frozen copy

- **Question**: do ablations of a Program shift its behaviour the way the published mutants and ablations shift a
  worm's - and does a fix for a disorder-like signature correct its mechanism rather than hide it?
- **Method**: before any ablation runs, fix the pairing of each module with the worm mutant or neuron ablation whose
  published phenotype matches what the module is meant to do (for example, an ablated reflex arc, H1, against worms
  whose touch circuit is lesioned), and write it into the plan's pre-registration. Compare *shifts*: the ablated
  Program against the intact Program, beside the mutant against N2, on the measures the published phenotype covers. A
  *sham ablation* is the same switch thrown on a module that does nothing in the test world. A *fix* is a change to
  the ablated Program meant to restore it; it is tested by A8's examination before and after.
- **Baselines and controls**: the intact Program, which must first match N2 on those measures by the frozen
  benchmark's per-feature comparisons (a control that can fail - if it does not match, the shifts are not comparable
  and the test waits); the sham ablation, which must shift nothing.
- **Refuted if**: on a measure the phenotype covers, an ablation's shift differs in direction from the mutant's, or
  its 95% interval includes zero where the mutant's excludes it (a null ablation against a clear phenotype), by the
  per-feature comparisons the frozen benchmark will fix; or the sham shifts any measure, with the benchmark's
  correction for multiple comparisons; or a fix makes a signature vanish while any unrelated probe of A8's examination
  leaves its pre-registered band.
- **Statistics**: per measure, the two shifts with bootstrap 95% intervals, compared in sign; the frozen benchmark's
  per-feature comparisons once it fixes them.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 seeds per ablation, the format's default.
- **Cost**: 20 lives per ablation, and 20 for the intact Program.
- **Waits on**: a Program with modules, the frozen benchmark, A8's examination, and mutant recordings. Yemini et al.
  2013 report phenotypes for "305 strains" ([doi:10.1038/nmeth.2560](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.2560), read in
  PubMed Central); whether those recordings sit in the Open Worm Movement Database beside the N2 ones ([data/README.md
  § The Phase 0 Survey](../../../data/README.md#the-phase-0-survey)), and which match a module, is for this
  experiment's preparation. The hunch's general part - disorder-like signatures (perseveration, loosened association,
  blunted affect) watched as they happen - waits on a Program with associative memory (H9) and affect (H8), where such
  signatures could arise; the worm comparison above is the part testable first.
- **Leads**: none named.
