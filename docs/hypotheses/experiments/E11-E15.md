# Plans for E11-E15: Learning and Memory, Third Part

Experiment plans for hunches E11 to E15 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). They follow the conventions at the head of [E1-E5.md](E1-E5.md) and
[E6-E10.md](E6-E10.md) - the decision between *refuted*, *passed* and *inconclusive* by the rule at the head of
[B.md](B.md), pass tests uncorrected and refuting tests corrected (k refuting tests, each at alpha 0.05/k), zero as the
smallest effect of interest for a clause that predicts only a direction, a check that holds by construction written
as an assertion and not a test, and a Program's free parameters fitted on one part of the worm's data and scored on a
held-out part - and do not repeat them. Each refuting clause says when it passes as well as when it refutes; anything
between is inconclusive.

As in E6-E10, two kinds of plan are here: tests of a mechanism alone, with no Grid and no worm, whose models are named
here and fixed in full at preparation, before any seed of the test is run; and comparisons of a Program with the worm,
where a source gives the worm's behaviour. Where no source does, the plan says the test waits.

The leads were looked up on 2026-11-05: in Crossref by title, and the arXiv preprint in arXiv's own listing. Found
means the work exists as named, with the DOI given. Where an abstract was read, in Europe PMC or arXiv on that date,
the plan says so and what it takes from it; nothing else was read, and no number from any source is used yet. Sources
already cited in the repository say where they were read.

## E11. Completion, with gates

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): gated completion - one or two steps, a threshold, a cap, a fan-out penalty - keeps
  indirect evidence from outweighing direct input, so false memories fall while partial cues still complete.
- **Question**: does completion limited to memories one or two associative steps from the cue, with a threshold, a
  cap and a penalty on fan-out, keep indirect evidence below direct input - fewer false memories - without losing the
  completion of a genuinely partial cue?
- **Method**: a mechanism test on an associative graph, the network of [C18](C13-C24.md#c18-spreading-activation-has-a-cliff)
  as built for its run (journal of 2026-11-03, [§ C18's Plan, Run](../../journal/2026-11-03.md#c18s-plan-run)), so
  that its cliff is the known starting point. Memories are lists: each list is a set of eight items, all linked to one
  *lure*, an item that is never itself presented, after the false-memory list design in people ([Roediger and McDermott
  1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.21.4.803), found in Crossref, not read). Two variants share the graph and the cues: *ungated*, C18's spreading activation; and
  *gated*, the same with activation stopped after two hops, an item admitted only above a threshold, at most a capped
  number admitted, and each node's outflow divided by its fan-out. Threshold and cap are fixed at preparation on seeds
  1001 to 1003 to the smallest values for which a cue of four of a list's eight items completes the other four on the
  probe seeds; the test seeds are 1 to 20. *Assertion, not a test*: the gated variant's reach is at most two hops, so
  C18's cliff cannot occur in it. What no rule sets is how strongly a lure linked to many cued items is admitted
  against a stored item linked to one, which is what the clauses measure.
- **Baselines and controls**: the ungated variant; a graph with the lure's links removed (its activation must be zero
  in both variants); a list cued in full (nothing to complete, so completion adds nothing).
- **Refuted if**:
    1. The gated variant does not lower false memory: the share of cues whose lure is admitted, ungated minus gated,
       paired over networks, has an interval wholly at or below zero. Passed if wholly above zero.
    2. Gating costs completion: the share of the four missing items recovered, ungated minus gated, has an interval
       wholly above 0.1 (a loss of a tenth, the smallest of interest, fixed here). Passed if wholly below 0.1.
    3. Indirect outweighs direct: in the gated variant, the lure's activation divided by the weakest cued item's has
       an interval wholly at or above 1. Passed if wholly below 1.

  Three refuting tests, alpha 0.05/3 each.
- **Statistics**: per-network shares and ratios; paired bootstrap over networks, 10 000 resamples.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 networks, the default; each network's 100 cues drawn from its seed.
- **Cost**: a hop counter, a threshold and a cap per retrieval; the graph's size is C18's.
- **Switch**: `gate = off` runs the ungated variant on the same graph.
- **Waits on**: nothing for the test, which runs in tested Rust beside C18's code. A worm test waits on a source:
  no worm work on false recall or on completion from partial cues was found.
- **Leads**: none given by the hunch. C18's run is the lab's own.

## E12. Drives retrieve what could satisfy them

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): an active drive raises the memories that could satisfy it, by a bounded multiplier that
  never admits a memory below threshold; satiation removes the bias.
- **Question**: does a Program whose active drive multiplies, within a bound, the retrieval of memories that could
  satisfy it match the worm's change of decision with hunger, and lose it with satiation, better than the same Program
  with the multiplier pinned at one?
- **Method**: by the abstract of [Ghosh et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.10.030) (already cited in
  [D1-D6.md](D1-D6.md); abstract read again on 2026-11-05), food deprivation increases the worm's willingness to cross
  a hyperosmotic barrier to reach a source of food odour, by suppressing a pathway that sensitises the osmosensory
  neurons. Two Programs, equations fixed at preparation: the *drive Program*, in which hunger h in [0, 1] multiplies
  the retrieved value of food-associated memories by 1 + g h, g at least zero. Admission is decided on the *unbiased*
  value, against the threshold, before the multiplier acts; the multiplier then reranks only memories already admitted.
  A multiplied value compared with the threshold could not keep the bound: for any g above zero, a memory whose value
  lies between the threshold divided by 1 + g and the threshold would cross it at h = 1. *Assertion*, from the equations:
  the set of admitted memories does not depend on h, only their order and weight - the hunch's "never admits a memory
  below threshold"; and the *foil*, the same with g = 0. Both run Ghosh et al.'s barrier assay, read from the paper at
  preparation, fed and deprived; g is fitted on the fed and deprived conditions and the Program scored on a held-out
  deprivation time the paper reports, if it reports more than one; if it does not, the clause waits.
  *Assertion*: with g = 0 the foil's crossing does not depend on h; that a drive changes only drive-congruent recall
  is asserted from the equations too (the multiplier touches food-associated memories alone), and the control below
  checks it on the running Program.
  The hunch's mechanism is a memory bias; the worm's, by the abstract, is a change of sensory gain. Matching the
  behaviour would not show the worm does it by retrieval, and the write-up must say so.
- **Baselines and controls**: the foil. Harness controls: a Program with no food memory must cross at the same rate fed
  and deprived; a non-food memory's retrieval must not change with h (the ablation clause of the hunch).
- **Refuted if**: on the held-out condition, the drive Program's crossing rate is no closer to the worm's than the
  foil's is - the interval of the difference in distances, drive Program minus foil, wholly at or above zero. Passed if
  wholly below zero. One refuting test, alpha 0.05.
- **Statistics**: per-life crossing rates; distances normalised by the worm's between-animal spread; bootstrap over
  lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program and condition, the default.
- **Cost**: one multiplier per drive and memory class; one parameter, g.
- **Switch**: `drive_bias = off` pins g at zero.
- **Waits on**: a Program with drives and memories; an osmotic barrier and a sense for it - the Grid's floor solutes
  exist but no Program senses taste today ([docs/benchmark/STIMULI.md § What a Program Senses Today, and What Only
  Exists](../../benchmark/STIMULI.md#what-a-program-senses-today-and-what-only-exists)), and whether any of the four
  solutes could make the barrier is to be read from Ghosh et al.'s protocol; Ghosh et al. read in full for its
  protocol and numbers. A Grid without the barrier would need an
  issue in master-control, by the rule *This repository, and the world's by their own rules*.
- **Leads**: none given by the hunch. Added: Ghosh et al., "Neural architecture of hunger-dependent multisensory
  decision making in *C. elegans*", *Neuron*, 2016 ([doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2016.10.030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.10.030)) -
  found, abstract read.

## E13. Curiosity as expected information gain

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): ranking where to look by expected information gain, not novelty or entropy, makes
  exploration fall away from unlearnable noise - and, for the worm, explains its search after food is gone.
- **Question**: does a Program that explores where a look is expected to move its belief most reproduce the worm's
  switch from local to global search after food is gone better than one driven by novelty, or by a fixed timer - and
  does its score on an unlearnable noise source fall to zero, as the hunch claims?
- **Method**: two parts.
    - *The score, a derivation first.* The hunch offers "perhaps twice its posterior variance" for a success-and-failure
      belief as a candidate to derive. For a Beta(a, b) belief, n = a + b, one observation moves the posterior mean by a
      variance of Var(p)/(n + 1), since the two possible posterior means, (a + 1)/(n + 1) and a/(n + 1), are taken with
      probabilities a/n and b/n; so the expected fall in variance is Var(p)/(n + 1), not twice Var(p). This is worked
      here and re-derived, with the mutual information between the next outcome and p, in a notebook at preparation;
      the candidate is not used. Both scores go to zero on a coin of fixed bias, but at different rates: Var(p) is
      about m(1 - m)/n for a posterior mean m, so the variance score falls as 1/n², while the mutual information falls
      as about 1/(2n). Computed for this plan at a = b = n/2 (m = 0.5), in Python 3.13 with the standard
      library alone: the variance score is ab/(n²(n + 1)²); the mutual information is H(m) + m(ψ(a + 1) - ψ(n + 1)) +
      (1 - m)(ψ(b + 1) - ψ(n + 1)), with H the outcome's entropy in nats and the digamma ψ by recurrence up to 6 and its
      asymptotic series beyond. The variance score times n² is 0.111, 0.207, 0.245 and 0.2495 at n = 2, 10, 100 and
      1000, tending to m(1 - m), and the mutual information times n is 0.386, 0.475, 0.4975 and 0.49975. The verifier of
      [#221](https://github.com/ai-quokka-wannabe/iso-lab/pull/221) reproduced these figures by the same series, and at
      m = 0.2 found the same limits (review 5784946712 and its successor on the pull request); the notebook redoes them
      at preparation. The hunch's "an unlearnable random source stops attracting" is therefore an *assertion* about
      either score, not a test. The two are not interchangeable rivals: at the same n the variance score is smaller by a
      factor of order n (about 2m(1 - m)/n), and a source's score falls below a threshold θ after a number of looks
      that grows as θ^(-1/2) for the variance score and as θ^(-1) for the mutual information, so a Program using the
      first stops looking far sooner; which one the infotaxis Program uses is fixed at preparation, and the other is reported as
      a variant, not tested.
      A novelty score (visits counted) and an entropy score (the outcome's uncertainty, which stays at its maximum on a
      fair coin) are the foils whose failure on the coin the hunch predicts, also checked as assertions.
    - *The worm.* By the abstract of [Calhoun, Chalasani and Sharpee 2014](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.04220), the
      worm searches for food locally before switching to a global search, and both stages and the switch "can be
      quantitatively explained by a maximally informative search strategy"; a drift-diffusion model of three neurons
      approximates it. By the abstract of [Hills, Brockie and Maricq 2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1569-03.2004),
      area-restricted search is a time-dependent fall in turning after the last encounter with food. Three Programs:
      the *infotaxis Program*, turning to maximise expected information about the food's place from its own senses;
      the *timer foil*, turning at a rate that decays with time since food, Hills et al.'s description; the *novelty
      foil*, turning away from places visited. Parameters fitted on the turning rate in the first minutes off food and
      scored on the switch time and the area covered later, the split read from Calhoun et al. at preparation.
- **Baselines and controls**: the two foils. Harness controls: with no food ever met, the infotaxis Program's belief
  stays at its prior and its turning must not depend on time; a Program fed the food's true place must be rejected by
  the interface (*No cheating*: the belief is built from the Program's senses alone).
- **Refuted if**: on the held-out measures, the infotaxis Program is no closer to the worm than the better of the two
  foils - the interval of the difference in distances wholly at or above zero. Passed if wholly below zero. One
  refuting test, alpha 0.05; the foils' ranking is reported, not tested.
- **Statistics**: per-life measures, distances normalised by the worm's spread, bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program, the default.
- **Cost**: Calhoun et al. call the exact search computationally demanding; a full belief over places is not lean. The
  plan scores the three-neuron approximation they name beside the exact one, and reports both sizes and costs; if they
  score alike, the leaner wins, by *Lean brains*.
- **Switch**: `curiosity = off` replaces the information score by the timer foil's rule.
- **Waits on**: the frozen benchmark with an off-food search measure (not among the drafted measures today:
  [docs/benchmark/FEATURES.md](../../benchmark/FEATURES.md)); reference data for the switch, read from Calhoun et al.
  in full; a Program.
- **Leads**: Oudeyer and Kaplan, "What is intrinsic motivation? A typology of computational approaches", *Frontiers in
  Neurorobotics*, 2007 ([doi:10.3389/neuro.12.006.2007](https://doi.org/10.3389/neuro.12.006.2007)) - found. Kakade and
  Dayan, "Dopamine: generalization and bonuses", *Neural Networks*, 2002
  ([doi:10.1016/S0893-6080(02)00048-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0893-6080(02)00048-5)) - found. Orseau, Lattimore and
  Hutter, "Universal knowledge-seeking agents for stochastic environments", *Lecture Notes in Computer Science*, 2013
  ([doi:10.1007/978-3-642-40935-6_12](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-40935-6_12)) - found. None of the three was
  read; none is about the worm. Added: Calhoun, Chalasani and Sharpee, "Maximally informative foraging by
  *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *eLife*, 2014 ([doi:10.7554/eLife.04220](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.04220)) - found,
  abstract read; Hills, Brockie and Maricq, "Dopamine and glutamate control area-restricted search behavior in
  *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *Journal of Neuroscience*, 2004
  ([doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1569-03.2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1569-03.2004)) - found, abstract read.

## E14. Sleep as a bounded replay pass, driven by pressure

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): sleep pressure rises with waking and learning and falls only in sleep, which is a
  triggered replay pass with a hard cap that never writes more than real experience did.
- **Question**: does a Program whose sleep pressure rises with waking and with learning, falls only in sleep, and is
  paid down by a replay pass with a hard cap match the worm's quiescence - its homeostatic rebound after deprivation and
  its sleep after stress - better than one whose quiescence is a fixed schedule?
- **Method**: by the abstract of [Raizen et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06535) (cited in
  [E6](E6-E10.md#e6-separate-complete-consolidate-replay)), the worm's lethargus is a sleep-like state with
  homeostasis; by the abstract of [Hill et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.08.040) (cited in
  [docs/research/pace.md](../../research/pace.md); abstract read again on 2026-11-05), cellular stress, among it noxious
  heat, induces a sleep-like quiescence that depends on the ALA neuron and helps survival. Two Programs, fixed at
  preparation: the *pressure Program*, a two-process-like pressure after the lead below, with replay capped per pass and
  never writing more updates than waking experience produced (an *assertion* from its equations); and the *schedule
  foil*, the same quiescence bouts at fixed times. Paired copies from one snapshot, one allowed to sleep and one kept
  awake by gentle stimulation, as the hunch asks; the measure is the rebound - quiescence in the hour after
  deprivation, against the undeprived copy. Parameters fitted on the undeprived quiescence and scored on the
  rebound, read from the worm source at preparation. Whether learning load raises the worm's sleep pressure has no
  worm source found; that half of the hunch is not tested and waits on one.
- **Baselines and controls**: the schedule foil, whose rebound is zero by construction. Harness controls: with the
  stimulation off, the paired copies must stay bit-identical (they share a snapshot and a seed); with pressure pinned,
  no rebound.
- **Refuted if**: the pressure Program's predicted rebound is no closer to the worm's than zero - the foil's - is: the
  interval of the difference in distances wholly at or above zero. Passed if wholly below zero. One refuting test,
  alpha 0.05. Stress-induced sleep is a second clause written at preparation, once a sense for one of its triggers
  exists.
- **Statistics**: per-pair rebounds; distances normalised by the worm's spread; bootstrap over pairs.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 pairs, the default.
- **Cost**: one pressure variable and a replay counter; the replay pass costs at most its cap per bout.
- **Switch**: `sleep_pressure = off` runs the schedule foil.
- **Waits on**: a quiescence measure in the benchmark; a worm source on sleep rebound after deprivation in adults,
  since lethargus is developmentally timed (the hunch's words; its timing is to be read from Raizen et al.) and the
  Grid's body does not develop ([E19](E16-E20.md#e19-development-by-stage));
  for stress sleep, a sense of one of the triggers in Hill et al.'s abstract - noxious heat, cold, hypertonicity or tissue
  damage. No Program senses temperature or taste today, the latter needed for hypertonicity ([docs/benchmark/STIMULI.md § What a Program
  Senses Today, and What Only Exists](../../benchmark/STIMULI.md#what-a-program-senses-today-and-what-only-exists)).
  The pace rule binds the bout lengths: each is read from a source, not chosen.
- **Leads**: Borbély, Daan, Wirz-Justice and Deboer, "The two-process model of sleep regulation: a reappraisal",
  *Journal of Sleep Research*, 2016 ([doi:10.1111/jsr.12371](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.12371)) - found, not read;
  about mammals. Tononi and Cirelli, "Sleep and the price of plasticity", *Neuron*, 2014
  ([doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2013.12.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.12.025)) - found, not read; about
  vertebrates. Raizen et al. 2008 - as above. Added: Hill et al., "Cellular stress induces a protective sleep-like state
  in *C. elegans*", *Current Biology*, 2014 - abstract read.

## E15. Lessons about attractor memories

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*, from Matej's unrecorded experience): six claims hold in a modern Hopfield network:
  (a) flat storage recalls better than tree-shaped; (b) exact recall needs a fixed high inverse temperature; (c) at
  low temperature, structured memories keep their category, so temperature chooses the level of abstraction; (d)
  greedy coarse-to-fine descent compounds its errors; (e) adding category prototypes to the store hurts recall of the
  specific memories; (f) annealing does worse than a fixed temperature.
- **Question**: do Matej's six claims about attractor memories, given without a record, reproduce in a modern Hopfield
  network?
- **Method**: one notebook, as the hunch asks, on the network of [Ramsauer et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217),
  whose retrieval is a softmax over stored patterns at an inverse temperature beta. By its abstract (read on
  2026-11-05) the network has fixed points that store a single pattern, metastable states averaging over a subset of
  patterns, and a global average - the ground for the claim on gist. Stores are built with a known structure: 20
  categories, 10 members each, members a category prototype plus noise at a level fixed on probe seeds. Each claim
  becomes one measure: (a) flat storage against a two-level tree (category, then member) - exact recall; (b) exact
  recall against beta; (c) at low beta, the share of cues that settle nearer their own category's prototype than
  any other's; (d) the tree's coarse-to-fine descent, its error at the second level conditional on the first; (e)
  exact recall with and without the 20 prototypes added to the store; (f) an annealed beta against the best fixed one,
  at an equal number of updates. Settings, cue noise and the annealing schedule are fixed in the notebook before seed 1
  is run; the probe seeds are 1001 to 1003.
- **Baselines and controls**: for each claim, the alternative it names. Harness control: one stored pattern is
  retrieved from every cue at every beta above a floor set in the notebook.
- **Refuted if**, each against its null - zero, or chance for (c) - in the direction the claim states: (a) the tree's exact recall is not lower than
  the flat store's; (b) recall does not rise with beta; (c) the category is not kept above chance (1/20) at low beta;
  (d) second-level errors are not more frequent after a first-level error; (e) adding prototypes does not lower exact
  recall; (f) annealing is not worse than the best fixed beta. Each is refuted when its paired interval lies wholly on
  the far side of its null and passed when wholly on the near side. Six refuting tests, alpha 0.05/6 each.
- **Statistics**: paired bootstrap over stores, 10 000 resamples.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 stores, the default.
- **Cost**: a notebook's; nothing here goes into a Program. A store that passes is still far from worm-sized.
- **Switch**: not applicable - no module is built. What passes and is used later moves into tested Rust, with the
  switch of the module that uses it, by the rule *Notebooks for thinking*.
- **Waits on**: nothing; it can run tonight's way, in `notebooks/` with its requirements pinned.
- **Leads**: Ramsauer et al., "Hopfield networks is all you need", arXiv:2008.02217, 2020 - found in arXiv's listing,
  abstract read; published later at a conference not checked here. Mézard, Parisi and Virasoro, *Spin Glass Theory and
  Beyond*, 1987 - a Crossref search by title found only a 1988 review of the book in *Physics Today*
  ([doi:10.1063/1.2811676](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2811676)), and a chapter of the book itself, "Dynamics", whose Crossref record
  gives the book (World Scientific Lecture Notes in Physics) as its container and November 1986 as its date
  ([doi:10.1142/9789812799371_0007](https://doi.org/10.1142/9789812799371_0007)); not read. The book's year, 1986 or
  1987, is not settled here.
