# Plans for E16-E20: Learning and Memory, Fourth Part

Experiment plans for hunches E16 to E20 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format), under the conventions at the head of [E11-E15.md](E11-E15.md), which
are not repeated. Three of these hunches are less about how a worm behaves than about how the lab works: E16 is a
monitor, E19 a method of fitting and E20 a rule of storage. Their plans test what is testable and say plainly where a
hunch is a discipline to adopt rather than a claim about the animal. The leads were looked up in Crossref by title on
2026-11-05; found means the work exists as named, with the DOI given, and none of them was read beyond its record
unless the plan says so.

## E16. The spectrum as a health check

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): the eigenvalue spectrum of the stored patterns' similarity matrix warns of a store's
  failure before retrieval fails, and more cheaply than retrieval can be measured.
- **Question**: does a statistic of that spectrum predict which stores will retrieve badly better than the simplest
  rival monitor, the mean overlap between stored codes? Whether it warns *before* failure is a second question, which
  this plan describes but does not test (below).
- **Method**: E6's store ([E6-E10.md § E6](E6-E10.md#e6-separate-complete-consolidate-replay)), which already runs at
  four overlaps (20, 25, 30, 35) with and without separation, on seeds 1 to 20 - 160 stores with a known share of
  cues retrieved. For each store, the P by P matrix of overlaps between stored codes and its eigenvalues. *Assertion*,
  for the similar stores without separation only: with ten families of five the spectrum has ten eigenvalues standing
  above forty, by construction, and the hunch's "regular cascades" are this, not a finding. With separation the codes
  pass through a random projection and k-winners-take-all, and how much family structure survives is an empirical
  question, reported, not asserted; so is the size of the gap at overlap 20. The candidate statistic, fixed here: the
  ratio of the eleventh eigenvalue to the tenth - the gap between families and members. A store *fails* when under 0.9
  of its cues are retrieved. Each monitor is judged by its area under the receiver operating curve (AUC) for failure,
  with its direction fixed here: a *lower* eigenvalue ratio (a deeper gap, members packed tighter about their family)
  is taken to predict failure, and a *higher* mean overlap is taken to predict failure. An AUC is never flipped after
  the data are seen; one below 0.5 counts as it falls.
    - *Early warning, not tested.* Across the 160 stores, failure is largely set by the condition - overlap and
      separation - and both monitors largely encode that condition, so the AUC measures how well each tells conditions
      apart, not whether either warns early. A load ramp is run and reported, with no test: in each store at overlaps 25
      and 30, the families are stored one at a time, from 1 to 10, and at each load the share of cues retrieved and both
      statistics are recorded. With k families stored there are k family eigenvalues, so the spectral statistic at
      load k is the ratio λ(k+1)/λk - the gap between the last family eigenvalue and the first member one - which at
      k = 10 is the main analysis's λ11/λ10; the mean overlap needs no change. Each statistic's cut-off is the one from
      the main analysis (the value that maximises sensitivity plus specificity there), carried unchanged to every load.
      The report says, per store, at which load each statistic first crosses its cut-off and at which load retrieval
      first falls below 0.9. E6's capacity check found 50 random patterns well within capacity, so retrieval may
      never fall below 0.9 on the ramp; if it does not, in a store or in all, the report says so, and an empty ramp is
      read as having nothing to warn of - never as evidence against early warning. A test of early warning would be
      written from that description, on new seeds, before it is run.
    - *Not tested.* The claim that "symmetry breaking splits [the cascades] predictably" is not stated precisely enough
      to test, and is left until it is.
- **Baselines and controls**: the mean pairwise overlap, as a rival monitor; a shuffled-label control, whose AUC must
  have an interval containing 0.5.
- **Refuted if**: the spectral monitor's AUC minus the rival's has an interval wholly at or below zero. Passed if wholly
  above zero. One refuting test, alpha 0.05. If fewer than 10 of the 160 stores fail, or fewer than 10 succeed, the
  test is inconclusive and not run.
- **Statistics**: AUC by the Mann-Whitney statistic; the difference by bootstrap over seeds, resampling a seed's eight
  stores together.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: E6's 20 seeds, at eight stores each, for the test, with no new runs; the load ramp reruns
  the same seeds' stores at overlaps 25 and 30, ten loads each.
- **Cost**: an eigendecomposition of a 50 by 50 matrix per check - trivial beside retrieval. For a Program, a store of
  worm size is far smaller still.
- **Switch**: a monitor changes no behaviour; `spectrum_monitor = off` stops computing it, and the score must not move.
- **Waits on**: E6's store, built and run.
- **Leads**: none given by the hunch.

## E17. Maps at several scales

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): place codes at geometrically spaced scales, as in grid-cell modules, identify position
  over a large range; a head-direction ring attractor serves once heading must be inferred.
- **Question**, for the worm: does any measured worm behaviour need such a map - does the map Program, with its multi-scale
  path-integrated place code, come closer to the worm than the same code restricted to a horizon H, with a reset?
- **Method**: the hunch is written for later rungs (its *Why*: "for rodent rungs"), and the lab judges a module only on
  the worm. The worm's search after food is gone - local, then global ([Calhoun, Chalasani and Sharpee
  2014](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.04220), abstract read for [E13](E11-E15.md#e13-curiosity-as-expected-information-gain)) -
  is the one worm behaviour found where a sense of place could matter. An information-seeking searcher, E13's
  infotaxis Program, already keeps a belief about where the food lies relative to itself, and a belief carried by
  the Program's own motor commands and proprioception is already path integration. So "with and without a place code"
  is not the contrast, nor is a contrast of frames: the contrast is *range and reset*. The *map Program* is E13's
  searcher with a path-integrated place code of three modules at scales spaced by a ratio r, which holds the belief
  over the whole range of a life and keeps it across meetings with food. The *egocentric Program* is defined as its
  restriction: the same code and equations, with the range limited to a horizon H - positions farther than H are
  dropped from the belief - and the belief reset at each meeting with food. The head-direction ring is the same in
  both, so heading is not what the test varies. H and r are fixed at preparation, on probe seeds 1001 to 1003 and
  before the test seeds are drawn, and written into the journal: H from the extent of the worm's local search, read
  from Calhoun et al. in full, and r from Stensola et al. 2012, a
  rodent source not yet read - if it gives no usable ratio, r is set on the probe seeds and the plan says so. Both are driven only by what the Program senses and does
  (*No cheating*: the world's coordinates never reach either). E13's timer
  foil, which keeps no belief at all, is scored beside them as a reference. The prediction, written here and resting
  on no source - nothing read says how the worm represents place: the map does not help on the worm's measures.
- **Baselines and controls**: the egocentric Program; the timer foil as a reference. Harness control: the map Program, with its
  range set to H and its reset switched on, must be bit-identical to the egocentric Program - true by construction,
  since one is the restriction of the other, and so a check on the code, not on the hunch.
- **Refuted if**: the map Program is closer to the worm on E13's held-out measures - the interval of the
  difference in distances, map minus egocentric, wholly below zero. Passed (the map is not needed) if the interval lies
  wholly within plus or minus 0.2 of the worm's spread, the margin of no interest fixed here. One test, alpha 0.05.
  Note the direction: here the hunch's module is expected to fail on the worm, and passing keeps it out of the worm.
- **Statistics**: as E13.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program, the default.
- **Cost**: three modules of a few units each and a ring of perhaps a dozen - small, but not free, and kept only if the
  worm needs it.
- **Switch**: `place_code = off` sets the range to H and switches the reset on, which gives the egocentric Program.
- **Waits on**: E13, and its waits. For the rodent rung, a rodent benchmark, which does not exist.
- **Leads**: Hafting, Fyhn, Molden, Moser and Moser, "Microstructure of a spatial map in the entorhinal cortex",
  *Nature*, 2005 ([doi:10.1038/nature03721](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature03721)) - found. Stensola et al., "The
  entorhinal grid map is discretized", *Nature*, 2012 ([doi:10.1038/nature11649](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11649)) -
  found. O'Keefe and Nadel, *The Hippocampus as a Cognitive Map*, 1978 - a book; a Crossref search found the authors'
  1979 précis in *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* ([doi:10.1017/S0140525X00064256](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00064256)),
  not the book. All three are about rodents, and none was read.

## E18. Innate core systems, and the smallest consistent hypothesis

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): a few separable innate systems bootstrap later learning; generalising by the smallest
  hypothesis consistent with the examples makes learning sharp from few exposures.
- **Question**: does the worm's generalisation from a learned stimulus to its neighbours tighten with the number of
  training exposures, as a learner by the size principle predicts and a learner by similarity alone does not?
- **Method**: two learners, fixed at preparation: a *size-principle learner* after [Tenenbaum and Griffiths
  2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01000061), whose hypotheses are intervals of a stimulus dimension weighted by
  the inverse of their size per example; and a *similarity foil*, whose generalisation is a fixed kernel around the
  mean of the examples. *Assertion*: by its equations, the size-principle learner's gradient tightens from one exposure
  to three and the foil's does not; this is construction, not a finding. The test is the worm's: whether its gradient
  tightens. A Europe PMC title search on 2026-11-05 for *elegans* with *generalization* or *generalisation* and odour
  learning returned nothing; no worm source on generalisation gradients against the number of trainings is known to
  the lab, so the test waits on one. The "innate core systems" half has no worm form beyond what the
  [C](C1-C12.md) plans already build (innate reflexes beneath learning), and is not planned separately.
- **Baselines and controls**: the foil; a learner with no training, whose gradient must be flat.
- **Refuted if**: once a source exists - the size-principle learner's predicted tightening, fitted at one exposure and
  scored at three, is no closer to the worm's than the foil's zero is. Interval of the difference in distances wholly
  at or above zero refutes; wholly below passes. One test, alpha 0.05.
- **Statistics**: gradient width per life; distances normalised by the worm's spread; bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per learner and number of exposures.
- **Cost**: the size-principle learner over intervals of one dimension is a sum over a small grid - lean. Over many
  dimensions it is not, and that would be its own question.
- **Switch**: `size_principle = off` uses the foil's kernel.
- **Waits on**: a worm source on generalisation against training; a stimulus dimension on the Grid the worm learns
  along (the odour of food orbs alone today).
- **Leads**: Spelke and Kinzler, "Core knowledge", *Developmental Science*, 2007, volume 10, pages 89-96
  ([doi:10.1111/j.1467-7687.2007.00569.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2007.00569.x)) - found, online in December
  2006 and in print in 2007; about human infants. Tenenbaum and Griffiths,
  "Generalization, similarity, and Bayesian inference", *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 2001 - found; about people.
  Neither was read.

## E19. Development by stage

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): a Program fitted to one stage of life is not right for another; targets per stage, from
  developmental data with error bars, fixed before any run, tuned on one probe set and judged on another, keep a
  Program honest across a life.
- **Question**: once the Grid's body has stages, does a Program re-tuned per stage match each stage's behaviour better
  than one tuned on the adult alone?
- **Method**: two parts. The *discipline* - targets fixed before runs, a fitting set and a held-out set - is already
  the lab's rule (*Realism over speed*, [A1](A1-A8.md#a1-a-validation-suite-with-pass-bands-fixed-before-any-tuning),
  and the held-out split at the head of [E1-E5.md](E1-E5.md)), and needs no experiment. The *stages* do. The worm passes
  four larval stages to the adult, or an alternative dauer larva when conditions are poor ([Cassada and Russell
  1975](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(75)90109-8); [Byerly, Cassada and Russell
  1976](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(76)90119-6); both found in Crossref, neither read, no abstract in Europe PMC;
  that the worm has four larval stages and that the dauer is an alternative to the third is the field's textbook
  account, held with high confidence but not read in these sources tonight; the timings are to be read from them). The test, when a
  staged body exists: per-stage benchmarks frozen before runs; a per-stage Program and an adult-only Program, both
  scored on held-out recordings of each stage.
- **Baselines and controls**: the adult-only Program; the adult stage itself, on which both must score alike.
- **Refuted if**: on the non-adult stages, pooled, the per-stage Program is no closer to the worm than the adult-only -
  interval of the difference wholly at or above zero. Passed if wholly below. One test, alpha 0.05.
- **Statistics**: per-life benchmark distances, bootstrap over lives, stratified by stage.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program and stage.
- **Cost**: a parameter set per stage - several times the adult's parameters, the brain's size unchanged.
- **Switch**: `per_stage = off` uses the adult parameters at every stage.
- **Waits on**: a body that grows and changes stage, which the Grid's body does not; reference recordings of larvae and
  dauers, which the lab's reference data (adult N2 recordings,
  [data/README.md § The Phase 0 Survey](../../../data/README.md#the-phase-0-survey)) does not hold. A staged body would
  be a need from master-control and tron-grid-lite, raised as an issue only once the adult benchmark is frozen; it is
  not raised tonight.
- **Leads**: none given by the hunch. Added: Cassada and Russell, "The dauerlarva, a post-embryonic developmental
  variant of the nematode *Caenorhabditis elegans*", *Developmental Biology*, 1975; Byerly, Cassada and Russell, "The
  life cycle of the nematode *Caenorhabditis elegans*. I. Wild-type growth and reproduction", *Developmental Biology*,
  1976 - both found, neither read.

## E20. Identity is the persisted pattern

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*, and mostly a rule of engineering): a long-lived creature is its persisted state; it should
  never be wiped, should be stored in a format that grows new fields without migrations, keep an active tier and a cold
  one, and be snapshotted at every pause.
- **Question**: does a Program's snapshot resume a life exactly within one build, and does every field of an old
  snapshot survive a later build?
- **Method**: a test in Rust, not an experiment on behaviour. A snapshot format with each field tagged and versioned,
  unknown fields kept and carried through, missing fields filled by a declared default. Two parts, kept apart because a
  later build may legitimately change behaviour, which is no fault of the format:
    - *Within one build: trajectory equality.* Run a life for T ticks; snapshot the Program at t; resume from the
      snapshot. The per-tick state hashes from t to T must equal the uninterrupted life's. The world's side is resumed by
      replaying the world from its seed and the input log to tick t, which is evaluation-side work the rules allow; the
      Program receives only its own snapshot and, from tick t on, its senses as before.
    - *Across builds: a field round-trip.* Golden snapshots from earlier builds are kept as test data. A later build
      loads each, saves it again, and the two are compared field by field: every field of the original, known to the
      build or not, must come back byte for byte. Fields the later build adds, filled by their declared default, are
      listed in the report and not counted as changes. Whether the resumed life then behaves as before is not asked.
  Red-first: a build that drops one field must fail the round-trip, and a resume that skips one field must fail the
  trajectory equality.
  Every snapshot carries a checksum over all its bytes, unknown fields included, checked on load; a snapshot whose
  checksum fails is refused.
  *Where it meets the rules.* "Never wipe it" does not conflict with scoring a population of fresh lives
  ([A2](A1-A8.md#a2-score-a-population-of-seeds-never-one-worm)): a benchmark life starts from a recorded state and seed,
  which is the snapshot, and a long life is never wiped to make it score better. Forgetting as demotion to a cold tier
  is [E8](E6-E10.md#e8-two-strengths-forgetting-loses-influence-not-the-trace)'s hunch and is tested there. A snapshot is
  the Program's own state, never the world's (*No cheating*).
- **Baselines and controls**: the uninterrupted life, for the first part; the original bytes, for the second. Control:
  a snapshot with one bit flipped - once in a known field and once inside an unknown one - must be refused by the
  checksum, never carried through silently.
- **Refuted if**: within one build, any resumed life's hash differs from the uninterrupted life's at any tick; across
  builds, any field of the original, known or unknown, does not come back byte for byte; or a flipped bit is not
  refused. These are pass-or-fail properties,
  not statistics: one failure refutes the format.
- **Statistics**: none; exact equality over every tick, and over every byte of every original field.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: seeds 1 to 20, each with snapshots at three ticks fixed here (a quarter, a half and three
  quarters of T); every golden snapshot kept, for the round-trip.
- **Cost**: the snapshot is the Program's state, a few kilobytes for a worm-sized brain; writing it costs nothing a
  score depends on.
- **Switch**: not applicable - no behaviour changes.
- **Waits on**: a Program with state that persists across ticks, its per-tick state hash, and the world's replay from
  seed and input log to a given tick.
- **Leads**: none given by the hunch.
