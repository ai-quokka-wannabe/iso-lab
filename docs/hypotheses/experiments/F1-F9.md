## F1. Phase is the missing ingredient: complex amplitudes

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): a network of co-active candidates lacks only interference to be a quantum-like system,
  and interference needs phase; for the worm, the one interpretable phase is timing against a body rhythm.
- **The mathematics, stated correctly.** Interference needs *signed* amplitudes, not complex ones: real-valued
  quantum theory has interference, and by Renou et al.'s abstract it reproduces the outcomes of any multipartite
  experiment whose parts share arbitrary real states. Their result is that real and complex quantum theory make different
  predictions in network scenarios with *independent* sources and measurements (that they differ only there is the
  lab's inference from the abstract's sentence on prior work, not the paper's claim); two experiments report the violation of the real
  bound (Li et al. 2022, photonic, over 4.5 standard deviations; Chen et al. 2022, superconducting, 43 standard
  deviations - both from the abstracts). This is about physics and says nothing about brains: the hunch's step "a
  real-valued brain would be a shadow" is an analogy, not an inference, and is not tested here. The merge rule
  cos²(Δθ/2) is exact for two unit amplitudes of phase difference Δθ: |e^(iθ1) + e^(iθ2)|²/4 = (1 + cos Δθ)/2.
  On the Cayley-Dickson ladder, the sedenions have zero divisors (Baez 2002, § 2.2, read).
- **Question**: does a Program whose candidates carry a phase show a behavioural signature that a signed-real Program
  and a classical stochastic Program, matched in size, cannot - and does the worm show it?
- **Method**: three Programs of the same candidate-competition shape: *classical*, non-negative weights summed;
  *signed*, real amplitudes with sign; *phased*, unit complex amplitudes whose phase is the timing of the candidate
  against the Program's own undulation rhythm, so that the phase has a meaning a worm could have. Three probes, each
  fixed here:
    - *Reconvergence*: two routes to one candidate, opened singly and together; the interference term is
      P(both) - P(route 1) - P(route 2). *Assertion*: it is zero in the classical Program by construction and can be
      non-zero in both others; so interference alone cannot tell phase from sign, and is reported, not tested.
    - *Order on two binary probes*: two stimuli A and B in both orders, each answered by a reversal or not. The test is
      the quantum question (QQ) equality of [Wang et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1407756111), whose abstract
      (read in Europe PMC) calls it an a priori prediction of quantum probability about the order of two successive
      questions. The quantity, as the lab writes it: q = [P(A yes, B no) + P(A no, B yes)] - [P(B yes, A no) +
      P(B no, A yes)], predicted zero. The derivation uses only orthogonal projectors, so real vectors satisfy it as well as complex ones:
      #225's verifier found q ≈ 1e-16 for random real states in R⁵ with real projectors of rank 2 and 3 (seed 1, three
      draws, review 5785463966), while the order effect on P(A yes) reached -0.19. So this probe cannot tell phase from
      sign either; a pass on it is consistent with the framework and is no evidence of phase - a classical Program with
      no order effect passes it too. The exact form and conditions are checked in the paper's body at preparation. Order
      effects alone (a non-zero difference of P(A yes) between orders) are no evidence of phase, since a classical
      Program with memory has them.
    - *Selection exponent*: a candidate is chosen with probability proportional to |amplitude|^p. In a built Program p
      is a design choice, so measuring it there is an assertion; it is a test only where p is fitted to the worm.
- **Baselines and controls**: the classical and signed Programs, at the same number of candidates and memory. The
  phased and signed Programs share one implementation, the amplitude's type aside, so that a phased Program with every
  phase pinned to zero is bit-identical to the signed Program with every sign pinned positive - a check on the code.
- **Refuted if**, the test on the worm, when a source gives two-stimulus responses in both orders: (a) the worm's QQ
  quantity q has a 95% interval lying wholly outside a band of ±0.05, fixed here as the smallest departure worth
  fitting - then the models that predict the equality do not fit, and the hunch's framework fails on the worm; passed if
  the interval lies wholly inside the band, which is consistent with the framework and no evidence of phase; (b), the
  one clause that contrasts phase with sign: on the benchmark, the phased Program's distance to the worm minus the signed
  Program's has an interval wholly at or above zero - phase earns no place; passed if wholly below zero.
- **Statistics**: q by bootstrap over animals (worm) or seeds (Program), 10 000 resamples, percentile intervals; (b)
  by bootstrap over seeds, one-sided, corrected for two refuting tests (alpha 0.025 each).
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per Program, the default. Probe seeds 1001 to 1003 for fixing stimulus timings.
- **Switch**: `phase = off` pins every phase at zero (the control above).
- **Cost**: two numbers per candidate instead of one, and a complex multiply per interaction; H10 measured twice the
  compute at the same memory for a phasor memory (STATE.md, *To Revisit*). Reported beside the score.
- **Waits on**: a Program with competing candidates and an undulation rhythm; for (a), a worm source with two stimuli
  presented in both orders - none is known to the lab; the benchmark, frozen, for (b). H10's re-run (STATE.md item 4)
  comes first.
- **Leads**: [Renou et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04160-4), abstract read in arXiv (2101.10873);
  the experiments the hunch asks to find: [Li et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.040402) and
  [Chen et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.040403), abstracts read in arXiv (2111.15128, 2103.08123);
  [Busemeyer and Bruza 2012](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511997716) and [Pothos and Busemeyer
  2013](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X12001525), found, not read; [Baez 2002](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-01-00934-X),
  full text searched as above (*Bulletin of the AMS* 39, 2002; Crossref's date, 2001, is its online date).

## F2. Cognition on the tree-like side of the numbers

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): a creature's learned similarity between its internal states is ultrametric - tree-like -
  while its modulator state stays continuous.
- **The mathematics, stated correctly.** By Ostrowski's theorem every non-trivial absolute value on the rationals is
  equivalent to the usual one or to a p-adic one, one for each prime p; the completions are the reals and the p-adic
  numbers Q_p - one real completion and infinitely many p-adic ones, not two. A p-adic metric is an ultrametric:
  d(x, z) ≤ max(d(x, y), d(y, z)), so every triangle is isosceles with its two longest sides equal. The test below
  needs only the ultrametric property, not p-adic numbers. (Ostrowski's theorem is a textbook result, not looked up
  tonight; it is cited from a text at preparation.)
- **Question**: is a Program's matrix of distances between its internal states closer to an ultrametric than matched
  controls?
- **Method**: from a Program's life, its internal state at each decision (D decisions), the distance between every
  pair. Ultrametricity is measured, as fixed here, by the *relative distortion* of the subdominant ultrametric - the
  largest ultrametric below d, given by single-linkage clustering (Rammal, Toulouse and Virasoro 1986, found, not
  read; the construction is checked against it at preparation): U = mean over pairs of (d - d_u)/d. U is 0 for an
  exact ultrametric and grows as the data depart from one. A trap fixed here: points scattered in many dimensions are
  nearly equidistant, and an equilateral triangle satisfies the ultrametric inequality, so a raw index flatters high
  dimensions. Hence the null below is matched in dimension, mean and covariance, and so in the scale of distances.
- **Baselines and controls**: *null*, for each seed 100 Gaussian surrogates with the same mean and covariance as the
  Program's states and the same D - dimension and the scale of distances kept, anything beyond second order broken;
  *positive control*, states generated by a branching process on a binary tree of depth 6, with Gaussian noise at a
  ratio of branch step to noise fixed at preparation on seeds 1001 to 1003 - its U minus its null's must have an
  interval wholly below zero, or the harness cannot see a tree and the plan stops.
- **Refuted if**: the Program's U minus its null's U has an interval wholly at or above zero; passed if wholly below
  zero. The same contrast is reported for the worm's posture states (eigenworm coefficients, from the benchmark), with
  no test, since the hunch is about learned states.
- **Statistics**: per seed, U and the mean U of its 100 surrogates; the difference by bootstrap over seeds, one-sided, alpha
  0.05.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20, the default.
- **Switch**: none - a measure changes no behaviour. If a Program built on the hunch (discrete attractor labels over
  continuous modulators) is written, `labels = off` replaces the labels with the continuous state, and the benchmark
  decides between the two.
- **Cost**: D² distances and a single-linkage tree, O(D²) memory; at D = 2000, four million pairs - an evaluation
  tool's cost, never a brain's.
- **Waits on**: a Program with learned internal states; the frozen benchmark for the worm's posture states.
- **Leads**: [Rammal, Toulouse and Virasoro 1986](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.58.765), found, not read.

## F3. Memories as codewords of an error-correcting code

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): memories stored as the codewords of a highly symmetric code are recalled as well from any
  few features as from any other, with a few corrupted features corrected and more detected.
- **The mathematics, stated correctly.** The extended binary Golay code has length 24, 4096 codewords and minimum
  distance 8; its weight distribution is 1, 759, 2576, 759, 1 at weights 0, 8, 12, 16, 24 (computed for this plan: the
  4096 multiples of g(x) = x^11 + x^10 + x^6 + x^5 + x^4 + x^2 + 1 of degree below 23, each extended by a parity bit,
  in Python 3.13 with the standard library). So it corrects any 3 flipped bits, or detects any 7, or corrects 3 and
  detects 4 at once; it recovers any 7 erased bits, and 8 erasures fail exactly when the erased positions are the
  support of one of the 759 weight-8 codewords. That its symmetry group is the Mathieu group M24, acting 5-transitively,
  is from Conway and Sloane (not read tonight; checked there at preparation). "Recall from any few features would be
  equivalent" therefore holds for any five features, by symmetry, and not for every larger set.
- **Question**: does a store whose memories are Golay codewords recall more robustly from corrupted cues than a store
  of random words, and than a store of random words with the same minimum distance?
- **Method**: a mechanism test. 64 memories of 24 binary features - far above a classical Hopfield network's
  capacity at 24 units, so recall is by a modern Hopfield network, a softmax over the stored patterns at inverse
  temperature β ([E15](E11-E15.md#e15-lessons-about-attractor-memories)'s model), with β fixed on seeds 1001 to 1003
  as the smallest value at which the controls below hold - from cues with f features flipped (f = 0 to 6)
  or k features given and the rest erased (k = 10 to 24). *Golay store*: 64 codewords drawn at random from the code.
  *Matched store*: 64 random words drawn one at a time, each rejected unless at least 8 from every word kept - a random
  code with the same minimum distance. Its distances are not otherwise matched (the Golay draw's lie only at 8, 12, 16
  and 24, which random words almost never reproduce); both histograms are reported. A random 64 of the 4096 codewords
  does not inherit the code's symmetry, so evenness across which features are given is not predicted for the
  Golay store by the mathematics, and is reported, not tested. *Assertion*: at large β the softmax recalls the nearest
  stored word, so any store with minimum distance 8 - the Golay store and the matched store alike - recalls every cue
  with f ≤ 3; the stores can differ only from f = 4, where a cue may lie as near to a second stored word as to its own.
- **Baselines and controls**: the matched store; an unstructured store of 64 random words; as harness checks, a store
  of two codewords at distance 8 must recall every cue with f = 3 at the chosen β (a cue 3 from one word is 5 from the
  other, so a β too low fails it), and so must both stores of minimum distance 8. A store of one codeword recalls it
  from any cue at any β, by construction - an assertion, not a check.
- **Refuted if**: (a) at f = 3, the Golay store's recall minus the unstructured store's has an interval wholly at or
  below zero; passed if wholly above - the expected outcome, since random words often lie closer than 8; (b) at f = 4,
  the Golay store's recall minus the matched store's has an interval wholly at or below zero - nothing in the code
  beyond its minimum distance helps; passed if wholly above. The readout, fixed here: after the softmax settles, the stored word
  nearest in Hamming distance to the final state, each coordinate of the final state first rounded to 0 or 1; a cue counts as recalled if
  that word is its own. A tie in that distance - which an exact tie between two patterns, whose softmax returns their
  mean, produces - counts as a failure. Reported, not
  tested: the variance of recall across 100 random k-subsets at k = 12, in
  both stores, and recall over all f and k.
- **Statistics**: recall as the share of cues that settle on their own memory; each difference by bootstrap over
  seeds, one-sided, corrected for two refuting tests (alpha 0.025 each).
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20, the default; each seed draws its own 64 codewords and its own matched store.
- **Switch**: `code = random` replaces the codewords with the matched store.
- **Cost**: 64 stored patterns of 24 bits and a softmax over 64 per step - tiny. The worm has no 24-bit memory known; this is a mechanism
  test, and a Program uses it only if a store of its own size is shown to need it.
- **Waits on**: nothing; a notebook.
- **Leads**: [Conway and Sloane 1999](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4757-6568-7), found, not read.

## F4. Stable thoughts as idempotents of an exceptional algebra

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): a stable thought is an idempotent of the 27-dimensional exceptional Jordan algebra h3(O),
  reached by repeated squaring.
- **The mathematics, stated correctly**, from Baez 2002 (searched as above): h3(O), the 3 by 3 self-adjoint octonionic
  matrices with the product a∘b = (ab + ba)/2, is 27-dimensional (3 real diagonal entries and 3 octonions); a
  projection is an element with p∘p = p; every element can be diagonalised by an automorphism; the trace-1 projections
  are the points of the octonionic projective plane, a 16-dimensional manifold. Two corrections to the hunch follow.
  First, the fixed points of x ↦ x∘x are exactly the idempotents, by definition - an assertion, not a finding.
  Second, plain repeated squaring of a diagonalisable element sends each eigenvalue λ to λ^(2^n), so it diverges or
  goes to zero unless every eigenvalue is 0 or 1; *normalised* squaring, x ↦ x∘x / tr(x∘x), goes to the projection
  onto the eigenvalues of largest absolute value, divided by its rank. That is power iteration, and it is the same in
  the real symmetric matrices h3(R), 6-dimensional.
- **Question**: does normalised squaring in h3(O), used as an attractor memory, store or separate more than the same
  dynamics in h3(R) and h3(C) at equal real dimension of state?
- **Method**: a mechanism test, in a notebook first. Normalised squaring uses no stored item: it maps any cue to its
  own top eigenprojection, so as a memory it is spectral denoising followed by decoding to the nearest stored item.
  The parts, fixed here: an *item* is a rank-1 projection in h3(O), and in the h3(R) and h3(C) memories a tuple of
  rank-1 projections, one per copy (5 and 3 copies, 30 and 27 real numbers; 30 is the nearest multiple of 6 at or
  above 27). A cue is an item plus independent Gaussian noise of the same variance σ² on every real coordinate, in
  every algebra. A cue is *recalled* when the stored item nearest to the recalled state, by the Frobenius distance
  summed over copies, is its own; *capacity* is the largest number K of stored items at which at least 0.9 of cues are
  recalled, at σ fixed on seeds 1001 to 1003. *What geometry alone predicts*: the rank-1 projections form manifolds of
  16 real dimensions in h3(O) (OP², Baez), against 3 × 4 = 12 for three copies of CP² and 5 × 2 = 10 for five
  copies of RP² (standard dimensions, not looked up), so at equal noise per coordinate h3(O) has more room and is
  expected to win on capacity whatever the dynamics - a property of OP², not of an attractor. The test is therefore
  on what squaring adds beyond decoding the raw cue. *Needs a redesign before it runs*: how to draw rank-1 projections
  in h3(O) uniformly - over OP², whose points need triples (x, y, z) with (xy)z = x(yz) (Baez) - is not settled
  tonight; until a sampler is written and checked (p∘p = p and trace 1 to rounding), the plan does not run. The
  hunch's "block structure of that dimension in learned representations" is not tested: no learned representation in
  the lab has 27 dimensions to look for, and a block of 27 found in a larger one would need a null that no source
  gives.
- **Baselines and controls**: h3(R) and h3(C) at matched dimension; nearest-neighbour decoding of the raw cue, with
  no squaring, in each algebra; a positive control - a cue equal to a stored projection returns it unchanged after one
  step (an assertion: p∘p / tr(p∘p) = p for a trace-1 projection).
- **Refuted if**: the gain of squaring over raw decoding in h3(O) (capacity with squaring minus capacity without)
  minus the larger of the same gain in h3(R) and h3(C) has an interval wholly at or below zero; passed if wholly
  above zero. The raw capacities are reported beside it, against the geometric expectation.
- **Statistics**: capacity per seed; the difference by bootstrap over seeds, 95% intervals, one-sided, alpha 0.05.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20, the default.
- **Switch**: `algebra = real` swaps h3(O) for the matched h3(R) memory.
- **Cost**: an octonionic product, written out, is 64 real multiplies; one square of a 3 by 3 octonionic matrix
  is at most 27 such products, about 1700 multiplies - an upper bound, since the diagonal is real and the matrix
  self-adjoint. Reported; a worm
  brain would have to need it.
- **Waits on**: a sampler for OP² (above), then the notebook. It is the least worm-relevant plan here, as the hunch itself says ("no
  engineering use is proposed").
- **Leads**: [Jordan, von Neumann and Wigner 1934](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968117), found, not read;
  [Albert 1946](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1946-0016759-3), *On Jordan algebras of linear transformations*,
  found, not read; Albert's 1934 paper in the same journal issue as Jordan, von Neumann and Wigner, [*On a certain
  algebra of quantum mechanics*](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968118), is also found, not read - which of the two the
  hunch means is not known; Baez 2002, as in F1.

## F5. Self-similar groups as the symmetry of concept hierarchies

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): concept hierarchies behave like a self-similar group acting on a rooted tree: growth
  between polynomial and exponential, statistics alike across scales, recall respecting subtrees.
- **The mathematics, stated correctly.** Grigorchuk's group, generated by four automorphisms of the binary rooted tree,
  was constructed in 1980 as a finitely generated infinite periodic group (the record found in Crossref is that paper);
  its intermediate growth - faster than any polynomial, slower than any exponential - was proved in a later paper of
  Grigorchuk's, not looked up tonight. Growth is a property of an infinite group's balls as their radius goes to
  infinity, so a finite graph has no growth type: only a range of radii over which balls grow like one. That is the
  maintainer's lesson (a small dense graph saturates before any regime shows), and it fixes the method.
- **Question**: (i) does the harness classify growth correctly on graphs of known type; (ii) in a Program's concept
  graph, are recall errors confined to subtrees more often than a matched null predicts?
- **Method**, (i): balls B(r) of the Cayley graphs of Z² and Z³ (polynomial), of the free product of three groups of
  order 2 and of the free group on two generators (the 3- and 4-regular trees, exponential), and of Grigorchuk's group
  with generators a, b = (a, c), c = (a, d), d = (1, b), computed by breadth-first search on its action on level 7 of
  the binary tree. A first classifier, fitting log |B(r)| against log r and against r, was shown by #225's verifier
  (review 5785463966, balls to r = 37) to label Grigorchuk's group exponential; it is withdrawn. The rule now proposed
  reads the trend of the local growth ratio ρ(r) = |B(r + 1)| / |B(r)|: κ is the least-squares slope of log log ρ(r)
  against log r over r in [R/4, R - 1]. Asymptotically κ tends to -1 for polynomial growth (log ρ ≈ d/r), to 0 for
  exponential growth, and to α - 1, between them, for growth like exp(r^α). The labels: *polynomial* if κ ≤ -0.75,
  *exponential* if κ ≥ -0.05, *intermediate* between.
    - *Shown on the controls, on this date.* At R = 20, 25 and 30, κ is -0.904, -0.921 and -0.933 for Z²; -0.008,
      -0.003 and -0.001 for the 3-regular tree; -0.205, -0.218 and -0.218 for Grigorchuk's group; and at R = 20 and 30,
      -0.894 and -0.928 for Z³, -0.001 and -0.000 for the 4-regular tree - every one labelled correctly. Method: Python
      3.13, standard library; Z² and the trees from their closed forms 2r² + 2r + 1, 1 + 3(2^r - 1) and 1 + 2(3^r - 1),
      Z³ by counting lattice points; Grigorchuk's balls by the breadth-first search above, which gives 1, 5, 11, 23,
      40, 68, 108, 176 for r = 0 to 7 (the verifier's figures) and 1 001 119 at r = 30. No script is committed; the
      method is enough to reproduce it.
    - *Caution, said plainly.* The thresholds -0.75 and -0.05 were chosen after these controls were computed, so the
      rule is fitted to its controls. It is frozen only after it labels, unseen, one more group of each kind at the same
      R - chosen at preparation and named in the journal before it is computed. If any is mislabelled, or if no rule
      separates all controls at the largest R the cap allows (about 37), F5's harness has failed: (ii) is not run, and
      the plan is parked as needing a redesign, with that failure recorded.
- **Method**, (ii): recall errors in a Program with a concept hierarchy: the share of errors that land in the cued
  concept's own subtree, against the same Program's errors with tree labels permuted.
- **Baselines and controls**: the known groups above, which the classifier must label correctly - otherwise (ii) is
  not run; the permuted-label null.
- **Refuted if**: (ii) the subtree share minus its null's has an interval wholly at or below zero; passed if wholly
  above. (i) is a harness check: a wrong label on any control stops the plan, and is reported.
- **Statistics**: bootstrap over seeds, 95% intervals, one-sided, alpha 0.05.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20, the default; the controls are deterministic.
- **Switch**: `hierarchy = flat` stores concepts with no tree.
- **Cost**: the breadth-first search stores every element of the ball: about a million at r = 30 and, by the
  verifier's count, 9 531 621 at r = 37, the largest under a cap of ten million.
- **Waits on**: a Program that grows concepts (G4's plan, unwritten); the worm has no concept hierarchy known, so (ii)
  is for later rungs and the plan says so.
- **Leads**: [Grigorchuk 1980](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01078416), found (Crossref's title reads "Bernside's
  problem", a typo for Burnside's), not read; [Bartholdi, Grigorchuk and Nekrashevych
  2003](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-0348-8014-5_2), found, not read; [Nekrashevych 2005](https://doi.org/10.1090/surv/117),
  found, not read.

## F6. Critical brains as optimal expanders

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): a brain at criticality wires like an optimal expander.
- **The mathematics, stated correctly.** A k-regular graph is Ramanujan when every eigenvalue of its adjacency matrix
  other than ±k has absolute value at most 2√(k - 1), the asymptotically best bound (Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak
  1988; Hoory, Linial and Wigderson 2006, both found, not read: the definition and the bound's optimality are
  stated as remembered, and checked in both at preparation). For a (q + 1)-regular graph this is the same as the
  Ihara zeta function's non-trivial *poles* lying on the circle |u| = q^(-1/2) - poles, not zeros, as the hunch says
  (Terras, found, not read; checked there at preparation). Neither definition applies as it stands to an irregular
  graph such as a connectome; a generalisation through the spectral radius of the universal cover is expected in
  Hoory, Linial and Wigderson (from memory, unchecked), and the plan does not depend on it.
- **Question**, the hunch's testable form here: is the worm's connectome a better expander than its degrees make it?
  Random graphs with given degrees are themselves expected to be near-optimal expanders (for random regular graphs,
  Friedman's theorem, a lead from #225's verifier, not looked up), so a connectome at the null's level is roughly what
  "optimal" would predict; a refutation below says the connectome is no better than its degrees, and does not touch
  "optimal".
- **Method**: a data test, with no Program. The hermaphrodite's chemical and electrical connectome (Cook et al. 2019,
  found, not read; its licence recorded in data/README.md before use), neurons only, as an undirected, unweighted graph on its
  largest connected component. The statistic, fixed here: the spectral gap of the normalised Laplacian, λ2 - larger means
  faster mixing. Null: 1000 degree-preserving rewirings by double edge swaps (10 swaps per edge), connected ones only.
- **Baselines and controls**: the rewired null; a positive control - a ring lattice with the connectome's number of nodes and mean
  degree must come out below its own null's 5th percentile. A negative control: 20 graphs drawn from the null itself (fresh rewiring chains on
  seeds 1001 to 1020), each against its own 1000 rewirings, of which at most 4 may come out above their null's
  95th percentile (the chance of 5 or more is 0.0026 when each has chance 0.05; binomial tail, computed in Python 3.13
  as the sum over k from 5 to 20 of C(20, k) 0.05^k 0.95^(20 - k)).
- **Refuted if**: the connectome's λ2 lies at or below the null's median - no better an expander than its degrees
  make it; passed if above the null's 95th percentile; between, inconclusive. A connectome below the null's 5th
  percentile is a worse expander than its degrees make it, and is reported as such. One test, alpha 0.05, one-sided.
- **Statistics**: the empirical one-sided p-value against the 1000 rewirings.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: rewiring seeds 1 to 1000; the connectome is one.
- **Switch**: none - no Program is changed. A Program wired from the connectome would compare against a rewired twin.
- **Cost**: an eigenvalue problem on about 300 nodes (the worm has 302 neurons, White et al. 1986), about 22 000 times
  with the controls (1 + 1000 for the connectome, 1 + 1000 for the lattice, 20 × 1001 for the negative control) -
  minutes at most on the cloud machine.
- **Waits on**: the connectome downloaded and its licence recorded. The hunch's renormalisation-flow clause is not
  stated precisely enough to test and is left until it is.
- **Leads**: [Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak 1988](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02126799), found, not read; [Terras
  2011](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511760426), found (Crossref dates it 2010, its online date), not read; [Hoory,

## F7. Concepts consolidate only when local and global agree

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): a concept should consolidate only when every context it appeared in agrees, where a flat
  network averages the disagreement away.
- **The mathematics, stated correctly.** The Hasse principle: a property holds over the rationals when it holds over
  the reals and every Q_p. By the Hasse-Minkowski theorem it holds for whether a quadratic form represents zero; it
  can fail for cubic forms (Selmer's example 3x³ + 4y³ + 5z³ = 0) - so local agreement is not always enough for a global
  answer. Both are textbook results, not looked up tonight. The analogy runs the other way from the hunch's rule: the
  rule asks local agreement as a *condition*, and the mathematics' interest is that local agreement can hold without
  a global solution.
- **Question**: does consolidating a concept only when its contexts agree give better predictions afterwards than
  averaging over them, below capacity?
- **Method**: a mechanism test. A concept is an association, cue to outcome, met in C contexts (C = 4). In
  *consistent* concepts every context gives the same outcome; in *inconsistent* ones the contexts disagree, with a
  context-specific binary outcome in each. Inconsistent concepts have outcome 1 in two contexts and 0 in the other two. Each
  of 1000 concepts per seed, half of each kind, is met n = 3 times in each context, and each observed outcome is
  flipped with probability ε (ε = 0, 0.1, 0.3). Rules: *averaging*, one association by the mean over all contexts;
  *context-bound*, one association per context, never pooled; *agreement*, pooled into one association only if the
  four contexts' majority outcomes are all the same, otherwise kept context-bound - a rule with no tolerance to fit. *Assertion*: at ε = 0 the agreement rule
  never pools an inconsistent concept, and averaging cannot represent one, so the comparison with averaging on
  inconsistent concepts is nearly settled by construction; the comparison that is open is with the context-bound rule,
  which pooling can beat only by using more observations on consistent concepts. Outcome measure: accuracy predicting
  a concept's true (unflipped) outcome in a known context.
- **Baselines and controls**: averaging; context-bound; an oracle told which concepts are consistent, as a ceiling.
  *The expected effect is small.* Pooling can beat the context-bound rule only where a context's majority is wrong:
  at n = 5 and ε = 0.1 a majority of five is wrong with probability 0.0086 (the binomial tail Σ_{k≥3} C(5, k) 0.1^k
  0.9^(5-k), the verifier's figure), so the gain is at most about 0.4 points of accuracy on the mix; at n = 3 and
  ε = 0.3 a rough calculation gives about 1.7 points (Python 3.13: context-bound error from the binomial tail; the
  agreement rule's accuracy approximated by using the pooled twelve-observation majority's unconditional error
  whenever all four majorities agree, and 0.5 on an inconsistent concept wrongly pooled). Hence n = 3 and ε = 0.3 for
  the test. A power calculation on these figures, at preparation, says whether 20 seeds of 1000 concepts decide (b); if
  they cannot, (b) is declared expected to be inconclusive before the run.
- **Refuted if**, at ε = 0.3, over a mix of half consistent and half inconsistent concepts: (a) the agreement rule's
  accuracy minus averaging's has an interval wholly at or below zero; (b) its accuracy minus the context-bound rule's
  has an interval wholly at or below zero. Passed if both intervals lie wholly above zero. How accuracy falls with ε
  is reported: strict agreement refuses consistent concepts when noise is high, which is the trade-off the hunch
  omits.
- **Statistics**: bootstrap over seeds, 97.5% one-sided intervals, corrected for two refuting tests (alpha 0.025
  each).
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20, the default.
- **Switch**: `consolidate = average` or `consolidate = never`.
- **Cost**: C estimates per concept until consolidation; a store's size times C.
- **Waits on**: nothing for the mechanism test. For the worm: context-dependent associative learning would need a
  source and a Program that learns; none is planned yet.
- **Leads**: none given by the hunch.

## F8. Three legs: fixed primitives, critical dynamics, learned geometry

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): a Program with fixed reflexes, drive dynamics poised at a bifurcation and learned
  geometric representations behaves more richly than one with any leg removed.
- **Question**: does removing each leg in turn make behaviour poorer, by a measure that noise cannot win?
- **Method**: on the worm's benchmark, a Program with three switchable legs. *Richness* is ill-defined; the trap is that
  entropy is largest for noise. The measure fixed here is the *excess entropy* - the mutual information between past and
  future - of the discretised behaviour sequence (forward, reversal, turn, pause, at the benchmark's time step), which is
  zero for independent noise and for a constant (Bialek, Nemenman and Tishby 2001, found, not read; the estimator is fixed
  from it at preparation, with block lengths 1 to 8). The second measure is the benchmark's distance to the worm.
- **Baselines and controls**: the full Program; each leg switched off; a noise Program with the full Program's
  behaviour frequencies; the worm's own excess entropy from the reference data. Every estimate is reported minus the mean estimate of 100
  copies of the same sequence shuffled in time, whose true excess entropy is zero, so that the estimator's bias drops
  out; with this correction, the noise Program's 95% interval must contain zero - which fails by chance about once in
  twenty; a failure is re-run once on seeds 21 to 40, and a second failure stops the plan.
- **Refuted if**, for each leg: the full Program's excess entropy minus that with the leg off has an interval wholly
  at or below zero; passed if wholly above. Three refuting tests, alpha 0.05/3 each. Richer is not closer to the worm:
  the benchmark distance of each variant is reported beside it, and if the full Program's excess entropy exceeds the
  worm's, the richness is not the animal's.
- **Statistics**: bootstrap over seeds, one-sided intervals at 1 - 0.05/3 for the three refuting tests.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per variant, the default.
- **Switch**: `reflexes`, `critical_drive` and `geometry`, each pinning its leg at baseline.
- **Cost**: the Program's own; each leg's share reported.
- **Waits on**: a Program with all three legs; the frozen benchmark. "Poised at a bifurcation" is checked as in C14's
  plan ([C13-C24.md § C14](C13-C24.md#c14-poised-near-criticality-monitored)).
- **Leads**: [Bialek, Nemenman and Tishby 2001](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976601753195969), found, not read.

## F9. Analogies from mathematics, kept for their testable parts

- **Hypothesis** (*a hunch*): of the analogies, three parts are testable - a cognitive temperature tied to exploration,
  sleep and waking as a phase transition with its signatures, and an order in which functions return after anaesthesia.
  The others (flow as superconductivity, attention as an energy gap, memory as a causal loop) are left as analogies,
  with nothing to test.
- **Question**: in a Program with a temperature and a sleep drive, does exploration rise with temperature, and does
  the switch between sleep and waking show hysteresis?
- **Method**: (i) the temperature is the softmax temperature of the Program's choice; exploration is the share of
  choices not of the highest value. *Assertion*: in a softmax over fixed values, that share rises with temperature by
  construction, so the test is on behaviour: area covered per hour on the arena, against temperature. (ii) The sleep
  drive is ramped up and then down at three rates, each ramp taking T, 2T and 4T of simulated time, with T fixed at
  preparation as ten times the Program's slowest sleep-state time constant; the state (quiescent or moving) is recorded against the drive. Hysteresis is
  the signed area between the curves of the share quiescent, the down ramp's minus the up ramp's, integrated over the
  drive - positive when the Program stays asleep further down the ramp than it fell asleep on the way up, the
  direction a bistable switch predicts. A monostable state with a first-order lag also shows positive
  area on any ramp of finite speed, and its area shrinks in proportion to the rate; a bistable switch's does not
  shrink toward zero. So bistability is read from the ratio of the slowest ramp's area to the fastest's, A(4T)/A(T):
  about 0.25 for a lag, near 1 for a bistable switch. A phase transition's other signatures - a
  bimodal state distribution and slower recovery from a nudge near the switch - are reported.
- **Baselines and controls**: a lagged monostable Program, matched to the Program in its sleep-state time constant,
  whose ratio A(4T)/A(T) must have an interval wholly below 0.5; a Program whose sleep state is a memoryless function
  of the drive, whose area must have an interval containing zero - which fails by chance about once in twenty; a failure is re-run once on seeds 21
  to 40, and a second failure stops the plan.
- **Refuted if**: (a) the slope of area covered against temperature has an interval wholly at or below zero; passed if
  wholly above; (b) the area at the slowest ramp, A(4T), has an interval wholly at or below zero, or the ratio A(4T)/A(T) has an
  interval wholly at or below 0.5 - lag, not bistability; passed if A(4T)'s interval lies wholly above zero and the
  ratio's wholly above 0.5. Two refuting
  tests, alpha 0.025 each. The direction of (a) is the hunch's; a walk that turns more often can cover less ground,
  so a refutation of (a) may mean that randomness of choice is not exploration of space, and the report says which
  of the two the data show. Whether the worm's lethargus shows hysteresis needs a source on how it starts and ends; none
  is known, and the plan says so.
- **Statistics**: (a) the per-seed slope by least squares over five temperatures fixed at preparation; (b) area by the
  trapezoid rule, and the ratio per seed; both by bootstrap over seeds, one-sided.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20, the default.
- **Switch**: `temperature = fixed` and `sleep = off`.
- **Cost**: none beyond the Program's.
- **Waits on**: a Program with a sleep drive (E14's plan) and a temperature; the arena. The order of return after
  anaesthesia waits on a Grid with an anaesthetic and a worm source on recovery; neither exists.
- **Leads**: none given by the hunch.
