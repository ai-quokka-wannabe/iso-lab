# Plans for C13-C24: Circuits, Rhythms and Dynamics, Second Half

Experiment plans for hunches C13 to C17 and C19 to C24 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). They follow the conventions set out at the head of
[C1-C12.md](C1-C12.md) - every module with a switch that pins it at baseline, every timescale matched to one measured
in the worm and cited, the decision between *refuted*, *passed* and *inconclusive* by the rule at the head of
[B.md](B.md), the life as the unit of analysis, costs counted in simulated lives until the arena is proven - and do
not repeat them. Where a plan departs from them, it says so. C18's plan is not here yet: its first version would have
refuted the hunch by construction, and it waits on a pilot of its model (STATE.md, *Blocked*).

The leads were looked up in Crossref by DOI on 2026-10-14. Found means the work exists as named, with the DOI given;
none was read in full.

## C13. Vigour from the average reward rate; one global slowing factor

- **Question**: does a tonic signal that tracks the recent average reward rate, and scales how fast the creature acts,
  bring the creature's dependence of speed and latency on food closer to the worm's than the same Program without it -
  and does the worm's own dependence run the way the hunch says, faster with more reward? And, the hunch's second half:
  does one global factor move the worm's separate timings together?
- **Method**: a Program whose action latencies and speed are scaled by one tonic variable, an exponentially weighted
  average of recent food intake, with its time constant matched to a measured one when the plan is prepared. The
  manipulated variable is the food density, fixed per life, at three levels; intake is recorded but not used as the
  independent variable, since the creature's own speed sets it. The worm's dependence of speed and touch latency on
  food is read from the literature at preparation, not from the reference recordings, which do not vary the food; the
  worm is known to slow on food, the basal slowing response of
  [Sawin, Ranganathan and Horvitz 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0896-6273(00)81199-X), the opposite of the hunch's
  direction, and the plan lets the data decide. For the second half of the hunch, one global factor scales every time
  constant of the Program's action generator - the touch-response latency, the reversal's duration timer and the
  rhythm generator's period - on a frozen copy, and their changes are recorded together.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program with the tonic variable pinned at its mean. The harness control: a
  Program whose speed is set directly by the food density, by construction, must show the ordering; if it does not, the
  food is not reaching the Program and the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) with the tonic signal, the Program's dependence of speed and latency on food density is no closer
  to the worm's than with it pinned - the paired difference in distance to the worm's curves, over lives, has an
  interval wholly at or above zero; (2) the worm's own dependence runs against the hunch - with more food, speed lower or
  latency longer, the interval from the sources wholly on that side of zero - so that a fit with the opposite sign would
  not rescue a hunch about vigour rising with reward; (3) the worm's speed is shown not to depend on food - its change
  across the densities, with its interval, lies wholly within 10% of its mean, a margin fixed here; (4) the worm's own timings do not
  move together - across the conditions the sources report that change them (temperature, age, feeding), the relative
  changes of touch latency, reversal duration and undulation period are shown to differ by more than a factor of two,
  a margin fixed here, by the interval of their ratio. That the Program's one factor moves its own timings together is
  true by construction and is checked as a harness property, not counted. Four
  refuting tests, alpha 0.05/4 each.
- **Statistics**: per-life curves of speed and latency against food density; distances to the worm's curves and their
  paired differences, intervals by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per food density, the default.
- **Cost**: one scalar state and a multiply per action - negligible.
- **Waits on**: food and a sense of intake (STATE.md, *Needs From Other Repositories*); touch (B1's audit of the
  interface); the worm's measured dependence of speed and latency on food, and of its timings on other conditions, from
  the literature.
- **Leads**: Niv, Daw, Joel and Dayan, "Tonic dopamine: opportunity costs and the control of response vigor",
  *Psychopharmacology* ([doi:10.1007/s00213-006-0502-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-006-0502-4)) - found; Crossref
  lists it online in 2006 and in print in 2007, as the hunch dates it. Kail, "Developmental change in speed of
  processing during childhood and adolescence", *Psychological Bulletin*, 1991
  ([doi:10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490)) - found; it concerns children's
  speed of processing, so it is a lead for the idea of one global factor, not for a worm. Sawin, Ranganathan and
  Horvitz 2000 (above), found in Crossref and already cited in the lab's hypotheses, not read here.

## C14. Poised near criticality, monitored

- **Question**: does the creature's behaviour come closest to the worm's when its brain runs near the boundary between
  ordered and chaotic dynamics, and can the distance from that boundary be measured while it lives?
- **Method**: a recurrent Program of `tanh` units with random weights scaled by one gain g, which moves it from ordered
  through critical to chaotic dynamics. At each of nine gains spaced across that range, lives are run and four measures
  of criticality recorded from the brain's own activity: the distribution of avalanche sizes (its exponent, after Beggs
  and Plenz), the autocorrelation time, the dynamic range of the response to graded touch, and the largest Lyapunov
  exponent from two copies started a small distance apart. The critical gain is where that exponent crosses zero.
  Behavioural realism at each gain is the number of the benchmark's features that fall within its per-feature bands.
- **Baselines and controls**: each measure is checked first on systems of known regime - a branching process with
  branching ratio below, at and above one for the avalanche exponent, and a random `tanh` network at gains well below
  and well above its transition for the Lyapunov exponent and the autocorrelation time, which should peak near the
  transition (the transition to chaos in such networks is
  [Sompolinsky, Crisanti and Sommers 1988](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.61.259), found in Crossref, not read);
  the dynamic range is checked on the branching model, whose range is largest at the critical ratio
  ([Kinouchi and Copelli 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys289), found in Crossref, not read). If a measure does not
  order its controls correctly, the run is void.
  The best gain by plain search is chosen on one half of the lives and its score measured on the other half, so that
  choosing the best of nine noisy means does not inflate it.
- **Refuted if**: realism at the critical gain is scored at the grid gain nearest to it, re-chosen in each bootstrap
  resample. (1) The gain at which realism peaks is shown not to be near the critical gain - the bootstrap interval
  of the difference between the two, with both re-estimated in each resample so that the critical gain's own
  uncertainty is carried, lies wholly more than one grid step from zero; (2) realism at the critical gain is shown lower
  than at the best gain, on the held-out half, by more than one benchmark feature. Two refuting tests, alpha 0.025 each.
- **Statistics**: the peak's position by a quadratic fitted to per-gain mean realism, taken at the nearest end of the
  grid when the fitted maximum falls outside it; intervals by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per gain, nine gains, 180 lives, halved for choosing and scoring. Before the run,
  the power of clause (2) to find a difference of one benchmark feature with 10 lives per half is computed by
  simulation from a pilot's variance, and the lives raised until it reaches 80%; more also if the difference's interval
  is wider than two grid steps.
- **Cost**: the Lyapunov monitor costs a second copy of the brain; the others are cheap counters.
- **Waits on**: the benchmark and its per-feature bands; the arena. Sharpens H7.
- **Leads**: Langton, "Computation at the edge of chaos: Phase transitions and emergent computation", *Physica D*,
  1990 ([doi:10.1016/0167-2789(90)90064-V](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(90)90064-V)) - found. Beggs and Plenz,
  "Neuronal Avalanches in Neocortical Circuits", *Journal of Neuroscience*, 2003
  ([doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-35-11167.2003](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-35-11167.2003)) - found. Turrigiano
  and Nelson, "Homeostatic plasticity in the developing nervous system", *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2004
  ([doi:10.1038/nrn1327](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1327)) - found; it is the lead for the slow gain control the hunch
  adds at larger scale, which this plan does not test.

## C15. A logistic-map arousal engine

- **Question**: does one logistic map, its parameter driven by internal state, produce the worm's dwell-time
  distributions and transition statistics between behavioural states better than a plain mapping from state to a
  random temperature?
- **Method**: arousal is the iterate of the logistic map x <- r x (1 - x), one step per decision, with r driven by a
  slow internal state; the behavioural state is read from x by thresholds. A deterministic map cannot be expected to match a
  recording's sequence step by step, since its phase is arbitrary, so it is scored on what the hunch names: the
  dwell-time distributions of forward, reverse and pause, and the matrix of transitions between them. r is driven by a slow
  internal variable s that follows an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with mean fixed at 0, through r = 2.8 + 1.2 / (1 + exp(-s)), which keeps r
  between 2.8 and 4 where the map stays within [0, 1]; the process's time constant and spread and the thresholds are
  fitted on half the reference recordings by minimising distance (1) below, the dwell times, and distance (2), the
  transitions, is left as a held-out check; the model is then run for as long as all the held-out
  recordings together, from initial conditions drawn by a seed fixed at preparation, and its dwell times and
  transitions compared with the held-out half.
- **Baselines and controls**: the plain alternative the hunch names - the same internal state mapped to the temperature
  of a random choice among the same states, with the same number of fitted parameters: its temperature is exp(s) for
  the same kind of slow variable, fitted the same way. The parameters, four each, listed so that the count can be
  checked: the engine fits the slow variable's time constant and spread and two thresholds on x, read in a fixed order
  (below the first, reverse; between, pause; above the second, forward); the baseline fits the same time constant and
  spread and two preferences, of reverse and of pause relative to forward, which the temperature divides. The harness control, with thresholds fixed for it at x = 0.5 and
  0.75 and the first 100 iterates discarded as transient: at r = 2.9 (a fixed point near 0.655) the engine must give
  one constant state, and at r = 3.2 (a cycle between about 0.513 and 0.799) it must alternate between two; and two runs
  from the same initial condition must agree exactly. If any fails, the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: the engine is no closer to the held-out recordings than the random-temperature baseline, whose
  temperature is driven by the same kind of slow variable, on either of two distances, kept apart since they are on
  different scales: (1) the sum, over the three states, of the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between simulated and
  held-out dwell times; (2) the total absolute difference between the transition matrices. For each, the interval of the
  engine's distance minus the baseline's lies wholly at or above zero. Two refuting tests, alpha 0.025 each; the
  simulated runs also discard their first 100 iterates.
- **Statistics**: those distances for each model, its interval by bootstrap over held-out recordings and over the
  models' initial-condition seeds.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: the engine is deterministic given its initial condition, so the repetitions are over the
  reference recordings, all of the drawn reference set, split in half by a seed fixed at preparation, and over 20
  initial conditions per model.
- **Cost**: a multiply and a subtraction per decision.
- **Waits on**: the feature tool's locomotion states (STATE.md item 2) and the reference statistics.
- **Leads**: Feigenbaum, "Quantitative universality for a class of nonlinear transformations", *Journal of Statistical
  Physics*, 1978 ([doi:10.1007/BF01020332](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01020332)) - found. Strogatz, *Nonlinear
  Dynamics and Chaos* ([doi:10.1201/9780429492563](https://doi.org/10.1201/9780429492563)) - found as the 2018 CRC
  edition; the hunch names 2015, the second edition's earlier printing, and the plan matches it as the likely work.

## C16. Golden-ratio spacing of rhythms

- **Question**: do rhythm generators whose frequencies are spaced by powers of the golden ratio fall into n:m locking
  with one another less often than generators spaced otherwise, at the same total spread of frequencies?
- **Method**: locking between rhythms of different frequency is n:m locking - n cycles of one to m of the other - which
  1:1 phase coupling cannot show, so the oscillators are coupled with n:m terms, sin(m theta_j - n theta_i) for every
  pair of whole numbers with max(n, m) at most five - 25 terms. The coupling's form is fixed here: for each pair of
  oscillators, i the slower and j the faster, every term adds a sin(m theta_j - n theta_i) to d theta_i/dt and
  subtracts it from d theta_j/dt, which makes each m theta_j - n theta_i relax towards a lock, with a = K / (25 (N - 1)),
  so that K is the total coupling an oscillator receives, shared over the 25 terms and the N - 1 others. Integrated by
  fourth-order Runge-Kutta with a step of 0.05 in units of the slowest period divided by 2 pi. N oscillators (N = 3, 5 and 8) have their frequencies
  spaced by powers of the golden ratio, so that the lowest and highest span a ratio of phi to the power N - 1. The
  comparison spacings hold that span fixed, so that spacing is not confounded with spread: frequencies drawn
  log-uniformly within the span and sorted, and frequencies spaced linearly across it. Every pair counts, neighbours or
  not, since the hunch is that independent rhythms stay independent as their number grows. That is a real risk for
  it: powers of phi lie close to whole numbers (phi to the 3rd is about 4.24, to the 4th about 6.85, near the Lucas
  numbers 4 and 7), so pairs that are not neighbours may lock readily, and the plan expects that to show at N = 5 and 8
  if it is so. Recorded: the share of oscillator pairs in n:m lock, across ten coupling strengths K spaced
  logarithmically from 0.01 to 1 (in units of the slowest natural frequency), fixed here. A pair is in n:m lock when, for
  some n:m in that set, m theta_j - n theta_i stays within pi/2 of its own mean over the last half of a run of 2000
  periods of the slowest oscillator.
- **Baselines and controls**: the log-uniform and linear spacings are the baselines. The harness controls use one
  pair at 2:1 detuned by 1%: uncoupled (K = 0), its phase combination drifts by about 20 cycles over the run and it must
  not lock; at K = 1 it must lock 2:1, which only working coupling can make it do. Piloted with the coupling as fixed above
  (journal of 2026-10-14, *C16's Coupling, Piloted*): the pair does not lock at K = 0 or 0.01 and locks 2:1 at K = 1,
  so the grid runs from no lock to lock; with each term at full strength K instead, it is pushed off 2:1 at K = 1. And if no spacing locks anywhere on
  the grid, the coupling is broken, not the hunch confirmed. If any of these fails, the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: the golden-ratio spacing locks no less than the log-uniform spacings - the interval of the difference
  in locked share, averaged over the ten coupling strengths, lies wholly at or above zero, at every N.
  One refuting test, alpha 0.05; the linear spacing is reported beside it.
- **Statistics**: locked share per run, compared with the log-uniform spacings' distribution by bootstrap over draws.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 draws of log-uniform frequencies and of initial phases per N and K.
- **Cost**: a few oscillators; seconds of compute. A notebook is enough for the first pass; a result that matters moves
  into tested Rust.
- **Waits on**: nothing for the dynamics; the worm's own rhythms' frequencies and coupling for the second step, which
  asks whether golden spacing matters at the couplings the worm has.
- **Leads**: Pletzer, Kerschbaum and Klimesch, "When frequencies never synchronize: The golden mean and the resting
  EEG", *Brain Research*, 2010
  ([doi:10.1016/j.brainres.2010.03.074](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2010.03.074)) - found. The maintainer's
  earlier small tests are unrecorded and are not evidence.

## C17. Memory timescales spaced by Feigenbaum's constant

- **Question**: does each pair of neighbouring timescales in the worm's memory layers stand in a ratio near
  Feigenbaum's delta, 4.669...?
- **Method**: no brain is built. The time constants of the worm's memories - from seconds-long sensory integration
  through minutes-long habituation to hours-long associative memory - are read from primary sources, each with its
  uncertainty, ordered, and each neighbour ratio computed. Which memories count as layers is fixed from the sources' own
  definitions before any ratio is computed. Each ratio is tested, not their geometric mean, which the end points and the
  number of layers alone would set.
- **Baselines and controls**: the chance of the outcome without the hunch: with the same two end points and the same
  number of layers, the interior timescales placed log-uniformly between them, the probability p0 that every neighbour
  ratio falls within the band below, by Monte Carlo.
- **Refuted if**: any neighbour ratio lies outside the band from 4.669 / 1.5 to 4.669 x 1.5, a margin fixed here, with
  its interval wholly outside; with k ratios, each interval is taken at 1 - 0.05/k (Bonferroni), from the sources'
  uncertainties. It passes only if every ratio's interval, at the same coverage, lies within
  the band and p0 is below 0.05, so that the pass is not what chance spacing gives anyway; anything else is
  inconclusive, as is fewer than four timescales read with their uncertainties.
- **Statistics**: each ratio's interval by Monte Carlo over the stated uncertainties; p0 by Monte Carlo.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 100 000 Monte Carlo draws each, seeds fixed at preparation.
- **Cost**: a calculation.
- **Waits on**: the pace note's time constants ([docs/research/pace.md](../../research/pace.md)), several still gaps.
- **Leads**: none named by the hunch; Feigenbaum 1978 (C15's lead) defines the constant.

## C19. Windowed tallies, not lifetime counts

- **Question**: does a Program that keeps the statistics it acts on as tallies over a recent window - every slot halved
  when the total reaches the window - follow a reversal of a contingency on the worm's time course, where a Program
  keeping lifetime counts does not?
- **Method**: a Program that learns an association (a cue predicting food, or its absence) by counting outcomes, in two
  versions: windowed tallies with the halving rule, and lifetime counts. Each life learns the association, then the
  contingency is reversed at a fixed time, and the time course of the switch in behaviour is recorded. Lives run with
  two lengths of the first phase, short and long, as far apart as the literature's reversal experiments allow. The
  window is set, before the run, from the worm's time to switch after the short first phase, read from the literature
  at preparation, so that matching it there is fitted, not evidence; the test is the long first phase, where lifetime
  counts must switch later the longer they were trained, and a window need not.
- **Baselines and controls**: the lifetime-count version is the baseline; a version whose counts are reset by hand at
  the reversal is the harness control, which must switch within the trials its rule needs from empty counts, computed
  at preparation from the rule itself - if it does not, the cue or the outcome is not
  reaching the Program and the run is void.
- **Refuted if**, after the long first phase only: (1) the windowed version's time to switch differs from the worm's by
  more than a factor of two, a margin fixed here (its interval wholly outside); (2) the windowed version is no closer to
  the worm's time to switch than the lifetime-count version (the paired difference in distance to the worm's time, over
  lives, has an interval wholly at or above zero). If the literature gives the worm's time to switch after one length
  of first phase only, the test cannot run and the plan says so in its write-up rather than testing on the fitted
  condition. That windowed tallies switch faster than lifetime counts after a long first phase follows from their
  construction and is checked as a harness property, not counted. Two refuting tests, alpha 0.025 each.
- **Statistics**: time to switch per life, from the first time the response crosses half-way between its two steady
  levels; intervals by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per version, the default.
- **Cost**: a halving per window; cheaper than lifetime counters, which grow without bound.
- **Waits on**: food and a sense of intake (STATE.md, *Needs From Other Repositories*); the worm's reversal-learning
  time course from the literature, after two lengths of training.
- **Leads**: none named by the hunch.

## C20. Bounded loops for rhythmic behaviour

- **Question**: does a global duty-cycle bound hold for a Program whose behavioural programmes are trees of named
  sub-programmes, each loop on a finite budget and pre-emptible by new input, even when loops are rebuilt from bounded
  parts?
- **Method**: an engineering test, not a test of the worm, and it departs from the C conventions on that account: it has
  no worm-likeness to measure, so its baseline is a guarantee rather than a simpler Program. The per-loop budgets and
  the pre-emption are built in, so they are checked as properties of the build, not counted as evidence; the one real
  test is the composed bound, which no single part guarantees. The bound - no rhythmic behaviour holding the body for
  more than a fixed share of any window - takes its share and window from the worm: the longest continuous bout of one
  rhythmic behaviour in the reference recordings and the window it falls in (the feature tool's states, STATE.md item
  2), fixed at preparation. The pre-emption limit is a reaction latency, so it obeys *The animal's pace*: it must lie
  within the interval of the worm's measured latency from the pace note, neither faster nor slower. The Program is driven by adversarial input: inputs
  chosen by a search written down before the run to keep loops running as long as possible and to arrive at the worst
  moments for pre-emption. The instrument is the harness, not the Program: what the body did is read from the world's
  logs, which a Program never sees, so a Program whose own logs lie is still caught.
- **Baselines and controls**: a planted unbounded loop, which the harness must catch - if it is not caught, the
  instrument is broken and the run is void. A planted loop that ignores pre-emption must be caught the same way.
- **Refuted if**: the composed duty-cycle bound is exceeded over any window, once; it is a guarantee, so one
  counter-example refutes. **Passed** when the search reaches the coverage fixed at preparation - every loop entered,
  every nesting of loops and every re-entry of a loop the tree allows reached, and every point at which a pre-emption
  can arrive hit at least once - with no bound broken; **inconclusive** when
  coverage falls short.
- **Statistics**: none beyond the counter-example and the coverage, which is reported (inputs tried, loops entered,
  pre-emption points hit).
- **Seeds and repetitions**: the adversarial search's seed fixed at preparation; 20 seeds of the Program's own noise.
- **Cost**: a counter per loop and a log; small. The flat Program without the tree is run beside it and its cost
  reported, so the guarantee's price is known.
- **Waits on**: a Program with behavioural programmes; the feature tool's states for the bound; the pace note for the
  pre-emption latency.
- **Leads**: none named by the hunch.

## C21. A noisy, log-compressed sense of elapsed time

- **Question**: does a Program that reads elapsed time through a pacemaker whose ticks lengthen geometrically, with
  Weber-like noise, match the worm's interval timing better than a noisy counter whose error does not grow in
  proportion to the interval?
- **Method**: whether the worm times intervals at all, and by what paradigm, is not known to the lab; it is searched for
  at preparation. If no study reports the spread of the worm's timed responses at two intervals or more, the
  experiment cannot run and the write-up says so; both clauses below need those data. If one does, the Program's task
  is built to match its paradigm - the same cue, the same intervals, the same response - and the worm's timing is read
  from it as the mean and standard deviation of the response time at each interval. Two Programs: the pacemaker, and
  the baseline. The pacemaker's Weber fraction is fitted on the shorter half of the worm's intervals and the test is on
  the longer half, so that what is tested was not fitted.
- **Baselines and controls**: the baseline is a counter with noise of fixed standard deviation, its size fitted on the
  same shorter half - it can lose, unlike an exact counter, whose zero spread makes any noisy Program look closer.
  Each component of the pacemaker has a switch: geometric ticks pinned to linear with the same noise, and noise pinned
  to none with the same geometric ticks; each is reported. Harness controls: the exact counter must show no spread, or
  the harness adds noise of its own; and the pacemaker's coefficient of variation must be constant across intervals in
  simulation, or it is not built as stated. If either fails, the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the worm's timing is shown not to be scalar: the slope of log standard deviation against log
  interval is 1 for a scalar timer, 0.5 for Poisson counting and 0 for fixed noise, and the hunch is refuted if the
  slope's interval lies wholly below 0.75, halfway between scalar and Poisson; (2) on the held-out longer intervals, the
  pacemaker is no closer to the worm than the baseline - the difference in summed absolute error of the standard
  deviations has an interval wholly at or above zero. Two refuting tests, alpha 0.025 each.
- **Statistics**: the slope by least squares, its interval by bootstrap over the worm's animals where the source gives
  them; where a source gives only pooled trials, a bootstrap over trials treats them as independent, which they are
  not, so the interval is too narrow and the write-up says so; the Programs' standard deviations over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per Program, 50 trials per interval in each.
- **Cost**: one state variable and a random draw per tick.
- **Waits on**: a study of the worm's interval timing; a task with a timed response on the Grid.
- **Leads**: Taatgen, van Rijn and Anderson, "An integrated theory of prospective time interval estimation: The role
  of cognition, attention, and learning", *Psychological Review*, 2007
  ([doi:10.1037/0033-295X.114.3.577](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.114.3.577)) - found.

## C22. Inhibition of return and foraging in memory

- **Question**: does a Program whose selected option briefly inhibits itself, and whose search exploits a patch and
  leaves it as it depletes, reproduce the worm's local search and dispersal after food is removed?
- **Method**: on the Grid, food is removed at a fixed time and the creature's turning rate is recorded over the
  following minutes. The Program has a self-inhibition after selection and a leaving rule driven by the falling rate
  of finds; each is switched off in turn. The leaving rule is the marginal value theorem's, leave when the rate of finds
  falls to the average rate over the life (Charnov 1976). Removing food drops the true rate to zero at once, so a search
  shows only through the Program's running estimate of the rate, and that estimate's time constant sets the half-time
  compared below: it is a fitted parameter, and the plan says so. It is fitted to the worm's half-time in one condition
  and tested in another: if the source gives the time course after two durations on food (or two food densities), the
  time constant is fitted on one and the half-time predicted for the other, with the life average taken over the life
  so far; if it gives one condition only, clause (1) cannot run and the write-up says so, rather than testing on the
  fitted condition. The inhibition's duration is fixed at preparation from the Program's own decision rhythm, before
  the reference is read.
  The worm's area-restricted search - frequent turns soon after food is removed, giving way to straighter dispersal -
  is the reference, read from the literature at preparation and summarised by its half-time: the time after removal at
  which the turning rate has fallen halfway from its peak to its dispersal level. The hunch's third part, that massed
  repetition satiates and spaced repetition strengthens, is about learning, not search, and is not tested here; it is
  left for a plan of its own.
- **Baselines and controls**: the Program with both mechanisms pinned off. The harness control: a Program whose turning
  rate is scripted to fall with time since removal must reproduce its scripted half-time within one bin; if the measure
  does not show it, the instrument is broken and the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) in the held-out condition, the full Program's half-time differs from the worm's by more than a
  factor of two - the interval of their ratio lies wholly outside 0.5 to 2, a margin fixed here; (2) switching off either mechanism leaves the
  half-time unchanged - the interval of the difference lies wholly within a tenth of the worm's half-time, an
  equivalence test, one for each mechanism. Three refuting tests, alpha 0.05 / 3 each (Bonferroni).
- **Statistics**: the turning rate in bins after removal, per life, and the half-time from it; intervals by bootstrap
  over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per version.
- **Cost**: a decaying inhibition per option and a running rate; small.
- **Waits on**: food on the floor (STATE.md, *Needs From Other Repositories*).
- **Leads**: Posner and Cohen 1984, the hunch's lead for inhibition of return, is a book chapter and was not found in
  Crossref; it stays an unverified lead. Hills, Jones and Todd, "Optimal foraging in semantic memory", *Psychological

## C23. A stated, measured bound on what is held at once

- **Question**: does a Program with an explicit, small capacity for what it holds in focus - for the worm, a window of
  a few seconds of sensory integration - behave more like the worm than one in which everything active is held?
- **Method**: the capacity is a window in simulated time, set to the worm's sensory-integration time from the pace note
  when it is filled. A span probe checks it: sequences of brief stimuli of growing length, and after each a response
  that depends on an early item; recall falls as the item's lag outgrows the window.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program with no bound. Harness controls, in the window's own unit: with a window
  of one simulation step, recall must fail for every lag beyond one step; and the lag at which the bounded Program's
  recall halves must lie within a factor of two of the window set. If either fails, the probe leaks the answer or the
  bound is not built as stated, and the run is void. A bounded Program's recall falls with lag by construction, and its
  window is set from the worm's, so neither is counted as evidence.
- **Refuted if**: the bounded Program is worse than the unbounded one on the benchmark, which the window was not fitted
  to: by the benchmark's per-life score, the number of features within their per-feature bands, the paired difference
  bounded minus unbounded has an interval wholly below zero. A tie does not refute: the bounded Program is the cheaper,
  and by *Lean brains* the leaner wins a tie. One refuting test, alpha 0.05. **Passed** when that interval lies wholly
  above zero.
- **Statistics**: the per-life score, paired over seeds; recall per lag per life for the controls; intervals by
  bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per version.
- **Cost**: smaller than unbounded, by construction.
- **Waits on**: a sense and an action the probe can use; the worm's integration time (the pace note); the benchmark.
- **Leads**: Cowan, "The magical number 4 in short-term memory: A reconsideration of mental storage capacity",
  *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 2001 ([doi:10.1017/S0140525X01003922](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922)) -
  found; it concerns human short-term memory, so it is a lead for the idea of a stated capacity, not for the worm's.

## C24. Instrument the state of attention

- **Question**: do two cheap readings of a brain's activity - how peaked it is, and how stable the active set is from
  one step to the next - track changes in the environment, with stability dropping when the environment changes?
- **Method**: an instrument, not a module. The active set is the most active tenth of units, rounded up, ties broken by
  unit index. Peakedness is the share of total activity in the active set; stability at a step is the share of the
  active set at the step before that is still in it at this one, so that a change at a step lowers that step's
  stability. Both are recorded through lives in which the environment changes at known times (a touch, a change of
  temperature), and reported as components, never summed. At each change, *before* is the
  mean stability over the 32 steps (1 s at the Grid's 32 Hz) before the step at which the change is sensed, and
  *after* the mean over that step and the next two; the drop is before minus after, so a real drop is positive. The
  window lengths are choices of the instrument, fixed here without a source, as they concern the reading, not the
  worm; the drop is reported beside them over the 1 and 10 steps after, to show how much they matter.
- **Baselines and controls**: a negative control, the readings on a Program of fixed random activity, unrelated to the
  world, which must not change at the environment's changes; and a positive control, a Program scripted to replace its
  whole active set at the step each change is sensed, whose drop must exceed the margin below - one such step in the
  three averaged after moves the mean by about a third of the control's usual
  stability, which clears the margin only if that stability is above 0.15, so the control is built with a stable active
  set between changes. The harness also logs
  each change as the Program's senses received it, and the windows are aligned to that, not to the world's clock. If
  any control fails, the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: stability is shown not to drop by a meaningful amount at changes - the mean drop, before minus after,
  has an interval wholly below 0.05, the smallest effect of interest fixed here: a twentieth of the active set
  replaced, on average over the three steps after a change, beyond its turnover before. One refuting test, alpha 0.05;
  peakedness is reported beside it, not tested, as the hunch makes no prediction of its direction. **Passed** when the drop's interval lies wholly above 0.05.
- **Statistics**: per-life mean drop at the environment's changes; interval by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives; the power at the margin is computed at preparation from the controls' spread.
- **Cost**: a sort of activities per step, or a running threshold; small, and run beside a brain, not inside it.
- **Waits on**: a Program with enough units to read; senses for the changes (touch, temperature).
- **Leads**: none named by the hunch.
