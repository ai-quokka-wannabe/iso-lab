# Plans for C13-C24: Circuits, Rhythms and Dynamics, Second Half

Experiment plans for hunches C13 to C24 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). They follow the conventions set out at the head of
[C1-C12.md](C1-C12.md) - every module with a switch that pins it at baseline, every timescale matched to one measured
in the worm and cited, the decision between *refuted*, *passed* and *inconclusive* by the rule at the head of
[B.md](B.md), the life as the unit of analysis, costs counted in simulated lives until the arena is proven - and do
not repeat them. Where a plan departs from them, it says so.

The leads were looked up in Crossref by DOI on 2026-10-14. Found means the work exists as named, with the DOI given;
none was read in full.

## C13. Vigour from the average reward rate; one global slowing factor

- **Question**: does a single tonic signal that tracks the recent average reward rate set how fast the creature acts -
  latencies falling as the rate rises, as Niv et al.'s model has the optimal latency fall with the inverse square root
  of the rate - and can one global speed factor stand in for several separate timing effects?
- **Method**: a Program whose action latencies are scaled by one tonic variable, an exponentially weighted average of
  recent reward (food taken in), with its time constant matched to a measured one when the plan is prepared. Lives are
  run under three food densities, fixed per life, and the latency from a cue (touch) to the response is recorded. For
  the second half of the hunch, the same factor is varied by hand on a frozen copy, and its effect on several timing
  measures - touch latency, reversal duration, the undulation period - is recorded together.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program with the tonic variable pinned at its mean (no vigour signal). The
  harness control: a Program whose latency is set directly by the food density, by construction, must show the
  ordering; if it does not, the food is not reaching the Program and the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) latency does not fall as the reward rate rises - the interval of the slope of log latency
  against the log of each life's measured intake rate (not the food density, which only sets it) lies wholly at or
  above zero; (2) the slope is shown to differ from -0.5, the model's square-root law, by
  more than 0.25, a margin fixed here (two one-sided tests at alpha 0.05 for the pass); (3) one global factor fails to
  move the timing measures together - the measures' changes under the factor, each relative to its own baseline,
  differ from one another by more than a factor of two, a margin fixed here, by the interval of their ratio. These
  three refuting tests share alpha 0.05 (Bonferroni).
- **Statistics**: slopes by least squares on per-life median latencies, intervals by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per food density, the default.
- **Cost**: one scalar state and a multiply per action - negligible.
- **Waits on**: food and a sense of intake (STATE.md, *Needs From Other Repositories*); touch (B1's audit of the
  interface); the worm's measured latencies and their dependence on feeding state, to be read from the literature,
  and the benchmark's measures for the comparison with the worm.
- **Leads**: Niv, Daw, Joel and Dayan, "Tonic dopamine: opportunity costs and the control of response vigor",
  *Psychopharmacology* ([doi:10.1007/s00213-006-0502-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-006-0502-4)) - found; Crossref
  dates it 2006, the year of its online publication, where the hunch says 2007. Kail, "Developmental change in speed
  of processing during childhood and adolescence", *Psychological Bulletin*, 1991
  ([doi:10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490)) - found; it concerns children's
  speed of processing, so it is a lead for the idea of one global factor, not for a worm.

## C14. Poised near criticality, monitored

- **Question**: does the creature's behaviour come closest to the worm's when its brain runs near the boundary between
  ordered and chaotic dynamics, and can the distance from that boundary be measured while it lives?
- **Method**: a recurrent Program with one gain parameter that moves it from ordered through critical to chaotic
  dynamics. At each of nine gains spaced across that range, lives are run and four measures of criticality recorded
  from the brain's own activity: the distribution of avalanche sizes (its exponent, after Beggs and Plenz), the
  autocorrelation time, the dynamic range of the response to graded touch, and the largest Lyapunov exponent estimated
  from two copies started a small distance apart. Behavioural realism at each gain is the benchmark's overall score.
- **Baselines and controls**: the measures themselves are checked on a network of known regime - a linear network
  with spectral radius below, at and above one - before the Program is measured; if they do not order those three
  correctly, the run is void. The behavioural score is compared with a Program of the same size at the best fixed gain
  found by plain search, so that "near criticality" is not credited for what any tuning would give.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the gain at which the benchmark score peaks is shown not to be near the critical gain - the
  bootstrap interval of the peak's position, over lives, lies wholly more than one grid step from the gain at which
  the largest Lyapunov exponent crosses zero; (2) the score at the critical gain is shown lower than the score at the
  best gain by more than the benchmark's smallest meaningful difference. Two refuting tests, alpha 0.025 each.
- **Statistics**: the peak's position by a quadratic fitted to per-gain mean scores, its interval by bootstrap over
  lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per gain, nine gains, 180 lives; more if the peak's interval is wider than two
  grid steps at preparation.
- **Cost**: the monitors cost a second copy of the brain for the Lyapunov estimate; the others are cheap counters.
- **Waits on**: the benchmark and its score; the arena. Sharpens H7.
- **Leads**: Langton, "Computation at the edge of chaos: Phase transitions and emergent computation", *Physica D*,
  1990 ([doi:10.1016/0167-2789(90)90064-V](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(90)90064-V)) - found. Beggs and Plenz,
  "Neuronal Avalanches in Neocortical Circuits", *Journal of Neuroscience*, 2003
  ([doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-35-11167.2003](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-35-11167.2003)) - found. Turrigiano
  and Nelson, "Homeostatic plasticity in the developing nervous system", *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2004
  ([doi:10.1038/nrn1327](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1327)) - found; it is the lead for the slow gain control the hunch
  adds at larger scale, which this plan does not test.

## C15. A logistic-map arousal engine

- **Question**: does one logistic map, its parameter driven by internal state, produce the worm's dwell-time
  distributions and transition statistics between behavioural states better than a plain mapping from state to a
  random temperature?
- **Method**: arousal is the iterate of the logistic map x <- r x (1 - x), one step per decision, with r driven by a
  slow internal state; the behavioural state is read from x by thresholds fitted on half the reference recordings.
  The dwell-time distributions of forward, reverse and pause, and the matrix of transitions between them, are compared
  with the other half of the recordings.
- **Baselines and controls**: the plain alternative the hunch names - the same internal state mapped to the
  temperature of a random choice among the same states, with the same number of fitted parameters. The harness
  control: with r fixed at 2.9 (a fixed point) the engine must give a constant state; with r = 3.2 (period two) it
  must alternate. If either fails, the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the engine fits the held-out recordings no better than the random-temperature baseline - the
  interval of the paired difference in held-out log likelihood per life lies wholly at or below zero; (2) the
  engine's variability is not deterministic as claimed - two lives from the same state give different sequences
  (checked exactly; one failure refutes).
- **Statistics**: held-out log likelihood of state sequences under each model, paired by recording, interval by
  bootstrap over recordings.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: deterministic by construction, so the repetitions are over the reference recordings, all
  of the drawn reference set, split in half by a fixed seed written down at preparation.
- **Cost**: a multiply and a subtraction per decision.
- **Waits on**: the feature tool's locomotion states (STATE.md item 2) and the reference statistics.
- **Leads**: Feigenbaum, "Quantitative universality for a class of nonlinear transformations", *Journal of Statistical
  Physics*, 1978 ([doi:10.1007/BF01020332](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01020332)) - found. Strogatz, *Nonlinear
  Dynamics and Chaos* ([doi:10.1201/9780429492563](https://doi.org/10.1201/9780429492563)) - found as the 2018 CRC
  edition; the hunch names 2015, the second edition's earlier printing, and the plan matches it as the likely work.

## C16. Golden-ratio spacing of rhythms

- **Question**: do rhythm generators whose frequencies are spaced by powers of the golden ratio lock to one another
  less often than generators spaced otherwise, at a coupling the worm's rhythms plausibly have?
- **Method**: N phase oscillators (Kuramoto-type, N = 3, 5 and 8), coupled all to all at strength K, their natural
  frequencies spaced by a ratio rho: the golden ratio, 2, 3/2, and ratios drawn uniformly from [1.3, 2.0]. For each, the
  share of oscillator pairs that phase-lock (their phase difference bounded over the last half of a long run) is
  recorded across a range of K. This is a pure dynamics experiment, run before any creature.
- **Baselines and controls**: the random ratios are the baseline. The harness control: two oscillators of equal
  frequency must lock at any positive K, and two uncoupled ones (K = 0) never; if either fails, the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: at the coupling range where any spacing shows locking, the golden-ratio spacing locks no less than
  the random spacings - the interval of the difference in locked share lies wholly at or above zero at every N.
- **Statistics**: locked share per run, compared with the random ratios' distribution by bootstrap over draws.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 draws of random ratios and of initial phases per N and K.
- **Cost**: a few oscillators; seconds of compute. A notebook is enough for the first pass; a result that matters moves
  into tested Rust.
- **Waits on**: nothing for the dynamics; the worm's own rhythms' frequencies and coupling for the second step, which
  asks whether golden spacing matters at the couplings the worm has.
- **Leads**: Pletzer, Kerschbaum and Klimesch, "When frequencies never synchronize: The golden mean and the resting
  EEG", *Brain Research*, 2010
  ([doi:10.1016/j.brainres.2010.03.074](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2010.03.074)) - found. The maintainer's earlier small tests are unrecorded and are not evidence.

## C17. Memory timescales spaced by Feigenbaum's constant

- **Question**: do the timescales of the worm's successive memory layers stand in ratios near Feigenbaum's delta,
  4.669...?
- **Method**: no brain is built. The time constants of the worm's memories - from seconds-long sensory integration
  through minutes-long habituation to hours-long associative memory - are read from primary sources, each with its
  uncertainty, ordered, and the ratios of neighbours computed. Which memories count as layers is fixed from the
  sources' own definitions before any ratio is computed.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same calculation on ratios drawn from a log-uniform spread over the same total range,
  to show how often a ratio near 4.67 arises by chance.
- **Refuted if**: the geometric mean of the neighbour ratios differs from 4.669 by more than a factor of 1.5, a margin
  fixed here, with its interval (from the sources' uncertainties, by Monte Carlo) wholly outside that band; it passes
  if the interval lies wholly within it. It is inconclusive if fewer than four timescales can be read with their
  uncertainties.
- **Statistics**: the interval of the geometric mean ratio by Monte Carlo over each timescale's stated uncertainty.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 100 000 Monte Carlo draws, seed fixed at preparation; the control by the same.
- **Cost**: a calculation.
- **Waits on**: the pace note's time constants ([docs/research/pace.md](../../research/pace.md)), several still gaps.
- **Leads**: none named by the hunch; Feigenbaum 1978 (C15's lead) defines the constant.

## C18. Spreading activation has a cliff

- **Question**: does purely excitatory spreading activation change behaviour abruptly when connectivity crosses a
  threshold, and do lateral inhibition, a conserved budget or a fan-out penalty remove the cliff?
- **Method**: a network of N units with random excitatory connections of mean degree d, activation spreading in steps
  with decay; the activated fraction after spreading from one seed unit is recorded as d is swept, at N = 100, 1000
  and 10 000 (the hunch's ten and a hundred times). The same sweep with each of the three cures added in turn. Two-hop
  priming against one-hop priming is recorded at the operating point.
- **Baselines and controls**: the pure network is its own baseline; each cure is compared with it. The harness control:
  at d = 0 nothing beyond the seed is activated, and with decay off on a fully connected network everything is; if
  either fails, the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) no cliff - the steepest slope of activated fraction against d, at N = 10 000, is shown smaller
  than five times its slope at N = 100, a margin fixed here (a phase transition sharpens with size; a smooth change
  does not); (2) a cure leaves the cliff - its steepest slope at N = 10 000 is not shown smaller than the pure
  network's; (3) two-hop priming is not well below one-hop - its ratio's interval lies wholly above 0.5, a margin fixed
  here. Three refuting tests, Bonferroni.
- **Statistics**: slopes from the per-d mean over draws, intervals by bootstrap over network draws.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 network draws per N and d.
- **Cost**: 10 000 units sparse - seconds per sweep; a notebook first, Rust if it matters.
- **Waits on**: nothing for the network; a creature that uses spreading activation for the question whether the cliff
  shows in behaviour.
- **Leads**: Shrager, Hogg and Huberman, "Observation of Phase Transitions in Spreading Activation Networks",
  *Science*, 1987 ([doi:10.1126/science.236.4805.1092](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.236.4805.1092)) - found.
  Collins and Loftus, "A spreading-activation theory of semantic processing", *Psychological Review*, 1975
  ([doi:10.1037/0033-295X.82.6.407](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.82.6.407)) - found. Anderson, "Retrieval of
  propositional information from long-term memory", *Cognitive Psychology*, 1974
  ([doi:10.1016/0010-0285(74)90021-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(74)90021-8)) - found.
