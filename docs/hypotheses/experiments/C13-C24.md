# Plans for C13-C24: Circuits, Rhythms and Dynamics, Second Half

Experiment plans for hunches C13 to C24 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). They follow the conventions set out at the head of
[C1-C12.md](C1-C12.md) - every module with a switch that pins it at baseline, every timescale matched to one measured
in the worm and cited, the decision between *refuted*, *passed* and *inconclusive* by the rule at the head of
[B.md](B.md), the life as the unit of analysis, costs counted in simulated lives until the arena is proven - and do
not repeat them. Where a plan departs from them, it says so.

The leads were looked up in Crossref by DOI on 2026-10-14. Found means the work exists as named, with the DOI given;
none was read in full.

## C13. Vigour from the average reward rate; one global slowing factor

- **Question**: does a tonic signal that tracks the recent average reward rate, and scales how fast the creature acts,
  bring the creature's dependence of speed and latency on food closer to the worm's than the same Program without it -
  and does the worm's own dependence run the way the hunch says, faster with more reward?
- **Method**: a Program whose action latencies and speed are scaled by one tonic variable, an exponentially weighted
  average of recent food intake, with its time constant matched to a measured one when the plan is prepared. The
  manipulated variable is the food density, fixed per life, at three levels; intake is recorded but not used as the
  independent variable, since the creature's own speed sets it. The worm's dependence of speed and touch latency on
  food is read from the literature at preparation, not from the reference recordings, which do not vary the food; the
  worm is known to slow on food, the basal slowing response of
  [Sawin, Ranganathan and Horvitz 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0896-6273(00)81199-X), the opposite of the hunch's
  direction, and the plan lets the data decide. For the second half of the hunch, one global factor scales every time
  constant of the Program's action generator - the touch-response latency, the reversal's duration timer and the
  rhythm generator's period - on a frozen copy, and their changes are recorded together.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program with the tonic variable pinned at its mean. The harness control: a
  Program whose speed is set directly by the food density, by construction, must show the ordering; if it does not, the
  food is not reaching the Program and the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) with the tonic signal, the Program's dependence of speed and latency on food density is no closer
  to the worm's than with it pinned - the paired difference in distance to the worm's curves, over lives, has an
  interval wholly at or above zero; (2) the worm's own dependence runs against the hunch - with more food, speed lower or
  latency longer, the interval from the sources wholly on that side of zero - so that a fit with the opposite sign would
  not rescue a hunch about vigour rising with reward; (3) the worm's speed is shown not to depend on food - its change
  across the densities, with its interval, lies wholly within 10% of its mean, a margin fixed here; (4) the one global
  factor fails to move the three time constants' behavioural effects together - their changes, each relative to its
  baseline, are shown to differ by more than a factor of two, a margin fixed here, by the interval of their ratio. Four
  refuting tests, alpha 0.05/4 each.
- **Statistics**: per-life curves of speed and latency against food density; distances to the worm's curves and their
  paired differences, intervals by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per food density, the default.
- **Cost**: one scalar state and a multiply per action - negligible.
- **Waits on**: food and a sense of intake (STATE.md, *Needs From Other Repositories*); touch (B1's audit of the
  interface); the worm's measured dependence of speed and latency on food, from the literature.
- **Leads**: Niv, Daw, Joel and Dayan, "Tonic dopamine: opportunity costs and the control of response vigor",
  *Psychopharmacology* ([doi:10.1007/s00213-006-0502-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-006-0502-4)) - found; Crossref
  lists it online in 2006 and in print in 2007, as the hunch dates it. Kail, "Developmental change in speed of
  processing during childhood and adolescence", *Psychological Bulletin*, 1991
  ([doi:10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490)) - found; it concerns children's
  speed of processing, so it is a lead for the idea of one global factor, not for a worm. Sawin, Ranganathan and
  Horvitz 2000 (above), found in Crossref and already cited in the lab's hypotheses, not read here.

## C14. Poised near criticality, monitored

- **Question**: does the creature's behaviour come closest to the worm's when its brain runs near the boundary between
  ordered and chaotic dynamics, and can the distance from that boundary be measured while it lives?
- **Method**: a recurrent Program of `tanh` units with random weights scaled by one gain g, which moves it from ordered
  through critical to chaotic dynamics. At each of nine gains spaced across that range, lives are run and four measures
  of criticality recorded from the brain's own activity: the distribution of avalanche sizes (its exponent, after Beggs
  and Plenz), the autocorrelation time, the dynamic range of the response to graded touch, and the largest Lyapunov
  exponent from two copies started a small distance apart. The critical gain is where that exponent crosses zero.
  Behavioural realism at each gain is the number of the benchmark's features that fall within its per-feature bands.
- **Baselines and controls**: each measure is checked first on systems of known regime - a branching process with
  branching ratio below, at and above one for the avalanche exponent, and a random `tanh` network at gains well below
  and well above its transition for the Lyapunov exponent and the autocorrelation time, which should peak near the
  transition; the dynamic range is checked on the branching model, whose range is known to be largest at the critical
  ratio. If a measure does not order its controls correctly, the run is void.
  The best gain by plain search is chosen on one half of the lives and its score measured on the other half, so that
  choosing the best of nine noisy means does not inflate it.
- **Refuted if**: realism at the critical gain is scored at the grid gain nearest to it, re-chosen in each bootstrap
  resample. (1) The gain at which realism peaks is shown not to be near the critical gain - the bootstrap interval
  of the difference between the two, with both re-estimated in each resample so that the critical gain's own
  uncertainty is carried, lies wholly more than one grid step from zero; (2) realism at the critical gain is shown lower
  than at the best gain, on the held-out half, by more than one benchmark feature. Two refuting tests, alpha 0.025 each.
- **Statistics**: the peak's position by a quadratic fitted to per-gain mean realism; intervals by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per gain, nine gains, 180 lives, halved for choosing and scoring. Before the run,
  the power of clause (2) to find a difference of one benchmark feature with 10 lives per half is computed by
  simulation from a pilot's variance, and the lives raised until it reaches 80%; more also if the difference's interval
  is wider than two grid steps.
- **Cost**: the Lyapunov monitor costs a second copy of the brain; the others are cheap counters.
- **Waits on**: the benchmark and its per-feature bands; the arena. Sharpens H7.
- **Leads**: Langton, "Computation at the edge of chaos: Phase transitions and emergent computation", *Physica D*,
  1990 ([doi:10.1016/0167-2789(90)90064-V](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(90)90064-V)) - found. Beggs and Plenz,
  "Neuronal Avalanches in Neocortical Circuits", *Journal of Neuroscience*, 2003
  ([doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-35-11167.2003](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-35-11167.2003)) - found. Turrigiano
  and Nelson, "Homeostatic plasticity in the developing nervous system", *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2004
  ([doi:10.1038/nrn1327](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1327)) - found; it is the lead for the slow gain control the hunch
  adds at larger scale, which this plan does not test.

## C15. A logistic-map arousal engine

- **Question**: does one logistic map, its parameter driven by internal state, produce the worm's dwell-time
  distributions and transition statistics between behavioural states better than a plain mapping from state to a
  random temperature?
- **Method**: arousal is the iterate of the logistic map x <- r x (1 - x), one step per decision, with r driven by a
  slow internal state; the behavioural state is read from x by thresholds. A deterministic map cannot be expected to match a
  recording's sequence step by step, since its phase is arbitrary, so it is scored on what the hunch names: the
  dwell-time distributions of forward, reverse and pause, and the matrix of transitions between them. Thresholds and the
  drive of r are fitted on half the reference recordings; the model is then run for as long as all the held-out
  recordings together, from initial conditions drawn by a seed fixed at preparation, and its dwell times and
  transitions compared with the held-out half.
- **Baselines and controls**: the plain alternative the hunch names - the same internal state mapped to the temperature
  of a random choice among the same states, with the same number of fitted parameters. The harness
  control, with thresholds fixed for it at x = 0.5 and 0.75: at r = 2.9 (a fixed point near 0.655) the engine must give
  one constant state, and at r = 3.2 (a cycle between about 0.513 and 0.799) it must alternate between two; and two runs
  from the same initial condition must agree exactly. If any fails, the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: the engine is no closer to the held-out recordings than the random-temperature baseline - by the sum,
  over the three states, of the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between simulated and held-out dwell times, plus the total
  absolute difference between the transition matrices, the interval of the engine's distance minus the baseline's lies
  wholly at or above zero. One refuting test, alpha 0.05.
- **Statistics**: that distance for each model, its interval by bootstrap over held-out recordings and over the
  models' initial-condition seeds.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: the engine is deterministic given its initial condition, so the repetitions are over the
  reference recordings, all of the drawn reference set, split in half by a seed fixed at preparation, and over 20
  initial conditions per model.
- **Cost**: a multiply and a subtraction per decision.
- **Waits on**: the feature tool's locomotion states (STATE.md item 2) and the reference statistics.
- **Leads**: Feigenbaum, "Quantitative universality for a class of nonlinear transformations", *Journal of Statistical
  Physics*, 1978 ([doi:10.1007/BF01020332](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01020332)) - found. Strogatz, *Nonlinear
  Dynamics and Chaos* ([doi:10.1201/9780429492563](https://doi.org/10.1201/9780429492563)) - found as the 2018 CRC
  edition; the hunch names 2015, the second edition's earlier printing, and the plan matches it as the likely work.

## C16. Golden-ratio spacing of rhythms

- **Question**: do rhythm generators whose frequencies are spaced by powers of the golden ratio fall into n:m locking
  with one another less often than generators spaced otherwise, at the same total spread of frequencies?
- **Method**: locking between rhythms of different frequency is n:m locking - n cycles of one to m of the other - which
  1:1 phase coupling cannot show, so the oscillators are coupled with n:m terms, sin(m theta_j - n theta_i) for every
  pair of whole numbers with max(n, m) at most five, at strength K. N oscillators (N = 3, 5 and 8) have their frequencies
  spaced by powers of the golden ratio, so that the lowest and highest span a ratio of phi to the power N - 1. The
  comparison spacings hold that span fixed, so that spacing is not confounded with spread: frequencies drawn
  log-uniformly within the span and sorted, and frequencies spaced linearly across it. Every pair counts, neighbours or
  not, since the hunch is that independent rhythms stay independent as their number grows. That is a real risk for
  it: powers of phi lie close to whole numbers (phi to the 3rd is about 4.24, to the 4th about 6.85, near the Lucas
  numbers 4 and 7), so pairs that are not neighbours may lock readily, and the plan expects that to show at N = 5 and 8
  if it is so. Recorded: the share of oscillator pairs in n:m lock (m theta_j - n theta_i bounded over the last half of
  a long run, for some n:m in that set), across a range of K.
- **Baselines and controls**: the log-uniform and linear spacings are the baselines. The harness controls: two
  oscillators at exactly 2:1 must lock 2:1 at any positive K, and two uncoupled ones (K = 0) never; and, on the circle
  map, a rotation number of the golden mean must be the last of a set of sampled rotation numbers to lock as K grows, a
  known property of the map. If any fails, the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: over the coupling range where any spacing shows locking, the golden-ratio spacing locks no less than
  the log-uniform spacings - the interval of the difference in locked share lies wholly at or above zero, at every N.
  One refuting test, alpha 0.05; the linear spacing is reported beside it.
- **Statistics**: locked share per run, compared with the log-uniform spacings' distribution by bootstrap over draws.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 draws of log-uniform frequencies and of initial phases per N and K.
- **Cost**: a few oscillators; seconds of compute. A notebook is enough for the first pass; a result that matters moves
  into tested Rust.
- **Waits on**: nothing for the dynamics; the worm's own rhythms' frequencies and coupling for the second step, which
  asks whether golden spacing matters at the couplings the worm has.
- **Leads**: Pletzer, Kerschbaum and Klimesch, "When frequencies never synchronize: The golden mean and the resting
  EEG", *Brain Research*, 2010
  ([doi:10.1016/j.brainres.2010.03.074](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2010.03.074)) - found. The maintainer's
  earlier small tests are unrecorded and are not evidence.

## C17. Memory timescales spaced by Feigenbaum's constant

- **Question**: does each pair of neighbouring timescales in the worm's memory layers stand in a ratio near
  Feigenbaum's delta, 4.669...?
- **Method**: no brain is built. The time constants of the worm's memories - from seconds-long sensory integration
  through minutes-long habituation to hours-long associative memory - are read from primary sources, each with its
  uncertainty, ordered, and each neighbour ratio computed. Which memories count as layers is fixed from the sources' own
  definitions before any ratio is computed. Each ratio is tested, not their geometric mean, which the end points and the
  number of layers alone would set.
- **Baselines and controls**: the chance of the outcome without the hunch: with the same two end points and the same
  number of layers, the interior timescales placed log-uniformly between them, the probability p0 that every neighbour
  ratio falls within the band below, by Monte Carlo.
- **Refuted if**: any neighbour ratio lies outside the band from 4.669 / 1.5 to 4.669 x 1.5, a margin fixed here, with
  its interval wholly outside; with k ratios, each interval is taken at 1 - 0.05/k (Bonferroni), from the sources'
  uncertainties. It passes only if every ratio's interval, at the same coverage, lies within
  the band and p0 is below 0.05, so that the pass is not what chance spacing gives anyway; anything else is
  inconclusive, as is fewer than four timescales read with their uncertainties.
- **Statistics**: each ratio's interval by Monte Carlo over the stated uncertainties; p0 by Monte Carlo.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 100 000 Monte Carlo draws each, seeds fixed at preparation.
- **Cost**: a calculation.
- **Waits on**: the pace note's time constants ([docs/research/pace.md](../../research/pace.md)), several still gaps.
- **Leads**: none named by the hunch; Feigenbaum 1978 (C15's lead) defines the constant.

## C18. Spreading activation has a cliff

- **Question**: does purely excitatory spreading activation change abruptly when connectivity crosses a threshold - a
  change that sharpens with size, as a phase transition does - and do lateral inhibition, a conserved budget or a
  fan-out penalty remove the cliff?
- **Method**: a network of N units with random excitatory connections of mean degree d, every edge of weight 0.2;
  the seed unit starts at activation 1, and for 20 steps each unit's activation is half its last value plus the
  weighted sum of its inputs, capped at 1; a unit counts as activated when its activation exceeds 0.1. The cures, each
  fixed here: lateral inhibition subtracts half the mean activation of a unit's inputs; a conserved budget divides each
  unit's output equally among its targets, so that its total output is 0.2 times its activation times its mean degree,
  whatever its own fan-out; a fan-out penalty divides each edge's weight by the natural logarithm of one plus its
  source's out-degree. The activated fraction is recorded as d is swept, at N = 100, 1000 and 10 000 (the hunch's ten and a hundred
  times), and the same sweep is run with each of the three cures in turn. Priming: at the operating point, half the
  degree at which the pure network's activated fraction first passes one half, the mean activation of units one hop and
  two hops from the seed.
- **Baselines and controls**: the pure network is its own baseline; each cure is compared with it. The harness control:
  at d = 0 nothing beyond the seed is activated, and with decay off on a fully connected network everything is; if
  either fails, the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: on a sparse random graph a transition's window narrows about as N to the power -1/3 (as the
  giant component's does; for spreading with decay this is an approximation, to be checked in the first sweep), so its steepest
  slope grows about 4.6 times from N = 100 to N = 10 000, while a smooth change does not sharpen at all; the margin
  between them is fixed here at a factor of two. (1) No cliff: the ratio of the pure network's steepest slope at
  N = 10 000 to that at N = 100 has an interval wholly below two. (2-4) A cure leaves the cliff: for that cure, the same
  ratio's interval lies wholly above two. (5) Two-hop priming is not well below one-hop: the ratio of their mean
  activations has an interval wholly above 0.5, a margin fixed here. Five refuting tests, alpha 0.01 each.
- **Statistics**: slopes from the per-d mean over draws, ratios and intervals by bootstrap over network draws.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 network draws per N and d.
- **Cost**: 10 000 units, sparse - seconds per sweep; a notebook first, Rust if it matters.
- **Waits on**: nothing for the network; a creature that uses spreading activation for the question whether the cliff
  shows in behaviour.
- **Leads**: Shrager, Hogg and Huberman, "Observation of Phase Transitions in Spreading Activation Networks",
  *Science*, 1987 ([doi:10.1126/science.236.4805.1092](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.236.4805.1092)) - found.
  Collins and Loftus, "A spreading-activation theory of semantic processing", *Psychological Review*, 1975
  ([doi:10.1037/0033-295X.82.6.407](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.82.6.407)) - found. Anderson, "Retrieval of
  propositional information from long-term memory", *Cognitive Psychology*, 1974
  ([doi:10.1016/0010-0285(74)90021-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(74)90021-8)) - found.
