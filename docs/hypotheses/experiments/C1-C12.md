# Plans for C1-C12: Circuits, Rhythms and Dynamics, First Half

Experiment plans for hunches C1 to C12 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). Section C is about how a brain is built, so these are the first plans
that test a brain's own structure; each is refuted when the structure does not produce what the hunch says it will,
or does no better than a simpler one of the same cost (the rule *Lean brains*). Every module tested here has a switch
that pins it at baseline, by the rule *Pre-register and ablate*, and every timescale in it is matched to one measured
in the worm and cited (*The animal's pace*).

The leads were looked up in Crossref by DOI on 2026-10-13. Found means the work exists as named, with the DOI
given; none was read in full. A lead that could not be found says so and stays an unverified lead; leads the lab
already cites elsewhere point there, with what was read of them. Where a hunch names a work as "et al." and Crossref
lists two authors for the work of that year and title, the plan matches it as the likely work, not certainly the one the
hunch meant.

Each refutation clause decides between *refuted*, *passed* and *inconclusive* by the rule set out at the head of
[B.md](B.md): a refutation is a finding - a difference where the hunch says there is none, a value outside a band, or
a predicted effect shown smaller than a smallest effect of interest the plan names - and a plan's refuting tests are
corrected together (Bonferroni, k tests each at alpha 0.05/k); a pass finds what the hunch predicts, and pass tests
run at alpha 0.05 uncorrected, since the hunch passes only when all of them do; anything else is inconclusive, kept to
be run again with more seeds. Where a hunch claims that two things do not differ, the plan fixes a margin and tests
equivalence: an interval wholly inside it passes, one wholly outside it refutes. Some clauses lean on the smallest
difference the frozen benchmark declares meaningful for a measure; the benchmark fixes one per measure when it is
frozen, and until then those clauses cannot be run. A clause that compares with the worm by the frozen benchmark's
per-feature comparisons passes by that benchmark's own rule, which must then be an equivalence rule with a stated
power, not a mere failure to find a difference; if the frozen benchmark's rule is not, the clause says so when it is
prepared. Seed counts are a floor: when a plan is prepared, its power is computed for both decisions and the seeds
raised if either is low.

Two plans depart from that rule, each for a stated reason. C4's hunch survives if any one comparison finds a
difference, so there the pass tests are corrected and the single refutation, which needs every pair shown equivalent,
is not. C12 refutes the worm's clustering by non-detection, which the rule otherwise does not allow; it does so only
behind a planted-rhythm control that bounds how often a rhythm of a stated strength is missed, and says that it refutes
only rhythms at least that strong.

Wherever a plan compares shares or rates, the unit is the life, not the trial: trials within one life share that life's
state, so per-life values are compared with intervals by bootstrap over lives.

Costs are counted in simulated lives. What one life costs in compute and memory is not known until the arena is
proven (STATE.md item 3), so no plan gives absolute figures yet; each will when it is prepared. Seed counts follow
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format), 20 per condition, unless a plan says why it differs.

The Grid has touch, with where the body was struck and with what impulse, and a thermal sense of radiant heat
(`irradiance`), but no chemical sense, no sense of the substrate's temperature and no sense labelled noxious
([PROGRAM_INTERFACE.md § Senses](https://github.com/ai-quokka-wannabe/tron-grid-lite/blob/c083262e473884cb60e83e95cddb851e4c7a8962/docs/PROGRAM_INTERFACE.md#senses),
pinned). Two stimuli are candidates for a noxious one, to be weighed with a source on the worm when a plan that needs
one is prepared: a hard strike, read through touch, and a hot source, read through `irradiance`. Plans that need a
gradient wait on the chemical sense the maintainer takes up (STATE.md *Needs From Other Repositories*), or run first
in B5's dish ([B.md § B5](B.md#b5-a-fast-agar-plate-world-for-iterating)) and are scored only on the Grid.

## C1. Two chemotaxis mechanisms at once

- **Question**: does a Program that runs a biased random walk and weathervane steering together reproduce both
  signatures the worm shows; does each mechanism carry its own signature; and does hunger shift each on its own
  timescale?
- **Method**: a chemotaxis Program with both mechanisms, each behind its own switch. Measure the hunch's two
  signatures per life: the slope of the signed curvature of runs against the bearing to the source (weathervaning),
  and the slope of the pirouette rate against the change in concentration over time, binned as in the worm's source
  (the biased random walk). Hunger: run the Program after food deprivation of several fixed lengths, set before the
  run from the worm's source, and measure both slopes at each.
- **Baselines and controls**: each mechanism switched off in turn, and both off - with both off, the chemotaxis index
  (as in [B.md § B4](B.md#b4-how-a-reversal-ends-decides-whether-chemotaxis-works)) must not have a 95% interval
  wholly above zero, or something else steers and the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1, 2) with both mechanisms on, either slope fails the frozen benchmark's per-feature comparison
  against the real worm's; (3) switching off the random walk is shown not to lower the pirouette slope's magnitude by
  a quarter - the one-sided interval of the fall, as a fraction of the intact magnitude, lies wholly below 0.25 (a
  margin fixed here); (4) the same for weathervaning and the curvature slope. Four refuting tests, each at 0.05/4.
  Clauses 1 and 2 pass if the slopes pass the comparisons; clauses 3 and 4 pass if the fall's one-sided 95% interval
  lies wholly above zero; any other outcome is inconclusive. Hunger: if the worm's source gives each mechanism's time
  course after deprivation, the Program's slopes at each deprivation length are compared with the worm's by the same
  per-feature comparisons, which then join the plan's refuting tests, so k grows by their number, and a failure at any
  length refutes that half; until such a source is read, the hunger half waits and says so.
- **Statistics**: slopes by least-squares regression per life; bootstrap intervals over seeds; the per-feature
  comparisons.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per condition; four conditions, and 20 per deprivation length for the hunger half.
- **Cost**: 80 lives, and 20 per deprivation length.
- **Waits on**: a chemical gradient (the Grid's chemical sense, or B5's dish first); the real worm's slopes, read at
  the source; for the hunger half, a source on each mechanism's time course after deprivation, to be found.
- **Leads**: found - Ferrée and Lockery 1999, "Computational rules for chemotaxis in the nematode *C. elegans*"
  ([doi:10.1023/A:1008857906763](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008857906763)); the hunch names it "Ferrée et al.", and
  Crossref lists two authors. Cited elsewhere - Pierce-Shimomura et al. 1999, read on its PMC page for [the
  behaviour-measures note](../../research/behaviour-measures.md); Iino and Yoshida 2009, found for
  [A1](A1-A8.md#a1-a-validation-suite-with-pass-bands-fixed-before-any-tuning).

## C2. A bistable forward-backward switch

- **Question**: does a pair of mutually inhibiting command units form a two-state switch that touch tips, give the
  worm's direction-specific escape, does the mutual inhibition earn its place, and do simulated ablations of the units
  match the worm's?
- **Method**: a switch of two units, forward and backward, each inhibiting the other; touch at the head drives the
  backward unit, at the tail the forward one. Deliver 20 touches per life, head and tail alternating, and measure the
  direction of motion after each, and how long the response lasts. Map each unit to the worm's neurons from the
  sources (Chalfie et al. 1985 name AVD and AVB for anterior touch and PVC for posterior touch, from its abstract;
  Zhen and Samuel 2015 for the command interneurons), written down before any run. Bistability: on a frozen copy, ramp
  the drive to the two units from favouring forward to favouring backward and back, each ramp lasting at least ten
  times the units' longest time constant (fixed here, so that lag cannot pass for hysteresis), and record the drive at
  which the switch flips each way; a bistable switch flips at different drives on the two ramps (hysteresis). Each
  seed runs 20 ramps, each with its own seed of the Program's noise; a Program with no noise gives one exact width per
  seed, and the interval is then over seeds alone.
- **Baselines and controls**: the switch without mutual inhibition (each unit driven alone) - if neither the switch
  nor this control responds to touch with any direction (for each, the 95% bootstrap interval, over lives, of the
  per-life share of backing after head touch minus that after tail touch includes zero), the touch is not reaching the
  Program and the run is void. The switch without mutual inhibition is also the ramp's negative control: its
  hysteresis width's 95% interval must lie wholly below the margin of clause 5, or the ramp is too fast and the run is
  void. Each unit ablated in turn.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the share of head touches followed by backing differs from the real worm's by more than 10
  percentage points, a margin fixed here - the interval of the difference at 1 - 0.05/4, from per-life shares
  bootstrapped over lives against the worm's share and its interval as the source gives them, lies wholly beyond the
  margin; it passes if its 90% interval lies wholly within it (two one-sided tests at alpha 0.05); (2) the same for
  tail touches and forward motion; (3) any unit's ablation changes a measure in the direction opposite to the worm's
  after ablating the neurons it is mapped to, read from the source - the change's interval at 1 - 0.05/4k, for k
  ablation-measure pairs, lies wholly on the opposite side of zero; (5) the switch is shown not to be bistable - the
  one-sided interval at 1 - 0.05/4 of the hysteresis width, the difference between the two flipping drives, lies
  wholly below a tenth of the ramp's range, a margin fixed here; it passes if the one-sided 95% interval lies wholly
  above zero. These four refuting groups (1, 2, 3 and 5) share alpha 0.05, a quarter each; the per-measure ablation
  clauses pass if each change's 95% interval lies on the worm's side of zero. And (4) the switch without mutual
  inhibition passes at least as many of the per-feature comparisons against the worm (response direction, share and
  duration) as the intact switch - the inhibition earns nothing; this is a count fixed here, not a test. If the
  two-unit switch cannot be mapped to the ablated neurons at all, clause 3 counts as fired: a model that cannot be
  ablated like the worm cannot show the hunch's second test.
- **Statistics**: per-life shares with bootstrap intervals over lives; the per-feature comparisons; bootstrap
  intervals for the ablations' changes and the hysteresis width.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per condition, 20 touches per life.
- **Cost**: four conditions (intact, no inhibition, each unit ablated), 80 lives; the ramps run on frozen copies of
  the intact switch and of the one without inhibition, 20 ramps per seed for 20 seeds each, not lives.
- **Waits on**: a touch stimulus the experimenter can deliver at a chosen place on the body in the arena (the Grid
  reports touch; it has no prod), a Program with the switch, and the worm's touch data, read at the source.
- **Leads**: cited elsewhere - Chalfie et al. 1985, in [H1](../README.md#h1-a-fast-reflex-arc), and its abstract read
  for [B.md § B1](B.md#b1-a-nervous-system-not-a-game-client). Found - Zhen and Samuel 2015
  ([doi:10.1016/j.conb.2015.03.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2015.03.009)).

## C3. Graded neurons and gap junctions

- **Question**: in a network of graded, non-spiking units with chemical synapses and gap junctions, does removing the
  gap junctions hurt coordination more than removing as many chemical synapses - and does the network's activity, seen
  through a model of calcium fluorescence, look like the worm's imaging?
- **Method**: one continuous-time equation per unit (leak, sigmoid synapses, symmetric diffusive gap junctions), with
  time constants in the range the hunch names (tens to hundreds of milliseconds), checked against the leads at the
  source before use, and synapse signs from the transmitter, taken from a published assignment of signs, to be found
  and read. Measure coordination as the phase coherence of the undulation between neighbouring body segments. Remove
  every gap junction; in a separate condition, remove an equal number of chemical connections (counted as connected
  pairs of units), drawn at random per seed.
- **Baselines and controls**: the intact network - its coherence must lie above that of the same lives with the
  segments' phases shuffled in time (the 95% interval of the difference wholly above zero), or there is no
  coordination to lose and the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: coherence after removing the gap junctions is shown not to be lower than after removing the chemical
  connections by at least 0.05 (coherence runs from 0 to 1; the margin is fixed here) - the one-sided 95% interval of
  the chemical condition's coherence minus the gap condition's lies wholly below 0.05 (the plan's one refuting test).
  It passes if a one-sided Welch t test at alpha 0.05 finds the gap condition lower, and is inconclusive otherwise.
- **Statistics**: phase coherence per life; the test and the bootstrap interval above.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per condition; each seed of the chemical condition draws its own set to remove.
- **Cost**: three conditions, 60 lives.
- **Waits on**: a network Program in Rust and the arena; a published sign assignment, read. The hunch's comparison
  with calcium imaging through a model of the fluorescence waits on calcium recordings the lab has not chosen (none is
  in [data/README.md](../../../data/README.md)); it is not dropped, and is planned when a recording is chosen.
- **Leads**: found - Goodman et al. 1998
  ([doi:10.1016/S0896-6273(00)81014-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0896-6273(00)81014-4)); Liu et al. 2018
  ([doi:10.1016/j.cell.2018.08.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2018.08.018)); Beer 1995
  ([doi:10.1177/105971239500300405](https://doi.org/10.1177/105971239500300405)); Cook et al. 2019
  ([doi:10.1038/s41586-019-1352-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1352-7)).

## C4. The connectome as a mask - or not

- **Question**: can experiment settle which of three positions gives the better worm - the wiring diagram as a mask on
  which connections may carry weight, the connectome only as a sanity check on a functional abstraction, or extracted
  circuits of a dozen neurons grown toward all 302?
- **Method**: build one Program per position, matched in compute and memory per simulated second (within 10% of each
  other, measured, and reported beside each score by the rule *Lean brains*). Score each on the frozen benchmark and
  on ablation predictions: the shifts of [A9-A15.md § A15](A9-A15.md#a15-computational-psychiatry-on-a-frozen-copy)
  against the worm's after the matching ablations.
- **Baselines and controls**: the mask Program with its connections shuffled, the same number kept - if the mask
  position does not beat it, any advantage of that position comes from sparsity, not from the worm's wiring, and is
  reported so.
- **Refuted if**: every pair of the three positions is shown equivalent, on the benchmark and on the ablation
  predictions - each pairwise difference's interval lies wholly within the smallest difference the frozen benchmark
  declares meaningful - so that experiment cannot settle what the hunch says it can. The question counts as settled if
  any pair is told apart by the frozen benchmark's comparisons, three pairwise tests each at alpha 0.05/3: here the
  hunch survives on any one finding, so the correction guards against a wrongful survival. The equivalence is shown by
  two one-sided tests at alpha 0.05 per pair (90% intervals), not corrected, since the refutation needs all three (an
  intersection-union test). Anything else is inconclusive. Which position wins is the result, not a refutation.
- **Statistics**: the frozen benchmark's score per Program; pairwise comparisons as above.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per Program.
- **Cost**: four Programs, 80 lives, and the build of three Programs - the largest of section C.
- **Waits on**: the frozen benchmark and the arena; A15's ablation comparisons.
- **Leads**: cited elsewhere - White et al. 1986, in [the rules](../../../.claude/CLAUDE.md). Found - Cook et al. 2019
  (as for C3); Varshney et al. 2011
  ([doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1001066](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1001066)); Witvliet et al. 2021
  ([doi:10.1038/s41586-021-03778-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03778-8)); Ripoll-Sánchez et al. 2023
  ([doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2023.09.043](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2023.09.043)).

## C5. A layered brain with its timescales written down

- **Question**: does a brain of six layers keep each layer at the timescale written down for it, stay always
  mid-thought, keep its learning bounded within each settle, and does each layer earn its place? The hunch names no
  test; these follow from its claims.
- **Method**: build the six layers as the hunch lists them - reflexes on raw senses; a behaviour sequencer with a
  readable transition matrix; attractor settling; a low-dimensional modulator state; senses perturbing the settling
  continuously; small bounded learning during each settle - with each timescale taken from a worm measurement read at
  the source (the pace note, STATE.md item 1). Measure each layer's autocorrelation time in a life; the share of ticks
  at which the settling layer is at rest (its state changing by less than a tolerance fixed with the layer); the
  largest weight change in any settle against its stated bound; then switch each layer off in turn and score the
  benchmark.
- **Baselines and controls**: all layers on; each off in turn. The autocorrelation estimator is first run on a
  synthetic process of known time constant, and must recover it within 10%, or its measurements are void.
- **Refuted if**: a layer's autocorrelation time has a bootstrap interval lying wholly outside a factor of two of its
  written timescale; or the share of ticks at which the settling layer is at rest, averaged over lives with the senses
  on, has an interval wholly above 5% (the creature is not always mid-thought). Or any settle's weight change exceeds
  the bound (exact). Or a layer is shown not to earn its place: switching it off leaves every benchmark measure
  equivalent to the intact brain's, each difference's interval wholly within the smallest difference the frozen
  benchmark declares meaningful; a layer whose removal changes some measure by the benchmark's comparisons earns its
  place, and anything between is inconclusive. That makes thirteen refuting tests - six timescales, the resting share
  and six ablations - each at 0.05/13; the factor of two and the 5% are fixed here, before any data. A timescale
  clause passes if its 95% interval lies wholly within the factor of two, the resting-share clause if its 95% interval
  lies wholly below 5%; anything between is inconclusive. The transition matrix is reported, and judged readable if
  every row is a stated behaviour and every entry a probability.
- **Statistics**: autocorrelation times per life with bootstrap intervals; shares and exact checks as above; the
  benchmark's comparisons per ablation.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per condition, seven conditions.
- **Cost**: 140 lives.
- **Waits on**: the pace note (item 1), the frozen benchmark, and the layers themselves.
- **Leads**: none named.

## C6. Blend the layers, do not override

- **Question**: does blending higher layers with reflexes, by an authority that falls with arousal, novelty and low
  confidence, let reflexes take over when startled, and keep the creature safe and crudely chemotaxing when the higher
  layers fail?
- **Method**: the hunch's blend. Log the authority weight, arousal, novelty and confidence every tick of intact lives.
  Make the higher layers fail mid-life in two ways: silent (their output removed) and faulty (their output frozen at
  its last value). Measure harm avoidance (contacts with a noxious stimulus) and the chemotaxis index.
- **Baselines and controls**: an override architecture (higher layers replace reflexes whenever they are active),
  failed the same way; a creature with no reflexes, its higher layers kept (it must have more noxious contacts than
  the intact blend, by a 95% interval wholly above zero, or the stimulus is not noxious enough to test and the run is
  void).
- **Refuted if** any of six predicted effects is shown smaller than its margin, all fixed here - six refuting tests,
  each at 0.05/6: (1-3) the authority weight's Spearman correlation with arousal, with novelty, and with confidence
  has the sign the hunch claims (falling with arousal and novelty, rising with confidence) with a size of at least
  0.3: refuted if the one-sided interval lies wholly short of 0.3 on that side; (4) with the higher layers silent, the
  blend has at least a quarter fewer noxious contacts than the creature with no reflexes; (5) with them silent, the
  chemotaxis index is at least 0.1 (crude chemotaxis); (6) with them faulty, the blend has at least a quarter fewer
  noxious contacts than the override architecture faulty - otherwise blending earns nothing. Each clause passes if its
  one-sided 95% interval lies wholly on the predicted side of zero, and is inconclusive otherwise.
- **Statistics**: Spearman's rho, and bootstrap intervals over seeds for every effect.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per condition.
- **Cost**: five conditions (intact blend, blend silent, blend faulty, override faulty, no reflexes), 100 lives.
- **Waits on**: reflexes (H1), a higher layer, a chemical gradient for the chemotaxis half, and a noxious stimulus - a
  hard strike or a hot source, as weighed at the head of this file.
- **Leads**: found - Brooks 1986 ([doi:10.1109/JRA.1986.1087032](https://doi.org/10.1109/JRA.1986.1087032)). Not found
  as a work - Arkin 1998, *Behavior-Based Robotics*: a Crossref search by title and author finds no record of the
  book, only a review of it in *Robotica*
  ([doi:10.1017/S0263574799241173](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0263574799241173)), which is public evidence that it
  exists; it stays an unverified lead.

## C7. Reflex interlocks the learning cannot reach

- **Question**: are the protective reflexes out of reach of every learned path - and does the Program meet the hunch's
  other safety-engineering clauses: several independent detectors, fail-safe on uncertainty, a watchdog budget per
  tick, a self-test at start-up, a protective action that never increases another forbidden quantity, and a log of
  what the actuator did?
- **Method**: one check per clause. (a) Threshold, static: trace every write to every reflex threshold in the
  Program's code and list its sources; none may be a learned parameter. (b) Threshold, dynamic: run lives with
  learning driven hard toward a reward that a lower reflex threshold would earn, and record every threshold every
  tick. (c) Detectors: list each reflex's detectors; at least two per reflex, with no input in common. (d) Fail-safe:
  withhold one sense's reading (as a dropped sense would arrive), and record whether the protective action follows.
  (e) Watchdog: give the higher layers a planted overrun of their per-tick budget, and record whether the reflex still
  acts in that tick. (f) Self-test: rez a Program with one reflex broken, and record whether the start-up test reports
  it. (g) No collateral harm: in a probe with two hazards, record whether the protective action from one drives the
  body into the other. (h) Actuator log: compare the logged values with the joint angles the Grid reports actually
  held (`joint_angles`), not the targets sent.
- **Baselines and controls**: blind planted faults, one per check. One agent prepares eight copies of the Program,
  each with one planted fault, and keeps the key: a learned parameter that writes a threshold directly (a); a
  threshold that drifts under learning pressure through a path a trace of direct writes misses, such as a buffer
  shared with a learned layer (b); a reflex with a single detector (c); a reflex that ignores a withheld reading (d);
  a reflex scheduled after the higher layers, so an overrun delays it (e); a start-up test that reports every reflex
  as sound (f); a protective action that ignores the second hazard (g); a log of the targets sent (h). A second agent
  who has not seen the key runs checks (a)-(h) on every copy and on the intact Program. A check that misses its
  planted fault cannot see what it tests, and the run is void for that clause.
- **Refuted if**: the intact Program fails any check - a threshold changes by any amount in (b), a reflex has fewer
  than two independent detectors, the protective action does not follow a withheld reading, the reflex misses a tick
  of overrun, the self-test passes a broken reflex, a protective action enters the second hazard in more than 1 of the
  20 probe lives, or a logged value is not the reported one.
- **Statistics**: exact for every check; for (g), the count of lives out of 20.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives for each of (b), (d), (e) and (g); (f) rezzes each Program once; (h) reads the
  logs of the (b) lives; (a) and (c) are static. The blind auditor runs every check on all nine Programs: the eight
  copies and the intact one.
- **Cost**: per Program, 80 lives and one rez for (f); nine Programs, 720 lives and nine rezzes, and the code reviews
  by two agents.
- **Waits on**: reflexes (H1) and a learning module; (a) and (c) can run in CI once both exist in Rust.
- **Leads**: none named.

## C8. A fast global stop and a slow appraisal; threat has an imminence axis

- **Question**: is the stop signal's latency unaffected by how busy deliberation is, does the stop carry nothing about
  what to do next, and does behaviour differ between near and distant threat?
- **Method**: deliver a stop-worthy stimulus while the Program deliberates under low and high load (few and many
  candidate actions), and measure the time from stimulus to halt in ticks. Deliver two stop-worthy stimuli of
  different kinds in matched situations, and record the next action reselected after each. Separately, present a
  threat at three imminences - distal and uncertain, proximal and imminent, certain but distant - and measure the
  behaviour under each.
- **Baselines and controls**: a Program whose stop runs through the slow appraisal - the 95% interval of its latency
  under high load minus low load's must lie wholly above two ticks, or the probe cannot see the difference and the run
  is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the difference in median stop latency between high and low load lies outside plus or minus one
  tick (31.25 ms, the Grid's resolution) - its one-sided interval wholly above one tick or wholly below minus one
  refutes, its 90% interval wholly inside passes (two one-sided tests at alpha 0.05); (2) the total variation distance
  between the next-action distributions after the two kinds of stop exceeds 0.1 - the lower bound of its one-sided
  interval above 0.1 refutes, the upper bound of its one-sided 95% interval below 0.1 passes (the stop says something
  about what to do); the plug-in distance is biased upwards and is positive even between identical distributions, so
  it is bias-corrected first, by subtracting its mean under a permutation null (each life sees one kind of stop, so
  the kind's labels are shuffled across lives, 1,000 permutations), and the intervals are taken on the corrected
  distance. That correction over-corrects when the distributions truly differ, which favours a wrongful pass; so when
  the plan is prepared, the pass threshold is calibrated by a procedure fixed here and run as a step of the analysis:
  simulate the pass test at a true distance of exactly 0.1, with the plan's numbers of lives and reselections, and
  take the worst case over the family made by moving mass 0.1 between the two commonest actions of the reselection
  distribution pooled over both kinds of stop (pooling hides the difference under test, so the calibration cannot peek
  at it; where the second commonest action holds less than 0.1, the mass moves from the commonest to all others in
  proportion, and where no action holds 0.1, which needs eleven or more actions, it moves from the actions in order of
  size, commonest first, until 0.1 has moved, to the remaining actions in proportion); lower the threshold until the
  test passes in no more than 5% of the simulations. Many pairs of distributions lie at that distance and the error
  depends on which, hence the worst case; the procedure, not its numerical result, is what is pre-registered here, and
  the result is written in the journal with the analysis; (3) the median stop latency fails the per-feature comparison
  against the worm's latency from touch to reversal, from the pace note (*The animal's pace*), passing if it passes
  them; (4, 5) behaviour under the near threat is shown equivalent to that under each distant one, every measure's
  difference within the smallest the frozen benchmark declares meaningful, where telling them apart by its comparisons
  passes. Five refuting tests, each at 0.05/5; the margins of one tick and 0.1 are fixed here; anything between pass
  and refutation is inconclusive.
- **Statistics**: bootstrap intervals over lives for the latency difference and the bias-corrected distance; the
  per-feature comparisons.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per condition.
- **Cost**: four load conditions (two Programs at two loads), two stop kinds and three imminences, 180 lives.
- **Waits on**: a deliberating Program (C10), the pace note's latencies, and a threat the creature can sense on the
  Grid (a hard strike or a hot source, as weighed at the head of this file).
- **Leads**: found - Aron and Poldrack 2006
  ([doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4682-05.2006](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4682-05.2006)); the hunch names it "Aron et
  al. 2006", and Crossref lists two authors. LeDoux and Brown 2017

## C9. Coincidence detection as a learned prior

- **Question**: does a unit whose structure is innate (it fires only when a few specified inputs are active together)
  and whose inputs are learned beat additive summation on a task that needs a conjunction?
- **Method**: a probe where the right response needs two cues at once and either alone is a lure. Compare a Program
  with innate coincidence units whose inputs are learned against one with additive units of the same parameter count
  and the same learning rule.
- **Baselines and controls**: a hand-wired coincidence unit, inputs set by hand - the lower bound of the 95% interval
  of its accuracy over lives must be at least 90% on the conjunction probe, or the probe cannot be solved and the run
  is void. A probe that needs only one cue, where the two Programs should tie (the advantage is the conjunction's, not
  the unit count's).
- **Refuted if**: (1) on the conjunction probe the coincidence Program's accuracy is shown not to exceed the additive
  one's by 5 percentage points - the one-sided interval of the difference lies wholly below 5 points; it passes if a
  one-sided Welch t test at alpha 0.05 finds it higher; (2) on the single-cue probe their accuracies differ by more
  than 5 percentage points - the difference's one-sided interval wholly beyond 5 points either way refutes, its 90%
  interval wholly inside plus or minus 5 points passes. Two refuting tests, each at 0.05/2; the 5-point margins are
  fixed here; anything else is inconclusive.
- **Statistics**: accuracy per life; the tests above.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per condition.
- **Cost**: two Programs on two probes and the hand-wired control, 100 lives; the probe can first be run off the Grid
  as a unit test.
- **Waits on**: a learning module, and two cues the creature can sense together.
- **Leads**: none named.

## C10. Many proposals, one commitment per cycle

- **Question**: does committing exactly one action per cycle, from candidates elaborated in parallel and reversibly,
  with a stop on quiescence or a fixed budget, a falling confidence bound, priority as salience times relevance, a tax
  on losers and a floor against starvation, avoid rumination by the deadline alone?
- **Method**: log every step of every cycle - the candidates, their salience, relevance and priority, the bound, the
  commitment. Offline, run a detector for cycling content over the logs (the same candidate set recurring three cycles
  running without a commitment changing it); it reads only the logs, never reaches the Program, and so is not a
  monitor inside it. Count how long each candidate waits. Snapshot the Program's state before and after elaborating a
  candidate that is not committed. Present situations with two near-equal candidates, which invite rumination. From
  the logs, also check the hunch's remaining parts: that each cycle ends either on quiescence (no candidate's priority
  changing by more than a tolerance fixed with the Program) or at its budget, never past it; and that every logged
  priority follows the one formula the Program states: salience times relevance, minus the stated tax for each cycle a
  candidate has lost since it was proposed.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program without the deadline - the detector must fire in at least one of its 20
  lives, or it cannot see rumination and the run is void; without the starvation floor - a candidate must wait longer
  than the floor in at least one of its 20 lives, or the probe cannot starve and the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: with everything on, any cycle ends with other than exactly one commitment; the detector fires in any
  life; any candidate waits longer than the floor; an uncommitted elaboration leaves the state changed (the snapshot
  diff of [B.md § B8](B.md#b8-a-simulated-body-can-be-rolled-back) is not empty); a logged priority differs from that
  formula, recomputed from the logged inputs with the same operations in the same order as the Program's own code, so
  that the comparison is bit-exact and rounding cannot fire it; a cycle runs past its budget or ends neither on
  quiescence nor at its budget; or the bound is shown not to fall within a cycle - the 95% interval of Spearman's rho
  between elapsed time and the bound lies wholly above -0.3, a margin fixed here. All but the last are exact; the last
  is the plan's one statistical refuting test, at alpha 0.05, and passes if the interval lies wholly below zero,
  inconclusive otherwise.
- **Statistics**: exact counts over every cycle of every life; Spearman's rho with a bootstrap interval.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per condition.
- **Cost**: three conditions, 60 lives, and the logs.
- **Waits on**: a deliberating Program; its cycle length matched to the worm's decision rhythm (the pace note).
- **Leads**: none named.

## C11. Imagination through the action pathway

- **Question**: does simulating candidate actions through the action pathway, motor output gated off, in a few noisy
  sweeps, make decision latency grow with the number of sweeps and with novelty - at the worm's pace, and earning its
  place?
- **Method**: vary the number of sweeps allowed (1, 2, 4, 8) and the novelty of the situation (prior encounters with
  its configuration: 0, 1, 3, 10); measure decision latency in ticks and decision accuracy. Sweeps are varied at a
  novelty of one prior encounter, and novelty at four sweeps. The two scans share one condition (four sweeps, novelty
  one), so they hold seven distinct conditions. Clause 3 takes its latencies from four sweeps at the novelty level
  that matches the reference recordings' conditions, fixed in the journal before the run - an extra condition unless
  that level is one; clause 4 compares four sweeps with the Program without sweeps, at that same novelty.
- **Baselines and controls**: a Program that decides without sweeps, at the same compute per simulated second (the
  baseline for accuracy); a sweep with the motor gate deliberately left open, which must move the body, or the gate is
  not where the plan thinks it is and the run is void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) latency is shown not to rise with the number of sweeps - the one-sided interval of Spearman's
  rho lies wholly below 0.3; (2) likewise with novelty; (3) the latency distribution fails the per-feature comparison
  against the worm's decision latencies, from the pace note (*The animal's pace*: imagining faster or slower than the
  worm decides is refuted whatever its accuracy), passing if it passes; (4) the Program with sweeps is shown not to be
  more accurate than the one without by 5 percentage points - the one-sided interval of the difference lies wholly
  below 5 points (*Lean brains*). Four refuting tests, each at 0.05/4; the margins are fixed here; clauses 1, 2 and 4
  pass if their one-sided 95% intervals lie wholly on the predicted side of zero; anything else is inconclusive.
  Separately and exactly: a gated sweep moves the body.
- **Statistics**: Spearman's rho and the accuracy difference with bootstrap intervals; the per-feature comparison.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 per condition.
- **Cost**: the seven conditions of the two scans, four sweeps at the recordings' novelty if it is not one, the
  Program without sweeps, and the open gate - nine or ten conditions, 180 or 200 lives.
- **Waits on**: a Program with an action pathway that can be gated, and the worm's decision latencies (the pace note).
  Whether the worm's own latencies grow with novelty is not known to the lab; if a source shows they do not, test 2 is
  replaced by its opposite, written in the journal before any run.
- **Leads**: found - Johnson and Redish 2007
  ([doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3761-07.2007](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3761-07.2007)); Battaglia, Hamrick and
  Tenenbaum 2013 (*PNAS*, [doi:10.1073/pnas.1306572110](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1306572110)).

## C12. A step clock from brain rhythms

- **Question**: do the worm's own decision latencies cluster at multiples of a cycle - and if they do, does a Program
  with a step clock of that cycle, its budgets counted in cycles, match them better than one without?
- **Method**: first the worm. Take decision latencies from the reference recordings (the time from a run's start to
  the next reversal) and, for each period in a grid fixed now - 0.1 s to 1.0 s in steps of 0.02 s, which brackets the
  mammalian theta cycle the hunch borrows (commonly given as about 4-8 Hz, 125-250 ms; to be checked against the
  leads, not yet read) and stays at least three frames of a recording at 30 frames per second - compute the Rayleigh
  statistic of the latencies taken modulo the period. The hunch's "ignition a little later" is a fixed offset, which
  the Rayleigh test's free phase absorbs. Its "handful of gamma packets" inside each cycle cannot be seen this way: a
  gamma period (commonly given as tens of milliseconds) is shorter than a frame at 30 frames per second, so that part
  is not tested on the worm's recordings, and is said here rather than dropped. The rhythms of the leads are
  mammalian; whether a worm has anything like them is for the data to say, and *The animal's pace* forbids giving a
  worm a rhythm it lacks. Only if the worm's latencies cluster is a step clock of the found period built, and its
  latencies compared with the worm's.
- **Baselines and controls**: 1,000 surrogate sets of latencies, drawn from a smooth density fitted to the worm's (a
  kernel estimate in linear time, reflected at zero so the density stays zero below zero and the hard edge at zero is
  kept, with a bandwidth of 1.2 s, wider than the largest period scanned) and rounded to the recording's frames like
  the real ones, so frame quantisation cannot pass for a rhythm. The surrogates must reproduce the data outside the
  scan, where no rhythm is sought: over periods of 1.5 s to 3.0 s in steps of 0.02 s, the data's maximum Rayleigh
  statistic must lie within the central 95% of the surrogates' maxima over the same band, or the surrogates
  misdescribe the data and the run is void; the maximum Rayleigh statistic over all periods is compared with the
  surrogates' maxima, which corrects for the number of periods scanned. A planted rhythm: 400 surrogate sets into
  which a known period is mixed, at a strength fixed before the scan - it must be found within one grid step (0.02 s)
  of its period in at least 396 of the 400, or the method is too weak to refute and the run is void. A refutation by
  non-detection is wrong as often as a rhythm of that strength is missed; with at most 4 misses in 400 the one-sided
  95% upper bound on the miss rate is about 0.023 (Clopper-Pearson; the command is in the journal), below the plan's
  0.05/2 per refuting test. A Program without the clock.
- **Refuted if**, by two refuting tests: the worm's maximum Rayleigh statistic is not above that of 95% of the
  surrogates - given that the planted control passed, this refutes clustering at least as strong as the planted
  rhythm, not every weaker one, and says so; the hunch then does not apply to the worm at that strength, and the clock
  is not built for it, a result kept for later rungs. Or, where the latencies cluster, the clocked Program's latency
  distribution is shown not to be closer to the worm's than the unclocked one's by 0.02 - the one-sided 97.5% interval
  of the clocked Program's Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance to the worm's minus the unclocked one's lies wholly above
  -0.02, a margin fixed here; it passes if the one-sided 95% interval lies wholly below zero, and is inconclusive
  otherwise. The worm's clustering passes when its maximum is above 95% of the surrogates' maxima and it shows at more
  than one multiple of the period. The second condition is needed because the surrogates' wide kernel erases any
  narrow peak, rhythmic or not, and a single narrow peak, wherever it lies, makes latencies cluster at every period.
  So, at the period and phase found, fix windows from the data - a quarter period on either side of each multiple of
  the period, from the first multiple up to the data's 95th percentile of latency - and impose the same windows on
  every surrogate set. Each planted set plays the data's part: it runs its own scan, as the planted rhythm must to be
  found within one grid step, and gets its own period, phase and windows, with the surrogates' counts taken in those
  windows; given the data's windows instead, the planted single peak could not fail and the planted rhythm could fail
  for no reason. In each window, standardise each set's count by the mean and standard deviation of the surrogates'
  counts there (each surrogate against the other 999), and take each set's second-largest standardised excess over the
  windows; a window whose surrogate counts have a standard deviation below one count uses one count, a floor fixed
  here, so that no window divides by zero. A rhythm needs the data's second-largest excess above 95% of the
  surrogates' - one statistic against its own distribution, so no correction over windows is needed, and 1,000
  surrogates resolve it; clustering carried by one window only is one peak, not a rhythm, and counts as not found. If
  there are fewer than two windows below the data's 95th percentile of latency, there is no second-largest excess;
  that outcome reflects the resolution, not the worm, and is inconclusive, not a refutation. A planted set with fewer
  than two windows counts against its control: as a miss for the planted rhythm, and as found for the planted single
  peak. The phase is chosen where the data's latencies concentrate, which inflates the data's window excesses a
  little; the window rule alone is therefore slightly lenient towards finding a rhythm, but it is joined to the
  calibrated Rayleigh test, which keeps the false-positive rate at or below 5%, and the planted single peak goes
  through the same selection, so it measures the rule's real rejection rate. The data, the surrogates and every
  planted set go through the same rule. Two narrow peaks whose separation fits a period in the grid would still pass
  as a rhythm of that period; at this resolution a rhythm of two beats cannot be told from two peaks, and that gap is
  said here rather than hidden. A second planted control shows the rule can fail: 400 surrogate sets into which a
  single narrow peak is mixed, with no rhythm - a Gaussian bump with a standard deviation of 0.05 s holding 10% of the
  latencies, at a latency drawn uniformly from 0.5 s to 5 s, both fixed here - must be called not found in at least
  396 of the 400, or the run is void. The planted rhythm goes through the same rule, so its count of 396 in 400 bounds
  the miss rate of the procedure as a whole.
- **Statistics**: the Rayleigh statistic per period, its maximum against the surrogates' maxima; the window counts,
  standardised against the surrogates, and their second-largest excess; the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distances with a
  bootstrap interval.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: every latency in the reference recordings, 1,000 surrogates, 400 planted rhythms and 400
  planted single peaks; 20 lives per Program.
- **Cost**: an analysis of the recordings first; 40 lives only if it is worth building.
- **Waits on**: the feature tool (item 2) and the reference recordings; their frame rate is read from the recordings
  and, if it is not 30 per second, the lower end of the period grid moves to three frames, written down before the
  scan.
- **Leads**: found - Buzsáki 2006, *Rhythms of the Brain*
  ([doi:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195301069.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195301069.001.0001)); Lisman
  and Jensen 2013 ([doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2013.03.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.03.007)).
