# Plans for D1-D6: Chemistry and Affect, First Half

Experiment plans for hunches D1 to D6 of [Matej's ideas and hunches](../matej-hunches.md), in the format of
[README.md § The Format](README.md#the-format). They follow the conventions set out at the head of
[C1-C12.md](C1-C12.md) - every module with a switch that pins it at baseline, every timescale matched to one measured
in the worm and cited, the decision between *refuted*, *passed* and *inconclusive* by the rule at the head of
[B.md](B.md), the life as the unit of analysis, costs counted in simulated lives until the arena is proven - and do
not repeat them. Section D is about modulators, the slow chemical states of
[H8](../README.md#h8-brain-chemistry-as-modelled-substances) and [H3](../README.md#h3-a-slow-internal-state-that-reshapes-everything-else),
so nearly every plan here waits on food and a sense of taking it in (STATE.md, *Needs From Other Repositories*): a
drive of hunger has nothing to track in a world without food.

Where a clause predicts only a direction - a module helps, an ablation hurts - its smallest effect of interest is
zero: it refutes when the interval lies wholly on the wrong side of zero. That is the margin
[C1-C12.md](C1-C12.md) uses for such clauses, and it has a cost: a module with truly no effect is then refuted only
when chance puts its interval wholly on the wrong side, and is otherwise left inconclusive, never passed. Margins other
than zero are named in each clause and marked as fixed here.

The leads were looked up in Crossref by title on 2026-10-31. Found means the work exists as named, with the DOI given.
For the five worm papers, the abstract was read in Europe PMC on the same date and nothing more, and a plan says what
it takes from an abstract. No paper was read in full, and no number is taken from any of them yet: each number a plan
needs is named and read out of its source when the experiment is prepared.

## D1. Roaming and dwelling from continuous drives

- **Question**: does a continuous hunger drive that scales reflex parameters - speed, reversal rate, steering gain -
  produce roaming and dwelling bouts whose statistics match the worm's at least as well as the same Program with a
  discrete two-state switch, and does it follow the worm when food is taken away?
- **Method**: two Programs that share every part but the drive. In the *graded* one, a hunger variable rises with
  time off food and falls with intake, with time constants matched to measured ones at preparation, and scales the
  three parameters through bounded monotone maps. In the *switched* one, the same variable crosses two thresholds with
  hysteresis and switches the three parameters between two fixed sets, chosen so that each set equals the graded maps'
  mean over the bouts it stands for. Roaming and dwelling are labelled from the track alone, by the rule used for the
  worm in [Ben Arous, Laffont and Chatenay 2009](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0007584), whose abstract
  describes only "a new quantitative method"; the rule is read from their methods and fixed at preparation, before
  any life is scored. Three conditions per life: steady food at two densities, and food removed at a fixed time.
- **Baselines and controls**: the switched Program is the baseline; a third arm pins the drive at its mean (no
  state at all), which must show neither bouts nor a response to food removal - if it shows bouts, the labeller is
  finding states in noise and the run is void. The harness control: a Program that switches state on a hidden clock,
  by construction, must have its bouts recovered by the labeller with their known lifetimes, within 10% (a margin
  fixed here).
- **Refuted if**: (1) the graded Program's distance to the worm over the measures - roaming-bout lifetime, the share
  of time dwelling, the latency to roam after food is removed - is larger than the switched Program's, by a paired
  interval over lives wholly above zero; (2) the graded Program shows no two-state structure: the share of its lives
  in which the labeller's two-state model beats one state, by the criterion fixed at preparation, has a Wilson
  interval wholly below 0.5, a margin fixed here. Two refuting tests, alpha 0.05/2 each. The two food densities are a
  condition only if the worm's dwelling share depends on density in the sources (Ben Arous et al.'s abstract, that
  the two states are "tightly controlled by the concentration of food", implies it but does not state it); if the
  numbers read at preparation show no dependence, the condition is dropped before any life runs.
- **Statistics**: per-life measures; distances to the worm's distributions (Kolmogorov-Smirnov for the bout lifetimes,
  absolute difference for the shares and latencies, normalised by the worm's spread); paired differences between the
  arms by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per arm and condition, the default.
- **Cost**: one state variable and three multiplies per tick - negligible.
- **Waits on**: food and a sense of intake; the reference statistics of roaming and dwelling, read from the sources
  at preparation (the lab's reference recordings are of worms on food but, at 15 minutes, may be too short for
  bout lifetimes that run to minutes - checked at preparation); the toy's graded hunger gain (journal of 2026-10-30,
  [§ Grading or Smaller Turns, With the Constant Matched](../../journal/2026-10-30.md#grading-or-smaller-turns-with-the-constant-matched))
  is a related result in the gr-fec toy only and decides nothing here. The hunch's mutant-like manipulations are left
  for a later plan: Fujiwara et al.'s case is a sensory defect, which needs the Program's food sense to have a
  switch, and is added once it has.
- **Leads**: Ben Arous, Laffont and Chatenay, "Molecular and sensory basis of a food related two-state behavior in
  *C. elegans*", *PLoS ONE*, 2009 ([doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0007584](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0007584)) -
  found, abstract read: the two states are controlled by food concentration, and the share of time in each is
  modulated by past nutrition. Fujiwara, Sengupta and McIntire, "Regulation of body size and behavioral state of
  *C. elegans* by sensory perception and the EGL-4 cGMP-dependent protein kinase", *Neuron*, 2002
  ([doi:10.1016/S0896-6273(02)01093-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0896-6273(02)01093-0)) - found, abstract read; it
  supplies a mutant-like manipulation (sensory defects alter behavioural state), not a number. Flavell et al. 2013 and
  Sawin et al. 2000 are cited and verified in [README.md § H8](../README.md#h8-brain-chemistry-as-modelled-substances).

## D2. Modulators that flip a smell's meaning

- **Question**: can a Program with opposing modulators driven by feeding state reproduce a valence flip - a cue
  avoided when fed and approached when starved - from internal state alone, with the same senses and the same
  wiring?
- **Method**: the worm's best-documented case is carbon dioxide: by the abstract of
  [Rengarajan et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1807454116), the response shifts from aversion to attraction
  during starvation, dopamine promoting repulsion in fed animals and octopamine attraction in starved ones, and
  animals lacking octopamine shift later. A Program with two modulator variables, one rising with intake and one with
  time off food, each a bounded gain on the same cue-to-turn pathway with opposite sign, is lived at several
  starvation times. A cue gradient is needed, and the Grid's chemical sense as designed (tron-grid-lite's
  `docs/CHEMORECEPTION.md`, at the pinned commit) gives odours as unlabelled mixtures over eight receptor types, carried
  by the food orbs, with no gas apart from food. A cue that can be placed away from food is needed, so that approach to
  the cue is not approach to food; if the world cannot place one, that is asked of it as an issue, and the plan waits.
  Whatever cue is used stands in for carbon dioxide, and the write-up says so. A second probe, from the abstract of
  [Ghosh et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.10.030): food deprivation makes the worm more willing to
  cross a hyperosmotic barrier to reach a food odour - the same Program, crossing a noxious band toward a food cue.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program with both modulators pinned at their fed values and at their starved
  values. These are harness controls: a pinned Program has no route from starvation to the cue's sign, so if the
  pinned-at-fed Program's index at the longest starvation and at none have intervals on opposite sides of zero, the
  flip is coming from outside the modulators and the run is void. A second harness control: a Program whose cue sign
  is set directly by a hidden flag must show approach and avoidance by the chemotaxis index, or the cue is not
  reaching it and the run is void. The octopamine-like modulator switched off alone is run and reported as an
  ablation, but it is not a refuting test. With the two gains as described, nothing is left to produce attraction once
  it is off, so the Program would fail by construction; and a slow octopamine-independent route to attraction, which
  the abstract's "delayed shift" implies, would make it pass by construction. Which of the two is built is decided at
  preparation from the paper's own account of the delayed shift, not by this run.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the starved Program does not approach the cue: at the longest starvation the chemotaxis index's
  interval lies wholly at or below zero (the fed Program's avoidance is the pass test's other half, not a refutation);
  (2) barrier crossing does not rise with starvation time, the slope's interval wholly at or below zero. Two refuting
  tests, alpha 0.05/2 each.
- **Statistics**: per-life chemotaxis index (the share of time nearer the cue, the convention fixed from the source's
  methods at preparation); per-life crossing; intervals by bootstrap over lives; the time of the flip by the starvation
  time at which the index crosses zero, interpolated, with its interval.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per starvation time, at four starvation times, the default.
- **Cost**: two state variables and two gains - negligible.
- **Waits on**: food and a sense of intake; smell, and a cue placed apart from food (above); a noxious band in the world
  for the second probe; the starvation times and indices, read from Rengarajan et al. and Ghosh et al. at preparation.
  The hunch's other claims are not tested here:
    - serotonin beginning and extending dwelling, and a neuropeptide roaming, which belong to D1's drive and to H8;
    - dopamine slowing the worm on food, and serotonin slowing a starved worm further (Sawin et al. 2000);
    - octopamine promoting roaming;
    - long starvation bringing a brain-wide quiescent state. That is D's link to
      [Skora, Mende and Zimmer 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2017.12.091), whose abstract reports transient
      arousal after acute food deprivation, and, under long starvation, intermittent sleep episodes during which brain
      dynamics are downregulated, while they stay robust to starvation in waking animals. It is a measure for a later
      plan, once the Program has sleep.
- **Leads**: Rengarajan et al., "Feeding state sculpts a circuit for sensory valence in *Caenorhabditis elegans*",
  *PNAS*, 2019 ([doi:10.1073/pnas.1807454116](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1807454116)) - found, abstract read.
  Ghosh et al., "Neural architecture of hunger-dependent multisensory decision making in *C. elegans*", *Neuron*,
  2016 ([doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2016.10.030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.10.030)) - found, abstract read; the
  hunch lists "Ghosh et al. 2016" without a title, and this is taken to be the paper meant, since its subject is one
  the hunch describes. Skora, Mende and Zimmer, "Energy scarcity promotes a brain-wide sleep state modulated by
  insulin signaling in *C. elegans*", *Cell Reports*, 2018
  ([doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2017.12.091](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2017.12.091)) - found, abstract read. The claim
  that octopamine promotes roaming still has no lead.

## D3. One modulator state, three effects

- **Question**: when one low-dimensional modulator state is moved, do the transition matrix of behavioural states,
  the regime of the settling dynamics and the weighting of the senses move together, as the hunch says, rather than
  each on its own?
- **Method**: a Program in which one modulator variable feeds three places: the transition rates between forward,
  reversal, turn and pause; the time constant of the internal state's settling; and the relative gain of two senses.
  The variable is set by hand to five levels on a frozen copy, each held for a whole life, and the three effects are
  measured from behaviour alone: the transition matrix from the track, the settling time from the decay of the
  response after a step in a cue, and the weighting from the response to the two senses' cues presented in conflict.
  That the Program's three effects move together is true by construction and is checked as a harness property; what
  the plan tests is whether they are *recoverable from behaviour* as one factor, which is what an observer of the worm
  could test.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program with three independent variables, one per effect, moved one at a time.
  Its three effects must not load on one factor, or the analysis finds coupling that is not there and the run is
  void.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the three behavioural measures, across the five levels, are not explained by one factor: the
  first principal component of their standardised changes explains less than 70% of the variance, a margin fixed
  here, with the interval of the explained share wholly below 70%. One refuting test, alpha 0.05. Since the coupling
  is built in, a refutation would refute only that it can be seen in behaviour, in this Program - not the hunch,
  whose claim about the worm is left to the literature (below). A harness test beside it: if the
  independent-variables control also gives one factor, its interval wholly above 70%, the method cannot tell one
  factor from three, and the run is void. Whether one state of the worm moves all three together is a question for
  the literature, read at preparation.
- **Statistics**: principal components over the per-level means; interval of the explained share by bootstrap over
  lives within each level.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per level, five levels, the default.
- **Cost**: one state variable feeding three gains - negligible.
- **Waits on**: two senses that can be set in conflict (food, smell and touch are on the way; see STATE.md, *Needs
  From Other Repositories*); a behavioural measure of settling time, defined at preparation.
- **Leads**: the hunch gives none. It sharpens H3's slow internal state.

## D4. Learning rate from uncertainty; surprise resets the current thought

- **Question**: does a Program whose learning rate is set by an expected-uncertainty signal re-learn faster after a
  reversal in a volatile world than the same Program with the signal pinned, and does a burst on surprise shorten its
  dwell in the current attractor?
- **Method**: a Program with a learned association (a cue that predicts food at a place), whose learning rate is
  scaled by a running estimate of how often the association has changed, in the spirit of
  [Yu and Dayan 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.04.026), and a burst variable that, on a large prediction
  error, perturbs the current internal state. Lives in two worlds: one where the cue-food pairing is reversed every
  few minutes (volatile), one where it is reversed once, late in the life (stable), with the reversal period matched to
  worm learning times read at preparation. Learning speed is the time to re-reach a fixed preference after a reversal,
  measured from behaviour in both worlds; dwell is the time the internal state stays in one attractor, measured from
  behaviour as the run length of a behavioural state.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program with the uncertainty signal pinned (a fixed learning rate equal to its
  mean) and with the burst switched off, each alone and both. The harness control: a Program given the reversal
  times by a hidden channel, by construction, must learn fastest; if it does not, the reversal is not reaching the
  measure and the run is void. A harness property, not a test: the internal learning rate must be higher in the
  volatile world than the stable one, as it is by construction.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the time to re-learn after a reversal in the volatile world is not shorter with the uncertainty
  signal than with it pinned, the paired interval wholly at or above zero; (2) switching the burst off does not
  lengthen the dwell after a surprise, the paired interval wholly at or below zero. Two refuting tests, alpha 0.05/2
  each. The stable world's single late reversal gives the behavioural comparison between the worlds, reported beside
  them. Whether the worm itself learns faster in a volatile world is not known to the lab; it is searched for at
  preparation, and without it the plan tests the mechanism, not its fidelity to the worm. The hunch's tonic
  noradrenaline setting the balance of exploring and exploiting, and its split of dopamine into tonic wanting and
  phasic prediction error, are not tested here.
- **Statistics**: per-life re-learning times and dwell times; paired differences by bootstrap over lives.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per arm per world, the default.
- **Cost**: two state variables and a multiply - negligible.
- **Waits on**: food and smell, for the cue; a measured time course of associative learning in the worm, at
  preparation; a learning module (E's plans).
- **Leads**: Yu and Dayan, "Uncertainty, neuromodulation, and attention", *Neuron*, 2005
  ([doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2005.04.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.04.026)) - found. Aston-Jones and Cohen,
  "An integrative theory of locus coeruleus-norepinephrine function: adaptive gain and optimal performance", *Annual
  Review of Neuroscience*, 2005
  ([doi:10.1146/annurev.neuro.28.061604.135709](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.neuro.28.061604.135709)) - found.
  Berridge, "The debate over dopamine's role in reward: the case for incentive salience",
  *Psychopharmacology* ([doi:10.1007/s00213-006-0578-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-006-0578-x)) - found; Crossref
  lists it online in 2006 and in print in 2007, as the hunch dates it. All three are about mammals; none says the worm
  works this way.

## D5. A modulator design law

- **Question**: is each modulator the lab builds identifiable - does removing it worsen its own probe and improve no
  other - or do many configurations pass the battery equally, as the hunch warns?
- **Method**: not an experiment on one Program but a rule applied to every Program with modulators, D1 to D4's among
  them. Each modulator is registered before it runs with its input, its one or two knobs, its probe, its predicted
  effect and its forbidden outcome. Each is then switched off in turn, on 20 lives per switch, and every probe is
  measured. A second test, after [Prinz, Bucher and Marder 2004](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1352): the modulators'
  gains are drawn at random over their registered ranges, and each draw is scored on the whole battery with the
  battery's own 20 lives, so that a draw fails for its parameters and not for want of power.
- **Baselines and controls**: the full Program. The harness control: a planted modulator with no effect at all must
  worsen no probe; if it does, the probes are noisier than the effects and the run is void.
- **Refuted if** (for each modulator; the hunch is the law, and a modulator that breaks it is refuted, not the law):
  (1) removing it does not worsen its own probe, the paired interval wholly at or below zero; (2) removing it
  improves another probe, the paired interval wholly above zero. With m modulators and their probes, 2m refuting tests,
  alpha 0.05/(2m) each. The law itself is judged by the second test, against a share of 0.5 fixed here: if the Wilson
  interval of the share of random draws that pass lies wholly above 0.5, the battery is too weak to support the claim
  that the modulators are what they are named, and that is recorded as a refutation of the battery; wholly below, the
  battery pins them down; otherwise inconclusive.
- **Statistics**: paired differences per probe by bootstrap over lives; the share of passing draws with a Wilson
  interval.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per switch; 200 random draws of 20 lives each for the second test, sized so that
  the Wilson interval on the passing share has a half-width of about 0.07 at a share of 0.5.
- **Cost**: m + 1 times the lives of one Program, plus 4000 lives for the draws - the largest cost in section D,
  affordable only once a life is cheap.
- **Waits on**: at least two modulators with probes - D1 and D2's Programs.
- **Leads**: Doya, "Metalearning and neuromodulation", *Neural Networks*, 2002
  ([doi:10.1016/S0893-6080(02)00044-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0893-6080(02)00044-8)) - found. Dayan, "Twenty-five
  lessons from computational neuromodulation", *Neuron*, 2012
  ([doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2012.09.027](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2012.09.027)) - found. Prinz, Bucher and Marder,
  "Similar network activity from disparate circuit parameters", *Nature Neuroscience*, 2004
  ([doi:10.1038/nn1352](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1352)) - found.

## D6. Receptors that tire, ten times faster than they recover

- **Question**: in the worm, does the response to a repeated identical reward or cue fade faster than it recovers,
  and by what ratio - and does a Program with receptor-like desensitisation at that ratio match the fading and the
  recovery after a gap?
- **Method**: first the number, which the hunch says has no source: the ratio of the time constant of recovery to that
  of desensitisation is searched for in the worm's habituation and sensory adaptation literature at preparation (the
  pace note lists tap-habituation time constants as a gap, [pace.md § Gaps, to Be
  Read](../../research/pace.md#gaps-to-be-read)). If a source gives it with an interval, it is used; if a source gives
  it without one, or none gives it, the ratio is fitted to the worm's habituation and recovery curves, with an
  interval, and a source's bare value is reported beside the fit. Then a Program with one desensitisation variable per
  receptor, falling with use at one rate and recovering at the other, is given trains of identical stimuli at the
  intervals the sources use, followed by gaps of several lengths.
- **Baselines and controls**: the same Program with desensitisation switched off (no fading, which must fail to
  habituate), and with equal rates (ratio one). The harness control: a train of stimuli of zero strength must produce
  no fading.
- **Refuted if**: (1) the worm's ratio, from a source's interval or the fit's, has an interval lying wholly outside
  the band 5 to 20, a band around the hunch's tenfold fixed here; (2) the Program at the worm's ratio is no closer to
  the worm's fading and recovery curves than at ratio one, the paired interval wholly at or above zero. Two refuting
  tests, alpha 0.05/2 each. If no curves can be found to fit, clause (1) is inconclusive and says so. The hunch's
  claim that sleep restores sensitivity is not tested here; it waits on a Program that sleeps.
- **Statistics**: per-life fading and recovery curves; fitted time constants with intervals by bootstrap over lives;
  distance to the worm's curves.
- **Seeds and repetitions**: 20 lives per arm, the default.
- **Cost**: one variable per receptor - negligible.
- **Waits on**: touch, for the tap-habituation case, or a smell whose repeated pulses the world can deliver; the worm's
  habituation and recovery curves from the literature.
- **Leads**: the hunch gives none, and none is added here: the search for the ratio is the first step of preparation.
  It sharpens H8's tachyphylaxis.
