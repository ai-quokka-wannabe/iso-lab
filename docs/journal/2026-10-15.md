- **What was read**, at the source, from public repositories cloned shallow into the session's scratch space and
  read, not run: the OpenWorm analysis toolbox
  ([open-worm-analysis-toolbox](https://github.com/openworm/open-worm-analysis-toolbox), `main` at `181ab13`) and
  Tierpsy ([tierpsy-tracker](https://github.com/ver228/tierpsy-tracker), `main` at `1298df8`), which carries its own
  copy of the toolbox with the same values. The toolbox commit the records name in their provenance, `95c2879f`, was

  Tierpsy's name table, `tierpsy/extras/features_names.csv`, maps the
  column `midbody_speed` to the toolbox's `locomotion.velocity.midbody.speed`, in micrometres per second. In the
  toolbox (`features/velocity.py`, `compute_speed`; the partitions in `prefeatures/basic_worm.py`; the options in
  `features/feature_processing_options.py`):
    - the speed is the distance moved by the centroid of skeleton points 21 to 29 of 49, counted from the head (indices 20 to 28; the `old_midbody_velocity`
      partition, used because `mimic_old_behaviour` is on by default) between two frames about 0.5 s apart
      (`velocity_body_diff = 0.5`; the window is `2 round(0.25 fps) + 1` frames, centred), divided by the time between;
    - where a window's end has no skeleton, that end moves outwards, up to twice as far, so a frame without a skeleton
      of its own still has a speed when both ends find one;
    - the sign is negative when the direction of that motion lies more than 90 degrees from the body's angle at the
      window's first frame, the body's angle being the mean direction of points 9 to 41 (indices 8 to 40), taken tail to head.
- **The check, fixed before it ran** (in the night's working notes; not committed separately before the run):
  re-implemented from that source in a short Python script (given in full in the pull request's description) and
  applied to the skeletons stored in the features files, the reading was to count as confirmed if the correlation
  with the published column was at least 0.99 and the slope between 0.98 and 1.02, in both of records 1007405 - the
  one the lab's measure failed - and 1024910, each fetched again from Zenodo and MD5-checked (Python 3.13.16, h5py
  3.16.0, numpy 2.5.4).
- **What came out**: confirmed, and exactly - in both records a correlation of 1.00000 and a slope of 1.00000, a
  median absolute difference of 0.0003 um/s, and the same frames defined (99.94% and 99.75% of frames); the largest
  difference is 0.002 um/s in 1024910 and, at a single frame of 1007405, 9.1 um/s, a flip of sign: there the motion
  lies 90.0003 degrees from the body's angle, so recomputing in double precision from stored single-precision points
  can land on the other side of the threshold (found in the pull request's verification). So, in the two records
  checked, the published speed is the toolbox's, computed from the very skeletons the files store.
- **What it means for the lab's measure**: the lab's eight-arc speed differs from it in kind, not only in resolution.
  It projects the midbody's displacement on the body's direction, where the published speed takes the whole
  distance and only signs it, and it takes the direction from the middle quarter of the body at the window's centre,
  where the published speed uses most of the body at the window's start. Those choices may part most where the body
  bends most, which would fit 1007405's failing the check; that is a reading, neither measured here nor tested. Which definition
  the benchmark should use is a choice to be made on what the Grid's worm can report, not by agreement alone; the
  next step is a version of the lab's measure with the published speed's choices at the eight-arc resolution, and
  the check run again on it.
- **What it means for the bends beyond the joint limit, an unexpected finding**: in Tierpsy's feature code
  (`tierpsy/analysis/feat_create/obtainFeatures.py` and `obtainFeaturesHelper.py`), the skeletons written to
  `/coordinates/skeletons` are the ones the features are computed from, after a Savitzky-Golay filter (a window of 5,
  a cubic) and a correction for the stage's motion. The filter runs **along each skeleton's length**, not along time:
  `_h_smooth_curve_all` loops over the frames and filters each frame's 49 points as a curve, with SciPy's default edge
  mode, which fits the end windows too, so the tips are filtered as well. The dataset's own commit, `cbfc23eb4f1a`
  (by record 1011832's provenance; read from a blobless clone of `ver228/tierpsy-tracker`), has the same filter, the
  same call with `smooth_window=5` and the same order of operations as the current master `1298df8`; the exact
  reproduction above shows the stored skeletons are the ones the features used, which fits. So every skeleton the
  lab has measured was already smoothed lightly along its length, over 5 of 49 points: the bends of 2026-10-14
  (journal, *The Bends Beyond the Joint Limit*) were measured on filtered skeletons, the raw outlines may bend more
  sharply than that, and the lab's own five-point smoothing check repeated a smoothing of the same kind. The
  averaging over frames is not touched by this: the stored skeletons are not smoothed in time. A first draft of this
  entry said they were, from a misreading of which axis the loop runs over; the code at both commits says otherwise.

## The Midbody Speed With the Published Choices, at Eight Arcs

STATE.md item 2(c): the published midbody speed is now read and reproduced (above), and it differs from the lab's
eight-arc speed in kind. This entry builds the published speed's choices at the eight-arc resolution and runs the
check of 2026-10-14 again on it. Written before any of it runs.

- **The question**: is the eight-arc speed's failure in record 1007405 (journal of 2026-10-14, *A Midbody Speed*) a
  matter of the definition's choices, which can be copied, or of the eight-arc resolution itself, which the Grid's
  worm imposes?
- **The measure**, `features::locomotion`, beside the existing one and not replacing it: at each frame, the
  midbody is the middle end of the eight arcs (the toolbox's centroid of points 21 to 29 of 49 lies about it); the
  window has `round(0.25 fps)` frames on each side, and an end with no eight arcs moves outwards, up to twice as far,
  as the toolbox's does; the speed is the whole distance the midbody moves between the window's ends, divided by the
  time between them, negative when that motion makes an angle of more than 90 degrees with the body's direction at the
  window's first end. The body's direction is the chord from the end seven eighths along to the end one eighth along,
  tail to head: the toolbox takes the direction from point 41 to point 9 of 49 (the mean of the steps between them),
  five sixths to one sixth of the length, and the nearest ends of eight arcs are seven and one eighths. An end
  whose eight arcs have no direction there (the two ends meeting) counts as having no eight arcs.
- **The check, the same bar as before**: over the frames where both speeds exist, the measure passes if in every
  record the Pearson correlation with the published `midbody_speed` is at least 0.95 and the slope of a least-squares
  line through the origin, the lab's on the published, lies between 0.9 and 1.1. A failing record is reported as
  failing. The records are the seventeen of 2026-10-14, fetched again and MD5-checked; all seventeen count this time,
  1024910 included, since no parameter of this measure was fitted to any record (the span and the window come from
  the toolbox's code). The old measure is run beside it on the same files, so the two are compared within one run;
  sign agreement and the median absolute difference are reported, not tested.
- **What would refute the hunch that the choices are the cause**: 1007405 failing again with the published choices,
  or the correlations not rising in most records. Either would put the departure in the resolution, and the next
  step would be the check's bar set from what moves a locomotion state.
- **The run**: `jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace notebooks/midbody_speed_published_choices.ipynb`
  at 12:15 UTC, from a fresh kernel, at commit `ef444b6` (the notebook's printing then widened to four decimals and
  run again the same way, the three-decimal figures unchanged), rustc 1.98.0, h5py 3.16.0, numpy 2.5.4 (2.5.3 on
  2026-10-14), on the seventeen records fetched again by `fetch-block 1` and MD5-checked; deterministic, no seed.
- **What came out**: **the check fails again, in the same record and in no other.** Record 1007405 has a correlation
  of 0.9499 with the published speed, below the 0.95 fixed beforehand, and a slope of 0.947, inside the band; the
  other sixteen pass. Over the seventeen the medians are a correlation of 0.982, a slope of 1.012, sign agreement of
  0.891 and a median absolute difference of 11.9 µm/s; the lowest correlations after 1007405's are 0.957 (1030984)
  and 0.961 (1025397). The measure of 2026-10-14, run beside it, repeats its own earlier figures for 1007405 exactly
  (0.947, 0.930, 26,337 frames), a check that the files and the code are what they were, and has medians of 0.979,
  0.998, 0.882 and 12.1 µm/s. With the published choices the correlation is higher in 16 of the 17 records, but by
  little (in 1007405 from 0.947 to 0.950), and over more frames: with the window's ends moving outwards past a
  missing frame, the new measure is defined on exactly the frames the published speed is, in all seventeen records
  (an exploratory count afterwards, `python3 -I coverage.py <records>`, given in the pull request), where the first
  measure misses some (26,337 frames against 26,982 in 1007405). So the two are not compared over the same frames,
  and the small rise is not attributed to any one choice; the identical frames do support the window's logic. The
  pull request's verification then compared the two over the frames both define (`python3 -I common.py <records>`,
  in the pull request, re-run by the author): there the published choices change the correlation by at most 0.004,
  and in 1007405 from 0.94691 to 0.94695, so the rise comes almost wholly from the extra frames, not from the choices.
- **What it means**: by the rule written before the run, the hunch that the definition's choices are the cause is
  refuted: copying them moves 1007405 by 0.003, almost all of it from the extra frames, and does not bring it over
  the bar. What is left lies in what the eight arcs themselves change - the midbody as the middle end by arc length
  rather than the centroid of nine points, and the body's direction as the chord between the ends one and seven
  eighths along by arc length rather than between points 9 and 41 by index (the toolbox's mean of the steps between
  those points is that chord too; a first draft of this entry said 33 points, wrongly) - since the 49-point version of
  the same speed agrees with the published one exactly (above). Two small departures from the toolbox remain, written
  in the code: a frame whose two direction ends meet is skipped as a window's end, where the toolbox keeps it with an
  angle of zero; and the window is never narrower than one frame, which matters only below 2 frames a second. The measure with the
  published choices stays in the code beside the first; neither is yet trusted as the comparison space's speed. The next step is the one this check points to: the
  check's bar set from what the benchmark needs, how far a speed may stray before it moves a locomotion state,
  instead of a round 0.95 - a question for the locomotion states, which are not yet built.

## Which End Is the Head

STATE.md, *Needs from other repositories*, the joint-limit entry: before the issue is drafted, read how the dataset
decides which end of a skeleton is the head, since the excess of bends beyond the Grid's limit was found greatest at
the head (journal of 2026-10-14). Written before the count below runs.

- **What was read**: Tierpsy at the dataset's commit `cbfc23eb` (blobless clone, read, not run). The features files
  record only the last two steps of their processing (`/provenance_tracking`: `FEAT_CREATE` and `WCON_EXPORT`, the
  former at `cbfc23eb`), run on skeletons made earlier; the steps that oriented those skeletons, and their commit,
  are not recorded in the files. The single-worm pipeline (`tierpsy/helper/params/docs_analysis_points.py`, the list
  `WT2`) orients in two steps, `SKE_ORIENT` then `INT_SKE_ORIENT`. Read at `cbfc23eb`, so at best the code of the
  version that ran, or close to it:
    - `SKE_ORIENT` (`tierpsy/analysis/ske_orient/checkHeadOrientation.py`, `isWormHTSwitched`) splits a track into
      blocks of skeletons: consecutive skeletons stay in one block while they are fewer than `max_gap_allowed` frames
      apart (`getBlocksIDs`). For each frame it takes the direction, in the camera's frame, from point 1 to point
      `1 + segment4angle` of 49 at one end and the same at the other, and their standard deviations over a rolling
      window of `window_std` frames. In a block with more than `min_block_size` such values, the end whose direction
      varies more in most frames is taken to be the head (a frame whose rolling deviation is undefined counts for the
      tail); a smaller block takes the orientation of the nearest qualifying block before it, or after it at a track's
      start, and a track with none is left as it is. The pipeline always passes `window_std` and `min_block_size` as
      -1 (`tierpsy/analysis/ske_orient/__init__.py`, `_get_head_tail_param`), which `head_tail_defaults`
      (`tierpsy/helper/params/get_defaults.py`) replaces by one second and ten seconds of frames; `max_gap_allowed`
      and `segment4angle` come from the run's settings, whose defaults are also -1
      (`tierpsy/helper/params/docs_tracker_param.py`), scaled to half a second and to 5 points at 49 - so points 1 to
      6 is the default reading, the settings file itself (`single_worm_on_food.json`, in another repository) not read.
    - `INT_SKE_ORIENT` (`tierpsy/analysis/int_ske_orient/`) first makes blocks consistent with the track's own median
      intensity profile (`correctHeadTailIntWorm`) - a check of consistency, not an identification of the head - and
      then decides the whole track's orientation in `checkFinalOrientation`: `p_tot = 0.75 p_mov + 0.15 p_int_bot +
      0.1 p_int_top`, where `p_mov` is the head's share of the two ends' mean rolling deviations over the track, the
      same movement measure; the track is switched if `p_tot` is below 0.5, and `p_tot` is `p_mov` where the
      intensity terms are undefined. So intensity can tip the decision only when `p_mov` lies between one third and
      two thirds; outside that range movement decides alone, as the code's own comment says.
- **What it means for the bends** (corrected after the pull request's verification, which found the first draft
  vague on the final step and wrong on which parameters come from the settings): both steps name as the head the
  end whose direction varies more, the second with three quarters of the weight. The head's excess of bends beyond
  0.9 rad is therefore built in to an unknown degree: wherever the tail's end varied more and the intensity did not
  overrule it, that end was labelled the head. The movement rule's quantity, the variation in time of the end
  segment's direction in the camera's frame (which includes turns of the whole body), is not the lab's first joint,
  the angle between the chords of arcs 1 and 2, though the two are related. The rule may be right
  most of the time - how often was not looked into here - but the gradient along the body is not independent evidence of where the
  worm bends most. The issue for tron-grid-lite should rest on the share of frames bending beyond the limit at any
  joint, which does not depend on which end is called the head, and should state the gradient only with this caveat.
- **The count, method fixed before it runs**: a check for isolated swaps the orientation steps would leave as
  frame-to-frame flips. Between two consecutive frames that both have a stored skeleton, a flip is counted where the
  skeleton lies closer, by the mean distance between corresponding points, to the previous one reversed than to it
  as it is. Run on the seventeen block-1 records of the speed check, with the script given in the pull request
  (`python3 -I flips.py <files>`). Reported per record: pairs, flips and flips per thousand pairs; no test, no bar.
  A flip in a moving worm is expected to be rare, since half a body length rarely passes in one frame; a worm curled
  so its ends meet can produce one without any swap, so a flip is a candidate swap, not a proven one. Whole blocks
  oriented wrongly do not show as flips except at their edges, so a low count does not rule them out.
- **The run**: `python3 -I flips.py data/raw/owmd-n2/*_features.hdf5` at 12:22 UTC, after the method was pushed at
  12:22:15Z (its second was not recorded), Python 3.13.16, h5py 3.16.0,
  numpy 2.5.4, on the seventeen MD5-checked records; deterministic, no seed.
- **What came out**: **no flip at all**, in any record - 0 of 502,941 pairs of consecutive frames with skeletons
  (5,912 to 177,368 per record).
- **A control, added after the count, since a check that never fires has not shown it can**: the same rule, with
  every thousandth stored skeleton reversed where both its neighbours have skeletons, counts every planted reversal
  twice, into it and out of it - 948 flips for 474 planted, in all seventeen records (`python3 -I flips_check.py
  <files>`, in the pull request). So the count can see a flip, and the stored skeletons hold none from one frame to the
  next.
- **Exploratory, after the count, said plainly**: the zero is close to guaranteed by how Tierpsy orients - whole
  blocks at a time, and blocks split only at gaps (`getBlocksIDs`; the intensity step's own block edges also fall only
  at gaps, `removeBadSkelBlocks`), which the pre-registered method skips. The same comparison made
  across each gap, short gaps inside a block included, from the last skeleton before it to the first after it, finds
  62 flips in 18,105 gaps (0 to 18 per
  record; same script). Each is a candidate change of orientation across a gap; a worm can also turn far during a
  long gap, and the gaps' lengths were not examined, so the 62 are candidates only. Whether a block's orientation is
  right cannot be read from the skeletons alone.
- **What it means for the issue**: isolated swaps are not what inflates the head's bends; the orientation's own
  rule may, block by block, and that is not measured here. The issue for tron-grid-lite should rest on the share of
  frames bending beyond 0.9 rad at any joint (31 to 46%, median 38%, in the twelve records of 2026-10-14), which
  holds whichever end is the head, and give
  the gradient along the body only with this caveat.
