## Which End Is the Head

STATE.md, *Needs from other repositories*, the joint-limit entry: before the issue is drafted, read how the dataset
decides which end of a skeleton is the head, since the excess of bends beyond the Grid's limit was found greatest at
the head (journal of 2026-10-14). Written before the count below runs.

- **What was read**: Tierpsy at the dataset's commit `cbfc23eb` (blobless clone, read, not run). The features files
  record only the last two steps of their processing (`/provenance_tracking`: `FEAT_CREATE` and `WCON_EXPORT`, the
  former at `cbfc23eb`), run on skeletons made earlier; the steps that oriented those skeletons, and their commit,
  are not recorded in the files. The single-worm pipeline (`tierpsy/helper/params/docs_analysis_points.py`, the list
  `WT2`) orients in two steps, `SKE_ORIENT` then `INT_SKE_ORIENT`. Read at `cbfc23eb`, so at best the code of the
  version that ran, or close to it:
    - `SKE_ORIENT` (`tierpsy/analysis/ske_orient/checkHeadOrientation.py`, `isWormHTSwitched`) splits a track into
      blocks of skeletons: consecutive skeletons stay in one block while they are fewer than `max_gap_allowed` frames
      apart (`getBlocksIDs`). For each frame it takes the direction, in the camera's frame, from point 1 to point
      `1 + segment4angle` of 49 at one end and the same at the other, and their standard deviations over a rolling
      window of `window_std` frames. In a block with more than `min_block_size` such values, the end whose direction
      varies more in most frames is taken to be the head (a frame whose rolling deviation is undefined counts for the
      tail); a smaller block takes the orientation of the nearest qualifying block before it, or after it at a track's
      start, and a track with none is left as it is. The pipeline always passes `window_std` and `min_block_size` as
      -1 (`tierpsy/analysis/ske_orient/__init__.py`, `_get_head_tail_param`), which `head_tail_defaults`
      (`tierpsy/helper/params/get_defaults.py`) replaces by one second and ten seconds of frames; `max_gap_allowed`
      and `segment4angle` come from the run's settings, whose defaults are also -1
      (`tierpsy/helper/params/docs_tracker_param.py`), scaled to half a second and to 5 points at 49 - so points 1 to
      6 is the default reading, the settings file itself (`single_worm_on_food.json`, in another repository) not read.
    - `INT_SKE_ORIENT` (`tierpsy/analysis/int_ske_orient/`) first makes blocks consistent with the track's own median
      intensity profile (`correctHeadTailIntWorm`) - a check of consistency, not an identification of the head - and
      then decides the whole track's orientation in `checkFinalOrientation`: `p_tot = 0.75 p_mov + 0.15 p_int_bot +
      0.1 p_int_top`, where `p_mov` is the head's share of the two ends' mean rolling deviations over the track, the
      same movement measure; the track is switched if `p_tot` is below 0.5, and `p_tot` is `p_mov` where the
      intensity terms are undefined. So intensity can tip the decision only when `p_mov` lies between one third and
      two thirds; outside that range movement decides alone, as the code's own comment says.
- **What it means for the bends** (corrected after the pull request's verification, which found the first draft
  vague on the final step and wrong on which parameters come from the settings): both steps name as the head the
  end whose direction varies more, the second with three quarters of the weight. The head's excess of bends beyond
  0.9 rad is therefore built in to an unknown degree: wherever the tail's end varied more and the intensity did not
  overrule it, that end was labelled the head. The movement rule's quantity, the variation in time of the end
  segment's direction in the camera's frame (which includes turns of the whole body), is not the lab's first joint,
  the angle between the chords of arcs 1 and 2, though the two are related. The rule may be right
  most of the time - how often was not looked into here - but the gradient along the body is not independent evidence of where the
  worm bends most. The issue for tron-grid-lite should rest on the share of frames bending beyond the limit at any
  joint, which does not depend on which end is called the head, and should state the gradient only with this caveat.
- **The count, method fixed before it runs**: a check for isolated swaps the orientation steps would leave as
  frame-to-frame flips. Between two consecutive frames that both have a stored skeleton, a flip is counted where the
  skeleton lies closer, by the mean distance between corresponding points, to the previous one reversed than to it
  as it is. Run on the seventeen block-1 records of the speed check, with the script given in the pull request
  (`python3 -I flips.py <files>`). Reported per record: pairs, flips and flips per thousand pairs; no test, no bar.
  A flip in a moving worm is expected to be rare, since half a body length rarely passes in one frame; a worm curled
  so its ends meet can produce one without any swap, so a flip is a candidate swap, not a proven one. Whole blocks
  oriented wrongly do not show as flips except at their edges, so a low count does not rule them out.
- **The run**: `python3 -I flips.py data/raw/owmd-n2/*_features.hdf5` at 12:22 UTC, after the method was pushed at
  12:22:15Z (its second was not recorded), Python 3.13.16, h5py 3.16.0,
  numpy 2.5.4, on the seventeen MD5-checked records; deterministic, no seed.
- **What came out**: **no flip at all**, in any record - 0 of 502,941 pairs of consecutive frames with skeletons
  (5,912 to 177,368 per record).
- **A control, added after the count, since a check that never fires has not shown it can**: the same rule, with
  every thousandth stored skeleton reversed where both its neighbours have skeletons, counts every planted reversal
  twice, into it and out of it - 948 flips for 474 planted, in all seventeen records (`python3 -I flips_check.py
  <files>`, in the pull request). So the count can see a flip, and the stored skeletons hold none from one frame to the
  next.
- **Exploratory, after the count, said plainly**: the zero is close to guaranteed by how Tierpsy orients - whole
  blocks at a time, and blocks split only at gaps (`getBlocksIDs`; the intensity step's own block edges also fall only
  at gaps, `removeBadSkelBlocks`), which the pre-registered method skips. The same comparison made
  across each gap, short gaps inside a block included, from the last skeleton before it to the first after it, finds
  62 flips in 18,105 gaps (0 to 18 per
  record; same script). Each is a candidate change of orientation across a gap; a worm can also turn far during a
  long gap, and the gaps' lengths were not examined, so the 62 are candidates only. Whether a block's orientation is
  right cannot be read from the skeletons alone.
- **What it means for the issue**: isolated swaps are not what inflates the head's bends; the orientation's own
  rule may, block by block, and that is not measured here. The issue for tron-grid-lite should rest on the share of
  frames bending beyond 0.9 rad at any joint (31 to 46%, median 38%, in the twelve records of 2026-10-14), which
  holds whichever end is the head, and give
  the gradient along the body only with this caveat.

## The Joint-Limit Issue, Drafted

STATE.md, *Needs from other repositories*, the joint-limit entry: the evidence is gathered (journals of 2026-10-14

session's GitHub access reaches iso-lab only. It follows tron-grid-lite's feature-request template
(`.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/feature_request.md` at `c083262`, the commit pinned in `external/`) and its vocabulary.
Every number in it is from the two journals named; no new measurement was made for it.

```markdown
Title: [FEATURE] A worm-shaped creature bends further than the chain's 0.9 rad joint limit allows

*Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer.*

## Problem Statement

The Grid's limit for a chain's joints is 0.9 rad (`src/roster.cpp`, `desc.max_joint_angle = 0.9f`, "about fifty
degrees of swing, what a lateral undulator's joints move through"). iso-lab compares a creature on the Grid with
real *C. elegans* and measures both at one resolution: the body as eight arcs of equal length, seven joints, the
angle at each joint between the chords of its two arcs. In the reference recordings (the OpenWorm Movement Database,
N2 on food, Tierpsy's stored skeletons), a large share of the frames bend further than 0.9 rad somewhere:

- At any joint: 9.8 to 68.6% of skeletonised frames, median 35.8%, in 34 recordings (31 to 46%, median 38%, in the
  first thirteen; the 34 include recordings of other lengths, among them a 5-minute and a 2-hour recording).
- Per joint, head first, median over the same thirteen: 13.1, 10.9, 6.6, 4.5, 3.7, 3.2 and 3.7%. The middle joints
  (4 to 6) pass the limit in 3.2 to 4.5% of frames each, not only the ends.
- Beyond pi/2, Master Control's own ceiling (`WORLD_MAX_JOINT_ANGLE` in master-control's `src/roster.rs`): median
  0.15%, at most 0.255%, in twelve recordings.

So a creature held to 0.9 rad cannot match the worm's posture in about a third of the frames, and a comparison of
bends at this resolution differs from the worm's by construction.

Two kinds of tracking noise are ruled out as the main cause: noise along the inner skeleton (smoothing each skeleton
along its length with a five-point mean keeps about 0.8 of each joint's share, the tail's 0.74; that smoothing leaves
the tips untouched) and noise that changes from frame to frame (averaging skeletons over about a tenth of a second
removes only 0.6 to 1.8% of the share per joint). Real bending and noise steady over several frames, such as a
tracker's bias, are not told apart by these checks.

Caveats, said plainly: the stored skeletons are already smoothed along their length (Savitzky-Golay over 5 of 49
points), so the raw bends may be sharper; only frames whose skeleton was found are counted, and the frames whose
segmentation failed likely include the most bent ones (coils, omega turns); the head is named by Tierpsy mostly as the
end that moves more, so the gradient from head to tail may be built in, to a degree not measured, while the share at any joint does not
depend on which end is the head; and 34 recordings of one block are not the lab's reference statistic.

## Proposed Solution

Raise the chain's joint limit to the world's ceiling, pi/2, which Master Control already validates against (its
reason for that ceiling: beyond it "two segments would fold through one another"). At that
limit the reference worms bend beyond it in 0.15% of frames (median), against 35.8% at 0.9 rad (34 recordings). If 0.9 rad rests on a
source for lateral undulators, a pointer to it would help the lab say why the worm differs.

## Alternatives Considered

- A limit for a worm-shaped class of chain rather than for every chain: the descriptor already states the limit
  "for the class", so this fits the design; it needs the Grid to know a worm-shaped body from another chain, which
  one raised constant does not.
- Keep 0.9 rad and have the lab account for the limit in its comparison: possible, but then in about a third of the
  frames the worm's posture is one the Grid's creature can never match, and the benchmark would measure the Grid as much as the Program.

## Additional Context

- The measurements, their commands and their reviews: iso-lab's journal of
  [2026-10-14](https://github.com/ai-quokka-wannabe/iso-lab/blob/main/docs/journal/2026-10-14.md) (*How Often the
  Reference Worms Bend Beyond the Grid's Joint Limit*, *Where Along the Body the Worm Bends Beyond the Grid's Limit*)
  and of [2026-10-15](https://github.com/ai-quokka-wannabe/iso-lab/blob/main/docs/journal/2026-10-15.md) (*Which End
  Is the Head*), with links to the pull requests that verified them.
- The eight-arc joint angle corresponds to the angle between two neighbouring segments of a chain of eight
  segments, though not exactly: the chords of equal arcs differ in length, and the chain's joint angle is a
  difference of yaw between its segments. The comparison assumes the Grid's worm is an eight-segment chain.
- Where it fits the roadmap (`TODO.md`): Phase 6, *Programs*, "Creature sensor interface plugs in", in progress -
  creatures already bring chains whose joints this limit binds. The proposed change is one constant and its comment.
```

## The Published Locomotion States, Read at Their Source

STATE.md item 2(c): the speed check's bar is to be set from what moves a locomotion state, so the states themselves
come first. The features files publish them per frame, `motion_modes` in `/features_timeseries` (1 forward, -1
backward, 0 paused, undefined where none applies). Written before the reproduction runs.

- **What was read**: the OpenWorm analysis toolbox at `181ab13` (the clone of the speed's reading above; the records'
  provenance names `95c2879f`, not read): `features/locomotion_features.py` (`MotionEvent`, `MotionMode`),
  `features/events.py` (`EventFinder`, `EventList.get_event_mask`), `utils.py` (`interpolate_with_threshold`) and the
  options in `features/feature_processing_options.py`. The definition, as read:
    - Inputs: the published `midbody_speed` and the skeleton's `length` per frame, with every run of undefined
      lengths between two defined ones filled by linear interpolation (`motion_codes_longest_nan_run_to_interpolate`
      is `None`); lengths before the first or after the last defined one stay undefined.
    - Per frame, a candidate: forward where the speed is at least 5% of that frame's length per second, backward
      where it is at most -5%, paused where it lies within ±2.5% (each bound inclusive); an undefined speed or length
      is no candidate.
    - Runs of candidate frames; the first run reaches back to frame 0 if every speed before it is undefined, the last
      forward to the end likewise. Runs whose gap (frames strictly between them) is under 0.25 s of frames are joined,
      greedily; a run shorter than 0.5 s of frames is dropped; then, for forward and backward, a run is dropped unless
      the distance travelled over it - the sum of `|speed| / fps` over its defined frames - exceeds the mean, over
      its frames, of 5% of the length. Paused has no distance rule.
    - Each frame takes the mode of the run that covers it, forward set first, then backward, then paused, so a
      later mode overwrites an earlier one where runs overlap; a frame in no run is undefined.
- **The check, written before it runs**: a short Python script (given in the pull request) applies that definition
  to each record's published `midbody_speed` and `length` and compares the result with the published `motion_modes`,
  undefined counted as a class of its own. The reading counts as reproduced if the two agree on at least 99% of
  frames in every one of the seventeen records of the speed check. Reported per record: frames, agreement, and the
  disagreements by class.
- **The run**: `python3 -I toolbox_modes.py data/raw/owmd-n2/*_features.hdf5` at 12:42:51 UTC, after the method was
  pushed (commit `1b94224`), Python 3.13.16, h5py 3.16.0, numpy 2.5.4, on the seventeen MD5-checked records;
  deterministic, no seed.
- **What came out**: **reproduced, exactly** - the two agree on every frame of every record, 602,314 frames in all
  (8,205 to 215,850 per record), with no disagreement of any class.
- **A control, added after the run, since an exact match must show it could have failed**: three edits to the script,
  run on records 1007405 and 1024910. Widening the paused band from ±2.5 to ±3% of length per second lowers the
  agreement to 0.969 and 0.962; allowing gaps under 0.3 s instead of 0.25 s, to 0.981 and 0.987; making the distance
  rule strict instead of inclusive changes nothing, as no run's distance equals its threshold exactly - a boundary
  case the data does not reach. The pull request's verification ran a wider set of edits on all seventeen records
  and found four more details the data cannot confirm: the gap's and the minimum duration's strict comparisons
  (every record's frame rate is non-integer, 25.25 to 30.03, so no whole number of frames equals a quarter or half a
  second of them), the inclusive speed bounds (no speed equals its threshold exactly) and forward written before

## C18's Plan, Attempted Again: the Width Test Cannot See a Transition

The rules ask that before a night stops, every *Blocked* item is tried again from a new angle. C18's plan
([#45](https://github.com/ai-quokka-wannabe/iso-lab/pull/45), parked) was to be rewritten from its pilot (journal of
2026-10-14, *C18's Model, Piloted*): a statistic taken among the draws in which the spread survives, more draws, a fine
sweep across each variant's transition, controls. Drafting that plan's controls showed something the pilot's reading
missed, and the plan was not committed.

- **The flaw**: both the first plan's statistic (the steepest slope of the activated fraction against the mean degree,
  its ratio between N = 100 and 10 000) and the draft's (the width between the fraction's crossings of 10% and 90% of
  its top) assume that a phase transition's curve grows steeper without end as N grows. A continuous transition does
  not: in the limit its order parameter rises from zero at the threshold over a finite range of degree, and only the
  rounding at the threshold narrows with size. The textbook case shows it. The giant component of a random graph, as a
  fraction S of N, solves S = 1 - exp(-d S) in the limit; computed here (`python3 -I` on that equation by fixed-point
  iteration and bisection, no seed), it crosses 10% of its top (0.9975 at d = 6) at d = 1.053 and 90% at d = 2.540, a
  width of 1.486 to which finite sizes converge from above rather than shrinking to zero, and its steepest slope, 2
  at the threshold, stays finite. That is the giant component's own fraction, the order parameter conditioned on
  the spread surviving; the fraction a single seed reaches, averaged over seeds, is about its square, whose width is
  larger still. So a margin of
  "narrows by more than a factor of two" would call a genuine phase transition "no cliff". The pilot's widths for the
  pure network (1.14, 0.68 and 1.27 at the three sizes) fit a continuous transition of finite width as well as they
  fit a smooth change; they cannot tell the two apart.
- **What would**: the mark of a transition is below its threshold, not across it. Under it, the seed's spread stays
  finite, so the fraction it reaches shrinks with N, roughly as 1/N; above it, the fraction converges to a positive
  value; a smooth change, with no threshold, shows neither. A sound test would take the order parameter at degrees a
  fixed distance below and above each variant's estimated threshold and ask whether it falls by more than a margin
  between N = 100 and N = 10 000 below, and holds above - with the giant component as the control that must show it,
  and a smooth logistic as the control that must not. Whether the hunch's "cliff" means such a threshold or an abrupt
  jump, a first-order transition, has to be settled first, from the hunch's own words and Shrager, Hogg and Huberman
  1987 ([doi:10.1126/science.236.4805.1092](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.236.4805.1092)), which is not yet read.
- **And the pilot's own model may jump** (from the pull request's verification, a scratch probe with the pilot's
  model, `numpy.random.default_rng(20261015)`, 12 draws per point, not committed): in the pure network each edge
  passes on 0.4 of its source's activation, so spread grows once 0.4 d exceeds one, near d = 2.5, where the seed's
  reachable set is already most of the network; at N = 10 000 the mean reached fraction went from 0.001 at d = 2.4 to
  0.703 at d = 2.6. That is a jump, not a continuous rise. For a jump the steepest slope would grow with N after all,
  so "both tests are unsound" holds for continuous transitions, not for jumps; and the next plan needs a jump-shaped
  control, or the pure model's expectation in the limit (nothing below 0.4 d = 1, about the reachable set's square
  above), beside the giant component, before its margins are written.
- **What it means**: C18 stays parked, now with a reason that is not about the model but about the test, and with the
  next idea written down. The first plan's flaw was more than its fixed horizon.

## Where in the Paused Runs the Lab's Undefined Frames Lie

STATE.md item 2(c): inside the published paused runs, the lab's in-band frames are broken by unbridgeable gaps, and
the runs' edges, before its first in-band frame and after its last, are about as large (*How the In-Band Frames Fall
in Time*, above). How much of the lab's undefined excess does each account for? Written before the count runs.

- **The method**: over the seventeen records, every frame the published states call paused and the lab's states
  (published-choice speed, one end) leave undefined is sorted, within its published paused run, into the first of:
  (e) an edge - before the lab's first in-band frame of the run or after its last, or in a run with none; (g) a long
  interior gap - between two consecutive in-band frames at least a quarter-second apart; (s) a short interior
  frame - anything else, an in-band frame or a short gap whose lab run was dropped as shorter than half a second.
  In-band is the speed's size at most 2.5% of the published length, interpolated inside, as before. Reported: the
  share of each class pooled over records, and its median over records. A short script, given in the pull request,
  does it. Descriptive; no bar.
- **The run**: `python3 -I shares.py <the seventeen records>` (in the pull request) at 13:47:23 UTC, after the method
  was pushed (`edde707`), Python 3.13.16, h5py 3.16.0, numpy 2.5.4, rustc 1.98.0; deterministic, no seed.
- **What came out**: of the 35,370 frames the published states call paused and the lab's leave undefined, (e) 52.3%
  lie at a run's edges (median over records 54.6%, 35.8% to 64.2%), (g) 29.2% in long interior gaps (26.8%, 16.8% to
  36.2%), and (s) 18.5% are short interior frames in runs the rule dropped (21.6%, 14.6% to 42.6%). Exploratory,
  after the count: class (e) includes runs with no lab in-band frame at all, which are missed runs rather than late
  starts or early ends; they are 2.1% of the frames, and the edges of runs with some in-band frame 50.1% (the same
  script with that split, `edges_split.py` in the pull request).
- **What it means**: the largest part of the lab's undefined paused frames is at the edges: its paused states start
  later or end earlier than the published ones, more than they break in the middle. Breaks in the middle, long gaps
  and dropped short runs, make up the other half. Why the lab's speed enters the paused band later and
  leaves it sooner at a pause's edges is not measured; that is the next step, beside the frame-to-frame variation
  of the two speeds on nearly-still frames.
