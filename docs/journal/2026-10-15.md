## The Published Midbody Speed, Read at Its Source

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent, at the maintainer's request; not by the maintainer.

- **The task**: STATE.md item 2(c). The lab's eight-arc midbody speed failed its check against the dataset's own
  `midbody_speed` in one record of sixteen (journal of 2026-10-14, *A Midbody Speed*), and the first of the three next
  steps written there was to read the published speed's definition in Tierpsy's code. Item 1, the pace note, came first
  on the list and was tried first: Raizen et al. 2008 on lethargus is not in Europe PMC, Singh and Sulston 1978 is not
  indexed there, and Clark et al. 2007's full text (PMC6672141, a candidate for a thermotaxis latency) was not served
  (HTTP 500, as for the papers of the night before), so no gap of item 1 could be filled tonight.
- **What was read**, at the source, from public repositories cloned shallow into the session's scratch space and
  read, not run: the OpenWorm analysis toolbox
  ([open-worm-analysis-toolbox](https://github.com/openworm/open-worm-analysis-toolbox), `main` at `181ab13`) and
  Tierpsy ([tierpsy-tracker](https://github.com/ver228/tierpsy-tracker), `main` at `1298df8`), which carries its own
  copy of the toolbox with the same values. The toolbox commit the records name in their provenance, `95c2879f`, was
  not read (it is not in the shallow clone); the exact reproduction below makes a difference that matters unlikely.
  Tierpsy's name table, `tierpsy/extras/features_names.csv`, maps the
  column `midbody_speed` to the toolbox's `locomotion.velocity.midbody.speed`, in micrometres per second. In the
  toolbox (`features/velocity.py`, `compute_speed`; the partitions in `prefeatures/basic_worm.py`; the options in
  `features/feature_processing_options.py`):
    - the speed is the distance moved by the centroid of skeleton points 21 to 29 of 49, counted from the head (indices 20 to 28; the `old_midbody_velocity`
      partition, used because `mimic_old_behaviour` is on by default) between two frames about 0.5 s apart
      (`velocity_body_diff = 0.5`; the window is `2 round(0.25 fps) + 1` frames, centred), divided by the time between;
    - where a window's end has no skeleton, that end moves outwards, up to twice as far, so a frame without a skeleton
      of its own still has a speed when both ends find one;
    - the sign is negative when the direction of that motion lies more than 90 degrees from the body's angle at the
      window's first frame, the body's angle being the mean direction of points 9 to 41 (indices 8 to 40), taken tail to head.
- **The check, fixed before it ran** (in the night's working notes; not committed separately before the run):
  re-implemented from that source in a short Python script (given in full in the pull request's description) and
  applied to the skeletons stored in the features files, the reading was to count as confirmed if the correlation
  with the published column was at least 0.99 and the slope between 0.98 and 1.02, in both of records 1007405 - the
  one the lab's measure failed - and 1024910, each fetched again from Zenodo and MD5-checked (Python 3.13.16, h5py
  3.16.0, numpy 2.5.4).
- **What came out**: confirmed, and exactly - in both records a correlation of 1.00000 and a slope of 1.00000, a
  median absolute difference of 0.0003 um/s, and the same frames defined (99.94% and 99.75% of frames); the largest
  difference is 0.002 um/s in 1024910 and, at a single frame of 1007405, 9.1 um/s, a flip of sign: there the motion
  lies 90.0003 degrees from the body's angle, so recomputing in double precision from stored single-precision points
  can land on the other side of the threshold (found in the pull request's verification). So, in the two records
  checked, the published speed is the toolbox's, computed from the very skeletons the files store.
- **What it means for the lab's measure**: the lab's eight-arc speed differs from it in kind, not only in resolution.
  It projects the midbody's displacement on the body's direction, where the published speed takes the whole
  distance and only signs it, and it takes the direction from the middle quarter of the body at the window's centre,
  where the published speed uses most of the body at the window's start. Those choices may part most where the body
  bends most, which would fit 1007405's failing the check; that is a reading, neither measured here nor tested. Which definition
  the benchmark should use is a choice to be made on what the Grid's worm can report, not by agreement alone; the
  next step is a version of the lab's measure with the published speed's choices at the eight-arc resolution, and
  the check run again on it.
- **What it means for the bends beyond the joint limit, an unexpected finding**: in Tierpsy's feature code
  (`tierpsy/analysis/feat_create/obtainFeatures.py` and `obtainFeaturesHelper.py`), the skeletons written to
  `/coordinates/skeletons` are the ones the features are computed from, after a Savitzky-Golay filter (a window of 5,
  a cubic) and a correction for the stage's motion. The filter runs **along each skeleton's length**, not along time:
  `_h_smooth_curve_all` loops over the frames and filters each frame's 49 points as a curve, with SciPy's default edge
  mode, which fits the end windows too, so the tips are filtered as well. The dataset's own commit, `cbfc23eb4f1a`
  (by record 1011832's provenance; read from a blobless clone of `ver228/tierpsy-tracker`), has the same filter, the
  same call with `smooth_window=5` and the same order of operations as the current master `1298df8`; the exact
  reproduction above shows the stored skeletons are the ones the features used, which fits. So every skeleton the
  lab has measured was already smoothed lightly along its length, over 5 of 49 points: the bends of 2026-10-14
  (journal, *The Bends Beyond the Joint Limit*) were measured on filtered skeletons, the raw outlines may bend more
  sharply than that, and the lab's own five-point smoothing check repeated a smoothing of the same kind. The
  averaging over frames is not touched by this: the stored skeletons are not smoothed in time. A first draft of this
  entry said they were, from a misreading of which axis the loop runs over; the code at both commits says otherwise.

## Which End Is the Head

STATE.md, *Needs from other repositories*, the joint-limit entry: before the issue is drafted, read how the dataset
decides which end of a skeleton is the head, since the excess of bends beyond the Grid's limit was found greatest at
the head (journal of 2026-10-14). Written before the count below runs.

- **What was read**: Tierpsy at the dataset's commit `cbfc23eb` (blobless clone, read, not run). The features files
  record only the last two steps of their processing (`/provenance_tracking`: `FEAT_CREATE` and `WCON_EXPORT`, the
  former at `cbfc23eb`), run on skeletons made earlier; the steps that oriented those skeletons, and their commit,
  are not recorded in the files. The single-worm pipeline (`tierpsy/helper/params/docs_analysis_points.py`, the list
  `WT2`) orients in two steps, `SKE_ORIENT` then `INT_SKE_ORIENT`. Read at `cbfc23eb`, so at best the code of the
  version that ran, or close to it:
    - `SKE_ORIENT` (`tierpsy/analysis/ske_orient/checkHeadOrientation.py`, `isWormHTSwitched`) splits a track into
      blocks of skeletons separated by gaps of fewer than `max_gap_allowed` frames. For each frame it takes the angle
      from point 1 to point 6 of 49 at one end and the same at the other, and their standard deviations over a rolling
      window of `window_std` frames. In a block with more than `min_block_size` of them, the end whose angle varies
      more in most frames is taken to be the head; a smaller block takes the orientation of the larger block before
      it, or after it at a track's start. The function's own defaults are 10, 25 and 250 frames; a value passed as
      zero or less is replaced by one scaled with the frame rate - half a second, one second and ten seconds
      (`tierpsy/helper/params/get_defaults.py`, `head_tail_defaults`). Which the run used depends on its settings file,
      `single_worm_on_food.json` in another repository, not read here.
    - `INT_SKE_ORIENT` (`tierpsy/analysis/int_ske_orient/`) then switches blocks whose intensity profile along the
      body looks reversed, and `checkFinalOrientation.py` weighs both kinds of evidence over the whole track.
- **What it means for the bends**: the first rule names as the head the end whose direction changes more. The head's
  excess of bends beyond 0.9 rad is therefore partly built in: in any block where the movement rule decides and the
  tail moved more, the more-bending end was labelled the head. Real worms do move the head more (it forages), so the
  rule is usually right, but the gradient along the body is not independent evidence of where the worm bends most.
  The issue for tron-grid-lite should rest on the share of frames bending beyond the limit at any joint, which does
  not depend on which end is called the head, and should state the gradient only with this caveat.
- **The count, method fixed before it runs**: a check for isolated swaps the orientation steps would leave as
  frame-to-frame flips. Between two consecutive frames that both have a stored skeleton, a flip is counted where the
  skeleton lies closer, by the mean distance between corresponding points, to the previous one reversed than to it
  as it is. Run on the seventeen block-1 records of the speed check, with the script given in the pull request
  (`python3 -I flips.py <files>`). Reported per record: pairs, flips and flips per thousand pairs; no test, no bar.
  A flip in a moving worm is expected to be rare, since half a body length rarely passes in one frame; a worm curled
  so its ends meet can produce one without any swap, so a flip is a candidate swap, not a proven one. Whole blocks
  oriented wrongly do not show as flips except at their edges, so a low count does not rule them out.
