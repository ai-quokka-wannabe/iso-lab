## Why Slow Learning and a Weak Nose Need Each Other

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

Last night's two-gene transplant (journal of 2026-10-26, § Building the Worm by Hand) moved two of the CMA-ES genome's
values onto e45 - slow learning (`eta` 0.01 to 0.00069) and a weak smell instinct (`g_odour` 1.72 to 0.38) - and
recovered 95% of its worst-case gap on the fresh panel. Taking both genes back out of the CMA-ES genome cost nearly
three times their single removals added. That night's journal wrote down an untested guess:

> a weak nose stops the worm being dragged to lying smells, and slow learning keeps the map it builds instead from being
> overwritten by noise. Each alone leaves the other failure in place.

and two ways it would be refuted. This experiment tests both, and the mechanism itself.

- **The genomes**: e45's median elite genome (`e45`); e45 with the CMA-ES genome's `eta` (`eta`); with its `g_odour`
  (`odour`); with both (`both`, last night's two-gene worm). The values are those of `build.py`.
- **The world** (gr-fec's Hardland, `reference/gr-fec/src/lifeworld.py`): the large, odourless food lies in a band on
  one side; small, smelly food that depletes fast lies on the other, and three decoys smell but feed nothing. The
  odourless food can only be found by what the worm learns, which in practice is its temperature (the band lies at one
  end of the gradient).
- **The probe**: `probe.py` (SHA-256 `d7439e284bad44671dce87ec8827ca787fe836053ab17c7e74c7978ce97c22d1`) lives each
  genome on the fresh panel exactly as `build.py` does: layouts 75000 (cold), 75001 (warm) and 75002 (rich), body
  seeds the layout's + 777, 50 worms, 48 h, gr-fec's code unedited. Beside each life, for evaluation only, it records
  over h24-48:
    - the exact pathway audit of last night's `audit.py`, each pathway's share of |dD|;
    - the share of time on odourless food, on smelly food, and *near a decoy* (within 10 of a decoy's centre, off food);
    - the *learned temperature map*: the hungry value of the temperature nodes, V(T) = Σ act_j(T)(W_j + W'_j), and its
      contrast c = V(T_food) − V(T_other) between the food band's temperature and its mirror, every 600 s. Per worm:
      the share of samples with c > 0 (the map points to the food), the mean of c and its standard deviation over
      time.
- **Scores across streams**: `build.py` (as last night) runs `e45`, `eta`, `odour` and `both` at body-seed offsets 778
  and 779 too.
- **Statistics**: `stats.py` (SHA-256 `3c2db2df3f1b53a71065eec2d255cb4eb98869dd9d57307ceb916971a9d80fc3`): a difference
  of means over worms between two genomes in one world, with a 95% percentile interval from 10 000 bootstrap resamples
  of worms, independently per genome, seed 84000.
- **Run**: `python3 -I <scratchpad>/probe/probe.py <out.npz>`, `python3 -I <scratchpad>/build/build.py e45 <genes|->
  <offset> <out.npy>` and `python3 -I <scratchpad>/probe/stats.py <out.npz> <world> <measure> <a> <b>`, from the
  repository's root; scripts in the pull request.
- **Rules**:
    - **H0, the control**: the probe's late energies for `e45` and `both` equal `build.py`'s at offset 777 (0.6235,
      0.8394, 0.8944 and 0.8726, 0.8603, 0.8916) to every printed digit, and the audit's sum matches the brain to
      within 1e-9.
    - **A1, the first registered refutation**: neither one-gene worm's worst case at offset 777 reaches 0.7481, half of
      the gap from e45's 0.6235 to the CMA-ES genome's 0.8726. If either does, the guess's "each alone leaves the other
      failure in place" is refuted.
    - **A2, the second registered refutation**: in the cold world the odour instinct's share of |dD| is lower for
      `both` than for `e45` (one aggregate per life; no interval). If not, the guess is refuted.
    - **A3, the nose**: in the cold world, time near a decoy is lower for `odour` and for `both` than for `e45` and
      for `eta` - all four differences with intervals below zero.
    - **A4, the map**: in the cold world, the share of samples whose map points to the food is higher for `eta` and for
      `both` than for `e45` and for `odour` - all four differences with intervals above zero.
- **What each outcome would mean**:
    - A1 to A4 holding: the guess's mechanism stands, each gene repairing one failure that the other cannot.
    - A1 failing: one gene alone does most of the work, and the interaction found last night is mostly saturation.
    - A3 failing: the weak nose works some other way than by keeping the worm off decoys.
    - A4 failing: slow learning works some other way than by a steadier map - for example by learning less of
      something harmful.

      +0.23 of both.

      levers on it, either of which alone recovers most of the loss.
- **What it does not show**: one layout per world; the mechanism measured in the cold world only; whether other genes
  are further levers on the same failure; what the weak nose does in the warm world, where its effect is small and mixed.

## A First Design Rule: Two Levers on One Failure

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

Tonight's probe (§ Why Slow Learning and a Weak Nose Need Each Other, on its own branch when this is written) found
that slow learning and a weak smell instinct are two separate levers on one failure: in the cold world e45 never
reaches the odourless food, either because its nose drags it to decoys or because its fast learning leaves its
temperature map at chance. Either repair alone gets the worm to the food most of the time. If the two failures are
independent ways of missing the food, their *chances* should multiply, and the score of any combination of the two
genes should follow from the two genes' effects measured alone. That is a composition rule, the kind of rule a
designer needs: measure each lever once, then predict combinations without running them.

- **The law (OR)**: with E the late energy, E₀₀ e45's, and a ceiling `EMAX` = 0.90 fixed here (about where the
  well-fed worlds sit, 0.89 to 0.90), each lever's remaining share of the failure is measured along its own edge,
  a(eta) = (EMAX − E(eta, e45's g_odour)) / (EMAX − E₀₀) and b(g_odour) likewise, and a combination is predicted as
  E = EMAX − (EMAX − E₀₀)·a·b.
- **The rival (additive)**: the gains add, E = E(eta, ·) + E(·, g_odour) − E₀₀.
- **Where the law came from**: tonight's four genomes suggested it. Applied to them after the fact it predicts the
  two-gene worm at 0.8707 against a measured 0.8726, where the additive rule predicts 0.9964. That one point was used
  to choose the law, so it is not evidence; the eight new interior cells below are the test.
- **The sweep**: `sweep.py` (SHA-256 `4705f1cb998a7484cf39d06278d3274b8c62af57c6e7c2aeb9f0b77a139f547a`), e45's median
  genome with log10 `eta` in {−2 (e45's), −2.4, −2.8, −3.1623 (the CMA-ES genome's)} and `g_odour` in {1.7237
  (e45's), 1.2, 0.75, 0.3786 (the CMA-ES genome's)}, sixteen cells, each a life of 50 worms in the fresh cold world
  (layout 75000, side −1), body seeds + 777, 48 h, late energy over h24-48, by `build.py`'s own life function,
  unedited. gr-fec's code unedited.
- **The test**: `law.py` (SHA-256 `0e5fcfcd2cfb3b67249984b4b1c0c5b3d80a44988b1b5e0040c6609fbb36ec4c`) takes a and b
  from the sweep's edges (row 0 and column 0) and predicts the nine interior cells by each model. It reports each
  model's mean absolute error over the interior and a 95% percentile interval for their difference, from 10 000
  bootstrap resamples of worms in every cell, seed 86000.
- **Run**: `python3 -I <scratchpad>/sweep/sweep.py <out.npy>`, then `python3 -I <scratchpad>/sweep/law.py <out.npy>`,
  from the repository's root; scripts in the pull request.
- **Rules**:
    - **D0, the control**: the four corners repeat the probe's lives: 0.6235 (e45), 0.8097 (slow learning alone),
      0.8102 (weak nose alone) and 0.8726 (both), to every printed digit.
    - **D1, the law predicts**: the OR law's mean absolute error over the nine interior cells is at most 0.03.
    - **D2, better than adding**: the OR law's error is below the additive rule's, with the interval of the difference
      below zero.
- **What each outcome would mean**:
    - D1 and D2 holding: the two levers compose as independent failures, and their combinations can be designed from
      two one-dimensional measurements - a first design rule for this worm, to be tested next on other genes and
      worlds.
    - D1 failing with D2 holding: saturation is right in kind but the law's form or its ceiling is wrong.
    - D2 failing: the levers interact in some other way, and the OR reading of tonight's probe does not extend beyond
      its four corners.

## A Positive Control for Steering

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

Last night gr-fec's greedy value-steering, ported onto the second CMA-ES brain, lowered its worst case on both panels
(journal of 2026-10-26, § Results of B and C, S1). gr-fec had recorded the same rule as a robust gain of +0.17 to
+0.24 in its never-seen cold world, on its own steering brain ([docs/research/gr-fec.md
§ 3.4](../research/gr-fec.md#34-steering-and-imagination); `reference/gr-fec/results/PREREG39.md`). Two things changed
at once in the port: the brain, and the body, whose lateral sense the lab had corrected. Without a positive control
the two cannot be told apart. This is that control.

- **The flaw the correction removes**: gr-fec's `DirBody.sense` asks the world about the two lateral points after the
  body's own position. The world keeps the patch of the *last* point asked, so `consume()` depletes the patch under
  the right-hand point, or none if that point is off food, while the worm eats what lies under its body. At a patch's
  edge a worm can feed without depleting anything.
- **The brain**: gr-fec's `SteeringBrain` exactly as its `experiments/e55_steer_imagine.py` builds it (`s0` 0.01,
  `integ` 10, separate instincts, `trust_k` 2; otherwise `embodied.Brain`'s defaults), `plain` (never steers) against
  `greedy` (every 10 s, steer towards the side whose neighbouring temperature concept has the higher learned value).
  gr-fec's code is used unedited.
- **The bodies**: `grfec`, gr-fec's `DirBody` as it is; `fixed`, which asks about the lateral points first, so the
  world's record is the body's own patch. Both draw the same random numbers in the same order.
- **The tool**: `ctl.py` (SHA-256 `62b588a4ffe17de8d1160631a49bd91c2d116d8e106f12469e8677b49113cf88`), 100 worms per
  life, 48 h, late energy over h24-48, layouts made as gr-fec makes them (`RandomHardWorld` with the layout seed and
  side, `RandomRichWorld` with the layout seed). Run as `python3 -I <scratchpad>/steer/ctl.py <out.json> <jobs>` from
  the repository's root, a job being `body:mode:layout seed:side:body seed`.
- **Statistics**: `ctlstats.py` (SHA-256 `8ff1e0dbd4af8a6cfea272e89da92bc7231c59a3884f4ee29dfc88b03885c28f`) gives
  greedy minus plain averaged over the body seeds given, with a 95% percentile interval from 10 000 bootstrap
  resamples of worms within each life, seed 84100.
- **The lives**:
    - gr-fec's replication (its `e56_greedy_rep.py`): layout 23000 cold, plain with body seeds 28100 and 28200, greedy
      with 28101 and 28201, on each body (8 lives);
    - common random numbers, plain and greedy on the same body seed (the greedy rule draws no random numbers): body
      seeds the layout's + 777 and + 778, on the `fixed` body in layouts 23000 (cold), 23001 (warm), 23002 (rich),
      75000 (cold), 75001 (warm) and 75002 (rich), and on the `grfec` body in the two cold ones (32 lives).
- **Rules**:
    - **P0, the control**: on the `grfec` body the replication's four lives give 0.451, 0.620, 0.450 and 0.623 at
      three decimals, as gr-fec's `results/e56_greedy_rep.json` records.
    - **P1, gr-fec's own rule on the fixed body**: in both replication pairs, greedy minus plain is at least +0.10
      (gr-fec's P39c).
    - **P2, the gain with common random numbers**: on the `fixed` body, in each cold layout (23000 and 75000), greedy
      minus plain over the two body seeds is at least +0.05 and its interval lies above zero.
    - Descriptive: the warm and rich layouts, and the `grfec` body against the `fixed` one in the cold layouts.
- **What each outcome would mean**:
    - P0 failing: the lab is not running gr-fec's experiment, and nothing below can be read.
    - P1 and P2 holding: steering's gain is real and survives the correction. Last night's failure then belongs to
      the brain it was ported onto, and steering is a layer still worth designing for.
    - P1 or P2 failing where P0 holds: gr-fec's gain depended, at least in part, on the consumption flaw, and the
      steering rule is not yet a layer the lab can build on.

## Steering Only When Hungry

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The positive control (§ A Positive Control for Steering) showed gr-fec's value-steering gaining about +0.2 in two cold
layouts but costing in both rich layouts (−0.06 and −0.22) and one warm one. *A hunch*: steering costs when the worm
is already fed - its learned map then pulls it to temperatures it has eaten at, away from the food around it - and
pays when it is hungry and the food lies at a learnable temperature. If so, a gate on the brain's own hunger reading
should keep the cold-world gain and remove the rich-world cost. The gate reads only a sense the brain already has,
the noisy hunger hormone `y[7]`.

- **The brain**: gr-fec's `SteeringBrain` in `greedy` mode, exactly as the control built it, whose steering command
  is held at zero whenever its hunger reading is below H = 0.3 (fixed here, between the fed rich worlds' hormone,
  about 0.1, and the cold worlds' at the plain worm's energy of about 0.45 to 0.55, about 0.5). The body is the
  control's fixed body. gr-fec's code is used unedited.
- **The tool**: `gate.py` (SHA-256 `24531668cf25b1bcd927476ec8525d824642c2626a8517026679c661acf0790d`) repeats the
  control's common-random-number lives with the gated brain: layouts 23000 (cold), 23001 (warm), 23002 (rich), 75000
  (cold), 75001 (warm), 75002 (rich), body seeds the layout's + 777 and + 778, 100 worms, 48 h, late energy over
  h24-48. Each life's plain and greedy partners are the control's own (`ctl.json`). It also records the share of
  h24-48 with the gate open. Run as `python3 -I <scratchpad>/gate/gate.py <out.json> <layout:side:body seed[:H]> ...`
  from the repository's root.
- **Statistics**: `gatestats.py` (SHA-256 `7e59a171e0db9ea89d5b4ca1ddaddaaf67194e424c24e16f79aa0316afbda900`) gives
  gated minus plain and gated minus greedy averaged over the two body seeds, with 95% percentile intervals from
  10 000 bootstrap resamples of worms within each life, seed 87000. Worm-level only, as in the control.
- **Rules**:
    - **G0, the control**: with H = −1 the gate never closes, and the life 75002, rich, body seed 75779 must equal the
      control's greedy life exactly, worm by worm.
    - **G1, the gain kept**: in each cold layout (23000, 75000), gated minus plain is at least +0.10, with its interval
      above zero.
    - **G2, the cost removed**: in each rich layout (23002, 75002), gated minus greedy has its interval above zero, and
      gated minus plain is at least −0.02.
    - Descriptive: the warm layouts, and the gate's open share in every layout.
- **What each outcome would mean**:
    - G1 and G2 holding: hunger is the switch on which steering's sign turns, and a gated steering is a lever the
      worm can carry into every world tested - a designed fix, built from a diagnosis rather than a search.
    - G2 failing: steering's rich-world cost is not about being fed; the hunch is refuted for those worlds.
    - G1 failing: the cold-world gain needs steering while the worm is not hungry, so the gate is too blunt.

- **G2 holds**: in both rich layouts the gate was almost never open, and the gated worm scored as the plain one

  steering. No registered outcome foresaw a gated worm doing worse than one that never steers. The registered
  reading of G1 failing - that the gain needs steering while the worm is not hungry, so the gate is too blunt - does
  not fit this: in 75000 the gate was open 80% of the time and the worm did worse than with no steering at all.
- **Descriptive**: in the warm layout 23001 the gated worm was also below both (−0.104 against plain); in 75001 it
  lay between them.
- **What it means**:
    - **A hunger gate removes the rich-world cost** (narrowed after verification). Shut steering off whenever the
      hunger reading is low, and in the rich layouts the gated worm scores as if it never steered, to within about
      0.005. But there the gate was shut 99 to 100% of the time and the worm was always fed, so this run cannot tell
      "steering costs when fed" from "steering costs in rich worlds"; it shows only that the gate shuts it off there.
    - **But hunger alone is not the switch on which steering's sign turns.** In two of four Hardland layouts the gated worm
      did worse than never steering, by up to 0.17. Steering only while hungry is not a safe middle between steering
      always and never; in those worlds it is worse than both. Why is not known. Candidates, none tested:
        - a worm that steers only while hungry may be led, each time it is hungry, to the temperature its map then
          values, and left to wander when fed - a cycle that never settles on the food;
        - the hunger reading's noise (σ 0.02) near the threshold may switch the gate on and off within seconds, and
          each shut holds the command at zero until the brain's next 10-step update;
        - the map itself may differ, since the worm's path, and so what it learns, differs from the start.
    - For design: a gate on a lever can fix one failure and create a worse one elsewhere. Every world a lever is
      tested in must be tested again after it is gated.
- **What it does not show**: one threshold (0.3), one brain, two body seeds per layout; why the gate harms in 75000
  and 23001. Next: record what the gated worm does in 75000 - time near the food band, the map's direction, how often
  the gate switches - before trying another gate.
