## gr-fec's Worm by Equation: Its Turning Rule, Predicted Before It Runs

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The maintainer's aim for gr-fec's worm ([docs/research/gr-fec.md](../research/gr-fec.md)) is to understand it fully
enough to design a worm for a chosen behaviour, with every step of its thinking auditable, rather than to search for
one by evolution. gr-fec found its best brain, e45, by evolving twelve numbers; nothing in the archive says which
behaviour a given set of numbers will produce. This is the first brick of that understanding: a closed-form account of
the layer that turns a change in the drive into a turn, tested against gr-fec's own code, unedited.

- **Question**: does a closed-form theory predict how fast an e45-family worm drifts up a gradient from its numbers
  alone, and can it be inverted to pick the numbers for a drift chosen in advance?
- **The worm**: `EvoBrain` and `Body` from `reference/gr-fec/src/` (`evolve.py`, `embodied.py`), unedited, with only
  the smell instinct on: `g_odour` 1, every other drive, reward, trust, roaming and curiosity gene 0, learning rate
  10^-5 (with no reward the learner's weights stay at zero). `tau` 0.3; `s0`, the integration time `integ` and the
  gradient vary. Odour rises linearly along x, 5 + G·x per mm; the plate has no wall (radius 10^12 mm).
- **The theory** (`theory.py`, SHA-256 `fc020153f6b6628d0bac7ff3c194a76ab9ce39d835c723365a632b816ea7c41c`):
    - The drive is D = g·(c + ε), ε the sensor's noise (σ 0.01). The brain's leaky integrator (α = 1/integ) and its
      10-step difference make the change it reacts to, dD, a fixed weighted sum of past steps' changes in D, with
      weights summing to 10.
    - The noise part of dD is Gaussian with a variance that follows from α and σ. The signal part, for a worm heading
      at angle θ to the gradient, is g·v·G·cos θ times the sum of the weights, each discounted by how far the heading
      has decorrelated since that step, ρ^k with ρ = (1 − λ0)·exp(−0.05²/2): turns and heading noise. That is the
      *memory* version. The *instantaneous* version leaves out the discount.
    - The turning probability per step at heading θ is the brain's sigmoid averaged over that noise (40-point Gauss
      quadrature). The stationary heading distribution then follows from the per-step Markov chain - turn and draw a
      uniform heading, or keep it, then add heading noise - solved on 720 angles. The drift is v·⟨cos θ⟩, with v 0.2
      mm/s.
    - The approximation it rests on is that the noise in dD is averaged step by step. That is exact only if the noise
      is uncorrelated between steps, and it is not, most of all at `integ` 30. This is where the theory is most
      likely to fail.
- **The simulation** (`sim.py`, SHA-256 `64d5d6405020ad3493031feb96ada616d21a76e7e13cb3d9f0eec93ef90a5c7b`): 3000
  worms per condition from the origin, 4000 steps of one second. The drift is each worm's x displacement from step 500
  to 4000 divided by 3500 s, then averaged over worms, with its standard error over worms.
    - Run as `python3 -I <scratchpad>/turn/sim.py test1 <0..35>` and `... test2 <0..3>` from the repository's root,
      Python 3.13.16, numpy 2.5.4.
    - Seeds 52000 + i (test 1) and 53000 + j (test 2).
- **Test 1, prediction**: the grid is `s0` ∈ {0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05}, `integ` ∈ {1, 10, 30} and G ∈ {0.0005, 0.002,
  0.005} per mm, 36 conditions. The memory theory predicts, in mm/s (the instantaneous theory after the bar):
    - `s0` 0.005: `integ` 1: 0.0048, 0.0183, 0.0454 | 0.0076, 0.0279, 0.0696; `integ` 10: 0.0314, 0.1133, 0.1486 |
      0.0436, 0.1265, 0.1486; `integ` 30: 0.0378, 0.1192, 0.1424 | 0.0539, 0.1296, 0.1427.
    - `s0` 0.01: `integ` 1: 0.0059, 0.0236, 0.0594 | 0.0082, 0.0326, 0.0780; `integ` 10: 0.0228, 0.0827, 0.1347 |
      0.0276, 0.0930, 0.1369; `integ` 30: 0.0200, 0.0748, 0.1300 | 0.0283, 0.0936, 0.1350.
    - `s0` 0.02: `integ` 1: 0.0074, 0.0295, 0.0714 | 0.0088, 0.0348, 0.0810; `integ` 10: 0.0122, 0.0471, 0.0999 |
      0.0143, 0.0542, 0.1070; `integ` 30: 0.0102, 0.0399, 0.0901 | 0.0143, 0.0542, 0.1068.
    - `s0` 0.05: `integ` 1: 0.0052, 0.0205, 0.0496 | 0.0055, 0.0220, 0.0528; `integ` 10: 0.0049, 0.0196, 0.0476 |
      0.0057, 0.0227, 0.0542; `integ` 30: 0.0041, 0.0163, 0.0400 | 0.0057, 0.0227, 0.0542.
    - It also predicts a turning rate per second for each condition. Without integration (`integ` 1) that rate is up to
      0.107, against 0.010 to 0.026 with it, because the sensor's noise alone crosses the threshold. This is a check on
      the noise term, reported beside the drift but not part of the rule.
- **Test 2, design**: `integ` 10, G 0.002, `tau` 0.3. For target drifts of 0.01, 0.02, 0.04 and 0.08 mm/s, the memory
  theory is inverted by bisection for `s0`, giving 0.0985638, 0.0489875, 0.0238844 and 0.0104963. The worms are then
  run on fresh seeds.
- **Rules fixed beforehand**: a condition *agrees* when |theory − simulation| ≤ max(0.15 × simulation, 2 × SE).
    - **T1**: the memory theory agrees in at least 32 of 36 conditions.
    - **T1b**: its median |relative error| is below the instantaneous theory's.
    - **T2, the design test**: all four designed worms agree with their targets.
- **What it would mean**: T1 and T2 holding would mean the turning layer is understood. Its drift could then be set by
  equation rather than evolved, and each decision would be accountable to the quantity it reacts to. T1 failing where
  `integ` is long would name the correlated-noise approximation as the gap to close next. T2 failing with T1 holding
  would mean the theory describes but cannot be used to design, most likely through the bisection's sensitivity.

### Results

- **The run**: all 40 conditions, four processes, from 18:23:25 to 18:36:47 UTC. The pre-registration was pushed in
  `8b3b514` in the same second that the runs were started, chained after it, so their order within that second is not
  shown. Both scripts' SHA-256 are as recorded above; the scripts and every output line are in the pull request.
- **Test 1**: by the rule, the memory theory agrees in **36 of 36** conditions, and the instantaneous theory in 10.
    - The median |relative error| is 0.031 for the memory theory, against 0.212 for the instantaneous one.
    - **T1 holds**, and **T1b holds**.
- **Test 2, the design**: the four worms whose `s0` was chosen by inverting the theory drifted at 0.00905, 0.01968,
  0.03891 and 0.07631 mm/s, against targets of 0.01, 0.02, 0.04 and 0.08. The errors are −9.5%, −1.6%, −2.7% and
  −4.6%, with standard errors 0.00046 to 0.00057. All four agree, so **T2 holds**. All four fell below their targets, as the theory's upward
  bias would make them.
- **What is not exact** (post hoc, not pre-registered):
    - **The theory runs high.** It is above the simulation in 29 of 36 conditions, by a median of about 3%. It exceeds
      +8% in six: four with `integ` 10 or 30 and `s0` 0.005 or 0.01 (+8.7% to +11.2%), and two without integration
      at the smallest gradient (+8.5% at `s0` 0.005 and +10% at `s0` 0.05, the latter about one standard error). No
      single number explains them. Measured in standard errors the differences are often well beyond chance (median
      |difference| 2.0 standard errors, largest 34), so the bias is real, not noise.
    - **One condition passes only on its allowance.** At `s0` 0.05, `integ` 10, G 0.0005, the theory is 18% low;
      that is within 2 standard errors (0.00108 against 0.00112), not within 15%.
    - **The turning rate is mostly right but not everywhere.** The predicted rate matches within 5% in 32 of 36
      conditions. It is 37% and 39% too low in the two strongest conditions (`s0` 0.005, G 0.005, `integ` 10 and 30),
      and 15% and 11% too low in two more. There worms point up the gradient nearly all the time, and the theory
      undercounts the turns they still make.
    - The likeliest cause of both is the approximation named beforehand. Noise in the decision quantity is correlated
      from step to step, which the theory averages away; and the slope a run starts with is ignored. That is an
      expectation, not a test.
- **What it means**: on gr-fec's own code, the layer that turns a falling drive into a turn is understood to within a
  few per cent. In the theory the drift follows from the gain, the gradient, `s0`, `tau`, the integration time and the
  sensor's noise (the gain scales the noise as well as the signal), through a calculation that takes about half a
  second; the test varied `s0`, the integration time and the gradient. Inverted, it set the worm's drift to
  within 10% of a target chosen in advance, with no search and no evolution. The memory term is needed: a theory that
  treats the worm as reacting to its present heading misses by about 21%. The noise term carries a design fact too:
  without integration, sensor noise alone sets the turning rate (0.11 per second against about 0.01 with it), the
  regime gr-fec's evolved elite sits in (median `integ` 1, `tau` 0.044, from its `results/e46_evo_eval.json`) -
  a pointer, since `tau` 0.044 lies outside what was tested.
- **What it does not show**:
    - The gain was fixed at 1 and `tau` at 0.3; the theory is untested at other values, including the elite's
      sharper `tau`.
    - It is the turning layer alone, with the learner held still, in a linear gradient without walls. gr-fec's plates
      have walls and Gaussian peaks, and its worms learn.
    - The 2-standard-error allowance carried one condition, and the theory's bias, though small, is real.
- **Next**, toward a worm whose every decision can be audited and designed:
    - **An audit trail of decisions.** The drive is a sum of named pathways: each concept node's learned value times its
      activation, each instinct, gut stretch and curiosity. The integrator and the 10-step difference are linear. So
      the quantity behind every turn splits exactly into those pathways' shares, checkable to rounding against the
      brain's own state. The aim is a tool that answers "why did this worm turn here?" for gr-fec's best brain, e45,
      in a whole life.
    - **The learning layer's fixed point.** With a small learning rate, the weights should approach the least-squares
      fit of reward on the worm's own experienced features. If they do, each learned value can be explained from the
      life that produced it.
    - Then composing the layers to design a worm for a chosen behaviour in a world, the test this one began.

## gr-fec's Worm by Equation: An Audit Trail of Its Decisions

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The second step of [STATE.md](../../STATE.md)'s item 9. The aim is a worm whose every decision can be audited: for
any turn, how much each part of its mind pushed for it.

- **The idea**: gr-fec's `EvoBrain` decides to turn from one quantity, dD, the change of its integrated drive over 10
  steps. The drive D is a sum of named terms, and the integrator and the difference are linear. So dD splits exactly
  into the shares of sixteen pathways:
    - the learned value of each pool of concept nodes - odour, salt, N, temperature and the intensity nodes - plain and
      hunger-gated, ten pools in all;
    - the odour and salt instincts, each times its learned trust;
    - gut stretch;
    - curiosity.

  Nothing about the brain is approximated: the shares must add up to the brain's own dD at every step, to rounding.
- **The tool**: `audit.py` (SHA-256 `93d26e1c6a4dc9f94342d331ccf06b622caf139be24882e3812bae44247ceaa1`) wraps
  `EvoBrain` from `reference/gr-fec/src/`, unedited. It records the brain's feature vector through an instance
  attribute, rebuilds each pathway's term, and carries each through the same integrator and difference. Every step it
  checks that the terms sum to the brain's drive and that the differences sum to the brain's dD, keeping the largest
  error. It is evaluation-only and changes nothing the brain sees.
    - **Red-first**: on a one-hour smoke life (seed 54099, excluded from the test), the largest error was
      4.4 × 10^-15. With the gut-stretch pathway deliberately left out, the check reported 1.77, so it fails when a
      pathway is missing.
    - That smoke life's first hour was seen before this was written: curiosity carried the largest share of |dD|
      (0.30), then the temperature nodes (0.20), gut stretch (0.13) and the odour instinct (0.12).
- **The lives**: gr-fec's e45 protocol (its `experiments/e46_evo_eval.py`). The 20 final elite genomes of
  `results/e45_evolve.json`, five worms each, 100 worms, live 48 h in Hardland (`lifeworld.HardWorld`, fixed layout,
  the plate's radius 50 mm). Five lives on seeds 54000 to 54004, run as `python3 -I <scratchpad>/audit/audit.py <0..4>`
  from the repository's root, Python 3.13.16, numpy 2.5.4.
- **The measure**: a pathway's share of the decision in a window is the sum over steps and worms of its |dD share|,
  divided by the same sum over all pathways. It is reported for the first two hours (h0-2) and the second day
  (h24-48), over all steps and over the steps where a worm turned.
- **Predictions**: P2 to P4 are taken from gr-fec's own account of Hardland - experience turns the lying smell down and
  builds a map of places by temperature - and from what curiosity is: node births, which stop once the world is known.
    - **P1, exactness**: the largest error is below 10^-9 in all five lives.
    - **P2, the smell instinct is turned down**: the odour instinct's share falls from h0-2 to h24-48 in at least 4 of
      5 lives.
    - **P3, a map of places takes over**: the temperature nodes' share, plain and hunger-gated together, rises from
      h0-2 to h24-48 in at least 4 of 5 lives. In h24-48 it exceeds the odour instinct's share in at least 4 of 5.
    - **P4, curiosity fades**: curiosity's share falls from h0-2 to h24-48 in at least 4 of 5 lives.
- **What it would mean**: P1 holding makes the audit an exact account of every decision, not a model of one. P2 to P4
  test gr-fec's story of how its best worm wins in Hardland, read directly from the decisions rather than from final
  weights. A failure would mean that story was not how the decisions were made.

### Results

- **The run**: five lives, five processes, from 18:41:06 to about 18:45:30 UTC (all five outputs were present by
  18:45:41). The pre-registration was pushed in `a2dfecd` in the same second the lives were started, chained after it,
  so their order within that second is not shown. The script's SHA-256 is as recorded; the script and every output line
  are in the pull request.
- **P1 holds**: the largest error was 8.9, 5.3, 7.1, 9.8 and 5.3 × 10^-15 in lives 0 to 4. In every step of every life
  the pathways summed to the brain's own drive and decision quantity, to rounding.
- **Correction, added after verification**: there are **fourteen** pathways, not sixteen as written in the
  pre-registration above - ten pools of nodes, two instincts, gut stretch and curiosity (`len(PATHS)` is 14). The
  count was wrong in the text; the code always audited fourteen.
- **P2 holds**: the odour instinct's share of |dD| fell from h0-2 to h24-48 in **5 of 5** lives: 0.165 to 0.082, 0.150
  to 0.064, 0.152 to 0.079, 0.157 to 0.084 and 0.155 to 0.086. Not only because other pathways grew: in life 0 the
  odour instinct's own summed |movement| per step fell 2.3 times, from 3.10 to 1.34 (`cancel.py`, below).
- **P3 holds**: the temperature nodes' share, plain and hunger-gated together, rose in **5 of 5** lives: 0.248 to
  0.553, 0.272 to 0.596, 0.256 to 0.564, 0.252 to 0.567 and 0.265 to 0.553. In h24-48 it exceeded the odour instinct's
  share in 5 of 5, by about six to nine times.
- **P4 holds**: curiosity's share fell in **5 of 5**, from 0.180 to 0.186 in h0-2 to 0.002 to 0.004 in h24-48.
- **Post hoc** (not pre-registered):
    - **What the second day's decisions are made of.** In all five lives, about 55 to 60% of the pathways' summed
      movement came from the learned temperature map. About 19% came from gut stretch, whose gene has a median of −1.55 over
      the 20 elites (`results/e46_evo_eval.json`): a fuller gut lowers the drive. About 8% came from the odour instinct and about 4% from the salt instinct.
    - **The shares at turns are close to the shares overall.** The shares on the steps where a worm turned differ from
      those over all steps by at most about four points. The largest gaps: curiosity in the first hours, 0.14-0.15 at
      turns against 0.18-0.19 overall; and the temperature nodes, two to about three and a half points higher at turns. No pathway is
      markedly more present when the worm turns.
    - **The worms turn about 0.11 times per second on the second day** (about 6.6 a minute), against about 0.10 in
      the first two hours. gr-fec flags its worms' turning as three to five times a real worm's, a figure it gave for
      10 to 15 turns a minute in its tests (`docs/ai_branch.md`).
    - **Late energy**: mean energy over h24-48 was 0.817 to 0.841. gr-fec's own e45 Hardland life gives 0.831 over
      the same hours (its `results/e46_evo_eval.json`, energy samples 144 to 287), inside that range. Its quoted 0.773
      is over h48-72 of a 72-hour life and is not comparable.
- **What it means**: the e45 worm's decisions can be audited exactly. For any turn of any worm, the tool says how much
  each of fourteen named parts of its mind moved the quantity that decided it, and the parts add up to the brain's own
  number. Read that way, the e45 elites' decisions are consistent with what gr-fec described for its hand-built trust
  worms (its `docs/ai_branch.md` § 6.13), not shown by it for e45 itself. Curiosity moves the decisions most in the
  first hours, then fades. The smell instinct's part shrinks. A learned map of temperatures makes the largest part of
  the movement on the second day. The shares are of the summed movement of all pathways, which partly cancel one
  another: in life 0 their summed |movement| is 1.74 times the brain's summed |dD| in h0-2 and 2.06 times in h24-48
  (`cancel.py`, below), so "share" is not "fraction of the decision". The temperature share may in part be
  amplified noise: most of the e45 elites (13 of 20; median `integ` 1) do not integrate their senses.
- **What it does not show**:
    - A pathway's share is how much it *moves* the decision quantity, whether that movement is signal or sensor noise.
      A pool of nodes jittered by noisy temperature readings also has a share. The tool does not yet split each share
      into what the world changed and what noise did.
    - Five lives of one fixed layout, one genome set.
- **The cancellation check** (added after verification, which found it first): `cancel.py` (SHA-256
  `9b93e05a1685bd0bde56e26a6c21647e8fe1f7109eb2d2ec64c63e62d88337c6`) re-lives life 0 (seed 54000) with `audit.py`
  unchanged and sums |pathway movement| and the brain's |dD| per window; run as `python3 -I <scratchpad>/diag/cancel.py`
  from the repository's root at 19:13 UTC. Its largest sum error was 8.9 × 10^-15, as in life 0.
- **Next**:
    - Split each pathway's share into signal and noise. The evaluation side may use the body's true readings to do
      it; the brain never sees them.
    - Then the learning layer: the least-mean-squares rule makes every weight an exact sum over past steps of η ×
      error × activation, so each learned value can be traced to the moments of experience that built it.

## gr-fec's Worm, Optimised: CMA-ES on e45's Genome

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The maintainer asked for the toy worm to be optimised as far as it will go. gr-fec's best brain, e45, has a worst case
of 0.828 in its never-seen worlds, and its own open thread 3 names the next optimiser: covariance-matrix adaptation
(CMA-ES) on the e45 genome. This is that, done so the result can be trusted: a training panel fixed in advance, common
random numbers, the worst life as fitness (gr-fec's principle 9), and a test on worlds the optimiser never saw.

- **The optimiser**: `opt.py` (SHA-256 `6be478b141b45eccaf97ce22cbd32d88642d740ef166acae2d2dc99b3c615a37`), a standard
  (μ/μ_w, λ) CMA-ES written for this, in numpy, over gr-fec's twelve e45 genes in their own ranges, scaled to [0, 1].
    - It starts from the median of e45's final elites (`results/e46_evo_eval.json`), with σ 0.15, λ 20 and μ 10; the
      current mean is always one of the candidates. 25 generations, optimiser seed 72000.
    - Each candidate is lived by five worms. gr-fec's `EvoBrain`, `Body` and `RandomHardWorld` /
      `RandomRichWorld` are used unedited.
- **Training**: three layouts fixed in advance and never used by gr-fec - a cold Hardland (side −1, layout seed 71000),
  a warm one (side +1, 71001) and a rich world (71002) - with body seeds the layout's + 777, the same every generation.
  Lives last 24 h. A candidate's fitness is the worst of the three worlds' mean energy over h12-24.
- **The test**: gr-fec's own never-seen layouts, as its `experiments/e50_eval_v7b.py` measures them: `RandomHardWorld`
  side −1 seed 23000, side +1 seed 23001, `RandomRichWorld` seed 23002, body seeds + 777, 50 worms, 48 h, late
  energy over h24-48. Three brains:
    - **e45 elites**: gr-fec's first 10 final genomes × 5, as gr-fec tested its brains;
    - **e45 median**: the median genome × 50;
    - **CMA-ES**: the optimiser's final mean genome × 50, chosen beforehand as the output, being less favoured by

## gr-fec's Worm by Equation: How Much of Each Decision Is Noise?

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The audit trail above says how much each pathway moves e45's decision quantity, but not whether the world or the
sensor's noise moved it. In e45 the integration time is 1 s and the turning threshold is sharp. gr-fec's
principle 7 says noise above the threshold acts as search. This measures how much of each decision is noise.

- **The split**: `audit2.py` (SHA-256 `4d0a164f15b8d9207febd87e6bd905e998deef6a8ad52d504bc7574da729e2a0`), beside the audit
  of `audit.py`. Every step, the evaluator rebuilds each pathway's term from the body's *true* readings, with the
  same formula, prototypes, weights and trust state, and calls it the signal. The noise is the brain's term minus the
  signal, so the two add up exactly. Both are carried through the same integrator and difference.
    - The true readings never reach the brain.
    - Curiosity has no sensor term of its own and is counted as signal (internal).
- **Red-first**: on a 600-step check (seed 5, not part of the test), the noise part's largest value was 0 with the
  body's sensor noise switched off, and 1.00 with honest senses. With nothing to split, the split finds nothing.
- **The lives**: as in the audit - e45's 20 elites × 5, Hardland, 48 h - on fresh seeds 55000 to 55004, run as
  `python3 -I <scratchpad>/audit2/audit2.py <0..4>` from the repository's root, at lowered priority (`nice`) beside
  the CMA-ES run. Window h24-48.
- **The measure**: for each pathway, its signal mass and noise mass are the sums over steps and worms of |signal dD|
  and |noise dD|. Its *noise fraction* is noise mass over the sum of the two.
- **Predictions**:
    - **N1**: the audit's sum check holds to below 10^-9 in all five lives.
    - **N2**: gut stretch's noise fraction is above 0.5 in at least 4 of 5 lives. The gut fills and empties slowly
      against its sensor's σ 0.02, and e45 does not integrate.
    - **N3**: over all pathways together, the noise mass exceeds the signal mass in at least 4 of 5 lives. Most of
      what moves e45's decisions is noise.
    - **N4**, a less certain direction: the plain temperature nodes' noise fraction is below 0.5 in at least 4 of 5
      lives - the learned map carries mostly signal.
- **What it would mean**: N2 and N3 holding would put a number on gr-fec's "noise as search" in its best worm. They
  would also point to a design change, not a tuning one: a gut-stretch pathway with a filter of its own. N2 failing
  would clear gut stretch of that charge.

## gr-fec's Worm by Design: A Smoothed Hunger Reading

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The noise split above found that e45's hunger-gated temperature map is about 80% noise. The gate reads a slow hormone
through a fast, noisy sensor and nothing smooths it. This tests one change designed from that finding rather than
found by search: the brain smooths its own hunger reading before using it.

- **The change**: `gate.py` (SHA-256 `8020c8560258aa8d6685b5760bc065d03c039347d5f4377797c83eacd2da218b`) defines
  `SmoothHungerBrain`, a subclass of gr-fec's unedited `EvoBrain`.
    - It passes column 7 of the senses, the hunger hormone, through a leaky integrator with time constant `tau_h`
      steps, and gives the brain the smoothed value in its place. The brain then uses it everywhere: as the gate of
      its hunger-gated copies, in its reward and in roaming.
    - It only filters a sense the brain already has, so it stays within the lab's rules.
    - With `tau_h` = 1 the subclass is the unedited brain, which is the switch that pins the module at baseline.
- **The test**: the CMA-ES test's protocol and worlds (gr-fec's never-seen layouts 23000 to 23002, body seeds + 777,
  48 h, late energy over h24-48), with e45's first 10 elites × 5. The runs are
  `python3 -I <scratchpad>/design/gate.py <tag> <tau_h> <out.npy>` from the repository's root, Python 3.13.16, numpy
  2.5.4, at `tau_h` 1 and `tau_h` 60. 60 s is half the hormone's own time constant of 120 s, chosen beforehand without
  a sweep.
- **Rules fixed beforehand**:
    - **D0, the control**: at `tau_h` 1 the three late energies equal, to every printed digit, those of the e45-elites
      test in the CMA-ES section (0.8319, 0.8177, 0.8968). They were seen before this was written; this is a check that
      the subclass pins at baseline, not a prediction.
    - **D1**: at `tau_h` 60 the worst case exceeds `tau_h` 1's by at least 0.01.
    - **D2**: a bootstrap over worms within each world (10 000 resamples, seed 74000) puts the 95% interval of that
      difference above zero.
- **What it would mean**: D1 and D2 holding would be a gain found by understanding rather than by search: one line of
  design, named by an audit, tested once. D1 failing would mean the noise the gate injects is harmless, or useful as
  search, as gr-fec's principle 7 allows. That would be worth knowing before anyone smooths it on the Grid.
