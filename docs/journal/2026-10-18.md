## H13: How Precisely Can 30 Worms Fix the Rate of Leaving Roaming?

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent, at the maintainer's request; not by the maintainer.

Yesterday's diagnosis (journal of 2026-10-17, § H13's Proof of Concept, a Diagnosis) found the fitter's noise a real
part of the bias in the rate of leaving roaming, `k_rd`. With 128 simulated worms per candidate, the noise-free fit
recovered it at 1.02 times the truth. On DEV's 30 worms, though, `k_rd` was within 0.2 for only 2 of 5 optimiser
seeds. It left an *untested* reading: what remains is the precision of 30 worms of 600 s. The maintainer asked for that
test. Written before it runs.

- **Question**: when the data is 30 worms of 600 s, how far does `ln(fitted k_rd / truth)` scatter around zero across
  fresh populations? How much of that scatter is the data, and how much the fitter?
- **Method**:
    - **The populations**: twelve, drawn from the truth as DEV was (population seed `5000 + 10 i`, simulation seed one
      more, `i` = 0 to 11), taking the first 30 worms of each.
    - **The fits**: each population is fitted twice, with optimiser seeds 7 and 8, using 128 simulated worms per
      candidate. X is pinned at its baseline, as the loop's refit has been: ablation dropped X and only X in all 25
      loops so far. Everything else is round 5's R4 statistics, round 4's fitter and DEV's start.
    - **The run**: `python3 -I h13_precision.py <0|1|2|3>` from the repository's root, four workers of three
      populations each, about 25 minutes. Script SHA-256 `74e232202481acf89355898c7bcf6e352ec71e33bdef4de57bb70912e8bf1d6c`.
- **The statistics, fixed before the data**: `python3 -I h13_precision_summary.py <the four outputs>` (SHA-256
  `e1290f5a345573ce4a02462cc1ebde5bb5adad3cde6e247fca0e1ba648e95247`). It was run once on an empty input and seen to
  refuse it. With `x = ln(fitted k_rd / truth)`, it reports:
    - each population's mean over its two seeds, `m`, and their difference, `d`;
    - the bias, the mean of `m` with a 95% t interval (11 degrees of freedom);
    - the sd within a population, `sqrt(mean(d^2) / 2)`, which is the fitter's remaining noise;
    - the data's own sd, `sqrt(var(m) - within^2 / 2)`, floored at zero;
    - the sd of one fit, from those two;
    - the predicted share of single fits within 0.2, under a normal law, and the observed share of the 24;
    - the correlation of `m` with the 30 worms' own mean `k_rd`;
    - the share within 0.2 for every fitted parameter.
- **Rules fixed beforehand**:
    - **R1, a bias left at 128 worms**: the 95% interval of the bias excludes zero.
    - **R2, the 0.2 tolerance is beyond 30 worms**: both the observed and the predicted share of single fits within 0.2
      are below 0.8. That is the rate criterion 1's "4 of 5" asks for.
    - **R3, whose noise**: the data's precision is the larger part if the data's variance exceeds the within-population
      variance; otherwise the fitter's remaining noise is.
- **What each answer would mean**:
    - R2 holding with R3 on the data: no brain or fitter can meet 0.2 from 30 worms. The benchmark's sample size, or the
      tolerance, must be set from the data's precision; the next step is the same test with more worms per population.
    - R2 holding with R3 on the fitter: more simulated worms, or averaging over optimiser seeds, is the next step.
    - R2 failing: 128 worms per candidate suffice, and DEV's 2 of 5 was in part the draw.

### Results

- **The run**: `python3 -I h13_precision.py <0|1|2|3>`, four workers, from 09:50:16 to 10:06:40 UTC. That was after
  the method was pushed (`5876e55`, the same second, before the workers started). Python 3.13.16, numpy 2.5.4;
  seeded. The summary script's hash was checked before it ran once on the four outputs. The scripts and outputs are
  in the pull request.
- **What came out**, by the summary fixed beforehand:
    - **The bias**: `ln(fitted k_rd / truth)` averages +0.139, 1.15 times the truth, with a 95% interval of +0.085 to
      +0.193. **R1 holds**: a bias is left at 128 worms per candidate.
    - **Single fits within 0.2**: 15 of 24 observed (0.62), and 0.62 predicted. **R2 holds**.
    - **Whose noise**: the sd within a population is 0.171, the data's own sd 0.000 (the floor), so one fit's sd is
      0.171. **R3 points to the fitter**, not the data.
    - The populations' means are uncorrelated with their 30 worms' own mean `k_rd` (+0.03).
    - Every fitted parameter's share within 0.2: `k_rd` 0.62, `k_dr` 0.79, `h_roam` 0.54, `h_dwell` 0.96, `dep` 0.54,
      `tau` 0.42, and the other seven 1.00.
- **What the fixed summary did not foresee** (*post hoc*, from the same outputs):
    - Optimiser seed 8 gives a higher `k_rd` than seed 7 in **all 12 populations**, by +0.074 to +0.468 in log (mean
      +0.215; a two-sided sign test gives p = 0.0005).
    - Seed 7's fits average +0.032, seed 8's +0.246.
    - So the within-population differences are not zero-mean noise, as the fixed formula for the within sd assumed.
      They are an offset that comes with the seed. The formula's 0.171 mixes that offset with noise, so it is
      reported but not leant on.
- **What it means**: the reading under test - that what remains is the precision of 30 worms - is **refuted**. Across
  populations, the data adds no measurable scatter. The fit tracks the optimiser's seed and not the worms.
    - **A reading, untested**: every fit shares the same simulation noise stream, seeded only by the generation. The
      seed then sets the path the search takes through it.
    - Along `k_rd`, the true gap in the distance is still below the noise, even at 128 worms. M5 (journal of
      2026-10-17) gave 0.005 at 1.2 times the truth, against a 16-worm sd of 0.025, about 0.009 at 128 if the noise scales as one over the
      square root of the worms (an assumption, not measured).
    - So the search settles wherever its path leaves it, and two seeds suffice to show that this is a different place
      per seed.
    - Whether the fitter is biased on average over seeds, or only seed-dependent, two seeds cannot tell. That is the
      next test.

## H13: Is the Fitter Biased over Seeds, or Only Seed-Dependent?

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The precision test above found the fit following the optimiser's seed: seed 8 above seed 7 in all 12 populations, and
a bias of 1.15 times on average. Two seeds cannot tell a biased fitter from a seed-dependent one. And every fit so far
has shared one simulation noise stream, seeded by the generation alone, which may be what ties a seed's path to the
same place for every population.

- **Question**: over many optimiser seeds, does the fitted `k_rd` average to the truth? Does a stream of each seed's
  own change that?
- **Method**:
    - **The target**: the noise-free one, 1024 worms simulated at the truth (seed 4242, as in M4 and M6), so the data
      adds no error.
    - **The fits**: optimiser seeds 7 to 14, with 128 simulated worms per candidate and X pinned at its baseline.
    - **Two arms**: *shared* is the fitter as it is, with the stream seeded by the generation alone. *Own* gives each
      seed its own stream, seeded by the optimiser seed and the generation. Nothing else differs; the two arms' fit is
      round 4's line for line, but for that one seed.
    - **The run**: `python3 -I h13_seeds.py <shared|own> <0..3>` from the repository's root, eight workers of two
      seeds each. Script SHA-256 `dcecec6957ff216784f12f0e31378f275d438bd5f6fddc8ef2470d9bca866aca`.
- **The statistics, fixed before the data**: `python3 -I h13_seeds_summary.py <the eight outputs>` (SHA-256
  `8a23ce10e939b81d7cd866a3d0fad21cdf5be402b97c489341a520ebaedf55a3`). It was seen to refuse an empty input. For each
  arm, with `x = ln(fitted k_rd / truth)` over the eight seeds, it reports:
    - the mean with a 95% t interval (7 degrees of freedom), and the sd;
    - the share within 0.2;
    - the seed-averaged estimate, `exp(mean)`;
    - every parameter's share within 0.2.
- **Rules fixed beforehand**, per arm:
    - **S1, biased on average**: the 95% interval excludes zero.
    - **S2, averaging cures it**: the 95% interval lies wholly within -0.2 to +0.2.
    - **S3, the stream matters**: the two arms' intervals do not overlap.
- **What each answer would mean**:
    - S1 failing and S2 holding: the fitter is unbiased but seed-noisy, and averaging seeds is a fix, at the cost of one
      fit per seed.
    - S1 holding: the search is biased in expectation, so averaging cannot cure it. The next suspect is the search's own
      update - an elite mean taken over a noisy, lopsided profile - and the next step is a different estimator.
