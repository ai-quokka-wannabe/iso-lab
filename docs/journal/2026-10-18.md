## H13: How Precisely Can 30 Worms Fix the Rate of Leaving Roaming?

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent, at the maintainer's request; not by the maintainer.

Yesterday's diagnosis (journal of 2026-10-17, § H13's Proof of Concept, a Diagnosis) found the fitter's noise a real
part of the bias in the rate of leaving roaming, `k_rd`. With 128 simulated worms per candidate, the noise-free fit
recovered it at 1.02 times the truth. On DEV's 30 worms, though, `k_rd` was within 0.2 for only 2 of 5 optimiser
seeds. It left an *untested* reading: what remains is the precision of 30 worms of 600 s. The maintainer asked for that
test. Written before it runs.

- **Question**: when the data is 30 worms of 600 s, how far does `ln(fitted k_rd / truth)` scatter around zero across
  fresh populations? How much of that scatter is the data, and how much the fitter?
- **Method**:
    - **The populations**: twelve, drawn from the truth as DEV was (population seed `5000 + 10 i`, simulation seed one
      more, `i` = 0 to 11), taking the first 30 worms of each.
    - **The fits**: each population is fitted twice, with optimiser seeds 7 and 8, using 128 simulated worms per
      candidate. X is pinned at its baseline, as the loop's refit has been: ablation dropped X and only X in all 25
      loops so far. Everything else is round 5's R4 statistics, round 4's fitter and DEV's start.
    - **The run**: `python3 -I h13_precision.py <0|1|2|3>` from the repository's root, four workers of three
      populations each, about 25 minutes. Script SHA-256 `74e232202481acf89355898c7bcf6e352ec71e33bdef4de57bb70912e8bf1d6c`.
- **The statistics, fixed before the data**: `python3 -I h13_precision_summary.py <the four outputs>` (SHA-256
  `e1290f5a345573ce4a02462cc1ebde5bb5adad3cde6e247fca0e1ba648e95247`). It was run once on an empty input and seen to
  refuse it. With `x = ln(fitted k_rd / truth)`, it reports:
    - each population's mean over its two seeds, `m`, and their difference, `d`;
    - the bias, the mean of `m` with a 95% t interval (11 degrees of freedom);
    - the sd within a population, `sqrt(mean(d^2) / 2)`, which is the fitter's remaining noise;
    - the data's own sd, `sqrt(var(m) - within^2 / 2)`, floored at zero;
    - the sd of one fit, from those two;
    - the predicted share of single fits within 0.2, under a normal law, and the observed share of the 24;
    - the correlation of `m` with the 30 worms' own mean `k_rd`;
    - the share within 0.2 for every fitted parameter.
- **Rules fixed beforehand**:
    - **R1, a bias left at 128 worms**: the 95% interval of the bias excludes zero.
    - **R2, the 0.2 tolerance is beyond 30 worms**: both the observed and the predicted share of single fits within 0.2
      are below 0.8. That is the rate criterion 1's "4 of 5" asks for.
    - **R3, whose noise**: the data's precision is the larger part if the data's variance exceeds the within-population
      variance; otherwise the fitter's remaining noise is.
- **What each answer would mean**:
    - R2 holding with R3 on the data: no brain or fitter can meet 0.2 from 30 worms. The benchmark's sample size, or the
      tolerance, must be set from the data's precision; the next step is the same test with more worms per population.
    - R2 holding with R3 on the fitter: more simulated worms, or averaging over optimiser seeds, is the next step.
    - R2 failing: 128 worms per candidate suffice, and DEV's 2 of 5 was in part the draw.
