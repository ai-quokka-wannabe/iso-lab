## H13's Proof of Concept on Synthetic Worms

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent, at the maintainer's request; not by the maintainer.

The maintainer proposed (in conversation, 2026-10-17) a brain built of modules each matched in mechanism to its job,
trained as a whole and then ground down by ablation and simplification, with held-out checks: hypothesis H13 in
[the hypotheses](../hypotheses/README.md#h13-mechanism-matched-modules-trained-as-a-whole-then-ground-down). The
benchmark is not frozen, and the rules forbid scoring a brain against the real worm before it is; so this proof of
concept tests the method, not a brain, on synthetic worms whose hidden brain is known. Written before any fit runs;
the simulator was written and timed once at its true parameters (64 individuals in 0.33 s) before this entry, and
nothing was fitted.

- **The question**: given only the behaviour statistics of a group of synthetic worms, does training a modular brain
  as a whole recover the hidden brain's parameters; does ablation tell the modules the behaviour needs from a decoy
  it does not; does dropping the decoy cost nothing; and does a held-out group flag a module that only memorises the
  development group?
- **The synthetic worm** (the notebook gives it in full): time steps of 0.2 s over 600 s. Five modules: **S**, a
  roam/dwell switch with two rates; **R**, a spontaneous reversal hazard that depends on the state, with reversals
  of exponential duration; **U**, an undulation oscillator whose frequency rises with speed (the slope `f1` is the
  module); **H**, a tap reflex from 300 s, a tap every 10 s for 30 taps, its response probability depressed by each
  tap and recovering with a time constant (the depression `dep` is the module); and **X**, a decoy - a reversal
  hazard that rises in the 2 s before each expected tap, set to zero in the truth. 14 parameters; 13 active in the
  truth. A population of 60 individuals, each parameter drawn log-normally around the truth's with a coefficient of
  variation of 15% (`g` stays zero), split 30 for development and 30 held out, seeded.
- **What the fit sees**: 12 statistics of each individual's observables only - its speed, bend and reversal onsets
  as read from the speed - never its hidden state: the share of fast 10-s windows, the speed in fast and in slow
  windows, the spontaneous reversal rate before the taps, the mean reversal duration, the tap response in taps 1-3,
  4-10, 11-20 and 21-30, the bend frequency in fast and in slow windows, and the reversal rate in the 2 s before taps
  against the rest of the tap period. The distance `D` between a model and a group is the mean over the 12 of the
  squared difference of the model's mean (over its simulated individuals) and the group's, in units of the group's
  between-individual standard deviation. The fitter is told the 15% individual variation; that is a simplification,
  stated.
- **The fit**: all 14 parameters together, in log space (the decoy's `g` as the exponential of a free number, so it
  can approach zero), by a cross-entropy evolution strategy: 24 candidates a generation, each simulating 16
  individuals with common random numbers, the best 6 setting the next mean and spread, 120 generations, from a start
  each parameter's log a seeded normal step of standard deviation 0.5 from the truth's (about a factor of 1.6).
  Scoring for the criteria: 64 simulated individuals, three seeds, against a group; the spread over the seeds is the
  scoring noise `s`.
- **The grind**: (a) ablation - from the fitted full model, each module pinned at its baseline (S: no switching,
  starting roaming; R: no spontaneous reversals; U: `f1` = 0; H: `dep` = 0; X: `g` = 0), scored on the held-out
  group, `ΔD` against the full model; (b) pruning - the fit run again with X removed (`g` pinned at zero), from the
  same start; (c) the held-out catch - on a development group of only the first 5 individuals, the mechanistic
  model fitted as above against a "fudge" model: the starting guess plus 12 free offsets added to its statistics,
  set to match the development group exactly.
- **What would refute it, said before the run** - each criterion judged on its own:
  1. *Recovery*: refuted if the median over the 13 active parameters of `|ln(fit / truth)|` is above 0.15 or any is
     above 0.4, or if the fitted `g` is above 0.02 per second (the start is about 0.2).
  2. *Ablation*: refuted if any of S, R, U and H raises the held-out `D` by less than five times `s`, or if X moves it
     by more than two times `s`.
  3. *Pruning*: refuted if the model without X scores worse on the held-out group than the full model by more than
     two times `s`.
  4. *Held-out catch*: refuted if the fudge model's held-out `D` minus its development `D` does not exceed the
     mechanistic model's by more than two times `s`.
- **The runs**: a smoke test of the code with 3 generations ran after the method was pushed, to check the code ran;
  its numbers are not used. Then `jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace notebooks/h13_modular_poc.ipynb`
  from a fresh kernel, Python 3.13.16, numpy 2.5.4, deterministic, every random number seeded - twice:
    - **First run, departing from the method** (08:48:13 to 08:50:12 UTC by the notebook's own timestamps, after the
      method was pushed in `97081fb` at 08:47:12; a draft of this entry said it finished at 09:18 - the time a polling
      loop of mine ended, not the run). The pull request's verification found that the fit did not use the common
      random numbers the method promises: each candidate in a generation drew its own individuals and its own
      simulation noise, so the selection of the best six was partly noise. Its results, kept in the pull request's
      first commit: recovery median 0.154, worst 0.92, `g` 0.072; held-out `D` of the fit 0.393.
    - **Second run, as written** (09:26:58 to 09:28:57 UTC): the same notebook with every candidate of a generation
      given the same individual variations and the same simulation noise. Two further changes, neither affecting a
      number: reversal onsets are read from the speed's sign inside the statistics code (the simulator's flag is
      ignored, so "observables only" can be read in the code), and every scoring's spread is printed. The results below
      are from this run. Unstated in the method and now stated: the evolution strategy moves its mean 0.7 of the way to
      the elite's mean each generation, keeps its spread at least 0.02, clips the decoy's log `g` to -12 to 3, and
      returns the mean of its final distribution, not its best candidate.
    - **Which `s`**: the method names one scoring noise, "the spread over the seeds", without saying of which scoring;
      the reading - the full model's spread on the held-out group, 0.048 - was chosen after the run. Criteria 2 and 4
      are given below under it and under three alternatives. Each `s` is the spread of single-seed scorings while each
      `D` averages three seeds, so the criteria are conservative on scoring noise; and none of the four allows for the
      variation between optimiser runs, as each arm was fitted once.
- **What came out** (the as-written run):
  1. *Recovery* - **refuted**. The median `|ln(fit / truth)|` over the 13 active parameters is 0.081, inside the bar of
     0.15, but four are beyond 0.4: the reverse speed at 2.41 times the truth, the rate of leaving roaming at 1.89,
     the spontaneous reversal hazards at 0.54 (roaming) and 0.60 (dwelling); and the decoy's `g` is 0.050, above
     0.02. The fit's held-out `D` is 0.166, against 0.036 for the truth itself.
  2. *Ablation* - passed under the chosen reading. Removing S raises the held-out `D` by 13.41, U by 2.29, R by 0.87
     and H by 0.41, each above `5s` = 0.24; removing X moves it by -0.04, inside `2s` = 0.10. Under each scoring's own
     spread it also passes; under the largest spread seen (0.179) R and H fall short of five times it; under the noise
     of a difference, the root of the two spreads' squares, H falls short by 0.006 (0.414 against 0.420).
  3. *Pruning* - passed: without X the held-out `D` is 0.048, against 0.166 for the full model - one fit in each
     arm, so this is an observation, not a measured effect of the decoy on the search.
  4. *Held-out catch* - passed under every reading. Trained on 5 individuals, the mechanistic model's held-out `D`
     exceeds its development `D` by 0.004 (0.224 to 0.227), the fudge model's by 0.386 (0.013 to 0.399); the
     difference, 0.382, is above twice any spread seen (the largest, 0.179, gives 0.358). The mechanistic model now
     also has the lower held-out `D` in absolute terms (0.227 against 0.399); in the departing run it did not. (The
     two development `D`s are scaled by a 5-individual spread and the held-out ones by a 30-individual spread; both
     models share that.)
- **A look afterwards, exploratory** (`python3 -I diag_r1.py`, in the body of pull request #87): three of the four badly recovered
  parameters stayed off by much the same factor as where the fit started - the reversal hazards from 0.48 and 0.41 of the truth to 0.54
  and 0.60, the reverse speed from 1.94 to 2.41 - and the rate of leaving roaming moved away, from 0.89 to 1.89. The
  fit's remaining misfit lies mostly in the statistics those parameters and the decoy drive: the anticipation
  statistic (a squared z of 0.97, the truth's 0.001), the spontaneous reversal rate (0.21 against 0.04) and the late
  tap responses (0.20 and 0.26 against 0.06 and 0.09). A fifth parameter, the habituation's recovery time, sits
  at 0.68 of the truth, just inside the bar (`|ln|` = 0.382, the bar 0.4).
- **What it means**: on this toy, the grind's tools work - ablation tells the needed modules from the decoy (under
  two of four readings of the noise, and narrowly short under a third), the decoy's removal leaves a better fit, and
  the held-out gap flags the memorising model, which also generalises worse than the honest one. Training the whole
  brain at once did not recover it, and the evidence points first to the search: the statistics do respond to the
  badly recovered parameters - the fit is worse than the truth just where they act - but they stayed off by much the same
  factor as at the start, and the as-written run reached a held-out `D` of 0.166 against the departing run's 0.393 (one fit each).
  So this run does not show behaviour matched without mechanism; whether some parameters are also weakly constrained
  (the reverse speed, which enters the statistics only through the 10-s window speeds, is a candidate) is a question
  for the next round. That round: the fit longer and from several starts and optimiser seeds (convergence, and the
  variation between fits), and slices of `D` along each badly recovered parameter (identifiability). The reading
  for the benchmark, kept as a reading: a score built only from group means is open to offsets fitted to a few
  individuals; here the held-out check caught it, and comparing distributions as well as means would make it harder.

## H13's Proof of Concept, Round 2: Search or Statistics?

The first round's as-written run (above) refuted recovery: four parameters of the hidden brain came back 1.7 to 2.4
times off, the decoy not driven to zero, three of the four staying off by much the same factor as at the start. Two
causes would give that, and they call for different cures: the search did not converge (cure: a better search), or
the 12 statistics barely constrain those parameters (cure: better statistics). This round tells them apart, on the
same synthetic worms, groups and code as round 1 (`notebooks/h13_modular_poc.ipynb`). Two wording notes from round 1's
third verification, which an edit failed to carry before that pull request merged, are applied above in this pull
request. Written before the count runs.

- **The method**: a short script (given in the pull request) that runs the round-1 notebook's code cells unchanged and
  then (a) **convergence**: the full fit for 360 generations instead of 120 from the same start; for 120 generations
  from the same start with optimiser seeds 8 and 9 instead of 7; and for 120 generations from two further starts drawn
  as round 1's was (seeds 2029 and 2030); each reported by its held-out `D` and its recovery, for the four badly
  recovered parameters (`k_rd`, `h_roam`, `h_dwell`, `v_rev`) and the decoy's `g`; and (b) **identifiability**:
  slices of the held-out `D` through the truth along each of the four - the parameter set to 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1, 1.25,
  1.6 and 2.5 times the truth's value, every other parameter at the truth - each point scored as in round 1 (64
  individuals, three seeds).
- **What would point where, said before the count**: *convergence* is the cause if at least three of the five fits
  recover all four parameters within `|ln(fit / truth)|` 0.4 and bring `g` under 0.02; *identifiability* is a cause
  for a parameter if its slice stays within 0.1 of the truth's held-out `D` at both 0.6 and 1.6 times the truth (a
  flat slice: the statistics barely see the parameter across that range). Both can hold; neither can. If neither, the
  next suspect is that the fit trades parameters against each other (a ridge), which slices through the truth cannot
  show.
- **The run**: `python3 -I h13_round2.py` from the repository's root (the script and its output in the pull request)
  at 09:37:03 UTC, finished 09:41:13, after the method was pushed (`5adc405`, 09:36:54); Python 3.13.16, numpy 2.5.4;
  deterministic, every random number seeded.
- **What came out**:
    - *Convergence*: none of the five fits recovers all four parameters within 0.4, and none brings `g` under 0.02
      (0.028 to 0.078). The 360-generation fit reaches a held-out `D` of 0.067 (s 0.019), nearer the truth's 0.036 than any other,
      with `h_dwell` within the bar (0.34) but `k_rd` (0.81), `h_roam` (0.70) and `v_rev` (0.93) still far off; the two
      re-seeded fits reach 0.157 and 0.104, the two re-started ones 0.178 and 0.488, each off in a different set of
      parameters (`h_roam` by up to 2.07 in `|ln|`).
    - *Identifiability*, the held-out `D` along each slice through the truth (0.036 at the truth):
        - `k_rd`: 0.316, 0.121, 0.068, 0.036, 0.048, 0.106, 0.313 at 0.4 to 2.5 times - within 0.1 of the truth's at both
          0.6 and 1.6 (by 0.085 and 0.070), so flat by the rule;
        - `h_roam`: 0.038, 0.034, 0.034, 0.036, 0.046, 0.065, 0.152 - flat, and the flattest: from 0.4 to 1.25 times the
          truth it is within the scoring noise;
        - `h_dwell`: 0.185, 0.086, 0.042, 0.036, 0.074, 0.191, 0.687 - not flat (0.155 above at 1.6 times);
        - `v_rev`: 0.305, 0.137, 0.049, 0.036, 0.066, 0.123, 0.261 - not flat, by 0.001 at 0.6 times (0.101 above).
- **The verification's look, beside the run** (`supp88.py`, given in
  [#88's review](https://github.com/ai-quokka-wannabe/iso-lab/pull/88#pullrequestreview-5557350098)): the
  360-generation fit has `k_rd` at 2.24 and `v_rev` at 2.54 times the truth - where each one's own slice gives a held-out
  `D` of about 0.25 to 0.3 - yet its `D` on the development group is 0.033, within the noise of the truth's 0.029 there; the
  four 120-generation fits stay at 0.05 to 0.40 on the development group, so they had not reached the bottom either.
- **What it means**: by the rules written before the count, convergence is not the cause, and identifiability is a
  cause for two of the four - but the rule's margin, 0.1 against the truth's 0.036, is wide, and the plain case is one
  parameter, not two. The spontaneous reversal hazard in roaming is barely seen: its slice stays within the scoring
  noise from 0.4 to 1.25 times the truth, and the long fit's value (0.50) lies inside that flat stretch. The rate of
  leaving roaming passes the rule, yet its slice is three times the truth's `D` at 0.6 and 1.6 times; and the reverse
  speed fails it by 0.0004 unrounded, far inside the scoring noise of about 0.011 - a coin-toss. The strongest
  argument against "more search" is the long fit's development `D`, equal to the truth's within noise: the search
  found a point as good as the truth on its own objective without finding the truth (on the held-out group its `D`
  doubles, to 0.067, where the truth's barely moves, so part of that match may be fitting the noise of those 30 worms). There the rate of leaving
  roaming and the reverse speed are both far off, each by enough to raise `D` to about 0.25 to 0.3 alone, so they offset each
  other: the ridge named beforehand, read from that one fit, not tested. So, on this toy, matching the behaviour on the
  data the fit sees does not pin the mechanism - round 1's withdrawn sentence comes back, now with evidence, for one
  parameter that the statistics barely see and two that trade against each other. For H13 the cure points to the
  statistics rather than more search: statistics that separate what the ridge trades - reversal rates conditioned on
  the state as seen from the speed, the bout lengths of roaming and dwelling, the reverse speed read directly - and
  for the benchmark the same lesson as round 1's, stronger: the worm's comparison space should include statistics
  conditioned on state and whole distributions, or a brain can match it by trading one mechanism against another. The
  next round: add those statistics, and see whether recovery passes round 1's criterion.

## H13's Proof of Concept, Round 3: Statistics That Separate the Trade

Round 2 (above) pointed to the statistics rather than the search: the roaming reversal hazard barely seen by the 12
statistics, the rate of leaving roaming and the reverse speed appearing to trade against each other. This round adds
statistics aimed at exactly those, and asks whether recovery then passes round 1's criterion 1. Written before the
count runs; the script was written and checked to parse, and not run.

- **The added statistics**, five, read from the speed alone as the others are: the spontaneous reversal rate within
  the fast 10-s windows and within the slow ones, before the taps (to separate the two hazards); the mean length of a
  bout of consecutive fast windows and of slow ones (to pin the switching rates directly); and each individual's
  median reverse speed (to pin `v_rev` directly). 17 statistics in all, the distance as in round 1 over all 17.
- **The method**: a short script (given in the pull request) runs the round-1 notebook's code cells with `stats`
  replaced by the 12 statistics plus the five, on the same population, groups and start; then the full fit as in
  round 1 (120 generations, optimiser seed 7) and, as a second look, 360 generations; each reported by its held-out
  `D`, the truth's held-out `D` on the 17 statistics, and its recovery.
- **What would count, said before the count**: round 1's criterion 1, unchanged - recovery passes if the median over
  the 13 active parameters of `|ln(fit / truth)|` is at most 0.15, none is above 0.4, and the decoy's `g` is at most
  0.02 per second - judged on the 120-generation fit; the 360-generation fit is reported beside it, not judged. An
  expectation, not a criterion: the decoy has no new statistic aimed at it, so `g` may stay above 0.02 even if the
  other parameters are recovered; if so, that is reported as recovery failing on the decoy alone.
- **The run**: `python3 -I h13_round3.py` from the repository's root (the script and its output in the pull request)
  at 10:18:02 UTC, finished 10:20:32, after the method was pushed (`e173bad`, 10:17:57 by GitHub's activity log); Python 3.13.16, numpy 2.5.4;
  deterministic, every random number seeded.
- **What came out**: on the 17 statistics the truth's held-out `D` is 0.077 (s 0.006). The 120-generation fit - the
  one judged - recovers every one of the 13 active parameters within the bar: the median `|ln(fit / truth)|` is 0.038
  (round 1, as written: 0.081) and the largest 0.348 (round 1: 0.878), the rate of leaving roaming at 1.36 times the
  truth, the two reversal hazards at 0.71 and 0.76, the reverse speed at 1.02 (round 1: 2.41); its held-out `D` is
  0.154 (s 0.034). But the decoy's `g` is 0.059, above 0.02, so **criterion 1 fails, on the decoy alone** - the case
  written down beforehand as possible. The 360-generation fit, reported and not judged: largest `|ln|` 0.308, median
  0.051, `g` 0.031, held-out `D` 0.067 against the truth's 0.077 on three scoring seeds; re-scored by the pull request's verification
  with ten seeds ([#89's review](https://github.com/ai-quokka-wannabe/iso-lab/pull/89#pullrequestreview-5557386472)), it
  is 0.076 against the truth's 0.067, a paired difference of +0.008 (sd 0.017) - level with the truth within the
  scoring noise on both groups (0.035 against 0.037 on the development group). A draft of this entry read the
  three-seed figure as below the truth and explained it by fitting the development group's noise, which would
  raise the held-out `D`, not lower it; both are withdrawn.
- **What it means**: in this fit, from round 1's start and optimiser seed, the added statistics did what round 2
  said they should - the reverse speed's side of the trade is broken (it now comes back at 1.02 times the truth) and
  every real parameter is recovered within the bar - so for H13 the statistics, more than the search, were what
  recovery needed; whether recovery holds across starts and optimiser seeds, as round 2 tested on the 12 statistics,
  is not yet measured. The rate of leaving roaming stays at 1.36 times the truth in both fits, unmoved by three times
  the search: a hint of a remaining bias, perhaps from the bout lengths, which are cut off at the recording's edges and
  run through the tap period. What
  is left is the decoy: no statistic is aimed at it, and the fit leaves a small anticipation term rather than
  driving it to zero. That is the grind's job, not the fit's: round 1 showed ablation finds the decoy and pruning it
  improves the fit. The next round tests the whole loop as one procedure - fit on the 17 statistics, ablate each
  module, drop what does not earn its place by round 1's ablation rule, refit - judged by criterion 1 on what is left.
  For the benchmark: the comparison space should hold state-conditioned rates, bout lengths and direct speed
  readings, the statistics that made recovery possible here.

## H13's Proof of Concept, Round 4: The Whole Loop

Rounds 1 to 3 (above) tested the grind's parts one at a time; round 3, on 17 statistics, recovered every real parameter
in one fit but left the decoy at 0.059. The decoy is the grind's job: ablation found it and pruning it helped in
round 1. This round runs the whole loop as one procedure and judges what is left by round 1's criterion 1. Written before
the count runs; the script was written and checked to parse, and not run. Its SHA-256 is
`16d24604209c936ab0ebb806a03b6c992fb0815c1514bac39ac515ab071e85e2` (`sha256sum h13_round4.py`), so the script run can be checked against this entry.

- **The loop**, on round 3's 17 statistics, population, groups and start: (1) the full fit, 120 generations; (2)
  ablation of each module on the held-out group, as in round 1; (3) every module whose ablation raises the held-out
  `D` by less than `2s` is dropped, `s` being the full fit's held-out scoring spread (round 1's chosen reading, fixed
  here before the run); (4) a refit with the dropped modules pinned at their baseline, 120 generations; (5) criterion 1
  on the refit, with two additions: no real module (S, R, U, H) may be dropped, and a dropped X counts as `g` = 0. The
  loop runs for optimiser seeds 7, 8 and 9, each through all five steps.
- **What would count, said before the count**: the loop works if it passes for at least two of the three seeds; it
  is refuted if it passes for fewer than two. Each seed's dropped modules, ablation deltas and refit recovery are
  reported whatever the outcome.
- **The run**: `python3 -I h13_round4.py` from the repository's root (the script and its output in the pull request;
  its SHA-256 checked equal to the one above just before the run) at 10:31:07 UTC, finished 10:35:15, after the
  method was pushed (`4215890`, 10:31:01); Python 3.13.16, numpy 2.5.4; deterministic, every random number seeded.
- **What came out**: **the loop is refuted by the rule written before the count - it passes for one seed of three.**
  Its pruning was right every time: in all three seeds ablation kept S, R, U and H (their deltas 43.3 to 43.5, 1.29
  to 1.37, 1.68 to 2.20 and 0.29 to 0.71, against `2s` of 0.06 to 0.09) and dropped X (deltas -0.025 to -0.052), and
  each refit, the decoy pinned at zero, brought the held-out `D` from 0.15-0.17 to 0.061-0.067. Recovery then passed
  for seed 7 (median `|ln|` 0.100, largest 0.374) and failed for seed 9 (largest 0.416: the rate of leaving roaming at
  1.52 times the truth, 0.016 over the bar) and seed 8 (largest 0.564: the habituation's recovery time at 0.57 times).
- **What it means**: the grind's decisions are sound on this toy - ablation removed the decoy and only the decoy,
  three times out of three, and the pruned brain fits better - but the loop as a whole does not yet recover the
  mechanism reliably. Two things stand in the way, both readings to test: the rate of leaving roaming comes back 1.34
  to 1.52 times too high in every seed, the bias round 3 hinted at (the bout lengths, cut at the recording's edges and
  run through the tap period, are the first suspect); and (amended in #91, from #90's review) the habituation's depression and recovery time appear
  to trade between seeds - the deeper the depression, the shorter the recovery (1.47 with 0.57, 1.28 with 0.86, 1.25
  with 1.10 times) - but that is three points in rank order, which chance gives one time in three, and no slice along
  them has been run, so whether the four tap-response statistics separate them is untested. The rate of returning to
  roaming is also high in every seed (1.13 to 1.39 times), so switching is fast in both directions, which points at the
  switching timescale the bout lengths carry. The refits' held-out `D` (0.061 to 0.067) is level with the truth's on
  these statistics (0.077 on three scoring seeds, 0.067 on ten in round 3's correction), so the failing
  seeds look like a matter of what the statistics can tell apart, not of the search. The next steps: bout lengths
  measured only from complete bouts before the taps, and a statistic of the habituation's recovery (the tap response
  after a pause) - then the loop again.

## H13's Proof of Concept, Round 5: The Two Obstacles, Targeted

Round 4 (above) left two obstacles to recovery once the decoy is pruned: the rate of leaving roaming 1.34 to 1.52
times too high in every seed, the bout lengths the first suspect (cut at the recording's edges, run through the tap
period); and an apparent trade between habituation's depression and recovery time, which four coarse tap-response bins

## H13's Proof of Concept, a Diagnosis: Why the Switching Rates Come Out Too High

The maintainer asked for the cause to be understood before anything more is changed. Round 5 left one bias standing:
the rate of leaving roaming (`k_rd`) above the truth in all 20 refits, 1.14 to 2.29 times, and the rate of leaving
dwelling (`k_dr`) above it too in R4's five (1.13 to 1.39). Both faster in the same direction is the signature of
switching sped up with the time spent roaming kept. This entry is a diagnosis, not a change to the brain: no variant
is scored, nothing is tuned.

- **Question**: where does the bias come from? Three candidates, each with what it predicts:
    - **C1, the data.** Every refit fits the same 30 DEV worms, so a sampling accident in them is shared by all 20,
      and would look exactly like a systematic bias. It predicts that at a large simulation size the distance to DEV
      is lower for faster switching than for the truth (M1), that fits to fresh populations scatter on both sides of
      the truth (M3), and that a fit to a target with no sampling error recovers `k_rd` within 0.2 (M4).
    - **C2, the fitter's noise.** Each candidate is judged on the mean of only 16 simulated worms, so its expected
      distance is its squared bias plus its simulation variance. If faster switching makes worms more alike (more
      switches averaged within 600 s), the fitter is pulled towards it. *A hunch*, from the arithmetic of the
      distance, not from a source. It predicts that faster switching wins at 16 worms (M2) but not at 1024 (M1),
      that the fresh populations' fits are all above the truth (M3), and that the fit to a target with no sampling
      error is above it too, beyond 0.2 (M4).
    - **C3, the statistics.** Round 5's reading - the fast and slow windows count reversals as slowness - cannot by
      itself bias the fit in expectation: the simulator's worms pass through the same statistics as the data's. At
      most it costs information. It is kept in reserve as the explanation if neither C1 nor C2 holds.
- **Method**: `python3 -I h13_diag.py <M1|M2|M3|M4>` from the repository's root, on round 5's R4 statistics and round
  4's fitter, unchanged (the script's SHA-256
  `bae7b95ae8e2fe740bbc9cc0f8b2e8dd2137a5b28048227c6b31aeca1c3fbc20`). FAST is the truth with `k_rd` and `k_dr`
  scaled by R4's median refit ratios, 1.39 and 1.20.
    - M1: the distance to DEV for the truth and for FAST, at 1024 individuals, seeds 201 to 204.
    - M2: the same at 16 individuals, seeds 301 to 500, and each one's between-worm spread relative to DEV's.
    - M3: the full fit, optimiser seed 7, to three fresh populations of 30.
    - M4: the full fit, seed 7, to 1024 worms simulated at the truth. It also prints DEV's own mean individual
      `k_rd` and `k_dr`.
- **Rules fixed beforehand**:
    - C1 holds if M1's FAST distance is lower than the truth's by more than twice M1's spread, M4's `k_rd` is within
      0.2 of the truth, and at least one of M3's three is below the truth.
    - C2 holds if M2's FAST distance is lower than the truth's by more than twice its standard error, M1's is not,
      and M4's `k_rd` is more than 0.2 above the truth.
    - Both may hold in part. If neither holds, C3 is next.
- **What each answer would mean for a fix**:
    - Under C1 no fitter can remove the bias. The 0.2 tolerance would be tighter than 30 worms of 600 s can resolve,
      and the next step is to measure that precision by refitting fresh populations, not to change the brain.
    - Under C2 the fitter is the fault, and the fix is more simulated worms per candidate, or a distance that
      subtracts its own simulation variance, followed by round 4's loop again.

### The Fix the Diagnosis Points To, Pre-registered

Written after M1, M2 and M4 had come in and before M3 had. Their numbers go in the results below. M2 and M4 point to
C2, and so does M2's spread: faster switching makes the simulated worms more alike. That is enough to state the fix
and its test now.

- **The change**: the fitter's distance subtracts its own simulation variance. Each statistic's term becomes
  `(mean - target)^2 - s^2 / 16`, scaled by the target's spread as before, where `s^2` is the candidate's sample
  variance over its own 16 worms. The term `s^2 / 16` is an unbiased estimate of the noise that the squared gap of a
  16-worm mean carries on average, so the corrected term's expectation is the squared bias alone. Nothing else
  changes: round 5's R4 statistics, round 4's loop, and the held-out scoring at 64 worms, which is the evaluation.
  It costs no simulation.
- **The command**: `python3 -I h13_fix.py <M4|7|8|9|10|11>` from the repository's root. M4 is the full fit, seed 7,
  to 1024 worms at the truth; a seed runs round 4's whole loop on DEV for that optimiser seed. Six processes run in
  parallel. The script's SHA-256 is `ef7c2ac2dd23618d6958991f77584cf5972ba874cd53a9a96ad42058fa23ebb9`.
- **It succeeds** - C2 then accounts for the bias, and the fix removes it - only if all three hold:
    - the corrected M4 recovers `k_rd` within 0.2;
    - the loop passes criterion 1 for at least 4 of the 5 seeds;
    - `k_rd` is within 0.2 for at least 4 of the 5.
- **Otherwise it is refuted**, and the shortfall is reported as it stands. R4 under the same seeds gave 3 of 5 and 0 of
  5. That habituation's two parameters still trade is expected: they are not this fix's target, and they are reported
  without a threshold.
