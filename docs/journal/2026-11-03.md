**Verdict, by the rule at the head of [B.md](../hypotheses/experiments/B.md): C18 is refuted**, by clauses (2), (4)
and (5), each at alpha 0.05 / 6. Its first half stands. The pure network has a cliff, a reach that stays finite below
d = 2.255 and becomes a share of the network above it, and two-hop priming is well below one-hop. Its cures do not
stand as cures. Lateral inhibition and the fan-out penalty move the cliff to higher connectivity rather than remove
it. So does the conserved budget at a gain above one, to lower connectivity. The one variant without a cliff, the
budget at a gain below one, also spreads nothing to speak of.

**Against the expectations registered on 2026-10-31:**

- *The pure network reads cliff near d = 2.5*: held. D is 2.255. The 1% crossing is interpolated from below the first
  grid point past 1%, 2.50, which is also where the coefficient of variation peaks.
- *The budget at c = 0.2 never passes 1%, so it has no cliff and no spread*: held. Its highest mean is 0.00023.
- *The budget at c = 0.6 reads cliff, firing clause (4)*: held. *Near d = 1, the lead's topological transition*: did
  not hold. D is 1.528, and the coefficient of variation peaks at 1.75, not at 1. Why is not settled here. A
  candidate, *a hunch* not tested: with a budget, a unit of out-degree k passes 0.6 / k of its activation to each
  target each step, so at the fixed point a target with that one input holds 1.2 / k of it. A target with one active
  input is then activated only when k is small, and the activation threshold of 0.1 raises the effective percolation
  point above the graph's own, which is 1. The lead's region III is likewise defined by units "above a given positive
  threshold" (journal of 2026-10-31), but in an unbounded linear model whose activity grows without end, where any
  threshold is crossed in time.
- *The fan-out penalty and inhibition read cliff at higher degrees, firing (5) and (2)*: held, at 7.502 and 4.753.
- *If that is how it comes out, the gain, not the budget, decides*: as far as these five networks go, yes. The budget
  removes the cliff only at a gain where nothing spreads; at a gain where it spreads, it has a cliff.
- *Clause (6)* had no registered expectation. Its ratio, 0.400, is what a linear reading predicts. Below the
  threshold, near the seed at N = 10 000, the graph is a tree, so a unit one hop out holds 0.4 at the fixed point and
  one two hops out 0.4 squared, 0.16. The clause tests the arithmetic of the edge weight more than any property of the
  spread.

**The descriptive readings.** All slopes are about -1 below (from -0.96 to -1.01) and about 0 above for every
variant that reads cliff, as the controls with a real threshold do. So the plan's one false *cliff*, a reach with no
threshold that outgrows 10 000 units by 2 D, shows no sign of biting. The 1.1 D share splits the cliffs into two
kinds. The fan-out penalty (0.998) and inhibition (0.830) are near one, the mark of a jump. The budget at c = 0.6
(0.009) is small, like the giant component's 0.074, the mark of a continuous rise. The pure network's 0.206 is
neither, and the controls show why this reading is fragile. The pure network's 1.1 D, 2.481, lies just under the
dynamic threshold at 2.5, as the jump control's 1.1 D, 2.478, lies just under its jump, which gives that control a
share of 0.000. A threshold located by an interpolated 1% crossing can sit a grid step before a jump, and 1.1 D need
not clear it. The lead's own locator, the peak of the coefficient of variation, agreed with the 1% crossing to within
a grid step or two for the pure network and the budget at c = 0.6. It did not for the fan-out penalty (0.25) or
inhibition (0.75), where the fraction is near zero along most of the sweep and the coefficient is noisy. It stays
descriptive.
