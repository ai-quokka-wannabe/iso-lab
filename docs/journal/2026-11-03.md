**Verdict, by the rule at the head of [B.md](../hypotheses/experiments/B.md): C18 is refuted**, by clauses (2), (4)
and (5), each at alpha 0.05 / 6. Its first half stands. The pure network has a cliff, a reach that stays finite below
d = 2.255 and becomes a share of the network above it, and two-hop priming is well below one-hop. Its cures do not
stand as cures. Lateral inhibition and the fan-out penalty move the cliff to higher connectivity rather than remove
it. So does the conserved budget at a gain above one, to lower connectivity. The one variant without a cliff, the
budget at a gain below one, also spreads nothing to speak of.

**Against the expectations registered on 2026-10-31:**

- *The pure network reads cliff near d = 2.5*: held. D is 2.255. The 1% crossing is interpolated from below the first
  grid point past 1%, 2.50, which is also where the coefficient of variation peaks.
- *The budget at c = 0.2 never passes 1%, so it has no cliff and no spread*: held. Its highest mean is 0.00023.
- *The budget at c = 0.6 reads cliff, firing clause (4)*: held. *Near d = 1, the lead's topological transition*: did
  not hold. D is 1.528, and the coefficient of variation peaks at 1.75, not at 1. Why is not settled here. A
  candidate, *a hunch* not tested: with a budget, a unit of out-degree k passes 0.6 / k of its activation to each
  target each step, so at the fixed point a target with that one input holds 1.2 / k of it. A target with one active
  input is then activated only when k is small, and the activation threshold of 0.1 raises the effective percolation
  point above the graph's own, which is 1. The lead's region III is likewise defined by units "above a given positive
  threshold" (journal of 2026-10-31), but in an unbounded linear model whose activity grows without end, where any
  threshold is crossed in time.
- *The fan-out penalty and inhibition read cliff at higher degrees, firing (5) and (2)*: held, at 7.502 and 4.753.
- *If that is how it comes out, the gain, not the budget, decides*: as far as these five networks go, yes. The budget
  removes the cliff only at a gain where nothing spreads; at a gain where it spreads, it has a cliff.
- *Clause (6)* had no registered expectation. Its ratio, 0.400, is what a linear reading predicts. Below the
  threshold, near the seed at N = 10 000, the graph is a tree, so a unit one hop out holds 0.4 at the fixed point and
  one two hops out 0.4 squared, 0.16. The clause tests the arithmetic of the edge weight more than any property of the
  spread.

## The Reference Statistics: the Plan, Written Before the Statistics

STATE.md item 2 (d2). This part was written, and pushed, before any statistic below was computed. Block 1 is being
fetched again on this machine (it had been deleted). Its rows were seen on 2026-10-31: the medians of 13 columns are in
that night's journal. No eigen-shape, interval or half has been computed.

**The set.** Block 1's 179 recordings that (d1)'s rule measures, unchanged. The binary recomputes their rows from the
files rather than reading (d1)'s TSV, because the eigen-shapes need every frame's angles, which the rows do not keep;
the rows it computes are checked against (d1)'s output, whose SHA-256 begins `7cebf503`.

**Where the code goes.** The eigen-shapes go into `features` (`features::eigenshapes`), because they define a measure
that both bodies are projected on (docs/benchmark/FEATURES.md § Common Units), and a measure is what `features` makes.
The medians, quartiles and bootstrap intervals go into `benchmark` (`benchmark::reference`), beside the comparisons that
will use them and the generator they share. The binary, `reference-stats <block>`, lives in `benchmark`, which now
depends on `features` to read the files.

**The eigen-shapes.**

- *Input*: every frame of each measured recording's 900 s window that has eight arcs, as its eight mean-removed arc
  angles (`profile::mean_removed_angles`), the frames (d1)'s angle spreads were computed from.
- *Fit*: the covariance of the eight angles about their mean, and its eigenvectors by Jacobi rotations. Removing the
  mean angle makes the eight sum to zero, so at most seven components carry variance.
- *Weighting*: each recording counts equally, each of its frames weighted by one over its frame count. The benchmark's
  unit is a worm, one row per recording; a recording's frame count depends on its camera's rate (about 25 to 32 frames
  per second) and on how many frames were tracked (0.3% to 40% missing), neither of which is a property of the worm's
  posture. With frames pooled equally, a well-tracked worm filmed at 32 frames per second would weigh about twice a
  poorly tracked one at 25. The frame-pooled fit is reported beside it as a check on how much the choice
  matters, not as an alternative.
- *Reported*: each component's eigenvalue and share of the variance (eigenvalue over trace), the cumulative shares, the
  fewest components carrying 95%, and the eight loadings of each component. The shares' 95% interval comes from the same
  bootstrap as below, resampling recordings: each resample's covariance is rebuilt from its recordings' moments.

**The measures' statistics.**

- The 23 measure columns of (d1): eight angle spreads, two speeds, four state shares and nine durations, rates and
  counts, the last nine marked *provisional* as (d1) marks them. The body length and the missing-frame share are
  context, summarised the same way and not compared.
- Each column's median, first and third quartiles and interquartile range (type 7 quantiles, as `benchmark::compare`
  computes them), and a 95% percentile bootstrap interval for the median: **9999 resamples of the rows, seed
  20261103**, one set of resamples for every column.
- A measured recording with a `NaN` measure (a mode with no event) would make the binary stop rather than drop or fill
  it; (d1) found none in block 1.

**The halves.** The first half is the records at draw positions 1 to 100 of block 1, the second positions 101 to 200,
in `data/manifests/owmd-n2-order.tsv`; each keeps only its measured recordings. The split is fixed by the draw's order,
chosen here before any half is computed.

- Each half gets the same statistics, with the same resample count and seed.
- *The test*: `benchmark::compare::compare_measures` on the 23 measures, with its default settings (999 shuffles, Holm's
  correction, alpha 0.05), seed 20261103. Both halves are random draws from one population, so no measure should differ.
  The chance that at least one is flagged when none truly differs is at most 5%.
- *Beside it*: the classifier test, `benchmark::two_sample_test`, with its default settings and the same seed.
  Its chance of telling the halves apart by chance is likewise 5%.
- *What a failure would mean*: a flagged measure, or halves told apart, would be reported as the check failing. It
  would not be explained away. It could be the 5% of chance; a bug in a row or a test; or something in the draw's
  order that is not random with respect to the recordings, such as date. The record ids and dates of each half would then
  be compared.
- *The eigen-shapes of each half*, fitted the same way, are described by their shares and by the absolute cosine between
  each half's first four components. No pass mark is set for these; the formal check is the per-measure test.

**What would make these statistics unfit to be the reference**: a halves comparison that fails beyond what chance
explains, or a recomputed row that differs from (d1)'s. Either would stop (d2) at a report, with the reference not
settled.

## E4's Pilot, and Its Plan Written Again

E4, *Hebbian learning needs a stabiliser and a "used together" rule*, was parked on 2026-10-31. Its plan failed
verification: its units took the sign of their input, so no rescaling of a unit's weights could change what they did,
and a stabiliser could not act by construction (journal of 2026-10-31, § The E1-E5 Plans, Taken Up Again). STATE.md,
*Blocked*, asked for a pilot first, and for the plan to be written only once the pilot showed Hebb's rule alone
capturing and a stabiliser able to change it. This is that pilot, and the plan it allows.

**Two dead ends first**, kept in the notebook's first section. Both use the first plan's own ±1 units and a whole
frequent pattern P, trained 50 times among ten others, on seeds `[41000, s]` for s = 0 to 5.

- **Hebb's rule** captured every cue on every seed. The controls, trained with P once, captured 0.03 to 0.16.
- **Oja's update with the unit's output clamped to the presented state** makes each weight an exponential average of

- **Clipping every weight** at 1/N captured 0.58 to 1; at 2/N, 0.96 to 1; at 4/N, every cue.
- **Why they fail.** With dense ±1 patterns, the frequent pattern wins the *sign* of nearly every weight, and bounding

**After review.** The second agent's first review of #197 reproduced the pilot exactly. It found that the pilot's

- **Capture is self-ignition, not a response to the cue.** Under the "used together" rule, no weight from the cued
  units into the pair grows, so no cue reaches the pair. The pair switches itself on from its resting drive once its
  weight passes w* = 1 - 2b = 0.9: its settled rate is b / (1 - w), which the notebook checks from 0.5 to 1. A
  network's capture share is therefore 0 or 1.
- **Which stabiliser "works" is decided by where its bound sits against w*, not by its kind.**
    - At the pilot's first constants, the bounds of Oja's rule and row normalisation (1) and of BCM (about 3.6) sit
      above w*, and homeostatic scaling's fixed point (about 0.06) sits below it.
    - At other constants each one reverses, on all five pilot seeds `[42000, s]`:
        - row normalisation to a norm of 0.8 or 0.5 stops the capture;

        - scaling at gamma 0.5 and target 0.8 captures on the three seeds whose W_01 passed 0.9 and not on the two
          whose did not.

  So "only a stabiliser that bounds activity works" is withdrawn. The 90-setting sweep only covered targets that put
  scaling far below w*, which the notebook now says.
- **Two of the plan's clauses could not fail.** Under the "used together" rule, every weight touching an undriven unit
  stays exactly zero; the notebook asserts this. So the hub can never capture, and the full variant's side of the rule
  clause was fixed.
- **The saturation clause would have fired on an artefact.** Under scaling, the pair's weight jitters around its fixed
  point by trial type, so the change over the last 50 trials reads which trials came last.

On this toy network, then, the hunch's outcomes follow from its constants and its construction. None of them is an
empirical question the network can answer.

**The plan is written again in that light.** On the network it states four assertions, to be built in Rust:

1. Hebb's pair weight passes ignition.
2. The pair ignites exactly above w*.
3. Each stabiliser captures exactly when its bound exceeds w*.
4. Undriven units' weights stay at zero.

The design rule the pilot leaves - a Hebbian loop needs its gain bounded below ignition, and resting activity must not

## The Interval's Level, Pinned

The verifier of #200 changed the reference statistics' bootstrap interval from the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles of the
resampled medians to the 5% and 95%, making it a 90% interval, and every test in `crates/benchmark/src/reference.rs`
still passed. The level is what a reader takes from every interval in § The Reference Statistics of Block 1, and it was
written twice: once in `summarise` and once in the binary's eigen-shape intervals.

- **One constant.** `INTERVAL_TAILS = (0.025, 0.975)` in `reference.rs` is now the one place the level is written.
  `summarise` and `reference-stats` both read it, and a test asserts its value.
- **The interval's behaviour.** The same test recomputes the resampled medians from the draws: 1000 distinct rows,
  `((i * 37) % 1000) / 1000 + sqrt(i) 1e-7`, 999 resamples, seed 5. It checks that the order statistics bracketing
  both ends do not tie (24 against 25, and 973 against 974), and that the interval is exactly the medians' 2.5% and
  97.5% quantiles. It must also differ from the quantiles at 2%, 2.4%, 2.49%, 2.51%, 2.6%, 3%, 3.5% and 5%, and their
  mirrors.
- **The narrowing test's window is tightened** from 0.06-0.14 to 0.088-0.11. At 400 uniform rows the median's
  standard error is about 1 / (2 sqrt(400)) = 0.025, so a 95% interval spans about 0.098 and a 90% one about 0.082.
  These are normal-theory widths for a continuous uniform, so they are approximate. The test's interval is 0.09875.

Two earlier versions of this change claimed more than they showed, and the verifier of #201 caught both:

- **The first** used 31 squared integers. Their resampled medians tie, so every level from about 91.4% to 95.4% gave
  the same interval, and a 93% interval passed every test.
- **The second** said untied data could not pin the level, because "the ties remained in every case". That was wrong.
  My search had asked for a whole run of order statistics around each end to be strictly increasing. That stricter
  criterion failed at 40, 400 and 1000 rows, and over seeds 1 to 399 at 40, 41, 64 and 100 rows. The criterion that
  matters is only the two bracketing order statistics at each end. By that criterion the verifier found 400 rows at
  seed 5, and 1000 rows at seeds 5 and 11, untied at both ends. In those cases no symmetric level from 0 to 0.1, in
  steps of 0.0001, other than 0.025 gave the same interval. The test now uses the 1000-row case at seed 5.

## Why Steering Trails Never Steering in Warm Layouts

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

STATE.md item 9 (b) leaves one question open: why gated steering of any kind still trails never steering in warm
layouts. Over the five warm layouts of 2026-10-26 (82100 to 82104), every gated arm so far ended below never steering.
The no-margin global rule was below in all five, each interval wholly below zero. The graded gain's warm band is
−0.0490 (−0.1087 to −0.0041) (journal of 2026-10-30, § The Global Rule Without the Margin on Ten Fresh Layouts and
§ H14's Graded Hunger Gain on Ten Fresh Layouts). In the cold band the same arms are mixed. A toy world: none of
this is evidence about *C. elegans*.

**The question**: in warm layouts, what part of the worm's steering costs it energy, and is warmth what matters?

**A reading of the code, done before the run.** Is the toy world the same on both sides? I traced gr-fec's code
(`reference/gr-fec/src/embodied.py`, `lifeworld.py`, `steer.py`), and it appears to be mirror-symmetric: an equivariant
system. Mirror a layout in x and every temperature t becomes 40 − t. The brain's temperature prototypes have a fixed
width, so they map onto mirrored ones. Its "colder" and "warmer" neighbours swap, and so do the left and right lateral
readings. A left turn becomes a right turn. The noise is symmetric, and so is the worms' starting heading. Steering
costs no energy and does not change speed: `DirBody.act` only rotates the heading by 0.15 rad a second, and
`Body.metabolise` never reads the steering command. If this reading is right, a "warm" layout and its mirror image,
a "cold" layout with the same geometry, give the same lives in distribution, though not worm for worm. The warm band's
harm would then belong to those five layouts, not to warmth. The reading is mine and unchecked by any run; the mirror
arms below check it.

**Candidate mechanisms**, each *a hunch*:

- **A, an immature map.** Steering on the first day's map leaves the worm worse placed, or its food worse depleted, by
  hour 24. Refuted if steering only from hour 24 (`Gl`) does not beat steering throughout (`G`): A's interval must
  lie wholly above zero.
- **B, steering pulls the worm off the band.** The greedy rule compares only the current concept's two neighbours,
  never the current concept itself, so a worm on the band can be sent off it. In a warm layout, colder means towards
  the gap and the smelly food. Refuted unless the cold-ward turns alone (`Gc`) cost against never steering, with the
  interval wholly below zero, while the warm-ward turns alone (`Gw`) do not.
- **C, steering overshoots the band to the plate's edge.** The band sits at x = 35 ± 6 on a plate of radius 50, and a
  10-s decision turns the head by up to 1.5 rad. Refuted unless the warm-ward turns alone (`Gw`) cost against never
  steering, wholly below zero, while the cold-ward turns alone (`Gc`) do not. The share of time beyond the band (x above
  41) is a descriptive check.
- **D, not warmth but the layouts** (from the reading above). Refuted if the mirrored layouts' harm differs from the
  warm layouts', the interval of (mG − mN) − (G − N) excluding zero.

**The design.**

- **The brain**: H14's graded hunger gain (`graded/graded.py`'s `GradedBrain`, imported unchanged), the best gated

## The Eigen-Shape Amplitudes: the Plan, Written Before the Numbers

STATE.md item 2 (d4). This part was written, and pushed, before any amplitude was computed. Block 1's eigen-shapes are
known from (d2) (§ The Reference Statistics of Block 1); no recording has yet been projected on them.

**The measure.** For each recording and each of block 1's first four eigen-shapes `e_k`:

- every eight-arc frame of the window has the amplitude `a_k(t) = sum_i e_k[i] θ_i(t)`, its eight mean-removed arc angles
  (`profile::mean_removed_angles`) projected on the shape;
- the measure is the sample standard deviation (divided by n - 1) of `a_k` over the frames, in radians, named
  `eigen<k>_amplitude_sd[rad]`.

These are the "eigen-shape amplitudes" FEATURES.md grades *Comparable*, as a spread per recording, like (d1)'s angle
spreads. Four, because four carry 95% of the variance in block 1 and did in every resample. A standard deviation does
not depend on a constant offset, so it needs no mean posture beside the shapes. The amplitudes' signs and phases, and the
travelling wave that shapes 2 and 3 seem to form (a reading by eye in (d2)), are not measured here.

**How the shapes are fixed.** Both bodies must be projected on the same four shapes, and the shapes must not move when a
machine's `atan2` rounds differently or a later block is fetched. So they are frozen in the code:

- a binary, `eigenshape-basis <block>` (crate `benchmark`), fits the shapes exactly as `reference-stats` does, each
  recording weighted equally over block 1's 179 measured recordings, and prints the first four as a Rust constant at
  full precision (17 significant digits, which round-trip an `f64`);
- that constant is committed as `features::eigenshapes::REFERENCE_SHAPES`, its doc comment naming the block, the
  command, the commit and the rustc it was made with;
- every later use, the Grid's poses included, projects on the constant and never refits.

A unit test holds the constant to what a basis must be: each shape of unit length, each pair orthogonal, each summing
to zero along the body as mean-removed angles do, and each with its largest component positive as `eigen_shapes` makes
it, all to 1e-12. Changing the constant is changing the benchmark, and is written up as such.

**Where the measure goes.** `features::profile` gains the four columns after the angle spreads, so `profiles` and
`reference-stats` carry them like every other measure, 27 instead of 23. The 23 earlier columns of (d1)'s TSV must come
out byte for byte as before, checked by `cut`. `reference-stats`' halves comparison is then re-run over 27 measures. Holm's
family grows, so its 23 earlier p-values are re-reported, not compared with (d2)'s.

**The statistics**, as in (d2): each new measure's median, quartiles and the median's 95% percentile bootstrap interval,
9999 resamples, seed 20261103, over the 179; and the halves compared by `benchmark::compare` (999 shuffles, Holm, alpha
0.05) and by the classifier test, seed 20261103. As before, no measure should differ between the halves.

**A check that must hold exactly.** With each recording weighted equally, the eigenvalue `λ_k` of block 1's covariance
splits by the law of total variance into two parts:

- the mean over recordings of each recording's variance of `a_k` about its own mean, taken with divisor n;
- the variance over recordings of their mean amplitudes.

The binary computes both from the amplitudes and reports their sum beside `λ_k`. A difference beyond rounding, 1e-9
relative, means the projection or the fit is wrong, and stops (d4) at a report. The same split says how much of each
shape's variance lies within recordings, which is what the new measure sees, and how much lies between their mean
postures, which it does not.

**What this run cannot show.** The shapes are fitted on the same recordings the amplitudes are measured on, so block
1's statistics are in-sample. Block 2, which (e3) reads, is the first out-of-sample test of the basis.
