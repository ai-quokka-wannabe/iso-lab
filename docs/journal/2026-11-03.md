## The Short Recordings of Block 1, Read From Their Metadata

STATE.md item 2 (d1) left an open question for (d2): block 1 holds 17 short recordings with record ids from 1031915 to
1033195, 16 of about five minutes and one of 52.7 s (journal of 2026-10-31, § One Row of Measures per Recording:
Built, and Run on Block 1). What are they? Their records were read on Zenodo through the API the `fetch` crate uses,
`https://zenodo.org/api/records/<id>`, one request a second, on 2026-11-03. The metadata is the form each record's
description carries (the same form `fetch::zenodo::field` reads for sex and food). What was read was every eligible
record (`data/manifests/owmd-n2-eligible.tsv`, 1646 records) dated before November 2009 by its file name, or with an
id in that range: 127 records. Record 1012947, block 1's 18th short recording, was read as well.

**What they are.** The 17 are ordinary records of the same protocol, as far as the metadata says. All 17 give the same
experimenter (Laura Grundy), the same "30m wait" habituation, OP50 on "NGM agar low peptone", a 35 mm plate, the
protocol text citing Yemini et al. 2013 ([doi:10.1038/nmeth.2560](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.2560)), and the same
version of the Tierpsy tracker (`cbfc23eb`). So does every one of the 127 records read; nothing in the form
distinguishes a short recording from a long one, and none states why a recording is short. The 16 five-minute ones give a
`total time (s)` of 299.3 to 300.0 s, so they are whole recordings of five minutes, not truncated files. Sixteen were
filmed between June and September 2009; the 17th, 1033195, on 2010-04-20.

**They are a period and a range, not strays.** Among the 127 records read:

- every eligible record filmed in June or July 2009 lasts under 400 s (69 of 69), and so do 39 of the 45 of September
  2009, whose other six last over 1000 s; none of the nine of August 2009 does;
- all 104 eligible records with ids from 1031915 to 1033195 last under 400 s; 100 of them are from 2009, and four, from
  2010 and 2011, last 59.9 to 153.6 s;
- 8 more five-minute records of June and July 2009 have ids just below the range (1031626 to 1031883) and one above it
  (1033967).

So at least 112 of the 1646 eligible records (6.8%) last under 400 s. The share may be higher: only these 127 were read,
and short records filmed after 2009 with ids outside the range would not have been found. The commands are in the next
paragraph.

**How it was read.** `curl -sS https://zenodo.org/api/records/<id>` for each id, the ids chosen by
`awk -F'\t' 'NR>1{print $1"\t"$2}' data/manifests/owmd-n2-eligible.tsv`, the date taken from the file name's
`_YYYY_MM_` and kept when before `2009-11` or when the id lies in the range. The fields were parsed from each
description's `<b>name</b> : value` items. The responses were kept in the session's scratch directory, not in the
repository.

**Two lengths do not match.** For two records, the length the metadata gives is longer than the window (d1) measured
from the file, while every other short one matches:

- 1033195: metadata 153.6 s at 29.85 frames per second; (d1) measured 52.7 s;
- 1012947 (filmed 2015-08-20): metadata 898.1 s at 24.04 frames per second; (d1) measured 824.5 s, and so excluded it as
  `short` by 30.5 s.

Why is not known yet; it is checked below once the files are fetched again (§ The Reference Statistics of Block 1, *The two lengths that did not match*).

**Does this change anything?** Not the rule: (d1)'s rule stands, and (d2) reports over the 179 recordings it measured.
What it changes is how the reference set is described. The `short` exclusion is not a random loss: it removes most of the
recordings filmed in the summer of 2009, a period of the database, so the reference describes the 15-minute
recordings of the other periods. Nothing in the metadata says the 2009 worms differed. Whether a five-minute window
could admit them is a question for the freeze (STATE.md item 2 (e5)), where the window is written down in full; the
stretch they share with the others is their first five minutes, which a fifteen-minute window does not compare like
with like.

## The Reference Statistics: the Plan, Written Before the Statistics

STATE.md item 2 (d2). This part was written, and pushed, before any statistic below was computed. Block 1 is being
fetched again on this machine (it had been deleted). Its rows were seen on 2026-10-31: the medians of 13 columns are in
that night's journal. No eigen-shape, interval or half has been computed.

**The set.** Block 1's 179 recordings that (d1)'s rule measures, unchanged. The binary recomputes their rows from the
files rather than reading (d1)'s TSV, because the eigen-shapes need every frame's angles, which the rows do not keep;
the rows it computes are checked against (d1)'s output, whose SHA-256 begins `7cebf503`.

**Where the code goes.** The eigen-shapes go into `features` (`features::eigenshapes`), because they define a measure
that both bodies are projected on (docs/benchmark/FEATURES.md § Common Units), and a measure is what `features` makes.
The medians, quartiles and bootstrap intervals go into `benchmark` (`benchmark::reference`), beside the comparisons that
will use them and the generator they share. The binary, `reference-stats <block>`, lives in `benchmark`, which now
depends on `features` to read the files.

**The eigen-shapes.**

- *Input*: every frame of each measured recording's 900 s window that has eight arcs, as its eight mean-removed arc
  angles (`profile::mean_removed_angles`), the frames (d1)'s angle spreads were computed from.
- *Fit*: the covariance of the eight angles about their mean, and its eigenvectors by Jacobi rotations. Removing the
  mean angle makes the eight sum to zero, so at most seven components carry variance.
- *Weighting*: each recording counts equally, each of its frames weighted by one over its frame count. The benchmark's
  unit is a worm, one row per recording; a recording's frame count depends on its camera's rate (about 25 to 32 frames
  per second) and on how many frames were tracked (0.3% to 40% missing), neither of which is a property of the worm's
  posture. With frames pooled equally, a well-tracked worm filmed at 32 frames per second would weigh about twice a
  poorly tracked one at 25. The frame-pooled fit is reported beside it as a check on how much the choice
  matters, not as an alternative.
- *Reported*: each component's eigenvalue and share of the variance (eigenvalue over trace), the cumulative shares, the
  fewest components carrying 95%, and the eight loadings of each component. The shares' 95% interval comes from the same
  bootstrap as below, resampling recordings: each resample's covariance is rebuilt from its recordings' moments.

**The measures' statistics.**

- The 23 measure columns of (d1): eight angle spreads, two speeds, four state shares and nine durations, rates and
  counts, the last nine marked *provisional* as (d1) marks them. The body length and the missing-frame share are
  context, summarised the same way and not compared.
- Each column's median, first and third quartiles and interquartile range (type 7 quantiles, as `benchmark::compare`
  computes them), and a 95% percentile bootstrap interval for the median: **9999 resamples of the rows, seed
  20261103**, one set of resamples for every column.
- A measured recording with a `NaN` measure (a mode with no event) would make the binary stop rather than drop or fill
  it; (d1) found none in block 1.

**The halves.** The first half is the records at draw positions 1 to 100 of block 1, the second positions 101 to 200,
in `data/manifests/owmd-n2-order.tsv`; each keeps only its measured recordings. The split is fixed by the draw's order,
chosen here before any half is computed.

- Each half gets the same statistics, with the same resample count and seed.
- *The test*: `benchmark::compare::compare_measures` on the 23 measures, with its default settings (999 shuffles, Holm's
  correction, alpha 0.05), seed 20261103. Both halves are random draws from one population, so no measure should differ.
  The chance that at least one is flagged when none truly differs is at most 5%.
- *Beside it*: the classifier test, `benchmark::two_sample_test`, with its default settings and the same seed.
  Its chance of telling the halves apart by chance is likewise 5%.
- *What a failure would mean*: a flagged measure, or halves told apart, would be reported as the check failing. It
  would not be explained away. It could be the 5% of chance; a bug in a row or a test; or something in the draw's
  order that is not random with respect to the recordings, such as date. The record ids and dates of each half would then
  be compared.
- *The eigen-shapes of each half*, fitted the same way, are described by their shares and by the absolute cosine between
  each half's first four components. No pass mark is set for these; the formal check is the per-measure test.

**What would make these statistics unfit to be the reference**: a halves comparison that fails beyond what chance
explains, or a recomputed row that differs from (d1)'s. Either would stop (d2) at a report, with the reference not
settled.

## The Reference Statistics of Block 1

The plan above was committed (`d5009ce`, 20:21 UTC) and pushed before any statistic was computed. This section is what
followed.

**What was built**, each test seen failing first (below):

- `features::eigenshapes` (`crates/features/src/eigenshapes.rs`). Each recording's frames are summarised by their
  `Moments`: the frame count, the angles' sums and their products' sums. `covariance` builds the weighted covariance from
  those, each recording weighted equally or every frame pooled, and `eigen_shapes` diagonalises it by cyclic Jacobi
  rotations in a fixed order, the eigenvalues largest first and each eigenvector's largest component made positive. No
  crate is added.
- `benchmark::reference` (`crates/benchmark/src/reference.rs`). `summarise` gives each column's median, quartiles and
  95% percentile bootstrap interval for the median (Efron and Tibshirani 1993,
  [ch. 13](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4899-4541-9_13)), resampling whole rows with the crate's seeded SplitMix64.
  `bootstrap_draws` hands the same resamples to other statistics. `compare::quantile` became public so the type 7 rule
  lives in one place.
- The binary `reference-stats <block>` (`crates/benchmark/src/bin/reference-stats.rs`) does everything the plan lists.
- `features::profile` now names its 23 measures once (`measure_names`, `Measures::values`). `profiles` builds its header
  from them; its output is unchanged, byte for byte (below).

**Tests, each seen failing first.** Twelve new unit tests:

- five in `eigenshapes`: two known orthogonal shapes recovered with variances 9 and 1; unit length and the sign rule; the
  covariance taken about the mean; the two weightings on two recordings of 2 and 4 frames; no frames giving no
  covariance;
- five in `reference`: the quartiles of 1 to 9; a constant column giving a point interval, in each column; the interval
  holding the median and narrowing from 25 rows to 400, at about the width a uniform's median predicts; the draws fixed
  by the seed and fair; a gap, or too few rows, refused;
- one in `profile`, that every measure has a name and the right value;
- one in the binary, that a summary line has a cell per column.

Each was broken deliberately by editing the code it guards, and was seen failing, before the edit was reverted
(`cargo test --locked -p features --lib eigenshapes`, `-p benchmark --lib reference`, `-p features --lib profile`,
`-p benchmark --bin reference-stats`):

- eigenvalues sorted ascending: the known-shapes and weighting tests failed;
- the sign rule inverted: the sign test failed;
- the mean not subtracted: the covariance-about-the-mean test failed;
- recordings weighted by their frames under `Weighting::Recordings`: the weighting test failed;
- an empty input not refused: the no-frames test failed;
- `carrying` made strict (`>` for `>=`): the known-shapes test failed;
- one Jacobi sweep instead of up to 64: the known-shapes and weighting tests failed;
- the first quartile taken at 0.2: the quartile test failed;
- column 0 resampled for every column: the constant-column test failed;
- the interval taken at the bootstrap medians' quartiles: the narrowing test failed;
- the seed ignored: the seed test failed;
- non-finite values let through: the gap test failed;
- the event count left out of `Measures::values`: the naming test failed;
- the interquartile range left out of a summary line: the binary's test failed.

One test failed before any break, for a fault in the test: its second "orthogonal" shape, alternating signs, was not
orthogonal to the first. It was replaced with one that is, `(1, -1, -1, 1, 1, -1, -1, 1) / sqrt 8`.

**The halves agree.** `benchmark::compare::compare_measures`, 999 shuffles, seed 20261103, Holm across the 23 measures:
**no measure differs**. The smallest raw p-value is 0.264 (the forward event count), and every Holm-adjusted p-value is

and seed 20261103, did not tell the halves apart: accuracy 0.436, p = 0.889. The halves' eigen-shapes agree too. Their
shares are 39.3%, 26.3%, 24.0%, 8.3% and 39.8%, 25.8%, 23.2%, 8.8%, four carry 95% in each, and the absolute cosine
between their corresponding first four shapes is 0.9991, 0.9999, 0.9989 and 0.9998. The check passed as the plan said
it must. It is a weak check, and should be read as one. Two halves of one block, about 90 recordings each, are what the
test was built to call alike, and a pass here says no more than that nothing in the rows or the draw's order splits
them. It does not say the test can see a real difference: that is (e3)'s positive control and (e4)'s.

**The two lengths that did not match** were traced in the files with h5py 3.16.0 (outside the lab's code; a reading,
not a measure). The `timestamp` field of `/features_timeseries` steps by one frame in every file of block 1. In ten of
the 200 files its first value is not 0 but 1 to 820, so the file starts that many frames into the video.

- 1012947's file holds frames 820 to 20639, 19820 rows, of a video the metadata puts at 898.1 s, about 21590 frames. It
  lacks 34 s at the start and about 40 s at the end, which is why (d1) measured 824.5 s. It is also 92.4% without a
  skeleton (1506 skeletons, where the metadata counts 8423), so it would have been excluded as `gappy` had it not been
  `short`.
- 1033195's file holds frames 0 to 1571, 52.7 s, of a video the metadata puts at 153.6 s.
- The other nine late starts are 1 to 152 frames, at most 5.1 s (152 frames at 30 frames per second); eight of them are
  measured, and one, 1032327, is a five-minute recording. The ten are listed by
  `h5py.File(path)['/features_timeseries']['timestamp'][0] != 0` over the block's files.

(d1)'s window counts rows from the first row, so for those eight measured recordings it starts up to 5.1 s after the
video does. The rule stands. This is (d3)'s to weigh with the other gaps, and the freeze's to word: whether the window
counts from the video's first frame or the file's.
