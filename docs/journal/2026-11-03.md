**Verdict, by the rule at the head of [B.md](../hypotheses/experiments/B.md): C18 is refuted**, by clauses (2), (4)
and (5), each at alpha 0.05 / 6. Its first half stands. The pure network has a cliff, a reach that stays finite below
d = 2.255 and becomes a share of the network above it, and two-hop priming is well below one-hop. Its cures do not
stand as cures. Lateral inhibition and the fan-out penalty move the cliff to higher connectivity rather than remove
it. So does the conserved budget at a gain above one, to lower connectivity. The one variant without a cliff, the
budget at a gain below one, also spreads nothing to speak of.

**Against the expectations registered on 2026-10-31:**

- *The pure network reads cliff near d = 2.5*: held. D is 2.255. The 1% crossing is interpolated from below the first
  grid point past 1%, 2.50, which is also where the coefficient of variation peaks.
- *The budget at c = 0.2 never passes 1%, so it has no cliff and no spread*: held. Its highest mean is 0.00023.
- *The budget at c = 0.6 reads cliff, firing clause (4)*: held. *Near d = 1, the lead's topological transition*: did
  not hold. D is 1.528, and the coefficient of variation peaks at 1.75, not at 1. Why is not settled here. A
  candidate, *a hunch* not tested: with a budget, a unit of out-degree k passes 0.6 / k of its activation to each
  target each step, so at the fixed point a target with that one input holds 1.2 / k of it. A target with one active
  input is then activated only when k is small, and the activation threshold of 0.1 raises the effective percolation
  point above the graph's own, which is 1. The lead's region III is likewise defined by units "above a given positive
  threshold" (journal of 2026-10-31), but in an unbounded linear model whose activity grows without end, where any
  threshold is crossed in time.
- *The fan-out penalty and inhibition read cliff at higher degrees, firing (5) and (2)*: held, at 7.502 and 4.753.
- *If that is how it comes out, the gain, not the budget, decides*: as far as these five networks go, yes. The budget
  removes the cliff only at a gain where nothing spreads; at a gain where it spreads, it has a cliff.
- *Clause (6)* had no registered expectation. Its ratio, 0.400, is what a linear reading predicts. Below the
  threshold, near the seed at N = 10 000, the graph is a tree, so a unit one hop out holds 0.4 at the fixed point and
  one two hops out 0.4 squared, 0.16. The clause tests the arithmetic of the edge weight more than any property of the
  spread.

## The Pace Note's Gaps, a Fourth Pass

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer.

- **The question**: which of the five gaps of [the pace note](../research/pace.md#gaps-to-be-read) - a touch-to-reversal
  latency, what Mohammadi et al. 2013's reaction times count, a thermotaxis latency, fitted time constants for tap
  habituation, and the primary sources for lethargus and satiety quiescence - can be closed from open sources, without
  repeating the searches the journals of 2026-10-14 and 2026-10-15 record?
- **The method**: Europe PMC's search service, each query with `AND OPEN_ACCESS:y AND IN_EPMC:y` and the first 15
  results; every full text was fetched and each sentence holding a time unit beside the words that matter (latency,
  delay, reaction or response time; lethargus, quiescence, bout; exponential, half-life, fitted) printed and read in
  context. The queries, with their hit counts: `elegans AND (optogenetic OR channelrhodopsin) AND ("touch receptor" OR
  ALM OR "mechanosensory neurons") AND reversal AND latency` (16); `elegans AND "latency to reverse"` (3); `elegans AND
  "reversal latency"` (4); `elegans AND "onset of reversal"` (9); `elegans AND ("backward locomotion" OR reversal) AND
  latency AND (ChR2 OR channelrhodopsin OR Chrimson) AND AVA` (13); `elegans AND "satiety quiescence"` (22); `elegans
  AND lethargus AND "duration of lethargus"` (9); `elegans AND lethargus AND ("lethargus duration" OR "length of
  lethargus")` (9); `elegans AND "L1 lethargus" AND microfluidic AND RIS` (4); `elegans AND (thermotaxis OR
  thermosensory) AND ("temperature step" OR "thermal pulse" OR "temperature steps" OR "warming step" OR "cooling
  step") AND (latency OR delay)` (5); `elegans AND thermotaxis AND (reversal OR turning) AND "temperature ramp" AND
  (latency OR delay OR "time lag")` (2); `elegans AND tap AND habituation AND ("exponential decay" OR "exponential fit"
  OR "half-life" OR "decay constant" OR "fitted")` (28); `elegans AND "tap habituation" AND ("habituation rate" OR "rate
  of habituation") AND "Multi-Worm Tracker"` (3). Besides these, papers named by author were looked up the same way.
  Two routes were new to the lab: where Europe PMC's full-text service answered an author manuscript with an HTTP 500,
  NCBI's BioC service (`https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/research/bionlp/RESTful/pmcoa.cgi/BioC_xml/<PMCID>/unicode`)
  served it for Mazzochette et al. 2018, Leifer et al. 2011, Ramot et al. 2008 and Nagy, Raizen and Biron 2014; and the
  University of Toronto's repository served, through its public DSpace API, the text it extracted from Mohammadi's 2014
  thesis. Patel et al. 2022's supplementary table came from Europe PMC's supplementary-files service, a zip of 502 MB
  (mostly videos) from which only the table's Word file was kept and read, the zip deleted after. Downloads were kept
  outside the repository, each source in its own directory, and read with `python3 -I`.
- **What came out**:
    - *Lethargus*, measured: in wild-type cohorts observed hourly, the L4 lethargus lasted 2.2 ± 0.4 h (N = 16), 2.0 ±
      0.0 h (N = 17) and 2.0 ± 0.0 h (N = 12), the L3 1.6 ± 0.5 h and 1.4 ± 0.5 h, the L2 1.3 ± 0.5 h (Patel et al. 2022,
      Supplementary file 1); within L4 lethargus, bouts of quiescence and motion last 1 to 100 s (Nagy, Raizen and Biron
      2014). The primary source usually cited, Singh and Sulston 1978, is cited as "1-2 h" by one paper and as "2–3 hr" by
      another; it is not open, and the lab now quotes measured values instead.
    - *Heat*: the first author's thesis gives the detection method behind Mohammadi et al. 2013 and a reaction time of
      "~250ms" whose arithmetic counts from the laser's onset; the lab reads the times so, at moderate confidence, since
      neither text says it in words.
    - *Thermotaxis*: the thermoreceptor's latency, 96 ± 5 ms (n = 3) and 132 ms in a fourth recording, in AFD (Ramot et
      al. 2008). The behavioural papers (Ryu and Samuel 2002, Zariwala et al. 2003, Clark et al. 2007, Luo et al. 2014)
      are not in the open-access subset, and their publishers' pages refused the request.
    - *Touch*: no latency to a reversal's start in text. The nearest: a filter from optogenetic activation of the touch
      neurons to acceleration peaks after 0.2 s and decays over 0.4 s, at 13 frames a second (Porto et al. 2019).
    - *Tap habituation*: still no fitted time constant in text. Abstracts give a recovery bound, recovered "by 30 min
      after training" at 4 and 7 days old (Beck and Rankin 1993); Giles et al. 2015 fit exponentials and report the
      half-lives in figures only; Porto et al. 2019 chose a 50 s decay for responses to continuous light stimulation.
    - *Satiety*: You et al. 2008 was again not served (Europe PMC HTTP 500, not in BioC's subset, publisher refused), and
      the open papers give only the protocol: 12 h fasting, 3 or 6 h refeeding (Hyun et al. 2016).
- **What failed or surprised**: the most wanted number, a touch-to-reversal latency, is still not in any open text read;
  the field reports it as a figure, as a filter or as a scoring window. And one primary source, Singh and Sulston 1978,
  is cited by two later papers with durations that do not overlap.
- **What comes next**: STATE.md's item 1 keeps what is open: a figure read of Giles et al. 2015's habituation curves,
  a re-analysis of Liu et al.'s or Porto et al.'s data if public, the thermotaxis papers, and You et al. 2008.

### After Review

The verifier read the sources again and found two faults, both now fixed in pace.md:

- **Leung et al. 2016.** The note had the paper's reasoning backwards. The authors gave "worms do not respond to the
  stimulus so quickly" as their reason for considering *removing* the first 0.16 s from their model. They then kept
  that window because removing it made no difference.
- **Porto et al. 2019.** The note called their 50 s decay "for light, not taps". In fact the stimulus is optogenetic
  activation of the touch receptor neurons, and the authors write that the value agrees with tap habituation, citing
  Timbers et al. 2013. That paper is now the lead in the Gaps and in STATE.md item 1. Their own methods write the
  50 s as a time constant (exp(-λt) with "λ is 50 s"), and the note now calls it that.

The minor points are taken up as well:

- Patel et al.'s GR1395 wild-type cohort is added: 2.2 ± 0.6 h, N = 20.
- The note now gives the methods' "5–60 s" observation window.
- It also says the plates were NGM-RNAi plates, so the wild-type rows may be control-RNAi conditions. The lab has not
  checked this.
- Hyun et al. 2016 is now described as measuring durations, which are shown in figures rather than stated in the text.
- Gallagher et al. 2013 (open) is a new lead for satiety quiescence. My quick scan of its text found no dwell time
  stated, and its figures are unread.
- Nagy, Raizen and Biron 2014's "2–3 hour period of L4leth" is added as a third secondary statement of the lethargus
  duration.
