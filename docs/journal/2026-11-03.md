## E4's Pilot, and Its Plan Written Again

E4, *Hebbian learning needs a stabiliser and a "used together" rule*, was parked on 2026-10-31. Its plan failed
verification: its units took the sign of their input, so no rescaling of a unit's weights could change what they did,
and a stabiliser could not act by construction (journal of 2026-10-31, § The E1-E5 Plans, Taken Up Again). STATE.md,
*Blocked*, asked for a pilot first, and for the plan to be written only once the pilot showed Hebb's rule alone
capturing and a stabiliser able to change it. This is that pilot, and the plan it allows.

**Two dead ends first**, kept in the notebook's first section. Both use the first plan's own ±1 units and a whole
frequent pattern P, trained 50 times among ten others, on seeds `[41000, s]` for s = 0 to 5.

- **Hebb's rule** captured every cue on every seed. The controls, trained with P once, captured 0.03 to 0.16.
- **Oja's update with the unit's output clamped to the presented state** makes each weight an exponential average of
  the patterns' outer products. At eta 0.01 and 0.1 it captured every cue, and at 0.3 it captured 0.87 to 1.
- **Clipping every weight** at 1/N captured 0.58 to 1; at 2/N, 0.96 to 1; at 4/N, every cue.
- **Why they fail.** With dense ±1 patterns, the frequent pattern wins the *sign* of nearly every weight, and bounding
  a weight's size does nothing to its sign.

**The pilot.** It follows the hunch's own test, from
[matej-hunches.md § E4](../hypotheses/matej-hunches.md#e4-hebbian-learning-needs-a-stabiliser-and-a-used-together-rule):

- co-activate a *pair* many times, then show unrelated cues;
- the pair must never capture the activity, and its weight must saturate;
- units resting high must not grow weights without input.

The notebook is [notebooks/e4_pilot.ipynb](../../notebooks/e4_pilot.ipynb).

- **The network.** 100 units with graded rates and a resting drive, so a rescaling of a unit's weights changes its
  rate.
- **The rules.** Each is an equation with its output named, the unit's settled rate on the trial:
    - Hebb;
    - Oja;
    - row normalisation;
    - BCM;
    - homeostatic scaling.

  Each is run with and without the "used together" rule.
- **The rule at zero input** is the model's own: a unit sits at its resting rate.

The notebook gives the constants. The run is
`PATH=/home/user/nbenv/bin:$PATH jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace notebooks/e4_pilot.ipynb`, with
numpy 2.5.4 and Python 3.13.16, on pilot seeds `[42000, s]` for s = 0 to 4 (the table) and `[43000, s]` for s = 0 to 2
(the sweep). It takes about five minutes.

**What this shows** (*corrected after review*: the second bullet below, and the plan described after it, are
withdrawn; see *After review* at the end of this section).

- **Hebb's rule alone captures, and a stabiliser can change it.** These are the two conditions STATE.md set.
- **Only a stabiliser that bounds activity works.** Of the hunch's three named kinds, homeostatic scaling stops the
  capture. Oja's normalisation and BCM's sliding threshold bound the pair's weight, so it saturates as the hunch asks,
  but a pair joined by a saturated weight still switches itself on.
- **The "used together" rule is what stops units at rest from growing weights.**
- **A runaway the pilot was not built to find.** Without scaling, the background units wire into a runaway assembly of
  their own.

**The plan, written again** in [E1-E5.md § E4](../hypotheses/experiments/E1-E5.md#e4-hebbian-learning-needs-a-stabiliser-and-a-used-together-rule).
The first plan is kept below it as the record of #180.

- **Variants.** The full variant is homeostatic scaling with the "used together" rule. The plan tests it against Hebb
  with the rule, which has no stabiliser, and against scaling without the rule.
- **Five refuting tests at alpha 0.05/5:**
    1. the full variant's pair captures;
    2. its hub captures;
    3. its pair's weight is still changing over the last 50 trials;
    4. the stabiliser does not matter;
    5. the "used together" rule does not matter.
- **Reported, not tested:** Oja, row normalisation and BCM.
- **Seeds:** 20 fresh seeds, and the run is to be in tested Rust.
- **What the pilot means for the test.** It already shows the outcome at these constants on every pilot seed, so the
  test is close to foregone, and the plan says so. What the run adds is fresh seeds, intervals and the three reported

- **Its real weakness is the model.** It is a toy rate network, and nothing here says the worm's synapses behave so.
- **Where it waits.** STATE.md's *Blocked* entry is closed, and the run is item 12 of *Next*.

**After review.** The second agent's first review of #197 reproduced the pilot exactly. It found that the pilot's
reading, and the plan built on it, were wrong, and its probes were right. They are now cells of the notebook,
re-run there:

- **Capture is self-ignition, not a response to the cue.** Under the "used together" rule, no weight from the cued
  units into the pair grows, so no cue reaches the pair. The pair switches itself on from its resting drive once its
  weight passes w* = 1 - 2b = 0.9: its settled rate is b / (1 - w), which the notebook checks from 0.5 to 1. A
  network's capture share is therefore 0 or 1.
- **Which stabiliser "works" is decided by where its bound sits against w*, not by its kind.**
    - At the pilot's first constants, the bounds of Oja's rule and row normalisation (1) and of BCM (about 3.6) sit
      above w*, and homeostatic scaling's fixed point (about 0.06) sits below it.
    - At other constants each one reverses, on all five pilot seeds `[42000, s]`:
        - row normalisation to a norm of 0.8 or 0.5 stops the capture;
        - BCM and Oja at eta 0.005 stop it, with W_01 at about 0.36 and 0.63;
        - scaling at gamma 0.1 and target 0.5 lets it through, with W_01 at about 0.91;
        - scaling at gamma 0.5 and target 0.8 captures on the three seeds whose W_01 passed 0.9 and not on the two
          whose did not.

  So "only a stabiliser that bounds activity works" is withdrawn. The 90-setting sweep only covered targets that put
  scaling far below w*, which the notebook now says.
- **Two of the plan's clauses could not fail.** Under the "used together" rule, every weight touching an undriven unit
  stays exactly zero; the notebook asserts this. So the hub can never capture, and the full variant's side of the rule
  clause was fixed.
- **The saturation clause would have fired on an artefact.** Under scaling, the pair's weight jitters around its fixed
  point by trial type, so the change over the last 50 trials reads which trials came last.

On this toy network, then, the hunch's outcomes follow from its constants and its construction. None of them is an
empirical question the network can answer.

**The plan is written again in that light.** On the network it states four assertions, to be built in Rust:

1. Hebb's pair weight passes ignition.
2. The pair ignites exactly above w*.
3. Each stabiliser captures exactly when its bound exceeds w*.
4. Undriven units' weights stay at zero.

The design rule the pilot leaves - a Hebbian loop needs its gain bounded below ignition, and resting activity must not
count as use - is tested where it can be: on a Program with a Hebbian module, switched off part by part on the frozen
benchmark. That waits on a Program.

The plan also says why the first plan's absolute overlap and capacity check no longer apply. It also names the model's
weaknesses, among them that scaling here is a fast activity clamp, where synaptic scaling in neurons is slow.
