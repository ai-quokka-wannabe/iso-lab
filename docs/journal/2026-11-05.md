- *E13's candidate score is not right as stated.* For a Beta(a, b) belief, one observation's expected fall in posterior
  variance is Var(p)/(n + 1) with n = a + b, not twice Var(p); the derivation is in
  [E11-E15.md § E13](../hypotheses/experiments/E11-E15.md#e13-curiosity-as-expected-information-gain), and it is to be

  by construction, so it is an assertion, not a test - but the variance score falls as 1/n² and the mutual information
  as about 1/(2n), so the two are not interchangeable: at the same n the first is smaller by a factor of order n, and
  falls below a threshold θ after a number of looks growing as θ^(-1/2) against θ^(-1). The figures and their
  computation are in the plan, reproduced by the verifier of #221. A first draft of this entry said both fell as 1/n;
  the verifier corrected it before merge.

## F1-F9 Planned, and a Triage

STATE.md item 5, its next step: a plan for each of hunches F1 to F9 of

and Baez 2002's full text was searched for the facts F1 and F4 use. Nothing else was read, and the plan says so at

What the planning found about the mathematics, before any run:

- *F1*: interference needs signed amplitudes, not complex ones - real quantum theory interferes too, and by Renou et
  al.'s abstract differs from complex quantum theory only in networks with independent sources. So an interference
  probe cannot tell a phased Program from a signed one; the plan reports it and tests the QQ equality on order instead.
  The step from Renou et al. to "a real-valued brain would be a shadow" is an analogy, not an inference.
- *F2*: Ostrowski gives one real completion and infinitely many p-adic ones, not two; and points in many dimensions
  look ultrametric for free, so the null is a Gaussian surrogate matched in covariance.
- *F3*: the extended Golay code's weight distribution, 1, 759, 2576, 759, 1 at weights 0, 8, 12, 16, 24, was computed
  (the method is in the plan); a random subset of its codewords does not keep its symmetry, so only robustness is
  tested.
- *F4*: the fixed points of squaring are the idempotents by definition; plain repeated squaring diverges or vanishes
  unless the eigenvalues are 0 and 1, and normalised squaring is power iteration, the same in the real symmetric
  matrices - so the test is h3(O) against h3(R) and h3(C) at matched dimension.
- *F6*: Ramanujan's bound is defined for regular graphs, and in the Ihara zeta function's language it is about poles,
  not zeros; the connectome test uses the spectral gap against degree-preserving rewirings.
- *F7*: the Hasse principle fails for cubic forms; the mathematics' interest is that local agreement can hold without
  a global answer, the reverse of the hunch's use.
