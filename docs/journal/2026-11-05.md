- *E13's candidate score is not right as stated.* For a Beta(a, b) belief, one observation's expected fall in posterior
  variance is Var(p)/(n + 1) with n = a + b, not twice Var(p); the derivation is in
  [E11-E15.md § E13](../hypotheses/experiments/E11-E15.md#e13-curiosity-as-expected-information-gain), and it is to be
  re-derived in a notebook before use. The hunch's main claim, that the score dies away on a coin of fixed bias, holds
  by construction, so it is an assertion, not a test - but the variance score falls as 1/n² and the mutual information
  as about 1/(2n), so the two are not interchangeable: at the same n the first is smaller by a factor of order n, and
  falls below a threshold θ after a number of looks growing as θ^(-1/2) against θ^(-1). The figures and their
  computation are in the plan, reproduced by the verifier of #221. A first draft of this entry said both fell as 1/n;
  the verifier corrected it before merge.

**What it found.**

  not a stand-in for sleep here.
- The behavioural marker turns the question round: Bronfman, Ginsburg and Jablonka 2016 state that *C. elegans* does
  not meet the conditions for unlimited associative learning, on the ground that it "has never been shown" to learn

- Exact IIT Φ is out of reach at 302 units; Φ\* with a polynomial partition search is the feasible stand-in, and is not
  the same quantity. The often-quoted ceiling for exact Φ, about a dozen units, was not found at a source and is not
  relied on.

**What was registered, before any data.** H17 § *Pre-registered, the sleep test*: segments by behaviour, never by the
measures; a guard for each measure against the trivial fall that lower activity alone would cause (binarising against
each unit's own distribution and dividing by phase-randomised surrogates for LZc and PCI-like; z-scoring for Φ\*, whose
surrogates sit near zero and so are a check, not a denominator; a per-unit pre-stimulus baseline for ignition); the
animal as the unit, a 95% bootstrap interval of the mean relative change; and three verdicts that cannot overlap, set
at a fall of one tenth: tracks the state, refuted, undecided. In the worm, a sensory perturbation may end the
quiescent bout, so a PCI-like response counts only from perturbations that leave the bout unbroken.

**What it cannot show.** A fall in sleep tracks a brain state. It does not show that anything is felt, in a worm or in a
Program; H17 says so in its first point.

**A question for the maintainer.** Whether perturbing a Program by writing into its internal state, rather than
through its senses, sits within the rule *No cheating*. H17 prefers a sensory pulse, which the worm can also receive,
and does not use the direct kick until this is ruled.

**Revised before any data, after the second agent's review** (pull request 229, review of 2026-11-05, *changes
needed*). The first registration could have decided things by construction:

- *Signal to noise.* Z-scoring and surrogates remove scale but not a falling signal-to-noise ratio. That would have
  made Φ\* *tracks the state*, and LZc *refuted*, close to automatic.
- *Trial selection.* Keeping only sleep trials that left the bout unbroken selects responses that did not spread.

- *noise-matched wake*, with an SNR floor per unit;

- *design*: a minimum of six animals, windows of matched length, datasets kept apart, and PCI-like binarised against

## gr-fec's Worm by Equation: The Learning Layer's Fixed Point, Predicted Before It Runs

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

STATE.md item 8 (g), by the maintainer's ruling of tonight ("Pause + equations"): the step named in the *Next* of
[the journal of 2026-10-26](2026-10-26.md#gr-fecs-worm-by-equation-its-turning-rule-predicted-before-it-runs) and never
taken ([gr-fec.md § 12.2](../research/gr-fec.md#122-why-it-stalled), point 1). It is a predict-then-check test of one
layer of gr-fec's toy worm. Still a toy world; none of it is evidence about *C. elegans*.

- **Question**: after long learning, do the weights of gr-fec's learner sit at the least-squares fit of its reward on
  the features it actually experienced, as its learning rule predicts?
- **The rule, read from the code** (`reference/gr-fec/src/evolve.py`, `EvoBrain.step`, unedited): each second the
  learner computes pred = W·F from the feature vector F (246 entries: the tuned nodes and intensity nodes, and their
  hunger-gated copies), then W ← W + η·(r − pred)·F with r = r_eat·max(pharynx, 0) − r_hunger·clip(hunger, 0, 1),
  W starting at zero. The instinct is outside the error. This is exactly the least-mean-squares (Widrow-Hoff, or
  delta) rule: there is no weight decay, no eligibility trace and no discounting - it predicts the reward of the same
  second, not a return, so it is not temporal-difference learning.
- **The prediction, derived**: averaged over the fluctuations of (F, r), the rule's mean step is
  η·(E[r·F] − E[F·Fᵀ]·W), which is zero where E[F·Fᵀ]·W = E[r·F]: the normal equations of the least-squares fit of r
  on F. Starting from zero, every update is a multiple of some F, so W stays in the span of the features the worm has
  had, and within that span the fixed point is the minimum-norm solution. Three conditions bound the claim, and each
  is a way it can fail:
    - *slow learning*: the averaging is exact only as η → 0. A worm whose weights move fast compared with how its
      experience changes - hunger over minutes, food and energy over hours - will track its recent experience rather
      than settle at the fit over a long window. Most of e45's elites sit at η = 0.01, the top of the gene's range;
    - *stationarity*: the fit is over a window of the worm's own life; if its experience changes within the window,
      there is no single fixed point to settle at;
    - *the closed loop*: W feeds the drive and so the turning, and so the experience it is fitted to. The fixed point
      is therefore self-consistent - the fit to the experience that W itself produces - which is exactly what a fit
      to the worm's own logged window measures.

  So the quantity predicted is the gap between the learned weights and the window's least-squares fit, which theory
  says should be small for slow learners and may not be for fast ones. A second, rougher prediction is the size of the
  remaining wobble: for a small step in a stationary world with independent inputs, the excess mean squared error of
  the online predictions over the fit's is about η·E|F|²/2 of the fit's, the LMS *misadjustment*
  ([Widrow, McCool, Larimore and Johnson 1976](https://doi.org/10.1109/PROC.1976.10286); its metadata checked at
  Crossref, its text not read here, so the formula is from the standard account and used only descriptively). The
  inputs here are strongly correlated from second to second, so it is a guide, not a test.
- **The measure**: per worm, over the window, A = Σ F·Fᵀ and b = Σ r·F from the features and rewards the learner
  itself used; W_LS the minimum-norm solution of A·W = b (`numpy.linalg.lstsq`, rcond 10⁻¹⁰). For any weight vector W,
  D(W) = √((W − W_LS)ᵀ·A·(W − W_LS) / W_LSᵀ·A·W_LS): the RMS gap between the predictions W and the fit make on the
  window's own features, relative to the fit's RMS prediction. D(W_LS) = 0 and D(0) = 1 by construction. Measured in
  this metric, directions the window never excites do not count, which is right: there the window cannot define a fit.
  The weight vector compared is W̄, the mean of the learner's weights over the window (the LMS fixed point is a
  statement about the mean); the end-of-window weights and the online predictions are reported beside it.
- **Worms, worlds and seeds**: e45's 20 elites × 5 worms, as in the ledger test, in `RandomHardWorld` (Hardland
  with a random layout), plate radius 50 mm as there; lives of 48 h; the window is hours 24 to 48 (86 400 steps).
    - **Layouts, fresh, drawn by a stated rule**: seeds 610000, 610001, 610002 and 610003, the first four of a range
      no journal has used (checked by a search of `docs/` and STATE.md before writing this); the side of the food band
      is drawn by the layout's own generator. Not the reused panel 82000 to 82104.
    - **Body seeds** 610100 to 610103, one per layout.
    - **Two arms**, same layout and body seed: η as evolved, and every worm's η × 0.1 (the theory's slow-learning
      condition; slow learning is also the lever found to matter in J10-26). Both arms are pre-registered.
- **Tests, fixed now**: per layout and arm, the median over the 100 worms, with a 95% interval from 10 000
  bootstrap resamples of the 20 genomes (each with its five worms; seed 610200, restarted for each file).
    - **C0, arithmetic**: D(W_LS) = 0 and |D(0) − 1| = 0 within 10⁻⁹ for every worm.
    - **C1, the measure can tell a wrong answer** (must pass for P1 to be read): D of another genome's mean weights
      (worm i + 5 mod 100) against worm i's fit has its interval's lower bound above 0.20 in every layout and arm.
    - **P1, the fixed point (primary)**, for each arm separately: *holds* if the upper bound of median D(W̄) is at most

### What the Plan Leaves Unfixed, Fixed Here Before Any Seed Is Run

Written and committed before the probe seeds (1001 to 1003) or the test seeds (1 to 20) were run.

- **Coding and similarity.** Words are coded ±1 (bit 1 as +1, bit 0 as -1); the modern Hopfield step is
  ξ ← Xᵀ softmax(β X ξ), X the 64 stored words as rows, as in E15's model ([Ramsauer et al.
  2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217)). For two ±1 words, x·y = 24 - 2 d(x, y), so the softmax weighs stored
  words by exp(-2β d). An erased feature enters the cue as 0, neutral between the two values.
- **Settling.** At most 100 steps; stop when no coordinate moves by more than 10⁻¹². Cues not settled within 100
  steps are counted and reported.
- **Rounding at the readout.** A coordinate of the final state rounds to 1 if positive and to 0 if negative; a
  coordinate within 10⁻⁹ of the midpoint is left undecided and counts one half towards either value. Without this,
  the plan's own rule - an exact tie between two patterns, whose mean the softmax returns, counts as a failure - would
  be decided by the rounding convention instead. Distances are then multiples of one half and compared exactly.
- **The cues.** Flipped cues: every set of f flipped positions for f = 0 to 4, for every stored word (at f = 4,
  10 626 sets × 64 words); for f = 5 and 6, 1 000 sets per word drawn uniformly, the same sets for every store of a seed.
  Erased cues: for each k = 10 to 24, 100 random sets of k given positions per seed, each applied to all 64 words of
  each store; the recall per set gives the variance at k = 12.
- **The stores.** Golay: 64 distinct codewords drawn uniformly from the 4096. Matched: uniform random 24-bit words
  drawn one at a time, each kept only if at least 8 from every word kept, until 64. Unstructured: 64 uniform random
  words, drawn independently (a repeat is possible and would count against recall). The code: the 4096 multiples of
  g(x) = x^11 + x^10 + x^6 + x^5 + x^4 + x^2 + 1 of degree below 23, each extended by a parity bit, as the plan says.
- **Random streams.** NumPy's `default_rng([seed, s])`, s = 1 Golay draw, 2 matched draw, 3 unstructured draw,
  4 the harness pair, 5 the f = 5 and 6 flips, 6 the erasure sets.
- **β.** On the grid β = 0.05 × 2^(j/4), j = 0 to 48 (0.05 to 204.8), the smallest value at which, on each of
  seeds 1001 to 1003, the harness pair (two codewords at distance 8 drawn by stream 4), the Golay store and the
  matched store each recall every f = 3 cue. The unstructured store plays no part in choosing β.
- **Statistics.** Per seed, recall is the share of cues settling on their own word. The two refuting differences
  are Golay minus unstructured at f = 3, (a), and Golay minus matched at f = 4, (b), paired by seed, a percentile
  bootstrap over the 20 seeds with 10 000 resamples (bootstrap generator `default_rng(20261105)`). A clause is refuted
  when the one-sided upper 97.5% bound is at or below zero (alpha 0.025, two refuting tests), and passed when the
  one-sided lower 95% bound is above zero (pass tests uncorrected, by [B.md](../hypotheses/experiments/B.md)); otherwise
  inconclusive.

**A defect in the plan's harness check, found before running.** The plan says the pair of codewords at distance 8
"must recall every cue with f = 3 at the chosen β (a cue 3 from one word is 5 from the other, so a β too low fails
it)". Under the readout the plan itself fixes, that parenthesis is true only at β = 0. With two stored words a and b
the state after any step is p a + (1 - p) b; a cue 3 from a gives a the larger logit for every β > 0, so p > 1/2;
the next step's logits, p·24 + (1 - p)·8 for a against p·8 + (1 - p)·24 for b, keep a ahead, and every coordinate
where a and b differ rounds to a's value. So the check passes at every β > 0 and cannot set β; it still catches a
softmax or readout that runs the wrong way. The check is run as written; its red-first break is β = 0, where every
cue becomes the mean and a tie, and a second break, the logits' sign reversed, which must recall the other word.
β is in effect set by the two stores of minimum distance 8, which can fail at low β because 63 other words share
the softmax.

**Correction, after an exploratory look at probe seed 1001 and before any test seed.** The paragraph above is wrong,
and is kept as written. It followed one step of the settling, not the iteration. Run to its fixed point, the pair's
state p a + (1 - p) b has p' = σ(16β(2p - 1)), whose fixed point p = 1/2 is stable when 8β < 1: below β = 1/8 every
cue settles on the mean, a tie and a failure, as the plan said. So the harness check can fail, and fails at low β; on
seed 1001 it fails at β = 0.119 and passes at 0.168 on the grid. The plan's parenthesis stands. Its red-first break
is a β on the grid below 1/8. The sign-reversed logits make the state oscillate between the two words rather than
recall the other one, so that break shows as cues that never settle.

**One rule added at the same point, for the same reason.** Near β = 1/8 the settling slows, and at β = 0.12 the pair
passed only because 100 steps were not enough to reach the mean. A readout taken at the step cap depends on the cap,
so **a cue not settled within 100 steps counts as a failure**. The previous paragraph said such cues are counted and
reported; they still are.

### What Was Run

`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace --ExecutePreprocessor.timeout=3000
f3_golay_memory.ipynb` in `notebooks/`, Python 3.13.16, NumPy 2.5.4, matplotlib 3.11.2, two processes, 931 s for the
20 test seeds. A first run with NumPy's default threading was stopped after 40 minutes with no result, crowded by
other runs on the machine; nothing from it was read. Every number below is printed by the notebook.

- **The code.** 4096 distinct codewords, weight distribution 1, 759, 2576, 759, 1 at weights 0, 8, 12, 16, 24, as
  the plan computed; closure under addition checked on 2000 random pairs.
- **The harness check.** On each probe seed the pair recalls no f = 3 cue up to β = 0.119, 97% at 0.141 (the cues
  not yet settled at the cap count as failures) and all from 0.168. Both red-first breaks fail it: β = 0.119, every
  cue settling on the mean; and the logits' sign reversed at β = 1, every cue oscillating, unsettled. The unbroken
  check passes at β = 1.
- **β = 0.400** (grid index 12), the smallest grid value at which the pair, the Golay store and the matched store
  recall every f = 3 cue on all three probe seeds; at the value below, 0.336, the Golay store fails on all three.
- **The assertion holds on the test seeds**: at β = 0.4 both stores of minimum distance 8 recall every cue with
  f ≤ 3, on all 20 seeds. The matched stores took 170 to 323 draws each.

### The Verdict

|---|---|---|---|

Recall, mean over the 20 test seeds [minimum, maximum over seeds], β = 0.4.

- **(a) passed.** Golay minus unstructured at f = 3: +0.0046, one-sided lower 95% bound +0.0041 (upper 97.5% bound
  +0.0054). As the plan expected: random words are sometimes closer than 8 (the unstructured stores' pairwise
  distances reach down to 3), and the Golay store's minimum distance does the work. This says nothing beyond the
  minimum distance; the matched store does as well.
- **(b) refuted.** Golay minus matched at f = 4: -0.0290, one-sided upper 97.5% bound -0.0276, wholly below zero,
  and not by a little: the Golay store recalls worse than random words of the same minimum distance. Nothing in the
  code beyond its minimum distance helps; at f = 4 the code's structure hurts.

**Why, by count.** At f = 4 a cue flipped in four of the eight places where its word differs from a stored word 8 away
is 4 from both, a tie, and a failure. A random 64 of the Golay code's 4096 words have all their distances at 8, 12, 16
or 24, and a mean of 377 pairs per store at exactly 8 (range 351 to 414); the matched stores, whose distances spread
from 8 to 22, have 103 (89 to 121). On seed 1, 7.3% of the Golay store's f = 4 cues are tied by distance, against 2.3%
of the matched store's. The code's virtue as a code - every distance a multiple of 4, packed at the bound - is the
store's weakness at the first distance it cannot correct. That the Golay failure rate, 1 - 0.9604, is below seed 1's tied share is
probably the softmax at β = 0.4 breaking some distance ties through the other 62 words; that was not measured.

**The hunch, then**: refuted by clause (b). Its premise, that a highly symmetric code makes a better memory than random
words of equal minimum distance, fails here at the first corruption beyond correction; below it, at f ≤ 3, the two
are equal, as the assertion said. The test covers one β, the smallest that passes the controls, and random 64-word
subsets of the code; at a larger β the softmax breaks fewer ties (on probe seed 1001, before the run, the Golay
store's f = 4 recall was 0.961 at β = 1 and 0.942 at β = 4); whether the gap to the matched store changes with β was
not tested. Not tested: a
store of the whole code, or of a subcode that keeps the symmetry.
