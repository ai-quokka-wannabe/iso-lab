# 2026-11-04

## Real Against Unseen Real: the Plan

STATE.md item 2 (e3): the benchmark's two tests on real data alone. Block 1's real worms are compared with block 2's,
recordings neither test has seen, and they must come out indistinguishable. A positive control, the same comparison
with one measure of block 2 deliberately altered by a stated amount, must be told apart. This part was written, and
pushed, before any number of block 2 was computed. Block 2 has never been fetched on any machine; block 1's rows were
seen on 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-03 (§ The Reference Statistics of Block 1 and § The Eigen-Shape Amplitudes of Block 1
in [the journal of 2026-11-03](2026-11-03.md)).

**The samples.** Block 1 and block 2 of the seeded draw ([data/README.md § The Reference
Draw](../../data/README.md#the-reference-draw)), 200 records each, by draw position in
`data/manifests/owmd-n2-order.tsv`. Each is measured by `profiles <block>` (`features::profile`) under (d1)'s rules,
unchanged: the first 900 s of each recording, `short` below 855 s and `gappy` with fewer than half its window's frames
in eight arcs, both excluded (journal of 2026-10-31, § One Row of Measures per Recording: the Rules, Written Before the
Data). Only the rows `profiles` marks `measured` enter. Block 1's are its 179; block 1's TSV must reproduce (d4)'s
SHA-256, `f898936e…ecf6`, or the run stops at a report. Block 2's eigen-shape amplitudes are its projections on the
basis frozen from block 1, `features::eigenshapes::REFERENCE_SHAPES`, never a refit. A measured row of either block
with a measure that is not a number stops the run rather than being dropped. The comparison reads the two TSVs, not
the recordings, so that the two blocks are never on disk together (each is deleted once its TSV is written); the TSVs
round every measure to six decimals, both blocks alike.

**The measures.** All 27 measure columns of `features::profile`, as (d4)'s halves comparison used them: the eight arc
angle spreads, the four eigen-shape amplitude spreads, the two midbody speeds, the four state shares, and the nine
counts, durations and rates of the three modes. The last nine are still *provisional* (STATE.md item 2(c)); they enter
as they did in (d2) and (d4), and a flag on one of them would be reported with that mark. The body length and the gap
shares are context and are not compared.

**The tests**, both with their defaults, fixed before any real data on 2026-10-31:

- the per-measure comparisons, `benchmark::compare::compare_measures`: a Cramér-von Mises statistic per measure, a
  999-shuffle permutation p-value, Holm's correction across the 27 measures, alpha 0.05;
- the classifier two-sample test, `benchmark::two_sample_test`: 21 nearest neighbours, five folds, 999 shuffles, alpha
  0.05.

**The seed.** The decision is made at one seed, **20261104**, for both tests and both comparisons. Beside it, both
comparisons are repeated at the 20 seeds 20261105 to 20261124 and counted: how often the null has any measure
flagged or is told apart by the classifier, and how often the control is seen. These counts describe how much a
verdict depends on the folds and shuffles; they do not decide anything. They are not independent replications: every
seed reads the same recordings.

**The positive control.** `eigen1_amplitude_sd[rad]`, the spread of the first eigen-shape's amplitude, of every
measured row of block 2 is moved up by one standard deviation of that measure over block 1's 179 rows (divisor n - 1),
and every other value is left as it is. Both tests are then run on block 1 against the altered block 2, at the same
seed.

- *Why this measure*: it is the one posture measure the benchmark owes to the frozen basis, and block 2 is its first
  test out of sample (§ The Eigen-Shape Amplitudes of Block 1); a control on it checks that the projection and the
  tests together carry a change through to a verdict. Its block-1 IQR is 0.156 rad around a median of 0.939 rad.
- *Why one standard deviation*: it is the shift at which (e1)'s power table measured the classifier on synthetic
  samples (84 of 100 pairs told apart at 60 worms a side, one measure of four changed), and twice the 0.5 sd shift at
  which (e2)'s comparison caught 68 of 100 at 60 a side (journal of 2026-10-31, § The Benchmark's Test, Built
  Red-First and § The Per-Measure Comparisons). At about 180 worms a side the per-measure comparison should see it
  nearly always. For the classifier no measured number applies: one measure changed among 27, many of them correlated,
  is a setting its power table never had. A larger shift would make the control easier to pass and tell less; how small
  a change each test sees is (e4)'s question, at several sizes.
- *What it is not*: a model of a real difference between worms. Moving one measure alone breaks its correlations with
  the others (for instance with the arc angle spreads), which a classifier may notice more easily than a coherent
  change. That makes this a check that the pipeline can see a stated change, not a measure of how sensitive it is.

**What each test must give, and what counts as failure.**

- *The null, block 1 against block 2*: no measure flagged after Holm's correction (every adjusted p above 0.05), and
  the classifier's p above 0.05. If either test tells the blocks apart at the primary seed, (e3) **fails** and is
  written up as failing. Chance alone does it at most 5% of the time for each test, so at most about 10% for either;
  the other candidates are a fault in the rows, or a real difference between the blocks' recordings, such as the
  periods of the database they come from (§ The Short Recordings of Block 1, Read From Their Metadata, in the journal of
  2026-11-03). The blocks' record dates, taken from the file names in the manifest, would then be compared, and nothing
  would be explained away.
- *The control*: the per-measure comparison must flag `eigen1_amplitude_sd[rad]` (adjusted p at most 0.05), and the
  classifier must tell the samples apart (p at most 0.05). Every other measure's raw p-value must equal the null's,
  since the shuffles do not depend on the values; a difference would be a bug. If the per-measure comparison misses the
  shift, the control **fails** and the benchmark cannot be said to see a change of this size in this measure. If only
  the classifier misses it, the control fails for the classifier, which is reported as a fact about the classifier's
  power at 27 measures, the gap (e2) was built to cover; it is not a reason to change the shift or the settings.
- *Other measures flagged in the control* beyond the null's would be a bug, for the same reason.

**Beside the test, as a description with no pass mark.** Before block 2's files are deleted, `reference-stats 2` is
run on them: block 2's own eigen-shapes, its median and interval per measure, its halves compared, and the frozen
basis checked against block 2's own fit by the law of total variance, which for any block but block 1 is a diagnostic
(§ The Eigen-Shape Amplitudes of Block 1, *After review*). It says how well block 1's basis fits a block it was not
fitted on.

**The tool.** A new binary, `compare-blocks <a.tsv> <b.tsv>` in crate `benchmark`, on a new module
`benchmark::holdout` that reads `profiles`' output (refusing a header whose measures are not today's, in order),
applies the control's shift and runs both tests. The seed, the extra seeds, the control's measure and its size are
constants in the binary, as above. Its tests are seen failing first. The runs: `profiles 1 > runs/profiles-block1.tsv`,
`profiles 2 > runs/profiles-block2.tsv`, then `compare-blocks runs/profiles-block1.tsv runs/profiles-block2.tsv`, each
output's SHA-256 recorded.

## Real Against Unseen Real: What Ran

The plan above was committed (`4c880c9`, 20:22 UTC) and pushed before block 2 was fetched. This section is what
followed.

**What was built**, each test seen failing first:

- `benchmark::holdout` (`crates/benchmark/src/holdout.rs`): `read_profiles` reads `profiles`' output and keeps the
  measured rows, refusing a header whose 27 measure columns are not `features::profile::measure_names` in order, and a
  measured row with a cell that is not a finite number; `column_sd` (divisor n - 1); `shifted`, the control's
  alteration of one column; and `both_tests`, the two tests with their defaults at one seed.
- The binary `compare-blocks <a.tsv> <b.tsv>` (`crates/benchmark/src/bin/compare-blocks.rs`), with the plan's seed,
  its 20 further seeds, the control's measure and its size as constants.

Seven unit tests: measured rows kept and exclusions counted; a header with other or fewer measures refused; a `NaN` or
a missing cell in a measured row refused; the standard deviation's divisor; the shift moving one column only; on
synthetic samples (80 a side, three measures), identical samples not told apart, a column shifted by 2 sd flagged
alone and told apart by the classifier, with the unshifted columns' p-values unchanged; and the binary's control
measure being a measure. Each was broken deliberately by editing the code it guards and seen failing
(`cargo test --locked -p benchmark --lib holdout`, `--bin compare-blocks`), then restored:

| Break | Test that failed |
|-------|------------------|
| Every row kept, whatever its status | Measured rows kept |
| The header's measure names not compared | Other measures refused |
| `NaN` accepted as a number | `NaN` refused |
| The standard deviation divided by n | The divisor |
| Column 0 shifted beside the chosen one | The shift; the shifted column flagged alone |
| `indistinguishable` always true | The shifted column flagged alone |
| Every measure flagged | The shifted column flagged alone |
| The control's measure misspelt | The control measure being a measure |

**Block 1.** Fetched afresh, `./target/release/fetch-block 1`, from 20:18 to 21:35 UTC: 200 of 200 files, each kept
only once its MD5 matched. Then `./target/release/profiles 1 > runs/profiles-block1.tsv` (the binaries built with
`cargo build --release --locked` under rustc 1.99.1; the `features` crate is unchanged on this branch from `main` at
`67d0ffb`): 201 lines, nothing on stderr, SHA-256
`f898936e2f7071f635469cded4b41efe2c7b3a55ac6ef09c422d839f7d2aecf6`, (d4)'s to the byte, as the plan required; 179
measured, 18 `short`, 3 `gappy`. Block 1's files were then deleted before block 2 was fetched.

**A run on block 1's halves first**, not in the plan, as a check of the binary on real rows before block 2 existed:
the TSV cut into its draw positions 1 to 100 and 101 to 200 (`head -101`, and the header with `tail -n +102`), and
`./target/release/compare-blocks h1.tsv h2.tsv > runs/compare-block1-halves.txt` (SHA-256 `55a01559…a923`).
It changed nothing in the plan, which was already pushed, and is reported because it bears on what followed:

- *Null*, 91 against 88 recordings: no measure differs (smallest raw p 0.066), and the classifier gives accuracy
  0.413, p = 0.974. At the 20 further seeds, no measure was flagged and the classifier told the halves apart at none.
- *Control*, a shift of 1 sd = 0.128 rad of the first half: the comparison flags `eigen1_amplitude_sd[rad]` alone (raw
  p 0.001, Holm 0.027) at the primary seed and at all 20 others. The classifier does **not** tell the samples apart at
  the primary seed (accuracy 0.525, p = 0.207), and does at 11 of the 20 others (p from 0.003 to 0.503).
- *A floor worth knowing*: 0.001 is the smallest p-value 999 shuffles can give, so Holm's correction over 27 measures
  cannot go below 27 × 0.001 = 0.027. With 51 or more measures (51 × 0.001 = 0.051) the per-measure comparison could flag nothing at any
  effect size, at 999 shuffles. This is a fact about the settings, for the freeze, (e5).

## Real Against Unseen Real: the Results

`./target/release/compare-blocks runs/profiles-block1.tsv runs/profiles-block2.tsv > runs/compare-blocks-1-2.txt`, at
commit `f0b9159` under rustc 1.99.1; SHA-256 `c3d5c5fa6cf3fef2d8f29f75438ba32bc66da74ebc1d750f8395be81637341ec`, the
same on a second run. Block 1's 179 recordings against block 2's 189, 27 measures, seed 20261104.

**The null: block 1 and block 2 are not told apart.** It passes as the plan said it must.

- *Per measure*: 0 of 27 differ after Holm's correction. The smallest raw p-value is 0.022, and the smallest adjusted
  one is 0.594.
- *The classifier*: accuracy 0.5245, p = 0.181.
- *At the 20 further seeds*: no measure was flagged at any of them. The classifier told the blocks apart at 1 of 20
  (seed 20261116, p = 0.021), and its p-values ran from 0.021 to 0.698. One in 20 is what its 5% level predicts; the
  seeds share the recordings, so the count describes the folds' and shuffles' influence and is no replication.

**The control: told apart by both tests, by the measure moved and by nothing else.** It passes as the plan said it
must.

- The shift was one standard deviation of block 1's `eigen1_amplitude_sd[rad]`, 0.1328 rad, added to each of block
  2's 189 rows. That moved block 2's median from 0.969 to 1.102 rad.
- *Per measure*: `eigen1_amplitude_sd[rad]` alone differs, with raw p = 0.001 and Holm-adjusted p = 0.027. That is the
  floor 999 shuffles and 27 measures allow (§ What Ran, *A floor worth knowing*). Every other measure's raw p-value
  equals the null's, as it must.
- *The classifier*: accuracy 0.6576, p = 0.001, the smallest p-value 999 shuffles can give.
- *At the 20 further seeds*: the measure was flagged at all 20, and the classifier told the samples apart at all 20,
  with p = 0.001 or 0.002.
- *Against the halves' run*: on block 1's halves, about 90 recordings a side, the same 1 sd control was missed by the
  classifier at the primary seed and seen at 11 of 20 further seeds (§ What Ran). At about 180 a side it was seen at
  every seed. So the classifier's power on a one-measure change among 27 depends steeply on the sample's size between
  90 and 180 recordings a side. That is one control on one measure; (e4) measures power properly.

**What was not hidden, and is worth reading.** Nothing was flagged, but the posture measures lean one way. Block 2's
median is higher than block 1's for all eight arc angle spreads and all four eigen-shape amplitude spreads. The
differences run from 0.001 to 0.030 rad. The four smallest raw p-values of the 27 are all posture measures:

- arc 8, the tail: 0.022;
- arc 1, the head: 0.034;
- eigen-shape 4: 0.039;
- eigen-shape 1: 0.055.

Under the null, about 1.4 of 27 raw p-values would fall below 0.05; here three do. The twelve posture measures are
strongly correlated with one another, so "twelve of twelve higher" is far from twelve independent coin tosses, and
no test of the direction was planned. It is noted, not claimed. If a difference in posture between the blocks were
real, the first places to look would be the recordings' dates and rates. That is the comparison the plan reserved for
a failure, and it was not run.

**Beside the test: block 1's basis on block 2.** `./target/release/reference-stats 2 > runs/reference-block2.txt`,
SHA-256 `5a06c63bf88fccf9aba123d61e0b085481a7c91bf378bb78933cb33e375855c9`, took 25 s. It is a description, with no pass
mark, as planned.

- *Block 2's own eigen-shapes*, each recording weighted equally over 4 144 839 frames: shares 39.6%, 26.0%, 23.5% and
  8.6% (block 1: 39.5%, 26.1%, 23.6%, 8.6%). Four carry 95% (97.7%), and did in all 9999 resamples.
- *Alignment with the frozen basis*: the absolute cosine between each of block 2's first four shapes and
  `REFERENCE_SHAPES` is 0.9987, 0.9969, 0.9981 and 0.9998. It was computed from the loadings as `reference-stats`
  prints them, to four decimals, so each figure is good to about 1e-3. These are about the same as block 1's two
  halves had with each other (0.9991, 0.9999, 0.9989, 0.9998).
- *The law of total variance on the frozen basis*: block 2's own eigenvalues differ from the frozen shapes' within and
  between parts by 4e-4 to 1e-3 relative. That is the size of disagreement expected from a basis fitted elsewhere,
  and the same order as block 1's second half alone gave, 2.6e-4 (journal of 2026-11-03, § The Eigen-Shape Amplitudes
  of Block 1, *After review*). Within recordings lies 92.9%, 95.8%, 98.7% and 98.0% of each shape's variance.
- *Block 2's halves*, 96 and 93 recordings: 0 of 27 measures differ, and the classifier gives accuracy 0.487, p =
  0.559.

**What this does and does not show.** It shows the benchmark's two tests, as fixed on synthetic samples, calling two
independent draws of about 180 real worms alike. It also shows them seeing a one-standard-deviation change in one
measure among 27, given those samples. It says nothing yet about how small a change they see, which is (e4); one
measure moved alone is not a model of any real difference between worms. Nine of the 27 measures are still
provisional (item 2(c)). The rows are those of a rule that is not yet frozen (e5). A Grid worm will be compared with
one block, presumably fewer than 180 lives, and the halves' run suggests the classifier's power falls quickly with
the sample's size.

**What is kept.** Both blocks' raw files are deleted. The rows and outputs are in `runs/` (172 KB), not committed, and
are regenerated by the commands above: `profiles-block1.tsv`, `profiles-block2.tsv`, `compare-blocks-1-2.txt`,
`compare-block1-halves.txt` and `reference-block2.txt`.

**What comes next:**

- the posture lean, if (e4) or a later block makes it worth a test of its own, read against the blocks' dates;
- for (e5): the Holm floor, which sets a ceiling of 50 measures at 999 shuffles.
