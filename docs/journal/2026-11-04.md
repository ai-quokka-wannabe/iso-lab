## Does the Greedy Rule's Direction Fail? Each Steering Decision Scored

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

STATE.md item 9 (b) names the next step left by the journal of 2026-11-03, § Why Steering Trails Never Steering in Warm
Layouts. There, on five fresh warm layouts:

- turns towards the band, kept alone, raised late energy by +0.1233 (+0.0421 to +0.2160) against never steering;
- turns away from it, kept alone, cost;
- the graded gain's own decisions split about evenly between the two (descriptive).

The reading left open was that the map's direction is what fails. A toy world: none of this is evidence about
*C. elegans*.

**The question**: when H14's graded gain steers, which link of the greedy rule fails to point it at the band?

Each decision passes three links:

1. the map chooses a temperature direction, warmer or colder;
2. the lateral sense says which side is warmer;
3. a turn towards that side is taken as a turn towards the food.

**The scores**, read for evaluation only from the world's ground truth after the brain has acted. Nothing reaches the
brain. Each scored decision is one made at a 10-s decision tick with a non-zero raw command, by a worm off band food
and outside the band's x-range. That means x below 29, where the band is warmer, or above 41, where it is colder.

- `map`: the chosen direction points the band's way in temperature.
- `lat`: the brain's lateral sense matches the noise-free sign of left minus right temperature.
- `tturn`: the turn truly rotates the heading towards the band's side in temperature.
- `bear`: the turn reduces the angle between the heading and the bearing to the nearest band patch's centre.

**The hunches**, each with what would refute it:

- **H-map, the map's direction is what fails**, *a hunch*. The share s_map is near chance. Supported if s_map's
  interval lies wholly below 0.6. Refuted if it lies wholly above 0.6. Undecided otherwise.
- **H-sense, the lateral sense does not fail.** Supported if s_lat's interval's lower bound is at least 0.9.
  Otherwise not supported, and the sense shares the failure.
- **H-geo, temperature is a fair guide to the band.** s_geo is the share of the turns towards the band in temperature
  that also reduce the bearing error to the nearest patch. Supported if its lower bound is at least 0.7. Refuted if its
  upper bound is below 0.6. Undecided otherwise.

If H-map is supported, the cost of steering is in the map. If it is refuted, the failure lies in the sense or the
geometry, and H-sense and H-geo say which.

## Q7: Headings Over a Quarter of a Body Length

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer.

The lab's own open question in the salt draft: how to keep low-speed heading noise from making false turns. The rule is
decided and written in [SALT_MEASURES.md](../benchmark/SALT_MEASURES.md), P5 and § The Open Questions, Q7, **before any
salt track is fetched or computed**. Nothing was downloaded or run for it.

- **Read for it**:
    - Chen et al. 2023's methods (PMC10425172, Europe PMC full text, re-read from the copy fetched for #215): the
      third-order polynomial smoothing in a 0.5 s window, turns as angle changes over 1 s above 60° "to mitigate effects
      of small displacements from tracking noise", and curvature over 1 mm of displacement;
    - the preprint's § 2.1 and § 4.2: its turn is a single step's 50°, and its pipeline is "modified from" Chen et
      al. 2023;
    - the lab's record: the pause threshold of 2.5% of the body length per second (behaviour-measures.md § Locomotion
      States, `features::states`), and FEATURES.md's tracking-noise item.

  Luo et al. 2014's supplement and Pierce-Shimomura et al. 1999's methods remain unread, as before.
- **The rule**: a heading only over a straight-line displacement of at least ℓ = 0.25 *L*, with short steps joined
  until it is reached. Turns (50°, counted at onset), runs and curvature are taken over these kept samples. It is the
  same rule for both bodies, each in its own body length. Neither candidate was taken as written. A speed floor at the
  lab's pause threshold is 10 µm a step, a third of a pixel, and protects nothing. A floor high enough to protect a
  heading (0.7 *L*/s) would discard slow crawling. Joining short steps keeps slow crawling and drops pauses.
- **The value**: with an assumed error of up to one pixel (32 µm, inferred) at each sample and Q2's assumed 1123 µm
  body, a heading at 0.25 *L* (8.8 pixels) errs by at most 12.8°, and a heading change by at most about 25.6°, half the
  threshold. At 0.125 *L* that bound is 48.9°. The arithmetic is atan(2 / (0.25 × 1123 / 32)), done by hand and checked
  with `python3 -c` (12.83°; 48.99° at the eighth).
    - *Corrected after #218's review*: the figures above are the perpendicular case, not the worst. The worst case is
      arcsin(2 / *d*): 13.18° per heading and 26.35° per change at 0.25 *L* (8.773 pixels), and 27.12° and 54.25° at
      0.125 *L* (4.387 pixels), which is beyond the threshold. The perpendicular figures are 12.84°, 25.68°, 24.51° and
      49.02°. The command was
      `python3 -c "import math; [print(f, round(f*1123/32,3), *(round(k*math.degrees(g(2/(f*1123/32))),2) for g in (math.asin, math.atan) for k in (1,2))) for f in (0.125,0.25,0.5)]"`,
      Python 3.13.16. The conclusion holds and is stronger: an eighth does not clear the threshold. SALT_MEASURES.md now
      gives the arcsin bound.
    - *Also after review*: a run's duration is now the sum of its kept samples' intervals, so a run of one sample has a
      positive duration. M2 is now defined interval by interval over the kept samples.
- **Disclosed**: the files' median steps (3.64-5.11 pixels) were published in #216 and were known when choosing. The
  rule will join two or three steps in ordinary crawling; the value was set by the noise bound, not by that median.
- **Registered**: item 11 (c) reports M2, M3, M4 and M6 at 0.125 *L* and 0.5 *L* beside the primary value, as a sensitivity
  check that cannot change the primary.
- **A lead for Q3**: the preprint's smoothing is probably Chen et al. 2023's 0.5 s cubic polynomial, but this is not
  confirmed, and Q3 stays open.
- **The two nits from #217's review**: the open-questions section's opening line now says which questions go to the
