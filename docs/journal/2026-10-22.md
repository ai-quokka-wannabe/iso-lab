## H13: A Tap Protocol That Tells Depression from Recovery?

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent, at the maintainer's request; not by the maintainer. Written before it
runs.

The profile of 2026-10-19 (journal, § H13: Does the Rest Protocol Sharpen the Valley at All?) found the statistics
nearly blind to the trade between habituation's depression, `dep`, and its recovery time, `tau`. Along the trade the
distance rises one to two hundred times less than across it, under both the old protocol and the rest protocol.

*A hunch*: taps at two different intervals give two different habituated levels, and two levels fix two parameters
where one cannot. The real assay's habituation depends on the interval between taps ([Rankin and Broster
1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.106.2.239), abstract). This tests that on the toy, in two stages fixed here.

- **The protocols**, each within the 600 s recording, taps from 300 s:
    - **old**: 30 taps at 10 s; bins of taps 0-3, 3-10, 10-20 and 20-30.
    - **rest**: 20 taps at 10 s, then probes at 520, 550 and 580 s; bins 0-3, 3-10, 10-20 and each probe.
    - **twoisi**: 10 taps at 10 s (300 to 390 s), then 6 at 30 s (420 to 570 s); bins 0-3, 3-10, 10-12, 12-14 and
      14-16.
    - **slowfirst**: 6 taps at 30 s (300 to 450 s), then 14 at 10 s (460 to 590 s); bins 0-2, 2-4, 4-6, 6-9, 9-14 and
      14-20.
    - **probe60**: 10 taps at 10 s, then probes at 450, 510 and 570 s; bins 0-3, 3-10 and each probe.
- **The script**: `h13_protocols.py` (SHA-256 `e8f280d883434e9fb1f6c1b24560ca3d82c37a0c132591d06e440ab361903bd6`)
  generalises the 2026-10-19 habituation script to a table of
  protocols. Its `check` mode reproduced the notebook bit for bit with the old taps, and failed with one bin moved,
  before this entry.
- **Stage 1, the profile**: `python3 -I h13_protocols.py profile <protocol>` for all five. It reports the trade cost
  (the mean distance over the anti-diagonal cells (1.4, 0.7), (1.2, 0.85), (0.85, 1.2) and (0.7, 1.4), less the
  truth's cell) at distance seeds 4343 to 4347, and the same for the diagonal, with each mean's 95% t interval.
    - **A built-in control**: old's and rest's trade costs must reproduce the 2026-10-19 values seed by seed
      (0.0003010 ... and 0.0005455 ... at seed 4343).
- **The selection rule**: a candidate (twoisi, slowfirst or probe60) qualifies if the lower end of its trade cost's
  interval exceeds five times old's mean trade cost. The winner is the qualifying candidate with the largest mean. If
  none qualifies, the hunch is **refuted** for designs that fit in 600 s, and stage 2 does not run.
- **Stage 2, the fit**: `python3 -I h13_protocols.py fit <winner> <7..14>`, on the winner's near noise-free target,
  with 128 worms per candidate, 360 generations and X pinned - the settings of the 2026-10-19 habituation test.
    - **H1**: `dep` and `tau` both within 0.2 (`|ln(fit / truth)| <= 0.2`) in at least 7 of 8 seeds. The old protocol
      gave 5 of 8 and the rest protocol 3 of 8.
    - **H2**: `k_rd` within 0.2 in at least 7 of 8.
- **What it would mean**: H1 holding would show the trade was the measurement's, and that two intervals cure it on
  the toy. The protocol would then go into the whole loop on fresh populations, pre-registered on its own. H1
  failing with a qualifying profile would mean a sharper valley is not yet enough at this fitter's budget.

## H13: Is the Trade the Model's or the Measurement's?

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The protocol search above left two suspects for why the statistics barely see the trade between `dep` and `tau`:
the toy's habituation model itself, or a measurement too noisy to read it.

- **Method**: a calculation, not a simulation. `h13_expected.py` (SHA-256 `2998cc5e625b242f21b7a3ed11ce039b8f56e11429caf32700438dce9ae45464`) iterates the notebook's own
  habituation rule exactly as the simulator does, with the mean parameters. At a tap the response probability is
  `p0 * w`, and `w` is then multiplied by `1 - dep`; at every step `w` recovers by `(1 - w) * DT / tau`.
    - It runs for the truth and the four trade points, under each of the five protocols above.
    - For each it reports the largest difference from the truth in a bin's expected response, and the worms needed to
      see the trade at about three standard errors: 9 divided by the sum over bins of (difference / per-worm sd)
      squared.
    - The per-worm sd of a bin is binomial over its taps. That is a lower bound, since worm-to-worm variation and
      reversals already in progress only add to it, so the worms needed is a lower bound too.
- **Decision rule, fixed here** (a calculation, so a reading rule rather than a test):
    - **The model is the limit** if, for every protocol, every trade point's largest difference is below 0.02 in
      response probability.
    - Otherwise **the measurement is the limit**, and the worms needed say how far: a protocol could work at DEV's size
      if it needs no more than 30 worms at the outer trade points, (1.4, 0.7) and (0.7, 1.4).

### Results

- **The run**: `python3 -I h13_expected.py`, at 19:16:15 UTC. The method was committed at 19:16:12 and pushed as
  `1819de5`; the push is logged at 19:16:15, the same second as the run. It takes under a
  second, with no random numbers. The output is in the pull request.
- **What came out**, for each protocol, the largest difference from the truth in a bin's expected response and the
  worms needed, at the trade points (1.4, 0.7), (1.2, 0.85), (0.85, 1.2) and (0.7, 1.4):
    - old: 0.019, 0.016, 0.013 and 0.035; 406, 917, 1294 and 197 worms.
    - rest: 0.037, 0.016, 0.019 and 0.035; 277, 772, 946 and 170 worms.
    - twoisi: 0.030, 0.016, 0.013 and 0.035; 282, 845, 993 and 202 worms.
    - slowfirst: 0.013, 0.011, 0.006 and 0.023; 1995, 2046, 7218 and 425 worms.
    - probe60: 0.039, 0.016, 0.017 and 0.035; 317, 809, 1029 and 191 worms.
- **By the rule fixed beforehand**: some differences exceed 0.02, up to 0.039, so **the measurement is the limit**, by
  the rule's letter. And none of the five could work at DEV's size: at the outer trade points every one needs 170 worms or
  more, against DEV's 30. (Narrowed after #104's stage 1 from "no protocol could", which the short-interval train
  below contradicts.)
- **What it means**, as *interpretation* beyond the rule's two outcomes (worded after verification): within these
  five designs, both suspects share the blame.
    - **These designs leave little to see.** At the outer trade points, (1.4, 0.7) and (0.7, 1.4) - +40% against
      -30% - no bin's expected response moves by more than about four percentage points, and at the inner ones by
      about two at most.
    - **A yes-or-no measurement cannot see so little.** These designs need, at this lower bound, 170 to 406 worms at
      the outer trade points (slowfirst 425 to 1995) and about 770 to 1300 at the inner ones.
    - The bound rests on two choices: three standard errors (the 9), and the binning. Per-tap bins lower old's
      figures to 319, 795, 1092 and 179. The binomial variance at a bin's mean probability slightly overstates the pure
      tap noise, by under 2% here. Everything else it leaves out - reversals already in progress, spontaneous onsets,
      variation between worms, other parameters absorbing the difference - pushes the true figure up.
    - So, for these five designs, `dep` and `tau` are not separately identifiable from tens of worms. That explains
      every round's trade and the protocol search's null result.
    - **Not tried, and not excluded**: short-interval trains. The verifier ran this same calculation on, for example,
      60 taps at 2 s and then probes at 450, 520 and 590 s, and found a lower bound of 29, 80, 94 and 16 worms. A bound
      does not show such a design works: at a 2 s interval many taps fall during a reversal, which the calculation
      ignores. But the bound does not rule it out, so the general claim is not established.
    - It also fits the fits on the 1024-worm target, where the fitter judges each candidate on 128 simulated worms -
      fewer than the trade needs.
- **For H13 and for the benchmark** - a question for the maintainer, not settled here. There are three options:
    - first test a short-interval protocol, which if it held up would need neither of the other two - the cheapest
      next step, profiled in seconds;
    - size the benchmark's tap assay for hundreds of worms per condition, by a power calculation like this one, before
      any recording is planned;
    - or judge habituation in criterion 1 on the combination the data can see - roughly the habituated level - and
      not on `dep` and `tau` apart.
    - Changing criterion 1 now, after these results, would move a goalpost, so it is left for the maintainer to rule
      on.

## H13: Does the Fitter's Own Noise Hold Back Habituation?

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The calculation above says a ±40% trade between `dep` and `tau` needs about 200 to 400 worms to see, and a ±20%
trade about 800 to 1300 - a lower bound. On the near noise-free target, the target itself has 1024 worms, but the
fitter judges each candidate on 128. That predicts the fitter's own noise as the limit there.

- **Prediction under test**: with 512 worms per candidate instead of 128, the fit recovers `dep` and `tau` better on
  the same target.
- **Method**: `h13_nind.py` (SHA-256 `c7271f97da1b8cb44bebc648f7cdff52d05e7a97a4420a4b7c65fa7ab59273b8`) is `h13_protocols.py`'s fit mode under the old protocol, with the
  number of worms per candidate as an argument. The run is `python3 -I h13_nind.py fit old <7..10> 512` from the
  repository's root: optimiser seeds 7 to 10, 360 generations, X pinned, four processes.
- **Baseline**, the same seeds at 128 worms and generation 360 (the long run's outputs, PR #97):
    - `dep` and `tau` both within 0.2 for 3 of 4;
    - the larger of `|ln dep|` and `|ln tau|` per seed 0.163, 0.480, 0.155 and 0.178, mean 0.244.
    - The long run's fit at 360 generations is the same computation as a 360-generation fit, with the same seeds and
      the same consumption of random numbers.
- **Rule fixed beforehand**: the prediction holds if both:
    - `dep` and `tau` are both within 0.2 for all 4 seeds;
    - the mean over seeds of the larger error is at most half the baseline's, 0.122.
- **What it would mean**: holding, the fitter's noise is the limit on the noise-free target, consistent with the
  calculation. Failing, something beyond worms per candidate keeps the pair apart, and the calculation's account is
  incomplete. Four seeds is a small test; it is sized to this session's time.

## H13: A Short-Interval Train, Profiled and Fitted

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The verification of the protocol search (PR #103) found that the expected-response calculation does not rule out a
short-interval train. Taps every 2 s gave a lower bound of about 16 to 30 worms at the outer trade points, against
170 or more for intervals of 10 s and longer. A bound ignores taps that land during a reversal, when the simulated
worm cannot respond; a profile through the simulator does not. This tests that design with the same two stages and
rules as the protocol search above.

- **The protocol, short2**: 60 taps every 2 s from 300 s (300 to 418 s), then probes at 450, 520 and 590 s. Its bins
  are taps 0-2, 2-5, 5-10, 10-20, 20-40 and 40-60, then each probe alone.
- **The script**: `h13_short.py` (SHA-256 `373141f22c562d6d8766b7f3a28fc8c879215db87e40bc3b675bb2083058cd6d`) is
  `h13_protocols.py` with that row added to its table, and its
  header changed.
- **Stage 1**: `python3 -I h13_short.py profile short2`, and `... profile old` as a control that must reproduce
  old's trade cost above (mean 0.0004399).
    - **Qualifies** if the lower end of short2's trade-cost interval exceeds 0.0022, five times old's mean - the
      protocol search's bar. If it does not, stage 2 does not run.
- **Stage 2**: `python3 -I h13_short.py fit short2 <7..14>`, 128 worms per candidate, 360 generations, X pinned,
  on short2's near noise-free target. It starts once the 512-worm fits above finish, so the two do not share the
  machine's four processors.
    - **H1**: `dep` and `tau` both within 0.2 in at least 7 of 8 seeds (old 5 of 8; rest 3 of 8).
    - **H2**: `k_rd` within 0.2 in at least 7 of 8.
- **What it would mean**: H1 holding would show that a short-interval train separates habituation's two parameters
  on the toy, at the fitter's present budget. That would be the first of the maintainer's three options, and the
  protocol would then go into the whole loop on fresh populations, pre-registered anew.
