## H13: Does the Pass Hold beyond DEV?

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent, at the maintainer's request; not by the maintainer. Written before it
runs.

Yesterday's last test (journal of 2026-10-18, § H13: The Whole Loop on DEV, with a Longer Search) passed its rule for
the first time. Criterion 1 held for 5 of 5 optimiser seeds, and the rate of leaving roaming, `k_rd`, was within 0.2
for all five, though still about 1.17 times the truth. All five fits, though, used one population, DEV's 30 worms, so
the pass may belong to that draw. This tests the same loop on populations it has never seen.

- **Question**: does the loop at 128 worms per candidate and 360 generations pass criterion 1 on fresh populations,
  and is the high bias in `k_rd` DEV's or the method's?
- **Method**:
    - **The populations**: five fresh ones of 60, drawn from the truth exactly as DEV's was (population seed
      `6000 + 10 i`, simulation seed one more, `i` = 0 to 4). In each, the first 30 worms are the fitting group and the
      last 30 are held out.
    - **The loop**: round 4's whole loop as in yesterday's `h13_loop360.py` - fit, ablate, drop below 2s, refit,
      criterion 1. It runs with optimiser seed 7 only. The script differs from `h13_loop360.py` in its header and in
      building the population from its index.
    - **The run**: `python3 -I h13_populations.py <0..4>` from the repository's root, five processes, about 25
      minutes. Script SHA-256 `642898bc7c27749b8fb057613603b100185b2463d3e073b0f6594ec94e3ec7ba`.
- **Rules fixed beforehand**:
    - **G1, the pass generalises**: criterion 1 passes for at least 4 of the 5 populations, and `k_rd` is within 0.2
      for at least 4 of the 5.
    - **G2, the bias is the method's**: the mean of `ln(fitted k_rd / truth)` over the five populations has a 95% t
      interval (4 degrees of freedom) that excludes zero. Whether it also covers DEV's +0.153 is reported, not ruled
      on.
- **What each answer would mean**:
    - **G1 holding**: the loop's recovery is not a property of DEV's draw.
    - **G1 failing**: yesterday's pass was in part DEV's luck. The rate of 4 in 5 is then not yet earned, and the next
      step is the cause of the failures, not a new feature.
    - **G2 holding**: the high bias in `k_rd` belongs to the method at this budget, not to one population. The next
      suspects are the fitter's floor spread and the statistics' weak view of the switching timescale.

### Results

- **What came out**, for populations 0 to 4 (seeds 6000 to 6040):
    - **Criterion 1 passes for all 5.** Ablation dropped X, and only X, in all five.
    - **`k_rd`** is 0.96, 1.05, 1.04, 1.25 and 1.05 times the truth, so within 0.2 for 4 of 5; population 3's 1.25 is
      0.223 in log.
    - The largest error per population: 0.353 (`tau`), 0.271 (`tau`), 0.318 (`dep`), 0.222 (`k_rd`) and 0.153
      (`h_dwell`). The median `|ln|` is 0.037 to 0.060.
    - **Habituation**: `dep` and `tau` are both within 0.2 for only 2 of 5 (populations 3 and 4). In populations 0
      and 2, `dep` is about 1.36 times high and `tau` 0.70 and 0.85 - the old trade.

- **By the rules fixed beforehand**:
    - **G1 holds**: criterion 1 passes for 5 of 5, and `k_rd` is within 0.2 for 4 of 5. The loop's recovery is not

- **What it means** (worded after verification): on populations it has never seen, the loop at 128 worms and 360
  generations passes criterion 1 every time. No bias in `k_rd` is shown, but five populations cannot tell no bias
  from one of DEV's size. The weak spot is now habituation: `dep` and `tau` are not both within 0.2 in three of the
  five. They trade in two (0 and 2), and in population 1 only `tau` is off. The errors reach 0.35 in log, inside
  criterion 1's 0.4 ceiling but only just. This is five
  populations with one optimiser seed each, on our own toy; it is a test of the method, not of a brain against the
  real worm.

## H13: A Tap Protocol with a Rest, to Separate Depression from Recovery

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

Habituation's two parameters - the depression per tap, `dep`, and the recovery time, `tau` - have traded against each
other in every round since round 2. In the long run on the near noise-free target (journal of 2026-10-18, PR #97's
outputs), both were within 0.2 at generation 360 for only 5 of 8 seeds. A protocol of taps at a fixed 10 s interval
never shows the recovery on its own, since every interval both depresses and recovers. The real assay measures it
directly: [Rankin and Broster 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.106.2.239) studied "the rate of recovery from
habituation" of the tap reflex. [Broster and Rankin 1994](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.108.6.1019) measured
"spontaneous recovery from habituation" after training (both read in their abstracts only).

- **Question**: with a rest and probe taps after a shorter train, does the fit recover `dep` and `tau` both?
- **The rest protocol, fixed here**:
    - **Taps**: 20 taps every 10 s from 300 s, then probe taps at 520, 550 and 580 s (30, 60 and 90 s after the train).
    - **Bins**: the tap-response bins are the train's taps 0-3, 3-10 and 10-20, and each probe alone. That makes six
      bins instead of four, and 19 statistics instead of 17.
    - Nothing else in the simulator or the statistics changes. The truth is the same; with the decoy X at zero, the
      anticipation term is zero.
- **Method**:
    - **The patch**: the notebook's code is patched only where the taps are listed and binned. `python3 -I
      h13_habituation.py check` verified, before this entry, that with the old taps the patch reproduces the notebook's
      statistics bit for bit on 16 worms. With one bin deliberately moved, the same check reports a mismatch.
    - **The fits**: the near noise-free target (1024 worms at the truth, seed 4242) under the rest protocol, optimiser
      seeds 7 to 14, 128 worms per candidate, 360 generations, X pinned. These are the long run's settings at its
      360 checkpoint, which is the baseline.
    - **The run**: `python3 -I h13_habituation.py rest <7..14>` from the repository's root, eight processes. Script
      SHA-256 `bed862c3d63fdc9f42c69bb033dd38cf70621f2d417b67acd67618e8b58c2797`.
- **Rules fixed beforehand**:
    - **H1, separated**: `dep` and `tau` are both within 0.2 in at least 7 of 8 seeds, against the baseline's 5 of 8.
    - **H2, no harm**: `k_rd` is within 0.2 in at least 7 of 8 seeds (the baseline had 8 of 8).
- **What each answer would mean**:
    - **H1 and H2 holding**: the trade was the protocol's, not the model's or the fitter's, and the rest protocol
      goes into the loop on DEV next. As a lesson for the benchmark, its tap assay should include a recovery probe.
    - **H1 failing**: the trade survives a direct view of recovery. The next suspects are the response statistic,
      which is binary within 1 s of a tap, and the noise at 128 worms.

### Results

- **The run**: `python3 -I h13_habituation.py rest <7..14>`, eight processes, started at 19:47:25 UTC by a waiter that
  launched them when the populations test finished; the last of the eight outputs was written at 20:06:53. The
  method was pushed as `03e45c1` at 19:24:59 (GitHub's log), before the run. Python 3.13.16, numpy 2.5.4; seeded. The script and outputs are in the pull request.
- **What came out**, `dep` and `tau` as multiples of the truth, for seeds 7 to 14:
    - 0.97 and 0.82; 1.18 and 0.70; 0.87 and 1.39; 0.79 and 0.99; 0.83 and 1.14; 1.21 and 0.86; 0.75 and 1.28; 0.86 and
      0.99.
    - Both are within 0.2 - meaning `|ln(fit / truth)| <= 0.2`, as in every round - for **3 of 8** seeds (11, 12 and
      14). The baseline, the old protocol at generation 360, had 5
      of 8.
    - `k_rd` is 1.036 to 1.053 times the truth, within 0.2 in all 8. Its mean in log is +0.043, about the same as the
      baseline's +0.045 at the same checkpoint (interval +0.030 to +0.060; journal of 2026-10-18, § the long run).
- **By the rules fixed beforehand**:
    - **H1 fails**: 3 of 8, where 7 were needed. That is fewer than the baseline's 5, though at eight seeds the
      difference is not shown.
    - **H2 holds**: `k_rd` is within 0.2 in 8 of 8.
- **What it means**: a rest with three probe taps does not separate the depression from the recovery time, at this
  budget.
    - The trade persists in both directions: weaker depression with slower recovery (seeds 9 and 13), and stronger
      depression with faster recovery (seed 8). *Untested here*: each pair may predict a similar habituated level.
    - The rule's failure branch named the next suspects, and they fit, though this run does not test them. Each probe
      is a single tap, scored yes or no per worm within 1 s, so a probe bin is one Bernoulli draw per worm. Three of
      them carry little against the fitter's 128-worm noise. The train is also shorter (20 taps, not 30), which may
      cost information on the depression.
    - A further reading, *untested*: the rest protocol may need more probes, or more worms per candidate, before the
      recovery's signal stands above the noise.
- **For the benchmark**, the lesson survives as a question rather than an answer.
    - The real assay does measure recovery from habituation, in what its abstract calls "a simple reflex circuit"
      ([Rankin and Broster 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.106.2.239)). The pre-registration above says "of
      the tap reflex", which is not the abstract's wording.
    - The same abstract adds that the rate of recovery depends on the interval between taps, and on whether the
      response had reached its floor. The toy's recovery ignores both.
    - Whether a protocol's probes identify a model's recovery time depends on how many there are and how the response
      is scored. That can be answered cheaply, by profiling the distance along the trade before any fit.

## H13: Does the Rest Protocol Sharpen the Valley at All?

Written by iso-lab's night staff, an AI agent; not by the maintainer. Written before it runs.

The rest protocol above did not help the fit separate `dep` from `tau`. There are two different reasons it might have
failed. Either the protocol carries more information about the pair and the fitter's noise hid it, or it carries
little more to begin with. A profile of the distance, with no fit, tells the two apart.

- **Question**: under the rest protocol, does the distance rise more steeply along the trade between `dep` and `tau`
  than under the old one?
- **Method**:
    - **The grid**: for each protocol, the distance at 1024 simulated worms (seed 4343) to that protocol's own near
      noise-free target (1024 worms at the truth, seed 4242). It is taken on a 5 x 5 grid of `dep` and `tau`, each at
      0.7, 0.85, 1, 1.2 and 1.4 times the truth, with the other parameters at the truth.
    - **The protocols**: *old*, 30 taps at 10 s, with 17 statistics; *rest*, the protocol above, with 19 statistics.
      The distance is a mean of squared z-scores per statistic, so it is comparable across the two.
    - **The run**: `python3 -I h13_profile.py <old|rest>` from the repository's root, two processes, a few minutes.
      Script SHA-256 `d9f3a9013f6b587ca2c7e9c6cedeec568b46c78ebdd3f0cf0a397bba6511ad63`. It is the habituation test's
      script, with only its header, its protocol names and its last lines changed.
- **The statistic, fixed before the data**: the *trade cost* of a protocol is the mean distance over the four
  off-truth cells of the anti-diagonal - (1.4, 0.7), (1.2, 0.85), (0.85, 1.2) and (0.7, 1.4), as `dep` and `tau`
  multiples - minus the distance at the truth's own cell. The anti-diagonal is where the fits' errors lay: high `dep`
  with low `tau`, or the reverse.
- **Rule fixed beforehand**:
    - **P1, sharper**: the rest protocol's trade cost is at least twice the old protocol's.
- **What each answer would mean**:
    - **P1 holding**: the rest protocol does carry more information on the pair, and the fit's failure is the fitter's
      noise. More worms per candidate is then the next step.
    - **P1 failing**: three binary probes add little. The protocol itself needs redesigning - more probes, or a graded
      response - before another fit.

### Results

- **The run**: `python3 -I h13_profile.py <old|rest>`, two processes, from 20:09:47 to 20:10:05 UTC, launched after the method was
  pushed (`51934eb`). Python 3.13.16, numpy 2.5.4; seeded. The script and outputs are in the pull request.
- **What came out**:
    - **The anti-diagonal, where the trade lies**, at (1.4, 0.7), (1.2, 0.85), (0.85, 1.2) and (0.7, 1.4):
        - old: 0.0040, 0.0050, 0.0039 and 0.0030, against 0.0037 at the truth's own cell;
        - rest: 0.0033, 0.0035, 0.0030 and 0.0028, against 0.0026.
    - **The other diagonal, both raised together**, climbs steeply: at (1.4, 1.4) it reaches 0.0965 (old) and 0.0614
      (rest).
    - **The trade cost**, from the printed values: 0.00028 for old and 0.00055 for rest, a ratio of 2.0.
- **By the rule fixed beforehand**: **P1 is undetermined.** The ratio sits exactly at the threshold of twice. The
  printed values are rounded to four decimals, and rounding alone could put the ratio anywhere from about 1.2 to 3.7.
  Both costs are also smaller than the truth cell's own distance, 0.0026 to 0.0037, which is the noise floor of a
  1024-worm comparison from one seed. The rule cannot be read either way.
- **What the profile shows, beyond the rule**: along the trade, the distance is flat to within its noise floor under
  both protocols, over ±40% in each parameter. Along the other diagonal it rises more than twentyfold.
    - So the statistics see one combination of `dep` and `tau`, much like the habituated level, and not the two
      separately - with the old taps or with the rest and three probes.
    - That explains the trade seen in every round, and why neither more worms nor longer fits removed it. No fitter
      can find a minimum the distance does not have.
    - It also answers the question this profile was set: the rest protocol's failure above is not the fitter's
      noise hiding information. The information is not in these statistics.
- **What it means for H13**: habituation's two parameters cannot be told apart by these statistics under either
  protocol. Criterion 1 has passed with them anyway, because the error stays inside its 0.4 ceiling. The fix belongs
  in the measurement: probes spread over longer rests, many more of them, or a graded response. Each can be profiled
  this way, in minutes, before any fit.
- **For the benchmark**: a profile like this, of the distance along every pair of parameters, is a cheap test of
  whether a comparison space can identify a model at all. It belongs before any fit.
