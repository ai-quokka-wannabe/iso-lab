Item 6 of STATE.md's *Next* list asks for a plan for every one of the maintainer's hunches. Section D, chemistry and
affect, has thirteen, more than one run can plan with care, so it was split: D1-D6 here, D7-D13 and A10's question

([data/README.md § Fetching a Block](../../data/README.md#fetching-a-block)), and fewer than two hours were left; items 1 and 3 to 5 are reading or
wait on a ruling, and none fitted the time left.

The plans are in [docs/hypotheses/experiments/D1-D6.md](../hypotheses/experiments/D1-D6.md). Every lead was looked up
in Crossref by title, and for the five worm papers the abstract was read in Europe PMC, by DOI through its REST search
(`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:"<doi>"&resultType=core&format=json`). No number is
taken from any of them; the plans name the numbers to be read at preparation.

Two things the abstracts showed:

- The hunch lists "Ghosh et al. 2016" among D2's three leads, without a title. The paper found under that name, in *Neuron*
  ([doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2016.10.030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.10.030)), is about worms crossing a
  hyperosmotic barrier toward a food odour, more willingly when deprived of food. The plan takes it to be the lead for
  starvation lowering avoidance, and says so.
- [Rengarajan et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1807454116) report octopamine promoting attraction to carbon
  dioxide in starved worms, not roaming. The hunch's claim that octopamine promotes roaming still has no lead, and D2
  does not test it.

One fact about the world shaped D2: the Grid's chemical sense as designed (tron-grid-lite's `docs/CHEMORECEPTION.md`,
at the pinned commit) carries odours as unlabelled mixtures on the food orbs, with no gas apart from food. A valence
flip needs a cue that is not food, so D2 waits on one, and asks the world for it when the time comes.

Every plan waits on food and a sense of intake; none can run before those land upstream (STATE.md, *Needs From Other
Repositories*). D5 is a rule over all modulators rather than one experiment, and D3 and D4 test mechanisms whose
fidelity to the worm the lab cannot yet judge, as their plans say.

The second agent's review of the first draft found ten things to change before merge, among them three refuting
clauses decided by the design rather than by a run:

- **D2.** Switching off the octopamine-like modulator could never show a delayed approach with only a repulsive gain
  left, so that ablation is now reported but not tested. The pinned-control clause became a harness check.
- **D4.** The learning rate's difference between the worlds was built in, so it is now a harness property, and the
  stable world gets one late reversal so that behaviour can be compared.
- **D1 and D5.** Clauses counted against "half" with no interval rule, and D5's random draws were scored on too few
  lives to pass. Both now have Wilson intervals against margins fixed in the plan, and D5's draws use the battery's
  own 20 lives, which raises its cost to 4000 lives.
- **The draft overstated Ben Arous et al.** It attributed a hidden Markov model to their abstract, which describes only
  "a new quantitative method".

The draft is kept in the pull request's history.

## Plans for D7-D13

The second half of section D is planned in [docs/hypotheses/experiments/D7-D13.md](../hypotheses/experiments/D7-D13.md),
which also answers the question [A10's plan](../hypotheses/experiments/A9-A15.md#a10-welfare-and-affect-read-from-behaviour-alone)
left to this section. The first draft of D1-D6 had clauses decided by the design rather than by a run. So this time
every check that holds by construction is written as an assertion over every tick, not counted among the refuting
tests.

What the reading found, by Crossref title searches and Europe PMC abstracts (the DOI query above; the play search's
queries are given in the plan):

- **D9's *Why*.** The hunch says the worm's decision to stay on or leave a patch is serotonin-modulated. Two worm
  papers on patch-leaving were found and their abstracts read: [Milward et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1106134109)
  and [Bendesky et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09821). Neither names serotonin; the second names a tyramine
  receptor. The claim still has no lead.
- **D12.** The worm probably has an opioid-like system: the abstract of
  [Cheong et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.06683) suggests one, with NLP-24 and NPR-17 regulating feeding. Exogenous opioids reduce its thermal avoidance, an effect
  reversed by antagonists, by the abstract of [Nieto-Fernandez et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10158-010-0099-5).
  Whether its own system damps avoidance after repeated noxious stimuli is left to preparation. The plan includes a
  control with desensitisation alone, because fading avoidance may be adaptation rather than analgesia.
- **D11.** The emotions-as-regions part of the hunch is for rodent-scale rungs, as the hunch itself says. For the
  worm, the plan asks how many slow dimensions the worm's own behaviour needs, from the reference recordings.
- **A10's question.** No report of play in a nematode was found. The search was narrow: Europe PMC titles with "play"
  and a worm word gave 34 records, none about play behaviour. A paper asking whether bumble bees play exists, but its abstract could
  not be reached. Play is set aside as a welfare measure for the worm until a source says otherwise.

Section D is now planned whole. Item 6 moves on to E1-E10.

- **D10's pass and refute outcomes could both hold at once.** The pass now needs the drift wholly above the ±5-point
  margin.
- **D11's method ignored time order.** It could not find *slow* dimensions, and its shuffled control could never differ
  from the run. It now counts components with a lagged autocorrelation above a frame-shuffled null.
- **The play search was misdescribed.** One title hit uses "role play" as a noun, and the exact queries were missing.

The smaller points were also fixed:

- D9's ordering clause and two of D7's three tests were demoted to harness properties or assertions.
- D7's owners are named honestly, most of them not yet planned.
- D10's cost now counts its control arm: 840 base-length lives.
- D13 says retention is reported, not tested.
- The Milward, Barrett and Cheong wording now follows the sources.

### C4 Again, After Review

Written before it runs. The second agent's review found two faults in the account above. The join-tick explanation is
contradicted by the journal of 2026-10-30, whose two lives joined on ticks 69 and 67 and ended in the same closing
state. And the old-pin life had 8 intents refused as stale (`judged … refused_stale`, intent ticks 373 to 378): its
creature's applied rows are 315 `fresh`, 1 `repeated` and 6 `coasted`, against 319 `fresh` and 1 `coasted` at the new
pin, and its world ran 322 ticks against 320. The journal left those refusals out. The explanation above is
withdrawn: the cause of the different closing state is not established.

The test, fixed before it runs: `life.sh` at the old pins, from the same build as above, up to five times. The first
life with no refused intent (`grep -c refused_stale life.log` is 0) is compared with the new-pin life. If its closing
state matches the new pin's to the printed digits, the refusals are the likely cause, and the bump changes nothing in
the placeholder's life. If it differs, the cause is still open and is investigated before the bump merges. If none of
the five is free of refusals, that is reported.

Two smaller omissions, recorded here:

- `arena.sh`'s own life step failed again on the library's name at both pins (`[FATAL] … no library at
  …/libplaceholder_worm.so`, `exit 1`), and `life.sh` stood in, as on earlier nights.
- The run did not record `git submodule status --recursive`. That the Grid's nested link stays at `9b1b771` rests on
  the tree: `git ls-tree` gives it at both tron-grid-lite commits.

#### Result

The first of the five lives, at 08:17:26 UTC from the old-pin build, had no refused intent. It ended at (1.529304,
3.330158, 4.510883), facing 0.174603 rad, the same to every printed digit as the new-pin life. It also joined on tick
67, so its log can be compared row for row. Its applied rows (706, `a0fa1bf5…`) and judged rows (`ffa4ff5a…`) are identical
to the new-pin life's, and Clu agreed 12 of 12 hashes. Its Disk is the same size (648,628 bytes) and differs from the
new-pin Disk only in two bytes, at offsets 65 and 66, which hold the recorded start time (`cmp -l`); the two logs
differ only in their `start` rows, 415 s apart. The commands are as above
(`bash life.sh <dir>`; `grep -c refused_stale`, `grep '^applied '` and `grep '^judged '` of `life.log`, then
`sha256sum`; `stat -c %s life.disk`).

So at the old and new pins, a life without refusals is the same life in everything but its wall-clock start: the bump changes nothing in the
placeholder's world. The earlier old-pin life differed, and it was the one with 8 stale refusals near its end. That
makes the refusals the likely cause, as the plan said, though one clean life does not prove it. Why those intents
arrived late is not known; the Grid's join and its pace run on the wall clock, and the machine was not otherwise
loaded. The lesson for later bumps is to count refused intents in every life before comparing closing states, and
to re-run a life that has any. The join tick alone did not matter, here or on 2026-10-30.

## A Plan to Make the Lab Easy to Enter

The maintainer asked for a plan to tidy the repository and make it presentable, so that new people are not lost,
with more plots, diagrams and flowcharts, as SVG rather than binary images. A survey of the tree as it stands:

- **Size.** The documents run to about 15,200 lines (`find docs -name "*.md" | xargs wc -l`). Fourteen journals
  hold most of them, the longest 1,571 lines. A newcomer has no short path through them: the README's table says where
  things are, not what to read first.
- **No picture of the whole.** How the Grid, Master Control, Link, a Program, the lab and the reference recordings
  fit together is told in prose, across the README, VISION.md and the world's own repositories.
- **Three kinds of idea.** The numbered hypotheses (H1-H15), the maintainer's 128 hunches (A-G) and the experiment
  plans for them are related, but nothing shows how.
- **STATE.md** is 276 lines (`wc -l STATE.md`), though the rules ask for it short. Its *Done* list repeats what the
  journals already hold.
- **Figures.** The ten in `docs/figures/` are all SVG, all from H11's notebooks. The only binary images in the
  repository are 13 PNGs in `reference/gr-fec/results/`, an archive the rules keep exactly as given; they stay, and
  any figure the lab draws from that work is made anew as SVG.
- **Tools.** No diagram tool is installed on the cloud machine (`which dot mmdc` finds none), so diagrams are written
  as SVG by Python in a notebook, which needs nothing beyond what `notebooks/requirements.txt` pins. Plots use
  matplotlib, already pinned.

The plan is item 11 of STATE.md's *Next* list, split into eight runs, (a) to (h). Diagrams come first because the
README's tour (d) is built around them. Every figure is made by the notebook, regenerates without a change, and is
captioned with what made it.
